## Ruleset 1 — Character Attributes, Skills, Potential, and Growth

### 1. The 0–100 scale

Attributes and skills use a **0–100 natural scale**. The scale is absolute enough that a 50 means roughly the same thing across generations, rather than being a percentile that changes depending on who is alive.

**100 is not the strongest something can ever become.** It represents the approximate upper boundary of naturally developed, unenhanced human/shinobi capability in that category. Transformations, gates, jinchūriki states, Sage Mode, drugs, cursed seals, special techniques, and other supernatural enhancements can push an **effective attribute above 100**.

| Score | General Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0–9 | Extremely deficient / undeveloped |
| 10–19 | Young child or very weak civilian |
| 20–29 | Ordinary civilian / early Academy level |
| 30–39 | Trained Academy graduate / rookie shinobi |
| 40–49 | Competent Genin-level capability |
| 50–59 | Experienced professional shinobi |
| 60–69 | Strong Chūnin / specialist capability |
| 70–79 | Jōnin-level excellence |
| 80–89 | Elite specialist; extremely uncommon |
| 90–94 | Master-level; among the best of an era |
| 95–99 | Legendary natural ability |
| 100 | Extreme natural limit |

The scale becomes progressively harder to climb. Going from 30 → 40 is dramatically easier than going from 80 → 90.

So **Strength 43** would mean something concrete: the character possesses clearly superhuman shinobi conditioning, is substantially stronger than ordinary adults and most Academy graduates, and would be physically appropriate for a developed Genin or physically weaker Chūnin. They are nowhere near a dedicated strength specialist.

A difference of 2–3 points usually isn't overwhelming. A difference of 5–10 is noticeable. A 15–20-point gap becomes extremely difficult to overcome through raw capability alone.

Technique, tactics, terrain, equipment, teamwork, injuries, and matchup can still overturn those advantages.

# 2. Core Attributes

I recommend nine primary attributes.

| Attribute | Represents |
|---|---|
| **Strength** | Muscular force, striking power, lifting, grappling |
| **Speed** | Movement speed, acceleration, burst movement |
| **Agility** | Balance, coordination, dexterity, body control |
| **Endurance** | Physical stamina, fatigue resistance, durability |
| **Chakra Reserves** | Total usable chakra capacity |
| **Chakra Control** | Precision and efficiency of chakra manipulation |
| **Perception** | Awareness, reaction processing, sensory attention |
| **Intelligence** | Learning, reasoning, memory, tactical comprehension |
| **Willpower** | Mental endurance, discipline, pain tolerance, resistance to panic |

Things such as charisma, attractiveness, reputation, loyalty, and emotional state should exist separately rather than being combat attributes.

---

# 3. Attributes are not skills

This distinction is extremely important.

A character may possess:

**Speed 70 / Taijutsu 32**

They are incredibly fast but poorly trained in hand-to-hand combat.

Another character might have:

**Speed 45 / Taijutsu 72**

They are considerably slower, but their timing, footwork, counters, positioning, and technique are far superior.

That prevents attributes from simply determining everything.

Broad skills can also use the 0–100 scale.

Examples include Taijutsu, Ninjutsu, Genjutsu, Shurikenjutsu, Kenjutsu, Stealth, Tracking, Medical Ninjutsu, Fuinjutsu, Sensory Techniques, Chakra Nature Manipulation, Survival and Strategy.

Individual jutsu can additionally possess their own **Technique Mastery**, so knowing Fire Release at 70 does not automatically mean every Fire Release technique is mastered.

---

# 4. How attributes affect skills

Actions combine skill with supporting attributes.

As a general engine rule:

**~55% Skill  
~35% Supporting Attributes  
~10% Practical Experience**

The weights can change depending upon the activity.

For example, Taijutsu performance could consider:

Taijutsu skill + Agility + Speed + Strength + combat experience.

Medical Ninjutsu could consider:

Medical Ninjutsu + Chakra Control + Intelligence + Perception + medical experience.

This means neither raw talent nor training completely replaces the other.

A brilliant technician with mediocre physical ability can succeed.

A physically extraordinary but poorly trained fighter can also be dangerous.

Neither automatically dominates the other.

# 5. Typical stat ranges

Rank should **never simply equal attributes**. Rank also measures judgment, mission experience, leadership, reliability, versatility, and village needs.

Nevertheless, useful population benchmarks are necessary.

| Character | Typical Attributes |
|---|---:|
| Young civilian child | 10–25 |
| Beginning Academy student | 15–28 |
| Average graduating Academy student | 25–40 |
| Rookie Genin | 28–42 |
| Experienced Genin | 35–50 |
| Typical Chūnin | 45–60 |
| Strong Chūnin | 55–65 |
| Typical Jōnin | 60–75 |
| Elite Jōnin | 70–85 |
| Exceptional/Kage-class specialist | 80–95 |

These are **ranges, not requirements**.

A Genin might have:

Strength 32  
Speed 47  
Agility 43  
Endurance 35  
Chakra 29  
Control 41  
Perception 38  
Intelligence 44  
Willpower 39

Another Genin might have Chakra Reserves 61 but Chakra Control 24.

That kind of uneven development should be extremely common.

Characters with perfectly balanced stats should actually be unusual.

# 6. Academy students

Academy students should change substantially during childhood.

A normal 6–7-year-old entrant might have physical attributes around **15–25**.

By graduation around age 11–13, ordinary students commonly reach approximately **25–40**, depending upon the attribute.

An unusually strong graduate might already possess a 45–50 somewhere.

A genuine prodigy might possess a 55–65 stat or skill at graduation.

That should immediately draw attention from instructors because it is very abnormal.

It should not automatically mean that child is destined to become Kage.

---

# 7. Potential is not one number

I strongly recommend **not having a universal "Potential: 87" statistic**.

Each character instead secretly possesses a development profile for different attributes and skill families.

Potential contains four major components:

**Growth Rate** — how quickly the character responds to effective training.

**Soft Limit** — where improvement begins becoming exceptionally difficult.

**Maturation Curve** — whether the character develops unusually early, normally, or late.

**Retention** — how effectively the character maintains ability during inactivity and aging.

These should mostly be **hidden from the player**.

The player can discover them indirectly.

An instructor might say:

> "Your chakra control is developing unusually quickly."

But the game should not reveal:

> Chakra Control Soft Cap: 82.7

That would turn character development into spreadsheet optimization rather than a life simulation.

---

# 8. Genetics

Genetics should influence development without determining destiny.

For each inheritable developmental trait, the simulation can approximately use:

**50% population baseline  
25% Parent A latent potential  
25% Parent B latent potential  
+ random developmental variance**

Importantly, it should inherit the parents' **latent traits**, not their current trained attributes.

A father with Strength 82 because he trained obsessively does not automatically give his child Strength potential 82.

This produces regression toward the population average while still allowing talented families to disproportionately produce talented children.

It also permits:

ordinary parents → prodigy child

and

legendary parents → ordinary child.

Clan genetics then modify particular domains.

An Uchiha lineage might affect Sharingan inheritance and certain developmental tendencies.

An Akimichi lineage affects physiology.

An Uzumaki lineage could strongly influence vitality and chakra reserves.

But being born into a famous clan should never automatically generate an elite shinobi.

---

# 9. How prodigies are generated

"Prodigy" should be an **emergent classification**, not a predetermined character class.

A prodigy might randomly receive several unusually favorable characteristics:

high learning rate,

high soft limits,

early maturation,

strong technique affinities,

exceptional memory,

unusually developed chakra,

or unusually rapid skill integration.

Approximately **2–3% of Academy-age shinobi** might qualify as notably gifted.

Perhaps **0.2–0.5%** would qualify as extraordinary prodigies recognizable across an entire village.

Much rarer individuals may appear once every several generations.

Crucially, high potential does not guarantee success.

A prodigy can become lazy.

They can receive poor instruction.

They can develop psychological problems.

They can become injured.

They can die young.

They can specialize badly.

Meanwhile, a less naturally talented character may surpass them through decades of discipline.

---

# 10. Soft limits

Every attribute has a hidden Soft Limit.

Suppose someone's Strength soft limit is **68**.

Growth might behave approximately like this:

| Current Strength | Training Efficiency |
|---:|---:|
| Under 48 | ~100% |
| 48–58 | ~80% |
| 59–64 | ~55% |
| 65–68 | ~30% |
| 69–71 | ~15% |
| 72–75 | ~5% |
| 76+ | Routine training nearly useless |

The Soft Limit is therefore **not a hard cap**.

Characters can exceed it.

Doing so requires disproportionately greater time, better training, stronger motivation, superior instruction, breakthroughs, or unusual circumstances.

This is how extreme dedication matters without making genetics irrelevant.

---

# 11. Can ordinary shinobi become elite?

Yes—with an important qualification.

An ordinary person should absolutely be capable of reaching **70–80+ in a particular field** through exceptional dedication.

What should be extraordinarily difficult is becoming elite in **everything**.

Someone with relatively ordinary genetic potential could spend twenty years specializing in Taijutsu and become an 82-level Taijutsu expert.

But that might mean their Ninjutsu is 38, Genjutsu is 24 and Fuinjutsu is 10.

This allows hard-working specialists to exist without making prodigies meaningless.

Reaching **90+ naturally** should generally require some combination of extraordinary aptitude, extraordinary training, extraordinary circumstances and long-term specialization.

Hard work matters enormously.

It simply cannot make every human biologically identical.

---

# 12. How rare high attributes are

Among **active trained shinobi**, rather than civilians:

**70+** in at least one attribute should be uncommon but visible among experienced professionals.

**80+** in a particular attribute should represent approximately the upper **~1%**.

**90+** should be closer to approximately **1 in 2,000 active shinobi** in that particular category.

**95+** might be on the order of **1 in tens of thousands**.

These numbers shouldn't be absolute population quotas. They're generation guidelines.

Multiple elite attributes are much rarer.

Having one 82 does not make someone legendary.

Having:

Strength 84  
Speed 86  
Agility 83  
Endurance 81  
Chakra 88

is an entirely different level of anomaly.

---

# 13. Monthly improvement

Growth should use hidden fractional progress rather than requiring every month to produce a visible stat increase.

For routine focused training, approximate maximum gains could be:

| Current Attribute | Exceptional Monthly Attribute Gain |
|---:|---:|
| 0–39 | up to ~3 |
| 40–59 | up to ~2 |
| 60–74 | up to ~1 |
| 75–84 | up to ~0.5 |
| 85–94 | up to ~0.25 |
| 95+ | generally requires breakthroughs |

Skills develop somewhat faster.

| Current Skill | Exceptional Monthly Skill Gain |
|---:|---:|
| 0–39 | up to ~5 |
| 40–59 | up to ~3 |
| 60–74 | up to ~2 |
| 75–84 | up to ~1 |
| 85–94 | up to ~0.5 |
| 95+ | extremely slow |

Those are **upper bounds**, not expected gains.

Most characters will gain substantially less because training time is divided between multiple subjects, missions, school, recovery, relationships, sleep, injuries and everyday life.

A typical Genin might therefore improve several related abilities by fractions of a point during an ordinary month.

Internal decimals should accumulate invisibly:

**Strength 43.17 → 43.61 → 44.04**

The player simply sees Strength increase from 43 → 44.

That prevents progression from feeling mechanically predictable.

---

# 14. Children and maturation

Children receive both **training growth** and **natural development**.

The rough developmental curve should be:

**Ages 4–7:** rapid neurological development but limited physical capacity.

**8–11:** extremely strong coordination and skill-learning period.

**12–16:** very rapid overall growth as physical maturation begins.

**17–24:** strongest period for physical development and intensive training.

**25–34:** physical peak and skill refinement.

**35–44:** physical growth becomes difficult; expertise can continue increasing.

**45–59:** gradual physical decline unless heavily maintained.

**60+:** increasingly noticeable physical decline, although technique, control and knowledge may remain exceptional.

Characters should also have individual **early/normal/late maturation profiles**.

A weak 10-year-old can therefore become an extraordinary adult.

That is important for avoiding the assumption that childhood rankings permanently determine everyone's future.

---

# 15. Aging and decline

Stats should not suddenly fall because someone turns 40.

Decline depends upon:

age,

injuries,

lifestyle,

training,

illness,

stress,

and the attribute involved.

Speed generally declines earlier than technical skill.

Strength declines more slowly if maintained.

Endurance varies heavily with lifestyle.

Chakra Control may actually continue improving for decades.

Intelligence and technical knowledge can continue increasing well into old age.

Practical experience can compensate for lost physical ability.

Thus an older Jōnin might go from:

Speed 74 → 65

while increasing:

Strategy 71 → 82  
Taijutsu 75 → 80  
Perception 72 → 77.

They become physically weaker while remaining extremely dangerous.

---

# 16. Training versus practical experience

Training and experience should produce different things.

**Training** develops raw capability and controlled execution.

**Practical experience** develops the ability to actually use those capabilities when circumstances become chaotic.

Real experience improves things such as:

timing,

target prioritization,

fear control,

adaptation,

reading opponents,

resource management,

team coordination,

and knowing when not to fight.

This can be represented by a hidden **Applied Experience** value for each activity.

A Genin might possess Taijutsu 55 from extensive training but only perform around a 45–50 level during their first genuine lethal confrontation because panic and uncertainty interfere.

A veteran with the same Taijutsu 55 uses almost all of it reliably.

This creates a very important distinction between:

**talented student**

and

**dangerous veteran**.

---

# 17. What prevents grinding?

The engine should judge **training periods**, not literal repetitions.

The player cannot say:

> I perform 100,000 punches.

and obtain unlimited Strength and Taijutsu gains.

Characters possess a finite monthly **Growth Capacity** based on age, health, recovery, available time and workload.

Training beyond sustainable capacity produces:

diminishing returns,

fatigue,

injuries,

burnout,

reduced mission performance,

or even regression.

Repeated identical training also becomes less useful through adaptation.

Doing the same basic exercise for three years will not continue providing beginner-level progress.

Higher development eventually requires:

better opponents,

stronger teachers,

more difficult exercises,

new techniques,

real-world application,

and sometimes conceptual breakthroughs.

This also prevents the optimal strategy from becoming "train every waking hour."

A character who neglects sleep, relationships, education and recovery should suffer consequences.

---

# 18. Breakthroughs

High-level development should increasingly depend on breakthroughs rather than linear XP.

A character stuck at Chakra Control 82 might train for months without visibly improving.

Then an exceptional teacher, difficult mission, personal insight or new technique could trigger enough accumulated progress to reach 83.

Breakthroughs should not magically grant enormous stat increases.

They unlock progress that routine training had difficulty producing.

This makes elite advancement feel qualitatively different from Academy advancement.

---

# 19. Transformations and temporary enhancement

Transformations modify **effective attributes**, not permanent base attributes.

Someone might normally have:

Strength **72**

and activate a transformation granting approximately +30 effective Strength.

Their current effective Strength becomes:

**102**

Their permanent Strength remains:

**72**.

Therefore scores above 100 are perfectly valid during enhancement.

Powerful forms may reach considerably beyond that.

The system should allow:

100–120 = extreme enhancement  
120–150 = extraordinary supernatural augmentation  
150+ = increasingly abnormal, usually requiring exceptionally powerful abilities.

There does not need to be a universal transformed hard cap.

The cost is what balances it.

Transformation systems can impose chakra consumption, duration limits, control penalties, physical damage, shortened lifespan, cooldowns, unconsciousness or death.

Most importantly, **temporary enhancement does not count as permanent growth**.

Using a form that gives Speed 115 does not allow the character to train as though their natural Speed were 115.

Their underlying Speed remains unchanged.

---

# 20. Transformation scaling

Forms should generally have **stat-specific modifiers rather than one universal power multiplier**.

For example, a hypothetical transformation could provide:

Strength +20  
Speed +25  
Agility +10  
Endurance +15  
Chakra output +30  
Chakra Control −8

Another transformation might greatly improve perception and chakra while barely affecting physical Strength.

This makes transformations mechanically distinct instead of simply being:

> Form A = +50% stronger.

It also makes matchup differences far more interesting.

---

## The most important design principle

The system should make this possible:

A character born with average potential can become exceptional.

A prodigy can waste their gifts.

A weak child can become a powerful adult.

An elite shinobi can decline.

An elderly veteran can remain dangerous despite losing physical ability.

A Genin can have one freakishly powerful attribute without secretly being "Kage level."

And two characters with the exact same overall statistical total can fight completely differently.

That gives **Shinobi Life** the kind of generational simulation we want instead of making every character's destiny apparent from their starting numbers.

The next thing I would build from this is **the exact hidden growth algorithm**—including monthly training capacity, training intensity, teacher quality, age modifiers, fatigue, specialization, injury risk, and the formula the game engine actually uses when deciding whether Strength 43 becomes 44. That would turn these principles into something we can reliably simulate.

# Skill Tier Framework

I would standardize important skills around something like:

### Tier 0 — Untrained
The character has little meaningful training.

### Tier I — Basic
Foundational competency.

### Tier II — Trained
Reliable use under normal conditions.

### Tier III — Proficient
Professional-level competence.

### Tier IV — Advanced
High-level professional application.

### Tier V — Expert
Exceptional technical mastery.

### Tier VI — Master
Rare expertise.

### Tier VII — Grandmaster
Historic or generation-defining skill.

Not every skill needs all seven tiers.

For example:

**Camping**
might only have I–III.

**Chakra Control**
could have I–VII.

**Medical Ninjutsu**
could have I–VII.

**Academy Mathematics**
might stop at III and then branch into more specialized knowledge.

This prevents unnecessary complexity.

Higher skill tiers should absolutely require minimum attributes.

That creates a natural relationship between talent and training.

For example:

### Chakra Control

**Basic Chakra Control I**
- Chakra 10
- Intelligence 10

**Basic Chakra Control II**
- Chakra 18
- Intelligence 15

**Basic Chakra Control III**
- Chakra 25
- Intelligence 20
- Agility 18

**Advanced Chakra Control I**
- Chakra 30
- Intelligence 28
- Agility 25
- Perception 20

**Advanced Chakra Control II**
- Chakra 38
- Intelligence 35
- Agility 30

**Advanced Chakra Control III**
- Chakra 45
- Intelligence 40
- Agility 35

And elite levels might eventually require very high attributes.

However, I wouldn't make every prerequisite a hard cutoff.

There should be two kinds.

### Hard requirements

The technique is physically or mentally impossible without reaching the requirement.

Example:

A character may simply lack enough Chakra to safely perform a technique.

### Soft requirements

The character can attempt progression below the threshold, but advancement becomes extremely slow.

Example:

An unusually dedicated character with Intelligence 36 might eventually master a skill normally requiring Intelligence 40.

That leaves room for exceptional effort.

# NPC rank soft limits

This is another very good addition.

NPC generation shouldn't just randomly select values from the entire possible range.

Each rank gets a **development expectation profile**.

For example:

### Academy Student
Typical:
- Attributes 15–30
- Skills Tier 0–II

Soft limit:
- ~35 attributes
- Tier III skill

Characters exceeding that need an explanation.

### Genin

Typical:
- Attributes 25–40
- Primary skills Tier II–III

Soft limit:
- ~45–50 attributes
- Tier IV skill

Beyond that, the simulation asks:

Why hasn't this character been promoted?

Possible answers:

- very young
- poor judgment
- failed exams
- politically blocked
- recently graduated
- extremely specialized
- village understaffing/overstaffing
- deliberately refusing advancement
- poor mission record
- disciplinary problems

So rank never becomes a hard power classification.

# Rank expectations should apply primarily to NPC creation

This distinction is important.

## Component C — NPC Rank Generation Ranges

This component should make NPC creation believable without turning rank into a strict power level.

The core rule:

> **Age primarily constrains Attributes. Rank primarily constrains Skills, Mastery, experience, and professional competence.**

A Genin can be unusually strong. A Jōnin can be physically unimpressive. But NPC generation should usually create characters who make sense for their rank unless another variable explains the exception.

---

# 1. Academy Student

Academy students are still developing foundational abilities.

### Typical profile

**Attributes**
- Usually within their normal age band
- Most often around the lower-to-middle portion of that range
- One unusually high Attribute is possible in talented students

**Skills**
- Mostly Tier I
- Some Tier II skills near graduation
- Very limited specialization
- Technique mastery usually low to moderate

**Experience**
- Minimal real combat experience
- Limited exposure to genuine danger
- Heavily training-dependent

### Soft expectations

A graduating Academy student might reasonably have:

- 3–6 skills at Tier I
- 1–3 skills at Tier II
- a handful of basic techniques with moderate mastery

A Tier III skill at this stage should be unusual.

A Tier IV skill should be exceptionally rare and require a clear explanation.

---

# 2. Rookie Genin

A newly promoted Genin is professionally qualified at a basic level, but still inexperienced.

### Typical profile

**Attributes**
- Primarily determined by age
- Generally normal to above-average for their age
- Some may have one exceptional Attribute

**Skills**
- Core shinobi skills around Tier II
- Strongest areas may reach Tier III
- Weak areas can remain Tier I

**Technique Mastery**
- Good mastery of Academy basics
- Moderate mastery of commonly used personal techniques
- New or advanced techniques remain inconsistent

**Experience**
- Low mission experience
- Limited real combat
- Decision-making under pressure still developing

### Example

A 13-year-old rookie Genin might look like:

**Attributes**
- Agility 35
- Intelligence 31
- Strength 28
- Endurance 30
- Perception 33
- Willpower 29
- Chakra 37

**Skills**
- Taijutsu II — 48/100
- Ninjutsu II — 31/100
- Chakra Control II — 63/100
- Hand Signs II — 52/100
- Shurikenjutsu II — 44/100
- Stealth I — 81/100

Nothing here needs to be extraordinary.

---

# 3. Experienced Genin

This should be a distinct generation category from rookie Genin.

Otherwise a 12-year-old fresh graduate and a 17-year-old who has spent four years on missions get treated identically.

### Typical profile

**Skills**
- Several Tier II skills
- 1–3 Tier III skills
- Sometimes one highly developed specialty

**Mastery**
- Reliable mastery of frequently used techniques
- Signature techniques begin emerging

**Experience**
- Meaningful mission history
- Some combat exposure
- Better judgment under pressure

### Soft limit

Tier IV skill proficiency should be uncommon among ordinary Genin.

Not impossible—just unusual enough that the engine should ask why the character remains Genin.

Possible reasons include:

- very young
- poor leadership
- poor academics
- disciplinary issues
- failed promotion exams
- extremely narrow specialization
- political or administrative reasons

---

# 4. Chūnin

Chūnin should represent **professional competence**, not simply higher stats.

A Chūnin should generally be able to operate independently, make reasonable decisions, and sometimes supervise others.

### Typical profile

**Attributes**
- Still primarily age-dependent
- More likely than Genin to sit in the middle or upper part of normal adult ranges

**Skills**
- Core professional skills around Tier III
- Several Tier II–III secondary skills
- One or more Tier IV specialties are plausible

**Mastery**
- Frequently used techniques generally reliable
- Some techniques highly mastered

**Experience**
- Significant mission exposure
- Real combat experience
- Better tactical judgment
- Demonstrated reliability

### Typical Chūnin skill pattern

For example:

- Taijutsu III
- Chakra Control III
- Hand Signs III
- Ninjutsu III
- Stealth II
- Tracking II
- Fire Release IV

That character is broadly competent but specializes in Fire Release.

---

# 5. Jōnin

Jōnin should be rare because they combine **high competence, experience, reliability, and specialization**.

They should not simply be Chūnin with +15 to every Attribute.

### Typical profile

**Attributes**
- Usually developed adult attributes
- Often one or more exceptional Attributes
- No requirement for uniformly high values

**Skills**
- Most relevant core skills at Tier III–IV
- Multiple Tier IV skills common
- Tier V specialization likely

**Mastery**
- Signature techniques often extremely refined
- Broad repertoire or very deep specialization

**Experience**
- Extensive mission history
- Strong real-world judgment
- High independence
- Experience with dangerous opponents and abnormal situations

### Important

A Jōnin might have:

Strength 44

but:

- Intelligence 68
- Perception 72
- Tracking V
- Stealth V
- Tactical Analysis V

They are still entirely believable as a high-level shinobi.

---

# 6. Elite Jōnin / Exceptional Specialists

I would avoid making this a universal formal rank unless the setting specifically needs it.

Instead, treat it as an **NPC generation category**.

These characters may possess:

- Tier V–VI skills
- 70+ Attributes
- 80+ exceptional Attributes
- very high technique mastery
- rare specializations
- extensive high-level experience

These are the shinobi other professionals recognize by reputation.

The key distinction is that this is a **capability classification**, not necessarily an official village rank.

We should formalize this lightly.

| Rank | Common Skill Ceiling | Unusual Above |
|---|---|---|
| Academy | Tier II | Tier III+ |
| Rookie Genin | Tier III | Tier IV+ |
| Experienced Genin | Tier III | Tier IV–V |
| Chūnin | Tier IV | Tier V+ |
| Jōnin | Tier V | Tier VI+ |

It should attach an explanation.

I think we should deliberately avoid rigid Attribute ranges by rank.

Instead, NPC generation should use something like:

### Academy
Age curve dominates almost entirely.

### Genin
Age curve + moderate training modifier.

### Chūnin
Age curve + substantial professional training.

### Jōnin
Age curve + extensive professional development + higher selection pressure.

| Rank | Typical Adult Attribute Pattern |
|---|---|
| Civilian | mostly 20–35 |

A 14-year-old Genin should still be judged using the **14-year-old age curve**, not the adult Genin range.

# 9. Rank should depend on more than Skill

This is critical for simulation consistency.

Promotion can consider:

- Skill proficiency
- Mission record
- Experience
- judgment
- leadership
- reliability
- teamwork
- psychological stability under pressure
- village needs
- exam results
- political or administrative circumstances

That means two characters with near-identical stats can hold different ranks.

And that is good.

When creating an NPC, the engine should follow this order:

1. **Determine age**
2. **Generate Attributes from age curve**
3. **Apply genetics/potential**
4. **Apply maturation profile**
5. **Apply training history**
6. **Determine rank**
7. **Generate skills appropriate to rank and history**
8. **Generate technique mastery**
9. **Check for unusual mismatches**
10. **If mismatch exists, create an explanation**

That last step is important.

Example:

**Age 15 / Genin / Taijutsu V**

The engine flags:

> Exceptional skill for rank.

Then gives the NPC a reason:

> Extremely talented taijutsu specialist who repeatedly fails promotion because of poor tactical judgment.

That turns statistical anomalies into character history.

We can keep these simple:

**Normal**  
Fits expected range.

**Notable**  
Slightly unusual but requires no special explanation.

**Exceptional**  
Should influence NPC background.

**Extreme**  
Must have a meaningful explanation.

That same framework can later apply to age-relative Attributes.

To avoid scope creep, this component does not determine:

- Chūnin Exam structure
- mission rank
- village politics
- leadership mechanics
- reputation
- combat effectiveness

It only tells the generator:

> "What kind of developmental profile is believable for this rank?"

That is enough.

## Component D — Skill Tier Definitions

This is one of the most important pieces because it determines how most long-term progression actually feels.

The core rule should be:

> **Attributes determine capacity. Skill tiers represent increasingly advanced levels of application. Progress within a tier represents refinement at that level.**

So:

**Taijutsu III — 15/100**

and

**Taijutsu III — 92/100**

are both Tier III practitioners, but the second is substantially more polished and much closer to being ready for Tier IV.

---

# 1. Universal Skill Tier Structure

I think six normal tiers are enough.

A seventh can exist only for exceptional skills where legendary mastery needs additional room.

| Tier | Name | General Meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| I | Novice | Understands the fundamentals |
| II | Trained | Can use the skill reliably |
| III | Proficient | Professional working competency |
| IV | Advanced | Highly developed professional skill |
| V | Expert | Exceptional specialist |
| VI | Master | Rare mastery |
| VII | Grandmaster | Historic or generation-defining ability |

Most skills should stop at **Tier III–V**.

Only major disciplines such as Taijutsu, Chakra Control, Medical Ninjutsu, Fūinjutsu, Genjutsu, and similar deep fields need Tier VI or VII.

This prevents every trivial ability from becoming enormous.

---

# 2. What Progress Within a Tier Means

Each tier has:

**0–100 proficiency**

This measures refinement **within that level of complexity**.

A useful interpretation:

| Progress | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0–19 | Newly entered tier |
| 20–39 | Developing |
| 40–59 | Competent |
| 60–79 | Strong |
| 80–94 | Highly refined |
| 95–100 | Near-complete mastery of tier concepts |

This means the character doesn't suddenly become incompetent when entering a new tier.

For example:

**Chakra Control II — 100/100**

might transition into:

**Chakra Control III — 0/100**

Tier III 0/100 is still superior overall because the character has mastered all previous concepts and has begun learning a more advanced level.

---

# 3. Tiers Are Cumulative

This should be a formal rule.

> Advancement never replaces previous training.

A Tier IV practitioner inherently retains Tier I–III competency unless something unusual causes skill degradation.

We therefore don't need to store:

Tier I 100  
Tier II 100  
Tier III 100  
Tier IV 47

The character sheet can simply show:

**Taijutsu IV — 47/100**

The completed previous tiers are assumed.

Internally, the simulation can preserve history if needed, but gameplay doesn't need the clutter.

---

# 4. Tier I — Novice

The character has learned the foundational concepts of the skill.

They:

- understand basic principles
- can perform simple applications
- require concentration
- make frequent mistakes
- struggle under stress
- lack efficiency and adaptability

Examples:

**Hand Signs I**  
Can correctly form common seals but slowly and deliberately.

**Chakra Control I**  
Can deliberately manipulate chakra in basic exercises.

**Taijutsu I**  
Knows basic stances, strikes, guards, and movement.

This is common among Academy students.

---

# 5. Tier II — Trained

The skill is functional and relatively dependable.

They:

- perform fundamentals consistently
- require less conscious thought
- can use the skill under moderate pressure
- understand common variations
- make fewer major errors

This is where someone begins looking genuinely trained rather than merely instructed.

Examples:

**Hand Signs II**  
Common sequences become fluid.

**Taijutsu II**  
Can fight using structured technique rather than basic instinct.

**Chakra Control II**  
Maintains basic chakra manipulation reliably during ordinary activity.

This should be common among Genin.

---

# 6. Tier III — Proficient

This should be our **professional competence threshold**.

A Tier III practitioner can use the skill reliably in real shinobi work.

They:

- perform efficiently
- adapt basic techniques to circumstances
- maintain the skill under pressure
- recognize errors
- understand underlying principles
- no longer need constant supervision

This is an important threshold because:

> **Tier III means "I can professionally rely on this skill."**

A Chūnin should generally possess several Tier III competencies.

---

# 7. Tier IV — Advanced

Tier IV represents substantial specialization.

The character:

- understands advanced applications
- adapts techniques creatively
- performs efficiently under difficult conditions
- diagnoses weaknesses in others
- handles unusual scenarios
- begins developing personal style

This is where the difference between a generalist and specialist becomes obvious.

A strong Chūnin might have one Tier IV specialty.

A Jōnin may have several.

---

# 8. Tier V — Expert

Tier V practitioners are recognized specialists.

They:

- have deep understanding of the discipline
- perform advanced applications consistently
- innovate within existing methods
- exploit subtle efficiencies
- teach advanced students
- handle highly abnormal situations

At this level, improvement becomes considerably slower.

Tier V should be uncommon even among Jōnin.

---

# 9. Tier VI — Master

Tier VI represents mastery rare enough to earn serious professional recognition.

The practitioner:

- possesses exceptional technical understanding
- has few meaningful weaknesses within the discipline
- can create or significantly modify advanced methods
- may teach other experts
- performs at a level difficult for most professionals to imitate

This should be genuinely rare.

A village might have only a limited number of Tier VI practitioners in certain disciplines.

---

# 10. Tier VII — Grandmaster

Tier VII should **not be a standard expectation**.

It exists for disciplines where we need room for legendary specialists.

A Tier VII practitioner may:

- redefine the discipline
- create techniques others consider revolutionary
- possess knowledge spanning nearly the entire field
- perform applications believed impossible by ordinary experts
- become historically remembered for that skill

Not every generation needs one.

And many skills should never use Tier VII at all.

---

# 11. Unlocking a Higher Tier

Reaching **100/100** is necessary but not always sufficient.

Tier advancement can require four categories.

### A. Prior-Tier Completion

Usually:

**Current Tier = 100/100**

This is the simplest prerequisite.

### B. Attribute Requirements

Advanced application may require stronger foundations.

For example:

**Hand Signs IV**
might require:

Agility 42  
Intelligence 35  
Perception 32

These aren't necessarily universal values—we'll define them per skill later.

### C. Knowledge / Access Requirements

Some levels can't reasonably be discovered through repetition alone.

They may require:

- instruction
- advanced texts
- mentorship
- observation
- experimentation
- specialized facilities

### D. Experience Requirements

Certain advanced skills need practical application.

Someone cannot become a master battlefield medic purely by practicing on training dummies.

---

# 12. Hard vs Soft Requirements

We should preserve the distinction we discussed earlier.

### Hard Requirements

The character fundamentally cannot progress without them.

Example:

A technique requires more chakra than the character can produce safely.

### Soft Requirements

Progress remains possible but becomes significantly harder.

Example:

Tier IV theoretical material recommends Intelligence 45, but someone with Intelligence 40 can compensate through extensive study.

This is important because otherwise the system becomes:

> Intelligence 44 = impossible  
> Intelligence 45 = suddenly possible

which feels artificial.

---

# 13. Attribute Requirements Should Increase with Tier

Low-level skills should be accessible to almost everyone.

Higher tiers increasingly depend on natural foundations.

A generalized template could look like:

| Tier | Typical Attribute Requirement |
|---:|---:|
| I | 10–20 |
| II | 15–30 |
| III | 25–40 |
| IV | 35–50 |
| V | 45–65 |
| VI | 55–75 |
| VII | 70+ |

These are not universal hard requirements.

They are a design guideline for building specific skills.

Different skills emphasize different Attributes.

---

# 14. Skills Can Depend on Multiple Attributes

This is important.

For example:

### Hand Signs

Primary:
- Agility

Secondary:
- Intelligence
- Perception

### Taijutsu

Primary:
- Agility
- Strength

Secondary:
- Endurance
- Perception

### Genjutsu

Primary:
- Intelligence
- Chakra

Secondary:
- Perception
- Willpower

### Chakra Control

Primary:
- Chakra
- Agility

Secondary:
- Intelligence
- Perception

This means characters can reach the same skill tier through somewhat different foundations.

A very intelligent character may compensate somewhat for lower Agility in Chakra Control.

Another may rely on extraordinary natural dexterity.

---

# 15. Skill Progress Should Slow by Tier

Training efficiency should decline sharply.

For the same amount of quality training:

Tier I progresses quickly.

Tier II progresses reasonably quickly.

Tier III requires sustained effort.

Tier IV takes significant time.

Tier V may take years.

Tier VI can take decades.

Tier VII may never be reached.

This is more important than artificially slowing every Attribute.

It creates a natural long-term progression curve.

---

# 16. Skill Decay

Skills should generally decay **much more slowly than Attributes**.

Unused skills may lose:

- speed
- precision
- confidence
- automatic execution

But foundational knowledge remains.

Someone who once reached:

**Kenjutsu V — 74**

and hasn't used a sword for fifteen years should not become:

**Kenjutsu I — 20**

Instead they might retain Tier V knowledge while temporarily performing below their previous refinement.

For now, the only rule we need is:

> Skill degradation affects proficiency before it removes tiers.

We can refine exact decay later if needed.

---

# 17. Practical Experience Matters Without Becoming Another Skill

To avoid scope creep, I would **not** create "Combat Experience 0–100."

Instead, relevant experience acts as a modifier to skill growth and execution.

Examples:

A character repeatedly using Taijutsu in real missions becomes better at applying their Taijutsu under pressure.

A medic treating real injuries develops differently from someone practicing controlled exercises.

Experience therefore:

- improves training quality
- helps unlock higher tiers
- improves consistency
- contributes to mastery

It doesn't need its own giant progression tree.

---

# 18. Broad Skill vs Specialization

We should keep this concept but not fully design it yet.

Example:

**Taijutsu IV — 52**

could later support:

- Strong Fist
- grappling
- defensive fighting
- counter-fighting

Likewise:

**Ninjutsu III**

could support elemental or technical specializations.

The important scope rule is:

> Ruleset 1 defines that specialization exists. Specific specialization trees belong to their relevant rulesets.

---

# 19. Technique Mastery Remains Separate

This now gives us three clean progression layers:

### Attributes

**Agility 51**

Foundation.

### Skill

**Hand Signs IV — 63/100**

General trained application.

### Technique Mastery

**Fire Release: Great Fireball — 84/100**

Specific familiarity.

That hierarchy should remain one of the central design rules.

They qualify.

Growth now becomes slower because the material itself is more difficult.

They currently have:

Chakra 43

## Component E — Growth and Training Calculation

This is the piece that makes the previous systems actually move over time. I think we should deliberately keep the underlying calculation **simple enough for the engine to use consistently**, while still producing believable variation.

The core rule:

> **Time spent does not directly equal growth. Training creates Development Progress, which is modified by training quality, aptitude, age, current level, recovery, and soft limits.**

This prevents "I train for 12 hours every day" from automatically producing absurd advancement.

---

# 1. Three kinds of growth

Every development event should fall into one of three categories.

### Attribute Growth

Slow changes to foundational capability.

Examples:

Strength 34.2 → 34.5  
Agility 41.7 → 41.9

Attributes should usually take **months or years** to change significantly.

### Skill Growth

Improvement in a general learned discipline.

Example:

**Taijutsu III — 42/100 → 49/100**

This should account for most normal training progression.

### Technique Mastery

Improvement in a specific jutsu or technique.

Example:

**Great Fireball Mastery 61 → 67**

This can often grow faster than broad Skills because the character is practicing something narrower.

---

# 2. Monthly Development Capacity

Every character receives a hidden **Development Capacity** each month.

This represents how much meaningful adaptation their body and mind can actually absorb.

It is influenced primarily by:

- age
- health
- recovery
- current workload
- injuries
- Willpower
- Endurance
- training history

The important point is:

> Training time and Development Capacity are not the same thing.

A character might train twice as many hours without receiving twice as much growth.

Once their useful capacity is exhausted, additional training provides sharply diminishing returns.

---

# 3. Training Load

Training should use broad intensity levels rather than hour-by-hour accounting.

### Light

Maintenance or casual practice.

Low fatigue.  
Low injury risk.  
Small development.

### Moderate

Normal sustainable training.

This should be the default for most Academy students and working shinobi.

### Heavy

Significant focused training.

Greater growth potential but meaningful fatigue.

### Extreme

Short-term intensive training.

High fatigue, injury risk, and recovery requirements.

Unsustainable over long periods.

This is enough granularity.

We do not need to calculate every push-up.

---

# 4. The basic growth formula

Conceptually:

**Growth = Base Progress × Training Quality × Aptitude × Age Modifier × Difficulty Modifier × Recovery Modifier × Soft-Limit Modifier**

We don't necessarily need to expose the formula to the player.

The engine simply uses it consistently.

Each term can remain fairly compact.

---

# 5. Base Progress

Base Progress comes from the amount and intensity of meaningful training.

For example, one month of focused moderate training in a primary Skill might generate approximately:

**8–12 base Skill progress**

before modifiers.

Heavy training might generate:

**12–18**

Light training:

**2–6**

These are starting values, not guaranteed gains.

---

# 6. Training Quality

Not all training is equally useful.

Suggested range:

| Training Quality | Modifier |
|---|---:|
| Poor / repetitive | ×0.5 |
| Basic unsupervised | ×0.75 |
| Normal structured | ×1.0 |
| Good instruction | ×1.15 |
| Excellent instruction | ×1.3 |
| Exceptional specialized training | ×1.5 |

We should probably avoid modifiers much larger than this.

A legendary teacher should help substantially, not magically multiply growth tenfold.

---

# 7. Aptitude

Every character has hidden aptitudes for different skills and Attributes.

Suggested broad modifier:

| Aptitude | Modifier |
|---|---:|
| Poor | ×0.65 |
| Below average | ×0.8 |
| Average | ×1.0 |
| Talented | ×1.2 |
| Gifted | ×1.4 |
| Exceptional | ×1.6 |

Extreme prodigies might occasionally exceed this.

A gifted child and an average child may look similar after one month.

| IV | ×0.7 |

| Heavy fatigue | ×0.4 |
| Severe exhaustion | ×0.1 |

**Strength Soft Limit: 63**

| Below 48 | ×1.0 |
| 48–55 | ×0.8 |
| 56–60 | ×0.55 |
| 61–63 | ×0.3 |
| 64–67 | ×0.15 |
| 68+ | ×0.05 |

The exact thresholds can scale relative to each person's limit.

Skills can use a similar concept through tier requirements and aptitude instead of needing a separate hard soft-limit calculation everywhere.

This is essential.

A month of focused training might produce:

**Skill:** +8 progress

while the supporting Attribute receives:

That feels appropriate.

A particularly productive year might raise a teenage character:

Strength 34 → 38

while their Taijutsu moves through a large portion of an entire tier.

This keeps Attributes foundational.

For example, a Genin spending a month mainly training:

- 50% Taijutsu
- 25% Chakra Control
- 15% Strength conditioning
- 10% Fireball practice

should not receive full monthly growth in all four areas.

The simulator can internally divide Development Capacity according to focus.

We don't need the player to manually assign percentages unless they want to.

Commands such as:

> Focus heavily on Taijutsu this month.

are enough.

The engine can interpret that distribution.

# 14. Cross-training

Training one thing can provide small benefits elsewhere.

Taijutsu training might contribute:

Primary:
- Taijutsu

Secondary:
- Agility
- Strength
- Endurance

Minor:
- Perception

Primary:
- Chakra Control

Secondary:
- Chakra
- Agility
- Intelligence

Practical experience should differ from controlled training.

### Training is better for:

- learning fundamentals
- correcting technique
- deliberate repetition
- experimenting safely
- targeted Attribute development

### Real experience is better for:

- adaptability
- consistency under pressure
- decision-making
- overcoming fear
- recognizing patterns
- Technique Mastery
- higher-tier advancement

**+0–10%**

Meaningful challenge:
**+10–25%**

Serious high-pressure use:
**+25–50%**

Exceptional breakthrough circumstances:
potentially more, but rarely.

This bonus should apply to development earned from the event—not simply award gigantic free stat increases.

Characters learn less from situations that are far beneath their ability.

A Tier V Taijutsu practitioner sparring against Academy beginners should receive almost no meaningful growth.

Likewise, fighting an opponent so overwhelmingly superior that the character cannot meaningfully participate may teach very little.

The ideal growth zone is:

> difficult enough to force adaptation, but not so overwhelming that meaningful practice becomes impossible.

This gives us a natural anti-grinding mechanic.

Repeated identical training becomes less useful.

For example:

Month 4:
still helpful.

Month 12:
minimal growth unless difficulty changes.

- greater difficulty
- new exercises
- stronger opponents
- better instruction
- environmental variation
- advanced techniques

We do not need to show Training Familiarity numerically.

Training beyond sustainable levels can cause:

**mild overtraining**
- fatigue
- reduced efficiency

**moderate overtraining**
- persistent exhaustion
- reduced mission performance
- slower recovery

**severe overtraining**
- injury
- burnout
- temporary Attribute penalties
- stalled development

Extreme dedication remains possible.

It simply has costs.

After heavy physical training, adaptation occurs during recovery.

**train → recover → adapt**

**train every second → become god**

It also makes Endurance useful because high-Endurance characters recover from workloads more effectively.

- the character encounters a new concept
- an instructor corrects a fundamental problem
- real experience reveals something training did not
- the character has accumulated stalled progress

A breakthrough might:

- unlock a higher Skill Tier
- temporarily improve learning efficiency
- resolve a soft requirement
- produce a noticeable burst of Skill progress

It should **not** randomly give +10 Strength.

## Component F — Potential, Soft Limits, Genetics, and Prodigy Generation

This component determines **why two characters exposed to similar training develop differently**.

The core rule should be:

> **Potential influences the ease, speed, and eventual difficulty of growth. It does not determine destiny.**

A character with average potential can still become exceptional through specialization and sustained effort. A prodigy can waste their advantages.

---

# 1. Potential should remain mostly hidden

The player should not normally see:

**Strength Potential: 83**  
**Taijutsu Aptitude: 1.34×**

That would encourage optimization around hidden numbers.

Instead, the engine stores those values internally and reveals them indirectly through:

- rate of improvement
- instructor observations
- unusually easy or difficult learning
- early achievement
- repeated plateaus

A teacher might say:

> "You pick up chakra-control exercises unusually quickly."

That tells the player something useful without exposing the spreadsheet underneath.

---

# 2. Potential has four required parts

We do not need a giant talent system.

Every character only needs:

### Attribute Potential

How easily each of the seven Attributes develops and where severe diminishing returns begin.

### Skill Aptitude

How efficiently the character learns particular skills or skill families.

### Maturation Profile

Whether development tends to occur early, normally, or late.

### Exceptional Traits

Rare deviations such as unusually large Chakra potential, extraordinary perception, unusual physiology, or inherited clan traits.

That's enough.

---

# 3. Attribute Potential

Each Attribute should receive a hidden **Soft Limit**.

For example:

**Strength Soft Limit: 58**

This does **not** mean:

> Strength can never exceed 58.

It means:

> Ordinary training becomes increasingly inefficient around 58.

Another person may have:

**Strength Soft Limit: 76**

and therefore continue developing much more easily.

---

# 4. Soft limits should be distributed around population norms

Most people should cluster relatively close together.

A reasonable adult shinobi distribution might look roughly like:

| Hidden Soft Limit | Population Meaning |
|---:|---|
| Below 40 | Low potential |
| 40–49 | Below average |
| 50–59 | Typical |
| 60–69 | Above average |
| 70–79 | Exceptional |
| 80–89 | Rare |
| 90+ | Extraordinary |

This is **potential**, not current ability.

Someone with a Strength soft limit of 78 might only have Strength 31 because they never trained seriously.

Likewise, someone with a soft limit of 52 might push themselves to Strength 61 through years of extreme specialization.

---

# 5. The soft limit is a curve, not a wall

Growth efficiency should gradually fall as the character approaches their limit.

For example, if the hidden soft limit is 60:

- Strength 35 → growth normally
- Strength 50 → somewhat slower
- Strength 57 → noticeably difficult
- Strength 60 → very difficult
- Strength 64 → extremely difficult
- Strength 70 → extraordinary commitment required

This avoids artificial behavior such as:

> 59.99 = easy  
> 60.00 = impossible

---

# 6. Exceeding the soft limit

Characters can exceed their limits through:

- extreme dedication
- superior instruction
- highly specialized training
- favorable circumstances
- sustained practical experience
- breakthroughs
- unusual physiology

But every point beyond the soft limit becomes increasingly expensive.

This is how ordinary people can become elite without making genetics meaningless.

A character with average Strength potential might become:

**Strength 67**

after dedicating much of their life to physical development.

What is unlikely is for that same character to also effortlessly reach:

Agility 75  
Chakra 78  
Perception 74  
Intelligence 76

There simply isn't enough time, developmental capacity, and aptitude to maximize everything.

---

# 7. Skill Aptitude

Attributes and Skill Aptitude should be independent enough to create interesting characters.

Someone might have:

High Agility  
Average Taijutsu aptitude  
Exceptional Kenjutsu aptitude

Another might have:

Average Intelligence  
Exceptional Fūinjutsu aptitude

Skill aptitude represents things like:

- intuitive understanding
- learning efficiency
- natural feel for the discipline
- pattern recognition specific to that field

It modifies growth, not the maximum tier directly.

---

# 8. Aptitude categories

We can keep the hidden aptitude system compact:

| Aptitude | Approximate Growth Modifier |
|---|---:|
| Poor | ×0.65 |
| Below Average | ×0.8 |
| Average | ×1.0 |
| Talented | ×1.2 |
| Gifted | ×1.4 |
| Exceptional | ×1.6 |

Rare anomalies could slightly exceed this.

Most characters should have mostly average aptitudes, with a few strengths and weaknesses.

---

# 9. Aptitudes should be correlated, not fully random

This helps characters feel coherent.

For example, someone with naturally strong:

Agility  
Perception

may have increased probability of aptitude in:

- Taijutsu
- Shurikenjutsu
- Kenjutsu
- Hand Signs

But it should not be automatic.

Attributes create **probability**, not destiny.

A very agile person can still have mediocre Taijutsu instincts.

---

# 10. Genetics

We should keep inheritance simple.

For each Attribute's potential, the engine should roughly consider:

**Parent A potential**  
**Parent B potential**  
**population average**  
**random variation**

The child should tend toward the parents while also regressing somewhat toward the population norm.

That means:

Exceptional parents often produce talented children.

But:

Exceptional parents can produce ordinary children.

And:

Ordinary parents can occasionally produce exceptional children.

That gives us realistic variation without needing chromosome simulation.

---

# 11. Suggested inheritance logic

Conceptually:

**Child Potential = parental influence + population pull + random variation**

Not necessarily a literal displayed equation.

Something around:

- 50% parental influence
- 35% population baseline
- 15% random developmental variation

would produce reasonable regression toward the mean.

The exact percentages can be tuned later.

---

# 12. Parents pass potential, not trained ability

This should be a hard rule.

Suppose:

Father:
**Strength 83**

but his hidden Strength soft limit was only 67 and he achieved 83 through twenty years of extreme specialization.

His child does **not** inherit "83 Strength genetics."

The child inherits from the father's underlying biological tendencies.

Conversely, a parent with:

Strength 42

might secretly possess excellent Strength potential but never trained enough to express it.

Their child could inherit that potential.

This prevents generational stat inflation.

---

# 13. Clan inheritance

Clan traits should modify the inheritance process rather than replace it.

Examples might include tendencies toward:

- larger Chakra foundations
- stronger vitality
- particular sensory capability
- unusual physiology
- hereditary techniques

But Ruleset 1 only needs this rule:

> **Clan genetics can alter potential distributions or provide inherited traits. The individual clan mechanics are defined elsewhere.**

No Sharingan probability tables here.

That would be scope creep.

---

# 14. Maturation inheritance

Early and late blooming should also have some hereditary influence.

Parents who matured very early increase the probability of early-maturing children.

But again, not deterministically.

This matters because a family can appear unimpressive at Academy age and develop dramatically later.

---

# 15. How prodigies emerge

A prodigy should **not be rolled as a special "Prodigy" class first**.

The generator creates the character normally.

Then it checks their resulting developmental profile.

A character may emerge with:

- several high Attribute soft limits
- unusually strong Skill Aptitudes
- early maturation
- strong starting Attributes
- exceptional Chakra
- advantageous inherited traits

If enough favorable factors align, the engine identifies them as a prodigy.

Thus:

> **Prodigy is a description of an unusual developmental profile, not a magical trait.**

Exceptional potential in:

Endurance

### Intellectual Prodigy

Exceptional:

Intelligence  
learning speed  
technical aptitudes

### Chakra Prodigy

Very high Chakra foundation and favorable chakra-related aptitudes.

### Technical Prodigy

Average Attributes but extremely high aptitude in a particular Skill.

### Broad Prodigy

Several unusually strong areas.

These should be much rarer.

### Extreme Prodigy

Broad exceptional potential plus early development and unusually high learning efficiency.

These are the children who become famous very quickly.

Among children entering shinobi training:

### Noticeably Talented

Approximately **10–15%**

Approximately **2–5%**

Teachers clearly recognize unusual ability.

### Major Prodigy

Approximately **0.3–1%**

Potentially one of the best students of their class or generation.

Approximately **0.05–0.1%**

Far rarer.

- Skill Aptitude hasn't been exposed
- their strongest Attributes mature later

- reputation
- unusually fast advancement
- selection for demanding roles

An 80 should almost never feel routine.

---

# 9. 90+ Attributes

A permanent Attribute above 90 should be genuinely extraordinary.

This should usually require some combination of:

- very high inherited potential
- strong aptitude
- years of specialization
- favorable maturation
- exceptional training access
- unusual physiology

A 90 should generally represent someone who is among the best natural examples of that trait in their generation.

Not every Jōnin needs a 90.

Most should have none.

---

# 10. 95+

Scores in the 95–99 range should be treated as **historic outliers**.

These should be rare enough that:

- entire villages may have none
- several generations may pass without one in a particular Attribute
- their existence may become part of that person's reputation

A 97 Perception should feel qualitatively different from a merely strong Perception score.

---

# 11. Attribute 100

I recommend defining **100 as a practical natural ceiling**, not a mathematically perfect biological maximum.

A character at 100 represents:

> The upper edge of what an unenhanced human/shinobi body can naturally sustain in that Attribute.

It does not mean no fictional person could ever exceed it.

It simply means that anything higher requires something abnormal:

- transformation
- enhancement
- special physiology
- temporary state
- supernatural modification

This keeps the scale usable.

---

# 12. Temporary values above 100

Temporary effective Attributes may exceed the natural scale.

For example:

Base Strength: **78**

Transformation modifier: **+32**

Effective Strength: **110**

The character's permanent Strength remains 78.

The engine tracks both:

**Base Attribute**

and

**Effective Attribute**

This distinction should always remain explicit internally.

---

# 13. Temporary enhancement bands

We only need broad interpretation here:

| Effective Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 100–119 | Beyond normal natural capability |
| 120–149 | Major supernatural enhancement |
| 150–199 | Extreme enhancement |
| 200+ | Exceptionally abnormal |

These are intentionally broad.

Ruleset 1 should not decide what specific transformations grant these values.

---

# 14. Transformations should not automatically improve every Attribute

A form might provide:

Strength +25  
Endurance +20  
Agility +10

while doing nothing for:

Intelligence  
Perception

Another form might primarily enhance:

Chakra  
Perception  
Agility

This keeps transformations mechanically distinct.

---

# 15. Temporary boosts do not bypass Skill requirements

This is important.

Suppose someone has:

Agility 41  
Hand Signs III

A transformation increases effective Agility to 78.

They do **not** suddenly gain Hand Signs V.

Their hands may physically move faster, but their technique, familiarity, and precision remain based on their actual Skill.

This preserves the Attribute/Skill distinction.

---

# 16. Temporary Attributes and tier prerequisites

Temporary enhancement should generally **not count toward permanent Skill Tier prerequisites**.

Otherwise a character could:

1. transform
2. temporarily meet Agility 60
3. learn an advanced tier
4. lose the transformation
5. somehow retain training their body normally cannot support

There can be rare exceptions later, but the default rule should be:

> Permanent progression checks use permanent Attributes.

---

# 17. Permanent enhancement

If some later system permanently changes physiology, that is different.

Examples might include:

- permanent medical modification
- bloodline mutation
- major biological alteration

Those could genuinely alter the base Attribute or its soft limit.

But that belongs to the relevant system.

Ruleset 1 only needs to allow it.

---

# 18. Skill rarity works differently from Attribute rarity

We should not say:

> Tier VI = equivalent to Attribute 90.

They measure different things.

An Attribute reflects foundational capability.

A Skill Tier reflects accumulated technical development.

A character with average Attributes might still reach Tier VI after decades of specialization.

So rarity should consider:

**Skill Tier + proficiency + character age + training history**

rather than converting everything into one universal power score.

---

# 19. Technique mastery rarity

Likewise:

**Mastery 95**

doesn't mean someone is globally legendary.

It means they are extraordinarily familiar with that specific technique.

A Genin could theoretically have:

**Basic Clone Technique Mastery 98**

without being remarkable overall.

Mastery rarity only matters relative to the **complexity and importance of the technique**.

---

# 20. No overall power rating

I strongly recommend Ruleset 1 explicitly reject a single:

**Power Level: 8,423**

or

**Overall Rating: 87**

Characters should be evaluated through their actual profile:

Attributes  
Skills  
Skill Tiers  
Mastery  
experience  
abilities  
condition

This keeps matchups and specialization meaningful.

---

# 21. Ordinary characters can still become extraordinary

This final rarity system should preserve one of our core goals.

A character with mostly ordinary potential can still eventually possess:

**Taijutsu VI**

or perhaps:

**Strength 72**

through extreme specialization.

What is rare is doing that **quickly**, doing it **young**, or doing it in **many areas at once**.

That distinction makes dedication matter.

---

# 22. NPC generation safeguard

Before finalizing an NPC, the engine should check:

### Age anomaly
Are any Attributes bizarrely high for their age?

### Rank anomaly
Are their Skills bizarrely high or low for rank?

### Combined rarity
Did the generator accidentally produce too many elite Attributes?

### Historical plausibility
Does the NPC's training history explain their current development?

If an unusual profile survives all four checks, it is valid.

The engine then gives it appropriate narrative context.

---

# Ruleset 1 now has all required major components

We have now developed:

**A. Exact Attribute Scale**  
Seven foundational Attributes on a 0–100 natural scale.

**B. Attribute Age Curves**  
Childhood development, maturation, peaks, and decline.

**C. NPC Rank Generation Ranges**  
Rank affects expected Skills and experience more than raw Attributes.

**D. Skill Tier Definitions**  
Tier I–VI with optional Tier VII and 0–100 proficiency within each tier.

**E. Growth and Training**  
Development capacity, training quality, aptitude, diminishing returns, experience, and anti-grinding.

**F. Potential, Genetics, and Prodigies**  
Soft limits, inheritance, aptitude, maturation, and emergent prodigies.

**G. Rarity and Natural Limits**  
70+, 80+, 90+, age-relative rarity, combined rarity, and temporary >100 values.

At this point, I would **stop adding major mechanics to Ruleset 1**. The remaining work should be refinement: checking the numbers against one another, removing contradictions, and then consolidating everything into a clean final ruleset the game engine can actually follow.
