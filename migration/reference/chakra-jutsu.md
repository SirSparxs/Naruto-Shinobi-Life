I’d frame it like this:

**Canon is a foundation, not a hard boundary.** Non-canon jutsu, techniques, chakra applications, transformations, clan arts, and special abilities are permitted when they can be reasonably derived from established Naruto mechanics and the simulation’s existing rules.

That means a non-canon technique should generally satisfy a few things: it should use an understandable chakra mechanism, fit the user’s attributes/skills/natures/knowledge, have believable prerequisites, carry appropriate costs or limitations, and make sense for how it was developed or learned. It should also respect rarity and progression—something being original does not justify it being unusually powerful.

This is especially important for the simulation because otherwise the world becomes too static. NPCs should be capable of creating techniques, clans should develop new variants over generations, villages should have local specialties that never appeared in canon, and individual shinobi should sometimes adapt known techniques into personal styles.

For example, all of these could be valid despite not being canon:

- A Wind Release user developing a thin chakra membrane around a blade that reduces air resistance and improves cutting speed.
- A sensor creating a short-range pulse technique that sacrifices range for extremely detailed chakra mapping.
- A Water Release specialist combining water manipulation and chakra control to create temporary mist lenses that distort depth perception.
- A medical-nin developing a specialized clotting technique designed specifically for battlefield stabilization.
- A clan gradually developing an original Hiden technique over several generations.
- An NPC modifying an existing Earth Release jutsu to work better in Kusagakure’s wet soil.

What we should **not** allow is arbitrary invention such as, “this character invented a technique that teleports anywhere, costs almost no chakra, has no counter, and they learned it as a Genin,” unless there is an extraordinary established explanation that actually supports those properties.

I also think we should separate **canon fidelity** from **mechanical legitimacy**. A technique can be:

**Canon** — directly established in Naruto.

**Canon-derived** — an original variation or logical application of a canon technique.

**Original** — entirely new, but constructed using established chakra principles.

**Exceptional/experimental** — pushes known chakra mechanics into unusual territory and therefore requires substantially stronger justification, research, prerequisites, risk, or rarity.

None of those categories automatically determines strength. An original C-rank technique could be mundane, while a canon S-rank technique remains extremely dangerous.

This also gives us a useful principle for NPC generation: **the simulation may invent abilities when the world reasonably produces them; it should not invent abilities merely because the story needs something interesting to happen.**

And that fits perfectly with the broader goal we've established: the player is living inside an evolving shinobi world rather than reenacting a fixed Naruto encyclopedia.

## Ruleset 3.1 — Chakra System Fundamentals

I think the chakra system should begin with one important distinction:

**The Chakra Attribute is not the character's chakra pool.**

The **Chakra Attribute** represents the character's underlying capacity to generate, contain, manipulate, and develop chakra. The actual resource used during play is a derived value: **Chakra Reserves**.

This prevents the foundational Attribute from having to represent too many different things at once and gives us room for characters who are unusual in believable ways—for example, someone with enormous reserves but mediocre control, or someone with modest reserves but extraordinarily efficient chakra usage.

---

### 3.1.1 The Core Chakra Model

Every chakra-using character can be described through four major concepts:

| Component | Represents | Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Chakra Attribute** | Innate/foundational chakra potential | Attribute |
| **Chakra Reserves** | Total usable chakra available | Derived Resource |
| **Chakra Control** | Precision and efficiency when manipulating chakra | Skill |
| **Chakra Output** | How much chakra can safely be released at once | Skill / derived capability |

There will also be **Chakra Recovery**, but I don't think it needs to become another major stat players constantly level.

Instead, recovery should emerge primarily from **Chakra + Endurance**, with traits, physical condition, sleep, nutrition, training, bloodlines, and special abilities modifying it.

That keeps us from creating unnecessary stats.

---

# 3.1.2 Chakra Reserves

Characters have:

**Current Chakra / Maximum Chakra**

For example:

> **Chakra: 84 / 120 CP**

We can use **CP (Chakra Points)** internally as the simulation resource even though characters in the world obviously aren't thinking in numerical chakra points.

Maximum Chakra represents the amount of chakra a character can safely maintain under normal circumstances.

It should primarily depend on:

**Chakra Attribute + physical development + training + genetics/traits + age + certain special abilities**

Endurance should contribute somewhat, but it should not be the primary determinant.

That distinction matters.

Someone with tremendous physical endurance does not automatically have enormous chakra reserves, while someone descended from a bloodline known for unusually powerful vitality or chakra may possess enormous reserves despite otherwise ordinary physical characteristics.

---

# 3.1.3 Chakra Attribute vs. Chakra Reserves

The Chakra Attribute should influence several things simultaneously without directly determining them.

A character with a high Chakra Attribute will generally have:

- Greater potential maximum reserves.
- Greater potential chakra output.
- Easier development of chakra-related Skills.
- Better ability to handle unusually powerful chakra.
- Greater potential recovery.
- Higher ceilings for advanced chakra techniques.

But **training and circumstances determine how much of that potential has actually been realized.**

Consider two characters who both have:

> **Chakra Attribute: 70**

Character A has spent years performing endurance and chakra-reserve training.

Character B has specialized almost entirely in genjutsu and precision control.

Their eventual profiles might resemble:

| | Character A | Character B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Chakra Attribute | 70 | 70 |
| Maximum Chakra | 310 CP | 205 CP |
| Chakra Control | Moderate | Exceptional |
| Chakra Output | High | Moderate |
| Efficiency | Moderate | Exceptional |

Neither is objectively "better."

They have developed the same foundation differently.

---

# 3.1.4 Reserve Potential

I think Chakra Reserves should have both a **current developed value** and an underlying **soft potential**.

This works similarly to what we've established for Attributes.

A young Genin might have:

> Chakra Attribute: 58  
> Maximum Chakra: 92 CP

That doesn't mean 92 is their biological lifetime maximum.

Continued maturation and training may increase that substantially.

However, gains should become progressively harder.

This prevents something like:

> 80 CP → train chakra for three years → 4,000 CP

unless extraordinary factors are present.

Characters such as jinchūriki, unusually powerful bloodlines, experimental subjects, or genuine statistical outliers can break normal expectations.

But those exceptions are produced by **world variables**, not protagonist privilege.

---

# 3.1.5 Current Chakra

Chakra expenditure should be persistent.

If a character begins a mission with:

> 240 / 240 CP

and spends 100 CP fighting bandits, they don't suddenly return to 240 because the encounter ended.

They now have:

> **140 / 240 CP**

That resource continues to matter for the remainder of the mission.

This is important because chakra management becomes strategically meaningful.

A shinobi may deliberately avoid using a powerful technique because they don't know what they'll encounter later.

NPCs should make the same calculations.

# 3.1.6 Chakra Is Not a Mana Bar

Although CP gives us a numerical resource, chakra should behave differently from generic RPG mana.

Low chakra has physiological consequences.

Chakra is produced through the interaction of the body's physical and spiritual energies. Exhausting it therefore places increasing strain on the user.

We should recognize several broad states.

### Normal

Approximately:

**50–100% reserves**

The character functions normally.

Minor expenditure generally produces no meaningful penalty.

---

### Taxed

Approximately:

**25–50% reserves**

The character is beginning to feel prolonged chakra expenditure.

This doesn't automatically impose severe penalties, but strenuous chakra use becomes increasingly noticeable.

Possible narrative effects include:

- Heavy breathing.
- Reduced stamina.
- Difficulty sustaining expensive techniques.
- Beginning mental fatigue.

Characters accustomed to prolonged combat may tolerate this extremely well.

---

### Low Chakra

Approximately:

**10–25% reserves**

Meaningful strain begins.

Possible consequences include:

- Reduced maximum safe output.
- Increased difficulty maintaining demanding techniques.
- Greater susceptibility to mistakes.
- Slower chakra recovery.
- Increased physical fatigue.
- Reduced effectiveness of techniques requiring exceptional control.

These should usually become **difficulty modifiers in Ruleset 2**, rather than automatic failures.

---

### Critical Chakra

Approximately:

**1–10% reserves**

The character is approaching dangerous exhaustion.

Symptoms can include:

- Severe fatigue.
- Trembling.
- impaired concentration.
- Difficulty performing complex chakra manipulation.
- Reduced physical performance.
- Techniques becoming increasingly unreliable.
- Difficulty sustaining transformations.
- Potential collapse.

Individual tolerance matters enormously.

---

### Chakra Exhaustion

**0 CP**

Zero does **not automatically mean unconsciousness**.

Instead, the character has depleted their readily usable chakra reserve.

They may remain conscious and capable of physical actions, depending on their overall condition.

However:

- Normal chakra techniques become unavailable.
- Chakra-enhanced physical abilities may fail.
- Maintaining active chakra effects becomes difficult or impossible.
- Further chakra use requires dangerous overexertion.

This gives us more interesting outcomes than treating 0 CP as an automatic knockout.

---

# 3.1.7 Overexertion

A shinobi can attempt to use chakra they do not safely have available.

This should be possible because Naruto repeatedly depicts characters pushing themselves beyond ordinary limits.

But it should never be free.

We'll call this **Chakra Overexertion**.

A character at:

> 7 / 180 CP

might attempt a technique requiring:

> 15 CP.

They are effectively attempting to force another 8 CP beyond their safe available reserves.

The technique is **not automatically impossible**.

Instead, Ruleset 2 evaluates whether they can force their body to produce it.

Relevant factors could include:

**Willpower + Chakra + Endurance + technique mastery + amount of overexertion**

Success causes consequences proportional to how far beyond safe capacity they pushed themselves.

Possible consequences include:

- Immediate exhaustion.
- HP/stamina damage.
- Collapse.
- Temporary Chakra penalties.
- Chakra pathway damage.
- Loss of consciousness.
- Injury.
- In extreme cases, death.

This creates the classic Naruto situation where someone can perform **one last technique**, but doing so actually matters.

---

# 3.1.8 No Universal "Last-Ditch Power-Up"

Overexertion should not secretly become a heroic bonus.

Being nearly exhausted does **not** automatically make someone stronger.

There should be no:

> "I'm at 2% chakra, therefore friendship gives me +50% power."

Characters can sometimes exceed their normal limits through exceptional circumstances, but there must be an actual mechanical explanation:

- Extreme Willpower.
- Adrenaline.
- Tailed-beast chakra.
- Curse mark.
- Stored chakra.
- Eight Gates.
- Sage chakra.
- External chakra transfer.
- Previously inaccessible reserve.
- Dangerous biological overexertion.
- A specific trait or special ability.

Narrative importance alone is never sufficient.

---

# 3.1.9 Chakra Output

Maximum reserves and output need to remain separate.

Imagine two containers.

One is a **huge water tank with a narrow pipe**.

The other is a **smaller tank with an enormous pipe**.

The first contains more water.

The second can release more water at once.

Chakra should work similarly.

A character might possess:

> **Maximum Chakra: 600 CP**

but lack the ability to safely pour 150 CP into a single attack.

Their Chakra Output Skill might currently restrict them to something closer to:

> **Maximum Safe Burst: 45 CP**

They could therefore have enormous staying power without immediately firing enormous attacks.

This solves an important balance issue.

**Large chakra reserves do not automatically create large destructive power.**

---

# 3.1.10 Safe Output vs. Maximum Output

We should distinguish:

**Safe Output** — chakra that can be released reliably without meaningful strain.

**Maximum Output** — the greatest amount the character can force through their chakra system under extreme circumstances.

Characters normally operate within Safe Output.

Exceeding it creates escalating difficulty and physiological stress.

That gives us another form of overexertion:

A character may have plenty of chakra remaining but still injure themselves trying to release too much at once.

Example:

> Current Chakra: 300 / 350  
> Safe Output: 42  
> Maximum trained Output: 63

Attempting to force **90 CP** through a single technique could be dangerous even though they technically possess the chakra.

This is extremely useful for balancing powerful techniques.

---

# 3.1.11 Chakra Control and Efficiency

We'll develop Chakra Control fully in **3.2**, but its fundamental relationship to reserves should be established now.

Every technique has a **theoretical chakra requirement**.

Poor chakra control can cause waste.

For example:

> Technique requirement: 10 CP

A beginner might actually expend:

> 14 CP

because 4 CP was inefficiently manipulated.

An experienced user might expend almost exactly:

> 10 CP.

An exceptional master may sometimes execute optimized versions below the normal expected cost.

However, Control should not allow someone to reduce every technique infinitely.

If a fireball physically requires a certain amount of chakra to produce its effect, perfect control cannot make that energy requirement disappear.

So efficiency has a **technique-dependent floor**.

This prevents elite Chakra Control from becoming effectively infinite chakra.

---

# 3.1.12 Chakra Cost Has Multiple Components

Eventually, jutsu costs can be understood as combinations of:

**Activation Cost**

Chakra necessary to initiate the technique.

**Output Cost**

Chakra actually used to generate the effect.

**Maintenance Cost**

Chakra required to keep the effect active.

**Expansion Cost**

Additional chakra required to increase size, distance, targets, clones, etc.

Not every technique uses all four.

A fireball might mostly involve activation + output.

A barrier might require activation + maintenance.

A shadow clone involves chakra division rather than normal expenditure.

A transformation technique might require activation plus a small ongoing maintenance cost.

This shared model will make later jutsu much easier to implement consistently.

---

# 3.1.13 Chakra Recovery

Chakra recovery should occur naturally, but **not at video-game regeneration speeds**.

Characters should not hide behind a tree for twenty seconds and regenerate half their reserves.

Recovery should depend primarily on:

**Chakra + Endurance + rest quality + nutrition + sleep + health**

with additional modifiers from:

- Age.
- Genetics.
- Medical conditions.
- Injury.
- Chakra pathway damage.
- Special abilities.
- Drugs/pills.
- Training.
- Environmental conditions.
- External chakra sources.

Recovery should happen on several timescales.

**Short Rest**

Minutes.

Primarily allows stabilization and minor recovery.

**Extended Rest**

Hours.

Meaningful chakra restoration becomes possible.

**Sleep**

Usually provides the most substantial normal recovery.

**Recovery Period**

Severe chakra exhaustion may require considerably longer than one night's sleep.

Someone who dangerously overexerted themselves may remain depleted the next day.

---

# 3.1.14 Maximum Chakra Can Temporarily Decrease

This is an important distinction.

Suppose someone normally has:

> **240 Maximum Chakra**

After severe overexertion, they might wake up at:

> **110 / 165 CP**

Their current chakra is 110.

But their body is also temporarily incapable of safely accessing its normal 240 CP capacity.

As they recover:

> 143 / 190  
> 181 / 215  
> 230 / 240

This lets us represent genuine chakra exhaustion without inventing enormous status penalties.

Certain injuries, seals, diseases, poisonings, or chakra pathway damage could similarly reduce **Effective Maximum Chakra**.

---

# 3.1.15 Chakra Regeneration During Combat

There should technically be some ongoing chakra generation because the body continually produces chakra.

But under ordinary circumstances it should be **too small to meaningfully replenish combat expenditure**.

Characters shouldn't gain +3 CP every combat turn.

Instead, passive regeneration during intense activity is generally abstracted away.

Special abilities can break this rule.

Examples include:

- Exceptional regenerative physiology.
- Sage techniques.
- Tailed beasts.
- Stored chakra.
- Chakra absorption.
- External transfers.
- Specific clan abilities.

Those are special mechanics precisely because normal shinobi cannot rapidly regenerate their entire chakra supply midfight.

---

# 3.1.16 Physical Stamina and Chakra Are Related but Separate

We should **not merge chakra and stamina into one resource**.

A character can be:

**Physically exhausted but chakra-rich.**

or

**Physically fresh but chakra-depleted.**

For example, a genjutsu specialist may spend enormous chakra while barely moving.

A taijutsu specialist may be physically exhausted despite retaining considerable chakra.

However, they influence one another.

Severe physical exhaustion can reduce:

- Chakra generation.
- Chakra control.
- Safe output.

Severe chakra exhaustion can cause:

- Physical weakness.
- Mental fatigue.
- Reduced endurance.

This creates interaction without collapsing both systems together.

---

# 3.1.17 Emotional State and Chakra

Emotion should influence chakra, but not through simplistic universal buffs.

Extreme emotion may affect:

- Control.
- Output.
- Decision-making.
- Special ability activation.
- Chakra signatures.
- Bloodline abilities.
- Willpower checks.

Anger might allow one character to produce greater output while severely degrading control.

Another may freeze.

Another may become unusually focused.

This should depend on **personality, traits, abilities, and circumstances**, not a universal "anger = stronger" rule.

---

# 3.1.18 Foreign Chakra

Characters may contain chakra that is not naturally their own.

Examples include:

- Tailed beast chakra.
- Chakra transferred by another shinobi.
- Curse marks.
- Sealed chakra.
- Experimental implants.
- Stored external chakra.
- Sage/natural energy mixtures.

Foreign chakra should usually exist as a **separate pool or modifier**, not permanently inflate normal Maximum Chakra.

For example:

> Personal Chakra: 190 / 240  
> Sealed Reserve: 450 CP

The character does **not** simply become:

> 640 / 690 CP.

They may need special rules to access the second pool.

This will be crucial for jinchūriki later.

---

# 3.1.19 Stored Chakra

Characters may deliberately store chakra over long periods.

Examples include techniques similar to the Strength of a Hundred Seal.

Stored chakra should also remain separate:

> Normal Chakra: 212 / 230  
> Stored Chakra: 1,480 CP

Accessing stored chakra may have its own release rate, conditions, training requirements, or risks.

This prevents stored reserves from distorting ordinary chakra progression.

---

# 3.1.20 Chakra Signatures

Every chakra-producing individual should possess a recognizable chakra signature.

The signature may contain information about:

- Identity.
- Approximate strength.
- Nature.
- unusual properties.
- Emotional disturbance.
- Foreign chakra.
- Certain bloodline characteristics.

But detecting those characteristics requires sensory ability.

Ordinary characters do not automatically perceive chakra numerically.

A sensor might think:

> "That chakra is enormous."

They don't think:

> "He has exactly 487 CP remaining."

Even exceptionally talented sensors should generally estimate rather than know exact resource numbers.

---

# 3.1.21 Chakra Reserves Are Partially Hidden Information

For gameplay purposes, the player should know their own approximate/current chakra reasonably well.

But enemy chakra reserves should usually remain uncertain unless relevant sensory information exists.

We might display things such as:

> **Enemy Chakra: High**  
> **Enemy Chakra: Appears moderately depleted**

rather than:

> **Enemy Chakra: 162 / 311**

unless some extraordinary ability allows that precision.

The simulation engine can still track the exact numbers privately.

---

# 3.1.22 Chakra Training

Characters can deliberately improve reserves.

But reserve training shouldn't simply involve repeatedly emptying the chakra bar.

Useful training might include:

- Sustained chakra exercises.
- Repeated controlled expenditure.
- Physical conditioning.
- Meditation.
- Chakra circulation exercises.
- Specialized clan methods.
- Progressive output training.
- Real-world technique usage.

Training gains should depend on:

**Chakra Attribute potential + age + current development + training quality + instructor + recovery + consistency**

And repeated reckless depletion can actually be harmful.

That stops optimal gameplay from becoming:

> Cast everything → hit zero → sleep → repeat forever.

---

# 3.1.23 Children and Chakra Development

Children should generally possess substantially smaller usable reserves than their adult potential.

As they mature:

**Maximum Chakra, Output, physical tolerance, and recovery capacity naturally develop.**

This is important for the life-simulation aspect.

An academy student with unusually high Chakra might clearly have enormous *potential* without possessing adult-level reserves at age eight.

Natural maturation therefore contributes to chakra growth alongside training.

# 3.1.24 Individual Variation

Characters with identical Attributes do not need identical chakra reserves.

The system should permit reasonable variation through:

- Genetics.
- Traits.

## Ruleset 3.2 — Chakra Control

Chakra Control should be one of the most important Skills in the entire system because it governs **how precisely and efficiently a character can manipulate chakra**, but it should never become a universal “magic skill” that replaces every specialized discipline.

A character can have exceptional Chakra Control and still know nothing about medical ninjutsu, genjutsu, sealing, or elemental transformation.

The relationship should be:

> **Chakra Control determines how well you manipulate chakra. Specialized Skills determine what you know how to do with that manipulation.**

---

### 3.2.1 Chakra Control Is a Skill, Not an Attribute

The foundational **Chakra Attribute** determines a character's underlying chakra potential.

**Chakra Control** is learned.

Its primary Attribute influences should probably be:

> **Chakra + Intelligence + Perception**

with their relative importance changing depending on the application.

For example:

- Fine medical manipulation may lean heavily on Chakra and Perception.
- Learning a complicated control exercise may involve Intelligence.
- Sustaining a difficult technique under pressure may rely partly on Willpower.
- Rapidly adjusting chakra during movement could incorporate Agility.

So we should avoid saying:

> Chakra Control = 70% Chakra + 30% Intelligence

for every situation.

Instead, Chakra Control has a Skill value, while Ruleset 2 determines which Attributes matter for the specific application.

---

# 3.2.2 Tiered Chakra Control

This is exactly the type of important Skill that should use the multi-tier model we established earlier.

I propose:

> **Basic Chakra Control I–III**  
> **Intermediate Chakra Control I–III**  
> **Advanced Chakra Control I–III**  
> **Expert Chakra Control I–III**  
> **Master Chakra Control I–III**

Each individual tier uses:

> **0–100 Mastery**

So someone could have:

> **Advanced Chakra Control II — 63/100**

This gives us much more progression space than simply having:

> Chakra Control: 74

It also means major developmental milestones actually matter.

---

# 3.2.3 Tier Meaning

The tiers should describe qualitatively different capabilities, not just larger numerical bonuses.

### Basic Chakra Control

Represents intentionally directing chakra through the body.

Common capabilities include:

- Activating simple jutsu.
- Basic chakra circulation.
- Simple Academy exercises.
- Crude reinforcement.
- Learning surface adhesion.
- Recognizing obvious chakra waste.

Most Academy graduates possess some degree of Basic Chakra Control.

---

### Intermediate Chakra Control

Represents reliable manipulation during normal shinobi activity.

Capabilities may include:

- Tree walking.
- Water walking with sufficient training.
- Adjusting chakra output during movement.
- Reliable control during combat.
- Moderately precise chakra distribution.
- Basic chakra suppression.
- Maintaining simple sustained effects.

A competent Genin or Chūnin may develop into this range, although rank never guarantees it.

---

### Advanced Chakra Control

Represents fine, deliberate manipulation.

Possible capabilities:

- Very low chakra waste.
- Maintaining several different chakra processes simultaneously.
- Advanced chakra suppression.
- Precise chakra enhancement.
- Entry-level medical ninjutsu.
- Sophisticated genjutsu control.
- Difficult shape transformations.
- Rapid adaptation to disrupted chakra flow.

This becomes increasingly uncommon among ordinary shinobi.

---

### Expert Chakra Control

Represents extraordinarily precise manipulation.

Possible applications include:

- High-level medical ninjutsu.
- Extremely precise chakra reinforcement.
- Complex sealing.
- Manipulating very small quantities of chakra reliably.
- Maintaining complicated techniques while distracted.
- Exceptional efficiency.
- Advanced simultaneous control.
- Near-instant corrections to chakra instability.

Most shinobi never reach this level.

---

### Master Chakra Control

Represents exceptional control even among elite shinobi.

Examples might involve abilities comparable to the chakra-control feats associated with characters such as Sakura or Tsunade.

Capabilities could include:

- Extremely precise chakra allocation.
- Negligible unnecessary waste in familiar techniques.
- Manipulating enormous output with remarkable accuracy.
- Maintaining exceptionally delicate chakra operations.
- Advanced stored-chakra techniques.
- Performing highly specialized control feats that would be impossible for most shinobi.

This should be extremely rare.

And importantly:

> **Master Chakra Control does not mean mastery of every chakra discipline.**

A character with Master Chakra Control and no medical training still cannot perform advanced surgery with chakra.

---

# 3.2.4 Advancement Requirements

Higher Chakra Control tiers should require both **practice and sufficient foundational Attributes**.

For example, reaching Advanced Chakra Control might require minimum levels in:

- Chakra.
- Intelligence or Perception.
- Relevant lower Chakra Control tiers.

Expert and Master tiers should demand increasingly exceptional foundations.

The exact thresholds should be determined when we finalize the global Skill progression formulas.

The important rule now is:

> **Practice cannot completely overcome insufficient foundational capability.**

Someone with extremely poor chakra aptitude should not grind tree walking for twenty years and automatically achieve Sakura-level control.

They could become remarkably skilled relative to their potential, but Attributes establish ceilings and difficulty.

---

# 3.2.5 Control Difficulty

Every chakra manipulation should have a **Control Difficulty**.

Simple techniques may require almost no meaningful check once learned.

Others might demand extreme precision.

Examples:

| Application | Approximate Control Demand |
|---|---|
| Basic Clone Technique | Very Low |
| Transformation Technique | Low |
| Tree Walking | Low–Moderate |
| Water Walking | Moderate |
| Maintaining chakra on moving water | Moderate |
| Fine chakra-thread manipulation | Moderate–High |
| Medical healing | High |
| Delicate surgery | Very High |
| Chakra storage seal maintenance | Extreme |

These aren't necessarily literal Difficulty Classes yet.

Ruleset 2 will determine how difficulty is represented.

Ruleset 3 merely defines that techniques possess a **Control Requirement / Control Difficulty**.

---

# 3.2.6 Minimum Requirement vs. Difficulty

These should be different concepts.

A technique might have:

> **Minimum Chakra Control: Intermediate I**

but still have:

> **Control Difficulty: Hard**

Meeting the minimum means the character understands enough chakra manipulation to attempt the technique properly.

It does **not** mean automatic success.

Similarly, someone far above the minimum may find routine applications trivial.

---

# 3.2.7 Chakra Efficiency

Control directly influences **wasted chakra**.

Suppose a technique theoretically requires:

> **20 CP**

A novice might expend:

> 28 CP

A competent user:

> 22 CP

An expert:

> 20 CP

A true specialist with extensive mastery might occasionally execute a refined version for:

> 18 CP

But efficiency needs limits.

There should be three conceptual costs:

**Theoretical Cost**

What the technique's standard execution normally requires.

**Actual Cost**

What the user spends after efficiency and waste are considered.

**Minimum Functional Cost**

The lowest amount of chakra capable of producing that particular effect.

No amount of control can reduce expenditure below the physical requirement of the technique.

---

# 3.2.8 Efficiency Is Technique-Specific

High general Chakra Control improves efficiency broadly.

However, **individual technique mastery should matter more for optimizing a particular jutsu**.

For example:

Character A:

> Expert Chakra Control  
> Fireball Mastery: Low

Character B:

> Advanced Chakra Control  
> Fireball Mastery: Exceptional

Character B might actually use the Fireball Technique more efficiently.

That gives us two complementary progression systems:

**General Control**

“How good am I at manipulating chakra?”

**Technique Mastery**

“How good am I at manipulating chakra for this exact technique?”

This prevents Chakra Control from replacing individual mastery.

---

# 3.2.9 Precision

Efficiency and precision are related but different.

**Efficiency** means minimizing wasted chakra.

**Precision** means putting the correct amount of chakra in the correct place at the correct moment.

A character could theoretically waste very little chakra but still struggle with extremely delicate manipulation.

Precision matters heavily for:

- Medical ninjutsu.
- Genjutsu.
- Chakra scalpels.
- Chakra threads.
- Sealing.
- Tenketsu-related techniques.
- Delicate barriers.
- Advanced transformations.
- Chakra-enhanced strength.

This distinction helps make specialist disciplines feel different.

---

# 3.2.10 Stability

Another important dimension is **stability**.

Stability represents maintaining chakra at the desired state once established.

Examples:

- Standing on water.
- Maintaining a transformation.
- Sustaining chakra threads.
- Maintaining a barrier.
- Keeping a medical technique active.
- Holding a complex chakra shape.

Control determines initial manipulation, but maintaining it for minutes or hours becomes increasingly difficult.

Therefore sustained techniques may use:

> **Chakra Control + Willpower + relevant Skill**

rather than Chakra Control alone.

---

# 3.2.11 Simultaneous Chakra Processes

Characters should be able to perform multiple chakra operations simultaneously, but doing so becomes progressively more difficult.

Examples:

- Water walking while fighting.
- Maintaining a clone while using another jutsu.
- Sustaining a barrier while sensing.
- Healing someone while maintaining chakra suppression.
- Controlling several chakra threads independently.

The character isn't necessarily casting multiple jutsu simultaneously.

They're maintaining multiple **chakra processes**.

Each additional process may increase concentration burden.

Very skilled characters eventually trivialize simple processes.

A Jōnin shouldn't need to actively think about tree walking during combat.

---

# 3.2.12 Automaticity

Repeated practice can make certain chakra operations effectively automatic.

Examples:

> Tree Walking Mastery: Very High

Eventually the character may maintain foot adhesion without meaningful conscious effort.

That means it no longer imposes significant concentration penalties during normal circumstances.

The same principle can apply to:

- Water walking.
- Chakra suppression.
- Weapon chakra flow.
- Basic transformations.
- Certain clan abilities.

However, severe injury, exhaustion, chakra disruption, or extreme environmental conditions can force the character to consciously manage even normally automatic processes.

---

# 3.2.13 Fine vs. Gross Chakra Manipulation

We should recognize two broad applications without making them separate Skills.

### Gross Manipulation

Moving relatively large amounts of chakra.

Examples:

- Reinforcing the body.
- Large elemental attacks.
- Chakra bursts.
- Massive transformations.

### Fine Manipulation

Managing very small, precise quantities.

Examples:

- Medical ninjutsu.
- Chakra scalpel work.
- Fine sealing.
- Tenketsu manipulation.
- Detailed genjutsu.

High Chakra Control helps both, but characters may develop **specializations**.

A character could be extremely good at managing large flows without possessing medical-level delicacy.

Individual discipline Skills should represent that difference.

---

# 3.2.14 Large Reserves Can Make Control Harder

This is one area where Naruto provides an interesting design opportunity.

Possessing enormous chakra reserves can sometimes make beginner-level control more difficult because very small outputs represent a tiny fraction of the user's total capacity.

But we should be careful not to punish high reserves universally.

Instead:

> **Large reserves can increase difficulty when extremely fine throttling is required, especially before the character has trained control over those reserves.**

Once properly trained, the disadvantage diminishes or disappears.

That creates believable cases where a child with gigantic reserves initially struggles with basic control while later becoming highly capable.

---

# 3.2.15 Foreign Chakra and Control

Manipulating chakra that is not one's own should usually be more difficult.

Examples:

- Tailed-beast chakra.
- Chakra transferred by another person.
- Stolen chakra.
- Curse-mark chakra.
- Artificial chakra.
- Chakra stored in foreign tissue.

Difficulty depends on:

- Compatibility.
- Familiarity.
- Amount.
- Volatility.
- Source.
- Relationship to the user.
- Special training.

A jinchūriki who has spent years cooperating with their tailed beast may eventually manipulate that chakra almost naturally.

A stranger suddenly flooded with foreign chakra may barely be able to control it.

---

# 3.2.16 Emotional Disruption

Extreme emotions can disturb chakra control.

Potential factors include:

- Panic.
- Rage.
- Grief.
- Fear.
- Excitement.
- Trauma.

But this should depend on the character.

Some shinobi become sloppy when angry.

Others become exceptionally focused.

Some special abilities may specifically respond to emotion.

Therefore emotions produce **contextual modifiers**, not universal penalties.

---

# 3.2.17 Injury and Chakra Control

Certain injuries should impair control.

Examples:

- Severe pain.
- Neurological damage.
- Hand injury.
- Chakra pathway injury.
- Major blood loss.
- Concussion.
- Exhaustion.

Different injuries affect different techniques.

A broken finger may severely interfere with hand seals but have little effect on a seal-less ability.

Damage to chakra pathways might directly reduce control regardless of physical mobility.

---

# 3.2.18 Chakra Disruption

Some abilities deliberately disrupt another person's chakra control.

Examples include:

- Tenketsu strikes.
- Chakra interference.
- Sealing effects.
- Certain poisons.
- Genjutsu disruption.
- Absorption abilities.

Disruption can potentially affect:

- Available chakra.
- Output.
- Control.
- Technique maintenance.
- Chakra circulation.

But those effects should be explicitly defined by the disrupting technique.

There should not be a generic “chakra disrupted = can't use jutsu” state unless the effect is severe enough.

---

# 3.2.19 Surface Adhesion

Tree walking should be one of the foundational Chakra Control exercises.

Mechanically it teaches:

> **Maintaining a stable output of chakra through a specific body surface.**

At first:

- Concentration is required.
- Too little chakra causes slipping.
- Too much causes rejection or damage to the surface.
- Movement makes control harder.

As mastery rises:

- Walking becomes easy.
- Running becomes possible.
- Combat becomes possible.
- Inverted surfaces become routine.
- Conscious concentration becomes negligible.

This makes tree walking both a training method and a persistent useful capability.

---

# 3.2.20 Water Walking

Water walking should build upon the same principle but remain more difficult because the surface is unstable.

It requires:

- Constant adjustment.
- Environmental perception.
- Stable output.
- Rapid correction.

Factors affecting difficulty may include:

- Waves.
- Current.
- Speed.
- Carrying another person.
- Injury.
- Combat.
- Running.
- Sudden impacts.

Eventually experienced shinobi perform it almost automatically.

---

# 3.2.21 Chakra Suppression

Characters should be able to deliberately reduce the chakra signature others can detect.

This requires Chakra Control because the user is suppressing unnecessary chakra circulation and leakage.

Effectiveness depends on:

> Chakra Control + relevant suppression/sensory training

and is opposed by the sensor's capabilities through Ruleset 2.

Important:

**Chakra suppression does not erase chakra completely.**

Very skilled sensors may still detect a suppressed individual.

Special abilities may permit deeper concealment.

---

# 3.2.22 Chakra Presence Management

Advanced users may manipulate how their chakra feels to others.

This could include:

- Suppressing intensity.
- Masking emotional disturbances.
- Reducing obvious elemental leakage.
- Controlling visible chakra manifestations.

But actually **imitating another person's chakra signature** should require specialized abilities.

General Chakra Control alone isn't enough.

---

# 3.2.23 Chakra Reinforcement

Characters can channel chakra into their bodies to improve physical actions.

At basic levels this may provide:

- Improved grip.
- Stronger footing.
- Minor impact reinforcement.

Advanced specialized methods can produce:

- Chakra-enhanced strength.
- Improved acceleration.
- Defensive reinforcement.
- Specialized martial techniques.

## Ruleset 3.3 — Chakra Output

Now that **Chakra Reserves** and **Chakra Control** are established, Chakra Output should define a third, separate question:

> **How much chakra can a character actually move through their system at once?**

This matters because a character can possess enormous reserves without being capable of safely channeling all of that chakra into a single technique.

A simple way to think about the three systems is:

> **Reserves = how much chakra you have**  
> **Control = how precisely and efficiently you manipulate it**  
> **Output = how much chakra you can safely push at once**

That distinction should remain fundamental throughout Ruleset 3.

---

# 3.3.1 Chakra Output Should Be a Skill

I think Chakra Output should be a trainable Skill rather than another Attribute.

Its strongest foundations should be:

- **Chakra**
- **Endurance**

with other Attributes contributing depending on the situation.

For example:

- Willpower may matter when exceeding safe limits.
- Strength could matter for certain body-based chakra reinforcement techniques.
- Control becomes important when trying to manage high output precisely.

A character could therefore have:

> **Chakra Attribute: 74**  
> **Maximum Chakra: 420 CP**  
> **Chakra Output: Intermediate IV**  
> **Chakra Control: Advanced II**

Another character might have:

> **Chakra Attribute: 61**  
> **Maximum Chakra: 205 CP**  
> **Chakra Output: Advanced V**  
> **Chakra Control: Intermediate III**

The second character has much less total chakra but may be capable of producing a much more violent short-term burst.

---

# 3.3.2 Output Skill Progression

Unlike Chakra Control, I don't think Chakra Output necessarily needs 27 tiers.

Chakra Control is unusually broad and affects nearly everything.

Output is important, but narrower.

A strong structure would be:

> **Basic Output I–V**  
> **Intermediate Output I–V**  
> **Advanced Output I–V**  
> **Expert Output I–V**  
> **Master Output I–V**

We can reserve longer Master tracks like Chakra Control's **Master I–VII** for the handful of Skills where enormous granularity is justified.

This also helps keep Chakra Control mechanically distinct as one of the deepest Skills in the game.

---

# 3.3.3 Safe Chakra Output

Every character has a **Safe Output Limit**.

This represents the amount of chakra they can channel rapidly without placing abnormal stress on their chakra system.

For example:

> **Safe Output: 24 CP**

A technique requiring 15 CP of immediate output is comfortably within their capacity.

A technique requiring 22 CP is demanding but safe.

A technique requiring 30 CP exceeds what they can normally handle.

This limit should be derived from:

> Chakra Attribute + Chakra Output Skill + Endurance + relevant traits

rather than Output Skill alone.

---

# 3.3.4 Output Is Not the Same as Chakra Cost

This distinction is crucial.

A technique may cost:

> **60 CP total**

while never requiring more than:

> **15 CP of output at one time.**

For example, a sustained barrier might cost:

> 10 CP activation  
> + 5 CP per minute for 10 minutes

Total:

> 60 CP

But its required output may still be modest.

Meanwhile another technique may cost only:

> 35 CP

but require nearly all 35 CP to be released instantly.

That second technique places a much greater demand on Chakra Output.

So every jutsu can eventually have both:

> **Chakra Cost**  
> **Output Requirement**

This gives us much better differentiation between techniques.

---

# 3.3.5 Burst Output

Characters can temporarily exceed their normal sustained output.

We should call this:

**Burst Output**

Burst Output is a short-duration spike in chakra flow.

Examples include:

- Launching a powerful ninjutsu.
- Explosive chakra-enhanced strength.
- Rapidly forming a large defensive technique.
- Forcing chakra outward to break restraint.
- Activating certain transformations.

A character might have:

> Safe Sustained Output: 25 CP  
> Safe Burst Output: 38 CP

The body can tolerate 38 CP briefly but cannot continuously channel that much.

---

# 3.3.6 Sustained Output

Sustained Output is how much chakra the user can continuously channel without destabilization.

This matters for:

- Barriers.
- Chakra cloaks.
- Chakra threads.
- Continuous medical ninjutsu.
- Large transformations.
- Chakra-enhanced movement.
- Maintaining powerful summons or constructs.
- Certain sensory abilities.

A character might be capable of an enormous short burst but struggle to sustain even half of it.

That creates another useful archetype:

> **High burst, low sustain**

versus:

> **Moderate burst, exceptional sustain**

---

# 3.3.7 Output Efficiency

Output and Chakra Control should interact heavily.

High Output without enough Control causes problems.

Imagine a character trying to force a tremendous volume of chakra through a delicate technique.

Even if they can physically produce the chakra, they may struggle to shape it correctly.

This means powerful techniques often require both:

> **Output Requirement**  
> **Control Requirement**

For example:

### Technique A

> Output Requirement: Advanced II  
> Control Requirement: Intermediate III

A brute-force destructive technique.

### Technique B

> Output Requirement: Intermediate II  
> Control Requirement: Expert I

A highly precise technique.

### Technique C

> Output Requirement: Expert III  
> Control Requirement: Expert IV

An extremely demanding high-level technique.

This lets jutsu require different kinds of talent.

When a character approaches their maximum output, precise control should become more difficult.

Someone comfortably using:

> 20 CP output

may possess excellent precision.

That same character forcing:

> 55 CP

may struggle to maintain exact chakra shaping.

This shouldn't be a universal massive penalty, though.

The relationship depends on the difference between:

> Current Output  
> Safe Output  
> Maximum Output  
> Chakra Control

High Chakra Control lets characters retain precision at much higher output.

This is one of the reasons Chakra Control remains so important even for destructive specialists.

Beyond Safe Output exists a character's **Maximum Output**.

This is the greatest amount of chakra they can physically force through their system under extraordinary strain.

Example:

> Safe Sustained Output: 24 CP  
> Safe Burst Output: 39 CP  
> Maximum Output: 57 CP

Attempting 57 CP would be dangerous.

It represents the edge of what their chakra network can tolerate.

Trying to exceed even that should usually be extremely difficult or impossible without a special circumstance.

### Output Overexertion

## Ruleset 3.4 — Chakra Nature & Affinity

Chakra nature should determine **what kind of elemental transformation a character can perform, how naturally it comes to them, and how difficult further elemental development becomes**.

The most important distinction should be:

> **Affinity is potential. Nature Transformation is skill. Jutsu are applications.**

A character may be naturally suited to Fire Release and still know almost no Fire techniques.

Likewise, someone can eventually learn an element they have no natural affinity for, but doing so should generally require substantially more training.

---

# 3.4.1 The Five Basic Nature Transformations

The five standard elemental transformations remain:

- **Fire Release**
- **Wind Release**
- **Lightning Release**
- **Earth Release**
- **Water Release**

Each should have its own trainable Skill.

For example:

> **Fire Nature Transformation — Intermediate III, 42/100**

This Skill represents the character's general ability to convert ordinary chakra into that elemental nature.

It does **not** represent mastery of individual Fire Release jutsu.

---

# 3.4.2 Affinity and Skill Must Remain Separate

A character might have:

> Natural Affinity: Fire  
> Fire Nature Transformation: Basic IV

Another might have:

> Natural Affinity: Water  
> Fire Nature Transformation: Advanced I

The second character is currently better at Fire Release despite lacking the natural Fire affinity.

Affinity affects:

- Learning speed.
- Initial ease.
- Efficiency.
- Natural ceiling pressure.
- Difficulty of advanced mastery.

It should not function as an automatic permanent superiority bonus.

---

# 3.4.3 Primary Affinity

Most chakra-using characters should have one **Primary Nature Affinity**.

This is the element their chakra naturally transforms into most easily.

Primary affinity should generally provide:

- Faster early learning.
- Reduced difficulty for basic transformation.
- Better initial efficiency.
- Easier progression into advanced elemental control.
- A higher likelihood of discovering the nature naturally.

The affinity itself does **not** grant jutsu.

A character can know they have Wind affinity and still be incapable of producing a useful Wind Release technique.

---

Affinity should not necessarily be known automatically.

It can be discovered through:

- Chakra paper.
- Specialized testing methods.
- Elemental training.
- An instructor recognizing tendencies.
- Accidental manifestation.
- Clan knowledge.
- Certain sensory techniques.

This matters particularly for children and civilians.

An Academy student might possess a Lightning affinity for years without knowing it.

Some characters should naturally possess more than one affinity.

This should be uncommon but not inherently extraordinary.

Possible configurations include:

> Fire — Strong  
> Earth — Moderate

or:

> Water — Strong  
> Wind — Strong

Multiple affinities can result from:

- Genetics.
- Clan heritage.
- Unusual chakra composition.
- Exceptional innate talent.
- Certain special abilities.

Having two affinities does **not** automatically mean possessing a combined Kekkei Genkai.

A character naturally talented in Water and Wind is not automatically an Ice Release user.

Affinity shouldn't necessarily be binary.

Internally, it can exist along a spectrum such as:

**Strong Affinity**  
The chakra readily transforms into that nature.

**Normal Affinity**  
A genuine natural inclination.

**Weak Affinity**  
Some innate compatibility, but not enough to count as a primary affinity.

**Neutral**  
No meaningful natural advantage or disadvantage.

**Difficult Compatibility**  
The character can potentially learn it, but transformation comes unusually poorly to them.

We don't necessarily need to expose these exact labels constantly.

The simulation can track underlying compatibility.

---

# 3.4.7 No Element Should Be Universally Incompatible

Ordinary characters should generally be capable of learning any of the five basic natures with enough training.

There should usually be no rule like:

> "You were born Fire affinity, therefore you can never use Water Release."

Instead, off-affinity elements become progressively harder.

This supports characters who intentionally broaden their arsenal over a long career.

> **Basic I–V**  
> **Intermediate I–V**  
> **Advanced I–V**  
> **Expert I–V**  
> **Master I–V**

for each element.

For example:

> Water Nature Transformation — Advanced II, 71/100

Characters therefore progress independently in every nature.

Someone might have:

> Fire — Expert I  
> Earth — Intermediate IV  
> Lightning — Basic II  
> Wind — Untrained  
> Water — Untrained

This creates meaningful specialization.

- Successfully converting chakra into the element.
- Transformation stability.
- Elemental purity.
- Efficiency.
- Intensity.
- Fine elemental control.
- Maintaining transformation.
- Manipulating elemental properties.
- Combining elemental chakra with Shape Transformation.
- Meeting prerequisites for elemental jutsu.

- Chakra Control.

- Individual jutsu mastery.

# 3.4.10 Basic Nature Transformation

At **Basic**, the character is learning to produce the nature itself.

Typical progression might involve:

- Brief elemental manifestation.
- Applying elemental chakra to an object.
- Changing chakra properties.
- Maintaining small amounts.
- Performing simple elemental exercises.
- Learning low-complexity elemental jutsu.

Basic proficiency does not mean the character can freely manipulate the element.

---

# 3.4.11 Intermediate Nature Transformation

At **Intermediate**, the shinobi can reliably produce and use that element.

Capabilities may include:

- Combat-usable elemental techniques.
- Moderate-scale transformation.
- Maintaining elemental effects.
- Basic environmental manipulation.
- Elemental weapon enhancement.
- Using the element while moving or under pressure.

This is likely where most ordinary elemental specialists spend much of their careers.

---

# 3.4.12 Advanced Nature Transformation

Advanced practitioners begin manipulating the element with considerable sophistication.

They may:

- Change intensity or properties.
- Shape large amounts reliably.
- Use complex elemental jutsu.
- Maintain transformation under disruption.
- Efficiently combine nature and shape.
- Develop original elemental techniques.

Advanced elemental proficiency should be notably uncommon.

---

# 3.4.13 Expert Nature Transformation

Experts possess exceptional understanding of the nature.

They might:

- Use highly demanding elemental techniques.
- Modify properties during execution.
- Adapt techniques to environmental conditions.
- Reduce unnecessary chakra loss.
- Maintain very large or very precise transformations.
- Create unusual elemental applications.

Expert users can start pushing beyond standard village instruction.

---

# 3.4.14 Master Nature Transformation

A Master represents extraordinary understanding of that chakra nature.

This should enable:

- Extremely refined elemental conversion.
- Exceptional efficiency.
- Highly unusual original techniques.
- Large-scale manipulation where reserves/output permit.
- Extreme precision where Control permits.
- Rapid adaptation.
- Potential development of entirely new applications of the nature.

Again:

> Master Fire Nature Transformation does not automatically mean infinite Fire power.

Reserves, Output, Control, Shape Transformation, and technique mastery remain relevant.

---

# 3.4.15 Training Primary Affinity

A character's primary affinity should be significantly easier to train than other natures.

For example, early Fire training for a Fire-affinity shinobi might progress relatively quickly.

Their instructor may describe it as:

> "The transformation feels natural."

This should translate mechanically into:

- Lower learning difficulty.
- Faster Skill XP under appropriate training.
- Lower early chakra waste.
- Reduced failure severity.

The advantage should become somewhat less dramatic at very high mastery.

Even naturally gifted users must work extremely hard to reach Expert or Master levels.

---

# 3.4.16 Training Secondary Natures

Learning additional natures should require progressively more investment.

The first nature is usually easiest.

The second requires significantly more training.

The third becomes increasingly difficult.

The fourth and fifth should be exceptional accomplishments.

However, I do **not** think we should impose an arbitrary rule like:

> "Only Jōnin may learn a second element."

Rank does not grant elemental ability.

Instead, most Jōnin know multiple natures because they have:

- More years of training.
- Better Attributes.
- Better instruction.
- Greater Skill development.
- Broader mission experience.

Rank correlation emerges naturally.

---

# 3.4.17 Additional Nature Difficulty

Learning multiple elements should face two separate challenges:

**Compatibility Difficulty**

How naturally the element suits the character.

**Development Load**

How much time and practice is required to maintain proficiency in multiple complex Skills.

This means even an exceptionally talented character has limited training time.

Learning five elements comes at the opportunity cost of not specializing as deeply elsewhere.

That is a much better balancing mechanism than arbitrary hard caps.

---

# 3.4.18 Elemental Breadth vs. Specialization

The system should strongly support both.

### Broad Elementalist

> Fire — Advanced II  
> Water — Advanced I  
> Earth — Intermediate V  
> Lightning — Intermediate IV  
> Wind — Intermediate III

Extremely versatile.

### Specialist

> Fire — Master III  
> Earth — Basic II

Far narrower but dramatically more refined in their specialty.

Neither should automatically be superior.

The environment, mission, opponent, and jutsu selection determine which matters more.

---

# 3.4.19 Learning Through Jutsu vs. Learning the Nature

A character should not simply learn an elemental jutsu while having no ability to produce its element.

There are two pathways:

**Nature-first training**

Learn elemental transformation, then learn jutsu.

**Integrated training**

Develop the nature while learning a particular technique.

The second can work for simple jutsu but may result in narrow proficiency.

For example, someone may learn one basic Fire technique while their general Fire Nature Transformation remains poor.

This creates believable cases where characters know a technique without being broadly skilled elementalists.

---

# 3.4.20 Nature Transformation Prerequisites

Elemental jutsu should have minimum Nature Transformation requirements.

Example:

> Fire Release: Great Fireball Technique  
> Fire Nature Transformation: Basic III  
> Chakra Control: Basic IV  
> Output: Basic III  
> Individual Jutsu Mastery: Unlearned → trainable

A more advanced technique might require:

> Fire Nature Transformation: Advanced II  
> Chakra Control: Intermediate V  
> Output: Advanced I

Requirements should reflect actual mechanics rather than rank alone.

---

# 3.4.21 Elemental Power Is Not Just Nature Skill

The strength of an elemental technique should depend on combinations of:

- Nature Transformation.
- Chakra Output.
- Chakra investment.
- Chakra Control.
- Shape Transformation.
- Jutsu mastery.
- Relevant Attributes.
- Environment.

Nature Skill determines **quality of transformation**, not raw energy by itself.

---

# 3.4.22 Elemental Purity

Higher Nature Transformation can produce increasingly stable and refined chakra.

Poor elemental transformation might result in:

- Weak flames.
- Unstable lightning.
- Crumbling earth.
- Poorly controlled water.
- Dispersed wind.

Higher mastery produces more consistent elemental characteristics.

This gives Nature Transformation direct mechanical value beyond meeting prerequisites.

---

# 3.4.23 Elemental Properties

At higher levels, users should learn to manipulate properties of their element.

Examples:

### Fire
- Temperature.
- Burn persistence.
- Spread.
- Concentration.

### Wind
- Sharpness.
- Pressure.
- Dispersion.
- Directional flow.

### Lightning
- Voltage/intensity.
- Concentration.
- Conductivity.
- Neuromuscular disruption.

### Earth
- Density.
- Hardness.
- Shape.
- Stability.

### Water
- Pressure.
- Flow.
- Volume distribution.
- Cohesion.

These are **applications of elemental mastery**, not separate Attributes.

---

# 3.4.24 Canon Elements Do Not Have Fixed Real-World Physics

Naruto elements should remain internally consistent without becoming a physics simulator.

For example:

Lightning Release does not require us to calculate volts and amperage.

Fire Release does not require thermodynamic simulation.

Instead, real-world principles can guide believable interactions while Naruto's chakra mechanics remain primary.

This protects us from unnecessary scope creep.

---

# 3.4.25 Elemental Advantage

The traditional elemental cycle remains:

> **Fire > Wind > Lightning > Earth > Water > Fire**

But this should represent a **natural interaction advantage**, not automatic victory.

A weaker advantageous element can still lose to a dramatically stronger technique.

For example:

A tiny Water Release technique should not automatically extinguish an enormous Fire Release attack merely because Water has the advantage.

Resolution should consider:

- Technique strength.
- Chakra investment.
- Output.
- Mastery.
- Scale.
- Environmental circumstances.
- Elemental advantage.

Elemental affinity becomes one factor among several.

---

# 3.4.26 Strength of Elemental Advantage

Elemental advantage should matter most when two techniques are reasonably comparable.

If two techniques are close in effective strength:

> Advantage can decide the interaction.

If one is overwhelmingly stronger:

> Raw difference can overcome the disadvantage.

This prevents elemental matchups from becoming rigid rock-paper-scissors combat.

---

# 3.4.27 Elemental Interactions Beyond the Main Cycle

We should allow logical secondary interactions.

Examples:

- Lightning interacting strongly with water.
- Fire consuming flammable terrain.
- Earth blocking physical projectiles.
- Wind spreading fire.
- Water altering terrain.
- Lightning traveling through conductive surfaces.

These should emerge from circumstances and technique design.

They are not universal bonuses.

---

# 3.4.28 Environmental Sources

Some elemental techniques can use existing material.

Examples:

- Water from a lake.
- Earth from the ground.
- Existing fire.
- Conductive metal.
- Strong natural wind.

Using environmental material can potentially:

- Reduce chakra cost.
- Reduce Output requirements.
- Increase available scale.
- Speed execution.

But the technique must actually be designed to use the environment.

---

# 3.4.29 Creating the Element From Chakra

Characters should generally be capable of producing elemental effects directly from transformed chakra where Naruto mechanics allow it.

This prevents:

> Water Release is impossible because you're indoors.

However, creating large quantities from chakra should usually be more expensive than manipulating abundant existing material.

This creates meaningful environmental advantages without making characters helpless outside the right terrain.

---

# 3.4.30 Environment Should Not Become Mandatory Micromanagement

The simulation should track environmental effects when meaningful.

We should not constantly calculate:

> humidity +7%  
> Fire cost +1.3%

Instead, the engine should recognize broader situations:

> Heavy rain significantly hinders this Fire technique.

or:

> Fighting beside a lake substantially reduces the resource burden of this Water technique.

This keeps the system readable.

---

# 3.4.31 Yin Release

**Yin Release** should be treated as a special chakra transformation associated broadly with spiritual energy, form, imagination, and nonphysical manipulation.

It should not simply be a sixth elemental nature.

Potential applications include:

- Genjutsu foundations.
- Mental/spiritual effects.
- Creating form from chakra.
- Certain Hiden techniques.
- Specialized sealing effects.

Yin Release should have its own trainable Skill where relevant:

> **Yin Transformation — Basic/Intermediate/etc.**

Most characters may use minor Yin-related chakra functions without formally mastering Yin Release as a discipline.

---

# 3.4.32 Yang Release

**Yang Release** broadly concerns physical energy, vitality, and giving life/physical substance to chakra effects.

Potential applications include:

- Body enhancement.
- Healing.
- Growth-related abilities.
- Certain clan techniques.
- Vitality manipulation.

Again, ordinary chakra use can involve Yang components without the character being an advanced Yang Release practitioner.

Formal Yang mastery should be specialized.

---

# 3.4.33 Yin–Yang Release

Yin–Yang Release should represent an exceptionally advanced combination of both principles.

It should be:

- Extremely rare.
- Extremely difficult.
- Usually dependent on extraordinary prerequisites.
- Mechanically distinct from merely knowing both Yin and Yang techniques.

A character having:

> Advanced Yin  
> Advanced Yang

does **not automatically unlock Yin–Yang Release**.

There must be specific knowledge, capability, or special circumstances.

---

# 3.4.34 Combined Elemental Releases

Some characters can combine two chakra natures into a new transformation.

Examples include:

- Ice Release.
- Lava Release.
- Boil Release.
- Magnet Release.
- Storm Release.

These should generally use a **Combined Nature Skill**.

Example:

> **Ice Release Transformation — Intermediate II**

Rather than simply averaging:

> Wind + Water.

The component Nature Skills remain prerequisites, but the combined transformation becomes its own discipline.

---

# 3.4.35 Kekkei Genkai Requirement

Knowing both component elements is **not enough** to create a genetic combined release.

For example:

> Water mastery + Wind mastery ≠ Ice Release

unless the character possesses the required Kekkei Genkai or another legitimate mechanism allowing that combination.

This protects the meaning of bloodline abilities.

---

# 3.4.36 Non-Genetic Element Combinations

Characters should still be capable of combining ordinary techniques without possessing a Kekkei Genkai.

For example:

One character uses:

> Wind Release

to intensify another person's:

> Fire Release.

Or one shinobi individually casts a Water technique followed by Lightning Release.

That is **combination tactics**, not a combined chakra nature.

The distinction matters.

---

# 3.4.37 Artificial Combined Natures

The world may sometimes produce artificial methods for accessing combined transformations.

Potential sources:

- Genetic experimentation.
- Transplantation.
- Scientific ninja tools.
- Chakra implants.
- Seals.
- Special training methods.

These should carry their own compatibility, risk, and rarity rules.

They do not invalidate natural Kekkei Genkai.

---

# 3.4.38 Kekkei Tōta

Three-nature transformations such as Dust Release should require:

- All relevant Nature Transformation prerequisites.
- The actual special ability/heritage necessary.
- Extremely advanced control.
- Specific combined-transformation mastery.

They should be dramatically rarer than ordinary dual-nature Kekkei Genkai.

Again:

> Mastering three normal elements does not automatically create a Kekkei Tōta.
