# Growth, potential and character generation

Source: S1 scope refinement and Components B–G; [source index](../../migration/source-index.md).

## Established — growth principles

Natural maturation, training and practical experience are distinct. Children develop with age; training builds application and supporting foundations; experience develops judgment, composure and use under real conditions.

Time spent does not guarantee growth. Training quality, aptitude, age, challenge, fatigue and recovery matter. Repeated trivial actions or self-destructive exhaustion do not generate unlimited progress. Rest, diminishing returns and finite development capacity constrain growth.

Maintain hidden fractional progress. Ordinary people can become exceptional through specialization; prodigies can waste their advantages. Potential and soft limits influence difficulty rather than fixing destiny.

Potential is largely hidden. Inheritance concerns latent tendencies rather than parents' current trained stats. Family/clan background affects chances, not automatic ability ownership. Early, normal and late maturation allow childhood rankings to change.

## Established — age and rank expectations

Use age to contextualize foundational attributes and rank to contextualize skills, mastery and professional experience. Rank soft limits guide NPC generation; they do not stop an existing Genin from improving.

A very strong young Genin needs a plausible explanation, such as prodigy development or specialization. Rank also depends on leadership, judgment, mission performance, politics and reliability. Do not assign an overall power total or secretly add a rank bonus to checks.

Aging is gradual and attribute-dependent, affected by health, injuries and maintenance. Exceptional values become rarer, especially at younger ages and when several attributes are exceptional together.

## Provisional — growth calculation

Component E proposes:

`Growth = Base Progress × Training Quality × Aptitude × Age Modifier × Difficulty Modifier × Recovery Modifier × Soft-Limit Modifier`

Apply a finite Development Capacity so stacked bonuses cannot create unlimited growth. Distribute available training time/capacity across activities; do not award every activity a full month. Cross-training supports related growth without duplicating the same work.

Proposed monthly progress for one actively trained ordinary skill: I 10–25; II 6–18; III 4–12; IV 2–8; V 1–4; VI 0–2; VII potentially imperceptible for long periods. These are broad expectations, not guaranteed rewards. Early technique mastery was proposed at 5–20 points/month, slowing later.

The original worked example uses 14 × 1.15 × 1.2 × 1.2 × 1.2 × 0.9, approximately 25 progress, then illustrates a capacity limit of 18. That 18 is an example, not a defined universal cap.

Potential profiles can track attribute potential, skill aptitudes, maturation and exceptional traits. The later inheritance model is parental influence plus population pull plus variation. Earlier exact inheritance percentages are historical proposals, not a locked formula.

## Provisional — NPC advancement

For less important off-screen NPCs, use periodic development commitments (negligible/casual/normal/serious/intensive), career, aptitude, age and environment. Increase detail when they matter. Existing state persists when simulation detail changes.

## Open — before implementation

Base Progress, Development Capacity, correlated generation distributions, decay rates and exact trait inheritance must be specified and validated. All numerical benchmark tables are [Provisional](calibration.md). Track work in [issue #1](https://github.com/SirSparxs/Naruto-Shinobi-Life/issues/1).
