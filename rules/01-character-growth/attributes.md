# Attributes and derived values

## Established — seven foundations

Source: S1 user revision, turn ee4e6af7-015a-4679-bc5a-f0640cad1081; scope refinement and Component A.

| Attribute | Foundation |
|---|---|
| Agility | Coordination, dexterity, balance, body control and fine movement |
| Intelligence | Reasoning, learning, memory, planning and comprehension |
| Strength | Raw physical force |
| Endurance | Stamina, resilience, fatigue resistance and recovery capability |
| Perception | Awareness, observation and sensory processing |
| Willpower | Discipline, perseverance and resistance to pain/emotional pressure |
| Chakra | Fundamental chakra capability and potential |

Use an absolute natural 0–100 scale. A score represents the same foundational capability at different ages and ranks; age/rank change how unusual it is. Retain fractional internal progress. Attributes develop more slowly than skills and become harder to raise at high values.

Attributes do not determine success alone. Skilled application, mastery and circumstances matter. Hand-sign performance can depend chiefly on Agility without making hand-sign speed another primary attribute.

## Established — derived values and enhancements

Movement/reaction speed, reserves, output limits, durability and recovery are derived when another system needs them. Do not add independent progression tracks for every derived statistic.

Keep permanent base values separate from temporary effective values. Temporary transformations may exceed 100 without permanently changing the base or automatically unlocking learned skills. Exact ability-specific effects belong in Ruleset 3. Exceptional permanent enhancements require their own defined rules.

Chakra is not the chakra pool; Chakra Control is a skill. See [chakra fundamentals](../03-chakra-jutsu/fundamentals.md).

## Provisional — interpreting a score

Component A's benchmarks are preserved in [calibration](calibration.md). For example, Strength 43 sits above ordinary civilian strength in the trained-shinobi foundation band. It is not an exact lifting-weight conversion or a guarantee of victory.

The natural maximum is described as 100; distinctions between practical biological limits and exceptional permanent enhancement need an explicit exception policy rather than an invented universal conversion.
