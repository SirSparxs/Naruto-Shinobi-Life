# 7.38 — Hidden Social State & Player-Facing Information

Status: Provisional design section pending explicit approval.

Ruleset 7.38 defines the distinction between internal social simulation state and what the player is allowed to know.

The governing principle is:

**The simulation may know exact social state internally, but the player should usually experience relationships through behavior, dialogue, context, and uncertainty rather than exposed internal numbers.**

## 7.38.1 Scope

This section governs:
- hidden relationship values;
- hidden motives;
- concealed attraction;
- concealed resentment;
- uncertainty;
- observable cues;
- social inference;
- Insight;
- lies and misleading behavior;
- explicit disclosure;
- player-facing social feedback;
- confidence in interpretation;
- revealing versus preserving ambiguity.

It does not replace:
- Ruleset 7.6 knowledge and belief state;
- Ruleset 7.7 memory;
- Ruleset 7.17 deception;
- Ruleset 2 hidden resolution.

## 7.38.2 Internal State vs Player Knowledge

The engine may internally know:
- exact Trust;
- exact Affection;
- Romantic Interest;
- Loyalty;
- Resentment;
- Hostility;
- adult Sexual Chemistry;
- current emotional state;
- hidden motives;
- relationship expectations.

The player should not automatically know these values.

## 7.38.3 No Default Social Meters

Normal gameplay should avoid showing:
- Trust 63/100;
- Romantic Interest 48/100;
- Jealousy 72/100;
- Loyalty 81/100.

These values may exist internally for consistency.

They should usually be represented through qualitative behavior.

## 7.38.4 Why Hidden State Matters

Hidden state creates:
- uncertainty;
- interpretation;
- miscommunication;
- meaningful Insight;
- genuine social risk.

If every relationship value is visible, many social systems become deterministic optimization problems.

## 7.38.5 Observable Behavior

The player may observe:
- tone;
- wording;
- facial expression;
- body language;
- response speed;
- avoidance;
- warmth;
- hesitation;
- initiative;
- changed routine.

Behavior provides evidence, not certainty.

## 7.38.6 Behavior Is Ambiguous

The same behavior may have multiple causes.

Example:
An NPC avoids eye contact because of:
- guilt;
- shyness;
- fear;
- attraction;
- distraction;
- cultural habit.

The engine should avoid presenting ambiguous cues as definitive facts.

## 7.38.7 Explicit Statements

When an NPC explicitly says:
- "I trust you.";
- "I'm angry with you.";
- "I don't want to date you.";

the player gains stronger information.

However, statements may still be:
- sincere;
- mistaken;
- incomplete;
- deceptive.

## 7.38.8 Self-Knowledge Is Imperfect

NPCs may not fully understand their own:
- motives;
- attraction;
- jealousy;
- resentment;
- fear.

An honest statement can still be psychologically incomplete.

## 7.38.9 Insight

Insight helps interpret:
- emotional cues;
- hesitation;
- tension;
- likely discomfort;
- possible deception;
- social dynamics.

Insight does not provide direct access to hidden state.

## 7.38.10 Insight Is Not Mind Reading

A strong Insight result should not reveal:
- exact thought;
- exact score;
- secret memory;
- objective truth.

It may reveal:
- "They seem uneasy.";
- "The answer felt rehearsed.";
- "Their friendliness seems strained.";

## 7.38.11 Degrees of Social Inference

Inference may be represented qualitatively as:
- weak impression;
- plausible read;
- strong read;
- near-certainty from direct evidence.

Even strong inference may be wrong when evidence is misleading.

## 7.38.12 Confidence Separate From Accuracy

The player character may feel confident and still be wrong.

Ruleset 7.6 already distinguishes belief confidence from truth.

This principle applies to social interpretation.

## 7.38.13 Hidden Motives

NPC motives should remain hidden unless revealed through:
- behavior;
- evidence;
- disclosure;
- investigation;
- inference.

Do not expose motive merely because it is mechanically relevant.

## 7.38.14 Mixed Motives

NPCs may act from several motives at once.

Example:
- genuine concern;
- desire for approval;
- fear of punishment.

A single visible action may not reveal the full mixture.

## 7.38.15 Concealed Resentment

An NPC may outwardly remain polite while internally:
- angry;
- resentful;
- distrustful.

The player may notice subtle changes without being told the exact state.

## 7.38.16 Concealed Affection

An NPC may hide:
- admiration;
- friendship;
- Romantic Interest;
- adult attraction.

Concealment may arise from:
- shyness;
- duty;
- reputation;
- fear;
- existing commitment.

## 7.38.17 Social Masking

Characters may deliberately present:
- confidence while afraid;
- politeness while hostile;
- indifference while jealous;
- calm while angry.

Ruleset 7.8 governs internal emotion.
Ruleset 7.17 governs deliberate deception where appropriate.

## 7.38.18 Habitual Presentation

Not all misleading presentation is deception.

Some characters naturally:
- smile under stress;
- speak bluntly despite affection;
- remain formal with close friends.

The engine should interpret behavior in context of personality.

## 7.38.19 Relationship Baseline

Player-facing interpretation should compare behavior to the NPC's normal baseline.

A normally warm character becoming formal may be meaningful.

A normally formal character being formal may not be.

## 7.38.20 Change Is Often More Informative Than Absolute Behavior

A sudden change in:
- responsiveness;
- warmth;
- initiative;
- eye contact;
- availability;

may provide stronger evidence than a single isolated action.

## 7.38.21 Social Cues and Culture

Meaning of:
- eye contact;
- touch;
- silence;
- honorifics;
- gifts;
- directness;

may vary by culture, clan, village, or relationship.

Insight should account for cultural familiarity.

## 7.38.22 Expertise and Familiarity

A close friend may read an NPC more accurately because they know:
- habits;
- tells;
- communication style.

A stranger with strong Insight may still lack this baseline.

## 7.38.23 Deception

A deceptive NPC may deliberately create false social cues.

Examples:
- fake warmth;
- fake fear;
- fake attraction;
- false apology.

The player should not automatically know the behavior is false.

## 7.38.24 Successful Deception

When deception succeeds, player-facing output should remain consistent with the false impression.

Do not undermine the deception with narration such as:
- "They convincingly lie."

Instead narrate what the player perceives.

## 7.38.25 Failed Deception

A failed deception may create:
- inconsistency;
- suspicious timing;
- conflicting details;
- visible strain.

It should not always reveal the exact truth.

## 7.38.26 Suspicion

Suspicion should often be represented as:
- "Something feels off.";
- "Their explanation does not fully match what you observed.";

rather than:
- "They are lying."

unless evidence is sufficient.

## 7.38.27 Relationship Labels Can Be Known While Dimensions Remain Hidden

The player may know:
- "friend";
- "partner";
- "rival";
- "commander";

without knowing exact:
- Trust;
- Resentment;
- Loyalty;
- satisfaction.

Labels summarize structure, not complete internal state.

## 7.38.28 Public vs Private Relationship State

A relationship may be publicly known while private quality remains hidden.

Example:
- publicly happy marriage;
- privately severe conflict.

Player knowledge depends on access.

## 7.38.29 Reputation Is Observable Differently

Reputation may be inferred through:
- how groups react;
- rumors;
- introductions;
- titles.

The player should not automatically receive exact reputation values across all audiences.

## 7.38.30 Group Sentiment

The player may observe:
- applause;
- avoidance;
- hesitation;
- hostility;
- deference.

These are evidence of group sentiment.

They do not necessarily mean every member feels the same.

## 7.38.31 Hidden NPC-to-NPC Relationships

NPC relationships may remain unknown to the player.

The player may discover them through:
- observation;
- rumor;
- introduction;
- explicit disclosure;
- shared events.

## 7.38.32 Secret Romance

A secret relationship should remain hidden unless:
- someone reveals it;
- evidence appears;
- the player witnesses relevant behavior.

Do not reveal it merely because the engine tracks it.

## 7.38.33 Hidden Rivalry

Two NPCs may privately dislike each other while maintaining professional behavior.

The player may notice:
- tension;
- unusual criticism;
- avoidance.

Exact Hostility remains hidden.

## 7.38.34 Player Character Internal State

The engine may track automatic internal reactions such as:
- surprise;
- fear;
- embarrassment;
- attraction;
- adult arousal where appropriate.

These can be presented as subjective experience.

The player still controls voluntary action.

## 7.38.35 Player Self-Knowledge

For the player character, information may be more direct because the character can perceive their own internal reactions.

However, complex motives may still remain ambiguous unless the player defines them.

The system should not dictate deep voluntary beliefs or commitments without established cause.

## 7.38.36 Hidden Resolution

Social checks may be hidden when revealing success or failure would expose unknown state.

Examples:
- detecting deception;
- reading concealed emotion;
- evaluating rumor credibility.

Ruleset 2 governs hidden resolution.

## 7.38.37 Do Not Announce Hidden Rolls

Player-facing text should not say:
- "Hidden Insight check failed."

Instead, present the resulting perception naturally.

## 7.38.38 Outcome Uncertainty

The player may leave an interaction unsure whether:
- request succeeded long-term;
- NPC believed them;
- NPC was sincere;
- relationship improved.

Uncertainty is valid when the character lacks evidence.

## 7.38.39 Delayed Social Feedback

Some social consequences become visible later.

Example:
- NPC agrees politely;
- later fails to follow through.

Do not reveal hidden unwillingness immediately if the player had no way to know.

## 7.38.40 Feedback Must Still Be Fair

Hidden state should not make the game arbitrary.

The player should receive enough contextual evidence to make reasonable decisions when their character could perceive it.

## 7.38.41 Fair Ambiguity

Good ambiguity means:
- multiple interpretations are plausible;
- cues exist;
- later revelations make sense.

Bad ambiguity means:
- no evidence;
- arbitrary reversal;
- hidden state contradicts all observable behavior without explanation.

## 7.38.42 Foreshadowing

Important hidden social developments may be foreshadowed through:
- changed routines;
- tone;
- unusual absences;
- inconsistent explanations;
- new alliances;
- changed priorities.

Foreshadowing should be subtle and causal.

## 7.38.43 Revelation

Hidden state may become known through:
- confession;
- confrontation;
- evidence;
- overheard conversation;
- rumor;
- direct observation.

Revelation should update player knowledge, not rewrite prior state.

## 7.38.44 Retrospective Meaning

After a revelation, earlier ambiguous events may gain new interpretation.

Example:
- prior avoidance now appears connected to guilt.

This is acceptable when the prior behavior was consistent.

## 7.38.45 Player Notes vs Objective State

If the interface stores player-facing notes, distinguish:
- known fact;
- character belief;
- suspicion;
- rumor.

Do not write suspicions as objective truth.

## 7.38.46 Qualitative Relationship Descriptions

When appropriate, player-facing descriptions may use terms such as:
- distant;
- cordial;
- warm;
- trusting;
- strained;
- hostile.

These should describe observable relationship quality, not expose exact hidden scores.

## 7.38.47 Relationship Milestones

Some states may become explicit because characters directly establish them.

Examples:
- "We're friends.";
- "We're exclusive.";
- "I don't trust you anymore.";

The player may know these expressed positions even if underlying dimensions remain more complex.

## 7.38.48 Contradiction Between Words and Behavior

An NPC may say:
- "I'm fine."

while behavior suggests otherwise.

The player may infer tension without knowing the cause.

## 7.38.49 Insight on Contradiction

Strong Insight may identify:
- mismatch between statement and emotion;
- discomfort;
- avoidance.

It should not automatically reveal:
- "They are angry because of event X."

unless enough evidence exists.

## 7.38.50 Information Thresholds

Different kinds of social knowledge require different evidence.

Low threshold:
- visible anger.

Medium threshold:
- likely resentment toward player.

High threshold:
- hidden motive or secret relationship.

The engine should scale certainty accordingly.

## 7.38.51 Player-Facing Dialogue Choice Information

If choices are presented, avoid meta-information such as:
- "[Persuasion 75%]";
- "[Romance +5]".

Choices should describe:
- action;
- tone;
- intent.

The player should reason from context.

## 7.38.52 Optional Mechanical Transparency

For debugging, testing, or explicit out-of-character design mode, hidden values may be inspected.

This should remain separate from normal in-character gameplay.

## 7.38.53 Developer / GM View

The simulation may maintain an internal view containing:
- exact values;
- motives;
- secrets;
- confidence;
- hidden event history.

This state should be persistent and consistent.

## 7.38.54 Save-State Separation

Player-facing save information should not accidentally expose hidden GM state.

If hidden state is persisted externally, it should be stored separately from player-visible summaries where feasible.

## 7.38.55 Memory of What Player Knows

The engine should track not only:
- what is true;

but:
- what the player character knows;
- suspects;
- believes.

This prevents accidental omniscience later.

## 7.38.56 Knowledge Loss and Forgetting

If the player character plausibly forgets minor social information over long periods, this may be represented narratively.

Major relationship events should generally remain accessible through memory unless another mechanic interferes.

## 7.38.57 NPC Beliefs About Player Knowledge

NPCs may have beliefs about what the player knows.

This affects:
- deception;
- secrecy;
- confrontation;
- disclosure.

An NPC may hide something they think the player has not discovered.

## 7.38.58 Double-Blind Social State

The player may misunderstand the NPC.

The NPC may also misunderstand the player.

Both beliefs can differ from objective reality.

This is desirable.

## 7.38.59 Player Intent Visibility

NPCs should not automatically know the player's true motive.

They infer intent from:
- words;
- behavior;
- history;
- reputation.

The player may deceive or be misunderstood.

## 7.38.60 No Narrative Omniscience

Narration should not casually reveal:
- secret motives;
- unseen conversations;
- exact emotional states;

unless the chosen narrative mode explicitly allows omniscient information.

Default simulation should remain character-limited.

## 7.38.61 NPC Perspective Scenes

If the game intentionally presents an NPC-perspective scene, hidden information may be revealed to the player as audience knowledge.

The engine must distinguish:
- player knowledge;
- player-character knowledge.

The character cannot act on audience-only information without a causal path.

## 7.38.62 Out-of-Character Requests

If the player explicitly asks for:
- debug information;
- rules explanation;
- save-state inspection;

the engine may expose internal state outside the fiction when appropriate.

This should not retroactively grant the character that knowledge.

## 7.38.63 Social Read Examples

Instead of:
- "Trust = 71."

Use:
- "She answers without the usual hesitation and seems comfortable leaving the decision to you."

Instead of:
- "Romantic Interest = 54."

Use:
- "He seeks you out more often than before, but his behavior remains ambiguous enough that you cannot tell whether it is romantic."

## 7.38.64 Misleading but Fair Example

An NPC who is secretly resentful may:
- remain polite;
- stop initiating contact;
- answer briefly;
- decline invitations.

The player can notice change without being told:
- "Resentment increased."

## 7.38.65 Social Feedback Audit

For important player-facing social feedback, the engine should be able to answer:

1. What is objectively true?
2. What does the NPC believe?
3. What does the player character know?
4. What cues are actually observable?
5. What could reasonably be inferred?
6. What remains uncertain?
7. Is any narration accidentally revealing hidden state?
8. If the player is wrong, was the misunderstanding fairly supported?

## 7.38.66 Design Standard

A hidden-social-state mechanic should be rejected or revised if it:
- exposes exact relationship values during ordinary play;
- makes Insight mind reading;
- announces hidden rolls;
- reveals deception by narration;
- hides all useful feedback;
- creates arbitrary surprises without prior causal evidence;
- confuses player knowledge with character knowledge;
- treats confidence as accuracy;
- automatically exposes secret NPC-to-NPC relationships;
- forces the player to act on audience-only information.

## Governing Rule

**Social information should be presented through what the character can actually observe, know, infer, remember, or be told, preserving uncertainty and deception while still providing enough fair evidence for meaningful player decisions.**
