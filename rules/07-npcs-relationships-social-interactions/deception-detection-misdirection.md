# 7.17 — Deception, Detection & Misdirection

Status: Provisional design section pending explicit approval.

Ruleset 7.17 defines how characters create, maintain, detect, challenge, and unravel false or misleading social information.

The governing principle is:

**Deception changes what another character believes or notices; it does not alter objective reality, erase contradictory evidence, or guarantee permanent belief.**

## 7.17.1 Deception Is an Information Action

Deception should target one or more of:
- belief;
- interpretation;
- attention;
- source attribution;
- identity;
- motive;
- emotional impression.

The engine should identify what false or incomplete belief the actor is trying to create or preserve.

## 7.17.2 Core Deception Types

### Direct Lie
A knowingly false statement.

### Omission
Leaving out relevant information.

### Half-Truth
Using true facts to support a misleading conclusion.

### Misdirection
Drawing attention toward an incorrect interpretation or irrelevant detail.

### False Identity
Misrepresenting who someone is.

### Cover Story
A broader constructed explanation designed to account for suspicious facts.

### Emotional Masking
Concealing or falsifying outward emotion.

### Fabricated Evidence
Creating false supporting material.

Fabricated evidence may also invoke other rulesets depending on how it is physically created.

## 7.17.3 Deception Intent

Before resolution, determine:
- what the target currently believes;
- what the deceiver wants them to believe;
- what facts the target already knows;
- what evidence exists;
- what contradictions could emerge.

The lie should be evaluated against the actual information environment.

## 7.17.4 Plausibility

A lie's plausibility depends on:
- consistency with known facts;
- target expectations;
- actor reputation;
- available evidence;
- cultural context;
- subject expertise;
- timing;
- motive.

Skill cannot make an impossible story plausible to someone who knows it is false.

## 7.17.5 Existing Belief

Deception is easier when it reinforces what the target already expects.

It is harder when it directly contradicts:
- firsthand observation;
- trusted evidence;
- strong prior knowledge;
- deeply held certainty.

## 7.17.6 Source Trust

A trusted source may be believed with less verification.

A known liar may face immediate skepticism.

Trust affects the initial burden of proof but does not make deception automatically succeed or fail.

## 7.17.7 Lie Construction

A convincing lie usually requires:
- internal consistency;
- plausible detail;
- appropriate emotional presentation;
- knowledge of the target;
- awareness of known facts;
- a believable motive.

Intelligence may help construct the story.
Perception may help adapt to reactions.
Willpower may help maintain composure.

## 7.17.8 Simplicity vs Complexity

Simple lies are often easier to maintain.

Complex lies create more:
- details;
- dependencies;
- opportunities for contradiction;
- memory burden.

A large cover story may require ongoing maintenance.

## 7.17.9 Truth Anchoring

Deceivers may embed lies inside true information.

This can improve plausibility because much of the story survives verification.

The false component still remains vulnerable.

## 7.17.10 Omission

Omission does not automatically count as a lie.

Whether it is deceptive depends on:
- intent;
- reasonable expectation of disclosure;
- how the omission shapes interpretation.

A character may truthfully answer a narrow question while intentionally hiding the more important fact.

## 7.17.11 Misdirection

Misdirection seeks to make the target focus on:
- the wrong suspect;
- the wrong motive;
- the wrong timeline;
- an irrelevant detail.

Success may redirect attention without creating a firm false belief.

## 7.17.12 Emotional Deception

Characters may conceal or fake:
- fear;
- anger;
- grief;
- attraction;
- confidence;
- guilt;
- adult arousal.

Emotional masking affects outward cues, not the actual internal state.

## 7.17.13 Deception vs Performance

Performance may support deliberate presentation.

Deception governs the false impression.

Example:
- Performance controls how convincingly someone acts distressed.
- Deception determines whether that false distress misleads the observer.

## 7.17.14 Deception vs Concealment

Deception creates or preserves a false impression.

Concealment primarily prevents access to information.

A character can hide a secret without lying if no misleading statement is required.

## 7.17.15 Deception vs Disguise

Physical disguise may involve equipment, transformation, chakra, or other systems.

Ruleset 7 governs the social identity claim and whether behavior supports it.

Other rulesets govern the actual physical transformation.

## 7.17.16 Detection Is Not Mind Reading

Detection should rely on:
- observed inconsistency;
- behavioral cues;
- contradictory evidence;
- implausibility;
- known motive;
- source unreliability;
- domain expertise.

Insight can improve inference.

It does not reveal:
- exact truth;
- exact motive;
- hidden memories;
- automatic "lie detected" status.

## 7.17.17 Nervousness Is Ambiguous

A nervous person may be:
- lying;
- afraid of being accused;
- embarrassed;
- traumatized;
- socially anxious;
- hiding something unrelated.

Therefore:
**nervousness is evidence of state, not proof of deception.**

## 7.17.18 Contradiction Detection

A contradiction may arise between:
- two statements;
- statement and evidence;
- statement and known timeline;
- statement and observed behavior;
- two witnesses.

Detecting a contradiction may increase Suspicion without revealing which claim is false.

## 7.17.19 Suspicion States

After encountering possible deception, a target may become:
- mildly uncertain;
- suspicious;
- strongly suspicious;
- convinced of deception.

Suspicion should affect:
- verification;
- questioning;
- disclosure;
- future trust.

Suspicion is not the same as knowing the truth.

## 7.17.20 Follow-Up Questions

Questioning can pressure a deception by forcing:
- additional detail;
- timeline consistency;
- motive explanation;
- source attribution.

Each added invented detail increases future consistency burden.

## 7.17.21 Cover Story Maintenance

Long-running cover stories should track important claims.

Example:

**Cover Story:** Karn was at the east gate during the theft.
- claimed location;
- claimed witnesses;
- claimed purpose;
- timeline;
- supporting evidence;
- known vulnerabilities.

The engine need not preserve every sentence, only facts necessary for consistency.

## 7.17.22 Lie Ledger

For major deceptions, the simulation may internally track:
- lie target;
- false claim;
- true state;
- who heard it;
- who believed it;
- who suspects;
- supporting lies;
- contradictory evidence;
- exposure risk.

This is useful for conspiracies and infiltration.

## 7.17.23 Memory Burden

The deceiver must remember their own important false claims.

Forgetting what was previously said can create contradiction.

Ruleset 7.7 governs memory quality.

## 7.17.24 Prepared Deception

Preparation may improve deception through:
- rehearsed story;
- forged records;
- coordinated witnesses;
- research;
- established false identity.

Preparation changes context.

It does not automatically guarantee success.

## 7.17.25 Improvised Deception

Improvised lies are more dependent on:
- rapid reasoning;
- composure;
- target knowledge;
- adapting to questions.

They may be easier to contradict later.

## 7.17.26 Coordinated Deception

Multiple people may maintain the same false story.

This requires:
- shared knowledge;
- agreed details;
- communication;
- discipline.

More participants create more possible leakage points.

## 7.17.27 Deception Through Third Parties

A character may intentionally spread false information through:
- rumor;
- intermediaries;
- forged messages;
- planted witnesses.

Ruleset 7.20 will govern wider rumor propagation.

## 7.17.28 Partial Belief

Targets do not need to accept or reject a story entirely.

They may believe:
- the event occurred;
but doubt:
- the motive.

Or believe:
- most of the timeline;
but suspect:
- one missing detail.

Partial belief should be common.

## 7.17.29 Temporary Deception

Some lies only need to work briefly.

Examples:
- distract a guard;
- delay pursuit;
- gain entry;
- conceal immediate emotion.

Later discovery may still cause consequences.

Short-term tactical success and long-term credibility are separate.

## 7.17.30 Long-Term Deception

Long-term deception requires:
- consistency;
- reinforcement;
- evidence management;
- behavioral compatibility;
- maintaining access.

It becomes increasingly vulnerable as more people and evidence become involved.

## 7.17.31 Exposure

A deception may be exposed through:
- confession;
- contradictory evidence;
- witness testimony;
- failed cover story;
- surveillance;
- document verification;
- repeated inconsistency.

Exposure should identify what the target now knows and what remains uncertain.

## 7.17.32 Exposure Severity

Consequences depend on:
- lie importance;
- duration;
- motive;
- harm caused;
- relationship;
- whether target was manipulated;
- whether confession was voluntary;
- whether accountability followed.

A trivial social lie should not equal years of major deception.

## 7.17.33 Confession Before Exposure

Voluntary confession may reduce damage compared with being caught.

Possible effects:
- Trust still decreases;
- Respect may increase for taking responsibility;
- Resentment may be lower than after forced exposure.

Context matters.

## 7.17.34 Caught in a Lie

Being caught may affect:
- Trust;
- Respect;
- Resentment;
- Hostility;
- reputation;
- future credibility.

The target may also reinterpret older memories.

## 7.17.35 Cascade Failure

One exposed lie may cause other claims to be reexamined.

This does not mean:
- "one lie exposed = everything ever said becomes false."

Instead it may:
- lower source credibility;
- reopen old doubts;
- trigger investigation.

## 7.17.36 Successful Deception Can Still Have Cost

A lie may succeed but create:
- stress;
- future maintenance burden;
- guilt;
- relationship vulnerability;
- blackmail risk;
- conflicting stories.

Success is not consequence-free.

## 7.17.37 Deception of Loved Ones

High Familiarity may make deception:
- harder because the target knows normal behavior;
- easier because Trust lowers skepticism.

Which effect dominates depends on context.

## 7.17.38 Self-Deception

NPCs may rationalize or misrepresent their own motives to themselves.

Self-deception is not resolved through ordinary Deception skill.

It arises from:
- identity;
- emotion;
- memory;
- belief systems.

## 7.17.39 False Accusations

A target may incorrectly conclude someone is lying.

This can happen because:
- bias;
- nervous behavior;
- contradictory evidence;
- poor interpretation.

Insight should not eliminate false positives.

## 7.17.40 Expert Detection

Experts may recognize inconsistencies others miss.

Examples:
- medic catches false medical claim;
- shinobi recognizes impossible mission timeline;
- seal expert notices forged formula.

Expertise provides better evidence for social inference.

## 7.17.41 Genjutsu & Supernatural Deception

Ruleset 3 owns supernatural:
- illusions;
- memory alteration;
- sensory manipulation;
- mind-affecting techniques.

Ruleset 7 handles:
- resulting beliefs;
- suspicion;
- relationship consequences;
- later discovery.

## 7.17.42 Deception and Interrogation

Interrogation may challenge lies through:
- repeated questioning;
- contradictions;
- evidence;
- separate witnesses.

The interrogator still cannot automatically know which answer is true.

## 7.17.43 Deception and Seduction

Adult Seduction may be used deceptively when someone:
- fakes attraction;
- conceals motive;
- uses intimate signaling to manipulate.

Seduction governs the intimate social method.
Deception governs whether the false interest or hidden motive is believed.

Discovery may cause severe Trust and Resentment consequences.

## 7.17.44 Romantic Deception

Deception in romance may involve:
- false exclusivity;
- hidden relationship;
- false emotional commitment;
- concealed motives.

The social impact should depend on expectations and harm.

## 7.17.45 Adult Sexual Deception

For adults, sexual deception may involve false statements about:
- exclusivity;
- identity;
- material relationship expectations;
- relevant boundaries or circumstances.

A deception check cannot transform deception into valid consent where the concealed fact is materially relevant to willingness.

Consent rules remain independent.

## 7.17.46 Group Deception

When deceiving a group:
- individuals may vary in skepticism;
- leaders may shape group response;
- one skeptical member may investigate;
- public contradiction can destabilize the lie.

Do not treat a group as one mind.

## 7.17.47 Deception Resolution

Default process:
1. Identify false or hidden information.
2. Identify what target already knows.
3. Check plausibility.
4. Determine target suspicion and source trust.
5. Select Deception method and attribute.
6. Resolve uncertainty through Ruleset 2.
7. Determine belief state.
8. Record important false belief and source.
9. Track future maintenance or exposure risk.

## 7.17.48 Outcome Degrees

### Strong Failure
- obvious contradiction;
- lie exposed;
- severe suspicion;
- relationship damage.

### Failure
- target does not accept the deception;
- may remain uncertain.

### Partial Success
- target accepts part;
- doubts details;
- delays verification.

### Success
- intended false impression is accepted for now.

### Exceptional Success
- strong temporary confidence;
- reduced immediate suspicion;
- clean cover.

Exceptional success still cannot erase external contradictory evidence forever.

## 7.17.49 Retry Rule

After suspicion or failed deception, repeating the same story usually does not provide a free new check.

A new attempt requires something such as:
- additional evidence;
- revised explanation;
- new witness;
- changed context.

Repeated insistence may increase suspicion.

## 7.17.50 Design Standard

A deception mechanic should be rejected or revised if it:
- treats Deception as reality alteration;
- makes Insight automatic lie detection;
- equates nervousness with guilt;
- ignores evidence;
- ignores prior knowledge;
- lets one successful check preserve a lie forever;
- forgets important false claims the deceiver must maintain;
- assumes discovering one lie reveals all truth;
- treats all lies as equally harmful;
- lets deception create information the actor does not possess;
- uses sexual deception to bypass adult consent requirements.

## Governing Rule

**A deception succeeds only by creating or preserving a plausible false interpretation in another character's mind, and that false interpretation remains vulnerable to memory, evidence, contradiction, verification, and future behavior.**
