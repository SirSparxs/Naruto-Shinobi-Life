# 7.26 — Flirting, Seduction & Reciprocal Interest

Status: Provisional design section pending explicit approval.

Ruleset 7.26 defines early romantic and adult intimate signaling, flirting, reciprocity, escalation, rejection, and the use of Seduction as an adult-only social skill.

The governing principle is:

**Flirting and seduction communicate or develop interest; they do not create entitlement, attraction, romance, or consent where those states are absent.**

## 7.26.1 Scope

This section governs:
- flirting;
- romantic signaling;
- adult sexual signaling;
- playful attraction;
- reciprocal interest;
- escalation;
- adult Seduction skill use;
- reading signals;
- rejection;
- flirtation that never becomes a relationship.

Later sections govern:
- courtship and relationship formation;
- established romance;
- adult intimacy;
- consent and boundaries;
- jealousy;
- breakups.

## 7.26.2 Flirting Defined

Flirting is behavior that communicates or explores possible romantic or intimate interest.

It may be:
- playful;
- subtle;
- direct;
- teasing;
- affectionate;
- verbal;
- behavioral;
- culturally coded.

Flirting may be serious or unserious.

## 7.26.3 Flirting Does Not Always Mean Intent to Date

A character may flirt because they:
- are genuinely interested;
- enjoy playful interaction;
- enjoy attention;
- are testing chemistry;
- are habitually flirtatious;
- want validation;
- are deliberately misleading someone.

Therefore:

**Flirting is evidence of possible interest, not proof of relationship intent.**

## 7.26.4 Romantic vs Adult Sexual Flirting

Romantic flirting may occur between age-appropriate characters.

Adult sexual flirting and Seduction mechanics apply only when all involved characters are age 18 or older.

Younger characters may engage in age-appropriate:
- crushes;
- teasing;
- dating interest;
- hand-holding;
- romantic compliments;
- awkward courtship.

They do not use adult sexual-state mechanics.

## 7.26.5 Flirting Styles

Possible flirting styles may include:
- playful teasing;
- sincere compliments;
- intellectual banter;
- shy signaling;
- confident directness;
- affectionate attention;
- gifts or gestures;
- deliberate proximity;
- adult suggestive humor where appropriate.

Style should emerge from:
- personality;
- culture;
- confidence;
- experience;
- relationship history.

## 7.26.6 Individual Variation

One NPC may flirt very obviously.

Another may communicate interest through:
- reliability;
- gifts;
- acts of service;
- quiet attention;
- repeated invitations.

The engine should not require one standardized flirt behavior.

## 7.26.7 Cultural Variation

Flirting norms may differ by:
- village;
- clan;
- family;
- generation;
- profession.

A gesture interpreted as romantic in one context may be ordinary politeness in another.

## 7.26.8 Context Matters

The meaning of flirtation changes with:
- privacy;
- audience;
- rank;
- existing relationship;
- professional setting;
- cultural norms;
- current emotion;
- reputation;
- prior rejection.

The same behavior can be welcome in one context and uncomfortable in another.

## 7.26.9 Signaling Interest

Flirting may signal:
- aesthetic attraction;
- romantic interest;
- adult sexual attraction;
- openness to further interaction;
- simple playfulness.

The exact intended signal should be inferred from behavior and character state.

## 7.26.10 Signal Clarity

Signals may be:
- ambiguous;
- moderately clear;
- explicit.

Ambiguity should be normal.

People do not always know whether another person is flirting.

## 7.26.11 Ambiguous Signals

Examples may include:
- unusual attention;
- frequent compliments;
- seeking proximity;
- teasing;
- prolonged conversation;
- repeated invitations.

These can have non-romantic explanations.

Insight may improve interpretation but cannot guarantee certainty.

## 7.26.12 Explicit Signals

Examples:
- asking for a date;
- directly expressing attraction;
- clearly stating romantic interest.

Explicit signaling reduces ambiguity but does not guarantee reciprocity.

## 7.26.13 Misread Flirting

A character may mistake:
- friendliness;
- hospitality;
- professional warmth;
- admiration;
- nervousness;

for flirtation.

They may also fail to recognize genuine interest.

Misreading should depend on:
- Insight;
- experience;
- expectation;
- bias;
- context.

## 7.26.14 Reciprocal Interest

Reciprocity exists when both people possess compatible interest and willingly engage with the interaction.

Reciprocity may be:
- weak;
- tentative;
- growing;
- clear;
- strong.

It should emerge from both directional states.

## 7.26.15 One-Sided Flirting

Flirting may be one-sided.

The target may respond with:
- friendliness;
- awkwardness;
- neutrality;
- avoidance;
- rejection.

The engine should not reinterpret politeness as reciprocal interest.

## 7.26.16 Reciprocal Escalation

When interest is mutual, interaction may gradually escalate through:
- increased attention;
- more personal conversation;
- invitations;
- compliments;
- romantic disclosure;
- closer emotional intimacy;
- adult intimate signaling where appropriate.

Escalation should occur through observable interaction.

## 7.26.17 Escalation Is Not Automatic

Even mutual attraction may not progress because of:
- shyness;
- duty;
- existing relationships;
- fear;
- rank difference;
- conflicting goals;
- cultural restrictions;
- incompatibility.

Mutual attraction creates possibility, not inevitability.

## 7.26.18 Social Momentum

A positive interaction may create temporary social momentum.

Momentum can make immediate continuation feel more natural.

It should decay with:
- time;
- interruption;
- changed context.

Momentum does not permanently increase attraction.

## 7.26.19 Flirtation Feedback

A character may observe:
- reciprocation;
- hesitation;
- discomfort;
- enthusiasm;
- deflection.

That feedback should alter the next reasonable action.

Ignoring clear negative feedback should worsen context.

## 7.26.20 Checking Interest

Characters may test interest through:
- low-stakes invitation;
- playful comment;
- compliment;
- direct question.

Testing should not become repeated pressure.

## 7.26.21 Directness

More direct communication:
- reduces ambiguity;
- increases social risk;
- may accelerate clarity.

Indirect flirting:
- lowers immediate vulnerability;
- increases chance of misunderstanding.

Different characters prefer different styles.

## 7.26.22 Flirting and Reputation

A known flirt may have their behavior interpreted differently.

Others may assume:
- low seriousness;
- social confidence;
- habitual teasing.

Reputation affects interpretation, not objective intent.

## 7.26.23 Flirting and Existing Relationships

Existing commitment may change:
- willingness to reciprocate;
- guilt;
- boundaries;
- reputational risk.

Attraction can still exist without pursuit.

## 7.26.24 Flirting and Power Imbalance

Rank, authority, employment, captivity, dependency, or other power imbalance can alter the meaning of flirtation.

The lower-power person may feel unable to respond freely.

The system should preserve this context.

## 7.26.25 Adult Seduction Defined

For adults 18+ only:

Seduction is the skillful use of communication, presentation, timing, and reciprocal cues to develop romantic or sexual tension where genuine potential already exists.

Seduction may involve:
- confidence;
- suggestive communication;
- pacing;
- reading responses;
- adapting presentation;
- creating an intimate atmosphere.

## 7.26.26 What Seduction Can Affect

Seduction may influence:
- clarity of attraction signaling;
- comfort;
- mutual tension;
- willingness to continue flirting;
- adult current arousal where plausible;
- adult Sexual Chemistry over meaningful repeated interaction.

It does not automatically create:
- Romantic Interest;
- adult Sexual Attraction;
- consent;
- compatibility;
- relationship commitment.

## 7.26.27 Seduction Requires Plausible Potential

Before a Seduction check, determine whether the target is plausibly open to the interaction.

Possible states:
- clearly receptive;
- potentially receptive;
- uncertain;
- uninterested;
- explicitly opposed.

Explicit opposition closes the current attempt.

## 7.26.28 Seduction Is Often Unnecessary

If mutual interest is already obvious and the interaction is natural, no roll may be needed.

Seduction rolls are most useful when:
- quality of execution matters;
- signals are uncertain;
- stakes are socially meaningful.

## 7.26.29 Seduction Skill Pairings

Possible attribute pairings include:
- Seduction + Perception for cue-reading and adaptation;
- Seduction + Willpower for confident directness;
- Seduction + Intelligence for strategic communication;
- Seduction + Agility for physical presentation where relevant.

Ruleset 7.14 owns the broader skill interface.

## 7.26.30 Seduction and Insight

Insight may identify:
- reciprocal attention;
- discomfort;
- hesitation;
- ambiguity.

Seduction determines how the actor responds.

Insight does not create permission.

## 7.26.31 Seduction and Performance

Performance may support:
- presentation;
- confidence;
- deliberately crafted persona.

If the persona is false, Deception may also become relevant.

## 7.26.32 Seduction and Deception

An adult may deceptively:
- fake attraction;
- hide motives;
- exaggerate feelings.

Then:
- Seduction governs the intimate social method;
- Deception governs whether the false impression is believed.

Discovery may create severe relationship consequences.

## 7.26.33 Seduction and Persuasion

Seduction should not be used interchangeably with Persuasion.

Persuasion changes:
- beliefs;
- willingness;
- decisions.

Seduction develops:
- attraction signaling;
- romantic or sexual tension;
- reciprocal engagement.

A clear rejection should not become a Persuasion route.

## 7.26.34 Adult Arousal

For adults, successful reciprocal Seduction may contribute to current arousal where plausible.

Current arousal remains:
- temporary;
- internal;
- separate from consent;
- separate from relationship commitment.

Ruleset 7.8 owns the state.

## 7.26.35 Sexual Chemistry Change

One successful interaction should not radically alter persistent adult Sexual Chemistry.

Chemistry may strengthen through:
- repeated reciprocal experiences;
- discovered compatibility;
- meaningful attraction;
- successful intimate communication.

## 7.26.36 Flirting Without Romantic Intent

A character may flirt while not wanting a relationship.

Possible reasons:
- fun;
- social habit;
- validation;
- curiosity;
- casual interest.

If the other person assumes commitment, misunderstanding may result.

## 7.26.37 Flirting Without Sexual Intent

Romantic flirting does not necessarily imply adult sexual intent.

A person may want:
- dating;
- affection;
- emotional closeness;

without wanting sexual escalation.

## 7.26.38 Adult Sexual Flirting Without Romantic Intent

Adults may also experience sexual interest without Romantic Interest.

The simulation should support:
- casual attraction;
- sexual chemistry;
- no desire for partnership.

Later relationship systems govern what agreements result.

## 7.26.39 Teasing and Banter

Teasing can be:
- friendly;
- rivalrous;
- flirtatious;
- hostile.

Interpretation depends on:
- tone;
- history;
- relationship;
- mutual enjoyment.

The same words may mean different things between different characters.

## 7.26.40 Compliments

Compliments may communicate:
- admiration;
- friendship;
- attraction;
- politeness.

Repeated or context-specific compliments may become stronger signals.

## 7.26.41 Physical Proximity

Seeking proximity can indicate interest but may also arise from:
- friendship;
- teamwork;
- crowded environment;
- cultural norms.

Physical proximity alone should not prove attraction.

## 7.26.42 Invitations

Invitations may clarify interest depending on context.

Examples:
- group activity;
- private meal;
- explicitly stated date.

The more explicit the framing, the less ambiguity.

## 7.26.43 Confession of Interest

A character may directly disclose:
- crush;
- Romantic Interest;
- adult attraction.

The confession itself normally does not need a roll.

The target's response may be uncertain.

## 7.26.44 Vulnerability of Disclosure

Confessing interest may create:
- hope;
- embarrassment;
- relief;
- fear;
- awkwardness.

The emotional effect depends on:
- relationship;
- expected reciprocity;
- personality.

## 7.26.45 Rejection

The target may reject:
- the flirtation;
- a date;
- romantic interest;
- adult sexual escalation.

Rejection is a valid final outcome for the current attempt.

## 7.26.46 Rejection Styles

Rejection may be:
- gentle;
- direct;
- awkward;
- apologetic;
- hostile;
- avoidant.

Style affects emotional and relationship consequences.

## 7.26.47 Rejection Does Not Require Hostility

A target may reject someone while still having:
- Affection;
- Respect;
- friendship;
- aesthetic attraction.

Rejection means the desired relationship outcome is not wanted.

## 7.26.48 Clear Rejection Stops Escalation

After a clear rejection:
- no immediate repeated Seduction attempt;
- no escalating Persuasion;
- no interpreting politeness as hidden consent.

Future reconsideration requires genuine contextual change.

## 7.26.49 Ambiguous Response

An ambiguous response may mean:
- uncertainty;
- discomfort;
- shyness;
- lack of clarity;
- social caution.

The safe mechanical interpretation is:
- do not treat ambiguity as reciprocal escalation.

The actor may seek clarification or allow space.

## 7.26.50 Social Consequences of Pushing

Repeated unwanted flirting may cause:
- discomfort;
- avoidance;
- Resentment;
- Trust loss;
- reputation effects.

This is a changing social context, not repeated neutral attempts.

## 7.26.51 Accepting Rejection Well

Respectful acceptance may:
- preserve friendship;
- preserve Trust;
- reduce awkwardness over time;
- create a positive memory of boundary respect.

It does not guarantee future interest.

## 7.26.52 Handling Rejection Poorly

Possible consequences:
- embarrassment;
- Resentment;
- Hostility;
- social avoidance;
- reputation damage.

Severity depends on behavior.

## 7.26.53 Flirtation and Friendship

Friends may flirt:
- seriously;
- jokingly;
- experimentally.

Flirting does not automatically change the friendship category.

## 7.26.54 Flirtation and Rivalry

Rivals may develop:
- playful chemistry;
- tension;
- mutual admiration.

This may or may not become romance.

## 7.26.55 Group Context

Public flirting may affect:
- embarrassment;
- reputation;
- group dynamics;
- jealousy;
- professional perceptions.

Private flirting may allow greater vulnerability.

## 7.26.56 Third-Party Interpretation

Observers may:
- correctly notice interest;
- misread friendliness;
- spread rumors;
- tease;
- become jealous.

Their beliefs are separate from the actual participants' states.

## 7.26.57 Mutual Interest Recognition

When both parties clearly recognize mutual interest, the relationship may transition from:
- ambiguous flirtation;

to:
- explicit romantic exploration.

That transition belongs partly to 7.27.

## 7.26.58 Flirtation as a Process

Early romantic interaction may evolve through:
1. attraction;
2. signaling;
3. response;
4. reciprocity or non-reciprocity;
5. escalation or withdrawal;
6. clarification;
7. possible courtship.

Not every relationship follows this exact order.

## 7.26.59 No Romance XP

Repeated flirting should not create a hidden grind bar that eventually forces romance.

Every interaction matters through:
- attraction;
- relationship state;
- chemistry;
- context;
- memory;
- willingness.

## 7.26.60 NPC-Initiated Flirting

NPCs may initiate flirting with:
- the player;
- other NPCs.

They should do so based on:
- their attraction;
- personality;
- confidence;
- goals;
- context.

NPCs should not wait passively for player initiation.

## 7.26.61 NPC-to-NPC Romantic Development

NPCs may:
- flirt;
- date;
- reject each other;
- develop mutual attraction;

off-screen.

Important developments should be simulated when causally relevant.

## 7.26.62 Player Agency

The player decides whether their character:
- reciprocates;
- pursues;
- asks someone out;
- continues flirting.

The engine may still determine:
- whether attraction exists;
- social signals noticed;
- emotional reactions;
- adult physiological reactions where appropriate.

## 7.26.63 Hidden State

NPC exact Romantic Interest, attraction, or adult Sexual Chemistry should normally remain hidden.

The player receives:
- behavior;
- dialogue;
- observable cues.

Certainty comes through explicit communication, not meter-reading.

## 7.26.64 Outcome Examples

### Successful Flirting, Low Interest
The target enjoys the interaction but does not develop Romantic Interest.

### Successful Flirting, Mutual Interest
The interaction strengthens reciprocal signaling and makes a date more plausible.

### Awkward Flirting, Existing Attraction
The target may still be interested despite poor execution.

### Excellent Seduction, No Plausible Attraction
The target recognizes skill or confidence but remains uninterested.

### Rejected Advance, Respectful Response
Romance stops while friendship may survive.

## 7.26.65 Design Standard

A flirting or seduction mechanic should be rejected or revised if it:
- treats flirting as proof of intent;
- treats friendliness as automatic flirting;
- makes Seduction create attraction from nothing;
- makes high skill override rejection;
- treats ambiguity as consent or reciprocity;
- turns repeated flirting into romance grinding;
- assumes mutual attraction must progress;
- treats rejection as automatic hostility;
- makes all flirting sexual;
- applies adult Seduction mechanics to characters under 18.

## Governing Rule

**Flirting and seduction should function as signaling and reciprocal social processes in which attraction, willingness, skill, context, and response all matter, with clear rejection ending the current escalation and mutual interest creating opportunity rather than obligation.**
