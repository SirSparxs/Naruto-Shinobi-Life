# 7.25 — Attraction, Interest & Chemistry

Status: Provisional design section pending explicit approval.

Ruleset 7.25 defines attraction, person-specific interest, chemistry, preference compatibility, orientation, and the early states that may lead toward romance.

The governing principle is:

**Attraction is multidimensional, person-specific, and uncertain; it can influence romantic possibility without guaranteeing affection, compatibility, relationship formation, or consent.**

## 7.25.1 Attraction Is Not One Stat

The system should distinguish at least:

- Aesthetic Attraction;
- Interpersonal / Emotional Attraction;
- Romantic Interest;
- Adult Sexual Attraction;
- Interpersonal Chemistry;
- Adult Sexual Chemistry.

These may overlap, but they should not be treated as interchangeable.

## 7.25.2 Aesthetic Attraction

Aesthetic attraction represents finding another person visually appealing, striking, beautiful, handsome, cute, stylish, or otherwise pleasing to look at.

Aesthetic attraction may exist without:
- romantic interest;
- sexual attraction;
- desire for closeness;
- compatibility.

A character may simply think:
- "They're attractive."

without wanting anything from them.

## 7.25.3 Interpersonal / Emotional Attraction

Interpersonal attraction represents feeling drawn toward someone's:
- personality;
- presence;
- humor;
- intelligence;
- warmth;
- confidence;
- values;
- mannerisms.

It may increase desire to:
- spend time together;
- talk;
- know the person better.

It does not automatically mean romance.

## 7.25.4 Romantic Interest

Romantic Interest represents person-specific desire for romantic closeness or a romantic relationship.

It may involve wanting:
- dates;
- affection;
- partnership;
- exclusivity where culturally or personally relevant;
- emotional intimacy.

Romantic Interest remains distinct from:
- Affection;
- Friendship;
- Adult Sexual Attraction;
- Adult Sexual Chemistry;
- compatibility.

## 7.25.5 Adult Sexual Attraction

Adult Sexual Attraction is an adult-only person-specific state representing sexual attraction toward another adult.

It may be influenced by:
- physical presentation;
- personality;
- voice;
- behavior;
- confidence;
- preferences;
- familiarity;
- context.

Adult Sexual Attraction does not imply:
- current arousal;
- romantic interest;
- consent;
- willingness to act.

## 7.25.6 Interpersonal Chemistry

Interpersonal chemistry describes the quality of interaction between two people.

It may emerge from:
- conversational rhythm;
- humor;
- complementary personalities;
- mutual attention;
- shared interests;
- mutual challenge;
- emotional responsiveness.

Chemistry is relational rather than a trait possessed by one character.

## 7.25.7 Adult Sexual Chemistry

Adult Sexual Chemistry is the persistent person-specific tendency for two adults to generate sexual tension or responsiveness with one another.

It is distinct from:
- baseline libido;
- current arousal;
- general sexual orientation;
- consent;
- relationship quality.

Two adults may have strong sexual chemistry while being:
- incompatible;
- hostile;
- emotionally distant.

Two loving partners may have lower sexual chemistry while maintaining strong Affection and commitment.

## 7.25.8 Current Arousal vs Sexual Chemistry

Ruleset 7.8 owns current arousal as a temporary emotional/physiological state.

Ruleset 7.10 tracks persistent adult Sexual Chemistry as a relationship dimension.

Ruleset 7.25 explains how attraction and chemistry emerge.

Current arousal may temporarily increase during an interaction without permanently changing chemistry.

## 7.25.9 Attraction Is Directional

A -> B and B -> A are separate.

Possible states include:
- mutual attraction;
- one-sided attraction;
- mismatched intensity;
- different forms of attraction.

Example:
- A feels romantic and sexual attraction.
- B feels strong friendship and aesthetic attraction only.

Both states are valid.

## 7.25.10 Attraction Is Not Always Conscious

Characters may:
- recognize attraction immediately;
- misinterpret it;
- deny it;
- remain uncertain;
- notice it gradually.

Internal attraction state and conscious understanding may differ.

## 7.25.11 Orientation

Orientation represents a character's baseline pattern of potential romantic and/or sexual attraction.

Where relevant, baseline may distinguish:
- romantic orientation;
- adult sexual orientation.

Orientation constrains plausible attraction but should not be reduced to a rigid procedural label when the character's established identity is more nuanced.

## 7.25.12 Orientation Does Not Guarantee Attraction

Being within someone's orientation means attraction is possible.

It does not mean:
- automatic attraction;
- equal attraction to everyone;
- romantic interest;
- sexual willingness.

## 7.25.13 Orientation Is Not a Social Check

Persuasion or Seduction cannot change another person's orientation through an ordinary interaction.

Long-term self-discovery or identity development may occur naturally where appropriate.

That process is not something another character can skill-check into existence.

## 7.25.14 Age Restrictions

Adult sexual attraction, adult sexual chemistry, adult arousal, Seduction, and adult intimacy mechanics apply only to characters age 18 or older.

Younger characters may experience age-appropriate:
- crushes;
- aesthetic attraction;
- romantic interest;
- jealousy;
- dating;
- rejection.

They do not use adult sexual-state mechanics.

## 7.25.15 Personal Preferences

Characters may have preferences involving:
- appearance;
- personality;
- communication style;
- lifestyle;
- ambition;
- humor;
- confidence;
- shared values;
- relationship style.

For adults, relevant sexual preferences may also affect adult sexual attraction or compatibility.

Preferences should be weighted tendencies, not a shopping checklist.

## 7.25.16 Hard Constraints vs Preferences

The system should distinguish:

### Hard Constraint
Something outside the character's plausible attraction or relationship range.

### Strong Preference
Very influential but not absolute.

### Mild Preference
A factor that may affect attraction slightly.

This prevents every listed preference from behaving like a hard rule.

## 7.25.17 "Type"

A character's "type" may summarize recurring attraction patterns.

It should emerge from:
- orientation;
- preferences;
- prior attraction;
- personality;
- life experience.

Being someone's type increases plausibility.
It does not guarantee attraction.

## 7.25.18 Attraction Can Defy Usual Preference

A character may occasionally be attracted to someone who differs from their typical preferences.

This should be plausible when:
- other attractive qualities are unusually strong;
- chemistry develops through interaction;
- emotional connection changes perception.

This should not be used to ignore genuine hard constraints.

## 7.25.19 First-Impression Attraction

Initial attraction may be influenced by:
- appearance;
- presentation;
- reputation;
- voice;
- manner;
- context;
- first interaction.

First-impression attraction is often uncertain and may change quickly.

## 7.25.20 Attraction Through Familiarity

Attraction may grow through:
- repeated positive interaction;
- increasing Familiarity;
- admiration;
- emotional intimacy;
- shared experiences;
- discovering appealing traits.

It should not automatically increase merely because people spend time together.

## 7.25.21 Attraction Through Admiration

Respect or admiration may contribute to attraction.

Examples:
- competence;
- courage;
- kindness;
- intelligence;
- humor;
- leadership.

Respect alone does not automatically become romance.

## 7.25.22 Attraction Through Vulnerability

Mutual vulnerability may increase emotional closeness and sometimes Romantic Interest.

It may also:
- deepen friendship;
- produce discomfort;
- reveal incompatibility.

No single intimacy event should guarantee romance.

## 7.25.23 Shared Hardship

Shared hardship may intensify:
- Affection;
- Familiarity;
- Trust;
- attraction.

It may also produce:
- trauma association;
- resentment;
- dependency.

The engine should not automatically convert danger into romance.

## 7.25.24 Attraction Decline

Attraction may weaken because of:
- discovered incompatibility;
- changed appearance or presentation;
- values conflict;
- betrayal;
- contempt;
- repeated rejection;
- changing life stage;
- emotional disengagement.

Different attraction types may decline at different rates.

## 7.25.25 Attraction Persistence

Attraction can persist after:
- breakup;
- rejection;
- conflict;
- long separation.

Persistent attraction does not require pursuit.

A character may remain attracted while choosing not to act.

## 7.25.26 Attraction After Rejection

Rejection does not instantly erase attraction.

The rejected character may experience:
- disappointment;
- embarrassment;
- grief;
- lingering interest.

But the refusal should still alter behavior and context.

Persistent attraction does not justify repeated unwanted pursuit.

## 7.25.27 Attraction and Affection

Attraction and Affection are independent.

Possible combinations:
- high attraction, low affection;
- low attraction, high affection;
- both high;
- both low.

This supports:
- crushes on strangers;
- loving friendships;
- sexually charged hostility;
- deeply affectionate but non-romantic bonds.

## 7.25.28 Attraction and Trust

Trust may facilitate emotional intimacy but is not required for attraction.

A character may be attracted to someone they do not trust.

That does not make a healthy relationship likely.

## 7.25.29 Attraction and Respect

Respect may contribute to attraction for some characters.

Contempt may suppress attraction for others.

Neither interaction is universal.

## 7.25.30 Attraction and Fear

Fear can coexist with attraction.

The simulation must not interpret that coexistence as:
- healthy compatibility;
- willingness;
- consent.

## 7.25.31 Attraction and Hostility

Attraction can coexist with:
- rivalry;
- resentment;
- hostility.

This may create strong tension but does not automatically imply romance.

## 7.25.32 Attraction and Status

Status, fame, rank, wealth, or reputation may influence attraction for some characters.

They should function through individual preferences and perception rather than universal bonuses.

## 7.25.33 Attraction and Presentation

Clothing, grooming, confidence, demeanor, and deliberate presentation may influence attraction.

Presentation modifies how existing traits are perceived.

It does not guarantee attraction.

## 7.25.34 Physical Compatibility

For adults, physical and sexual compatibility may affect:
- adult Sexual Chemistry;
- relationship satisfaction;
- adult intimacy expectations.

Compatibility should remain distinct from immediate attraction.

Two people may strongly desire each other and still be poorly compatible.

## 7.25.35 Emotional Compatibility

Emotional compatibility may involve:
- communication;
- reassurance needs;
- conflict style;
- intimacy preferences;
- independence;
- emotional availability.

This influences long-term relationship viability more than initial attraction.

## 7.25.36 Lifestyle Compatibility

Possible dimensions include:
- career priorities;
- danger tolerance;
- family goals;
- village loyalty;
- travel;
- privacy;
- social needs;
- long-term plans.

Romantic attraction can exist despite major lifestyle incompatibility.

## 7.25.37 Values Compatibility

Shared values may support:
- Respect;
- Trust;
- long-term relationship stability.

Values conflict may become a major obstacle even when attraction is high.

## 7.25.38 Compatibility Is Not One Score

Avoid a universal compatibility rating.

Compatibility should be represented through relevant dimensions and specific conflicts.

A couple may be:
- emotionally compatible;
- sexually incompatible;
- politically incompatible;
- professionally compatible.

## 7.25.39 Chemistry vs Compatibility

Chemistry answers:
- "Do we naturally generate a compelling interpersonal reaction together?"

Compatibility answers:
- "Can our needs, values, expectations, and lifestyles function together over time?"

High chemistry can coexist with poor compatibility.

Low initial chemistry can coexist with strong long-term compatibility.

## 7.25.40 Romantic Availability

Relationship status may affect whether someone is willing to pursue attraction.

It should not automatically determine whether attraction exists.

A partnered person may notice attraction while choosing not to act on it.

## 7.25.41 Relationship Orientation / Structure

Adult characters may differ in expectations around:
- monogamy;
- non-monogamy;
- exclusivity;
- casual dating;
- long-term partnership.

These preferences affect compatibility and willingness, not attraction itself.

## 7.25.42 Attraction and Existing Commitment

Existing commitment may create:
- boundary;
- guilt;
- restraint;
- conflict;
- temptation.

The presence of attraction does not imply betrayal.

## 7.25.43 Jealousy Is Separate

Jealousy may arise from perceived threats to a valued relationship.

It should not be treated as proof of attraction.

A person may feel jealousy because of:
- insecurity;
- abandonment fear;
- status;
- exclusivity expectations.

Ruleset 7.29 will govern this in depth.

## 7.25.44 Attraction Discovery

The engine may determine attraction privately before the character consciously recognizes it.

Recognition may occur through:
- repeated thoughts;
- emotional reactions;
- comparison;
- jealousy;
- physical response in adult contexts;
- conversation with others.

## 7.25.45 Player Character Attraction

The engine may generate plausible:
- first impressions;
- aesthetic reactions;
- chemistry;
- adult physiological attraction or arousal where appropriate.

But it should avoid unnecessarily dictating the player's deeper voluntary romantic commitment.

The player retains control over:
- pursuit;
- confession;
- dating;
- relationship decisions.

## 7.25.46 NPC Attraction

NPC attraction should be determined by:
- orientation;
- preferences;
- personality;
- relationship history;
- chemistry;
- context.

NPCs should not become attracted simply because the player wants them to be.

## 7.25.47 Hidden Attraction State

Exact attraction values should normally remain hidden.

Players infer attraction through:
- attention;
- behavior;
- flirtation;
- body language;
- invitations;
- conversation;
- disclosure.

Those cues can be ambiguous.

## 7.25.48 Attraction Signals

Possible attraction signals may include:
- seeking proximity;
- increased attention;
- remembering details;
- playful interaction;
- compliments;
- voluntary contact;
- flirtation.

None is conclusive alone.

## 7.25.49 Misread Attraction

Characters may mistake:
- friendliness;
- admiration;
- professional respect;
- nervousness;
- hospitality;

for romantic or sexual interest.

Insight can improve interpretation but does not provide certainty.

## 7.25.50 Concealed Attraction

Characters may hide attraction because of:
- fear;
- embarrassment;
- professionalism;
- existing relationship;
- taboo;
- rank;
- danger;
- uncertainty.

Deception or emotional masking may affect what others perceive.

## 7.25.51 Attraction Feedback

Mutual attraction may reinforce:
- attention;
- flirting;
- chemistry;
- Romantic Interest.

But reinforcement should arise from actual interaction.

It should not create automatic runaway escalation.

## 7.25.52 Familiarity Saturation

Repeated exposure alone should not infinitely increase attraction.

Once the person is well known, further attraction change requires meaningful new experiences or changing perception.

## 7.25.53 Novelty

Novelty may temporarily increase interest for some characters.

This may fade with Familiarity.

Novelty is character-dependent and should not be universal.

## 7.25.54 Attraction Update Events

Meaningful updates may result from:
- discovering a valued trait;
- seeing competence;
- shared vulnerability;
- major kindness;
- betrayal;
- values conflict;
- physical presentation change;
- significant flirtation;
- learning major incompatibility.

Minor routine interaction should rarely move attraction substantially.

## 7.25.55 Attraction Memory

Strong attraction-related experiences may become memories.

Examples:
- first crush;
- first confession;
- striking first meeting;
- major romantic rejection;
- discovering unexpected chemistry.

These can shape future behavior without permanently fixing attraction.

## 7.25.56 Attraction Across Time

Attraction may:
- grow;
- stabilize;
- fade;
- disappear;
- return.

Rekindling should require plausible triggers such as:
- reunion;
- changed circumstances;
- renewed interaction;
- personal growth.

## 7.25.57 Attraction Toward Multiple People

Characters may experience attraction toward more than one person at once.

This does not automatically determine:
- behavior;
- commitment;
- exclusivity;
- betrayal.

Those depend on values and relationship agreements.

## 7.25.58 Attraction Does Not Create Entitlement

Attraction gives a character no claim over another person.

It does not justify:
- access;
- persistence after rejection;
- jealousy-driven control;
- boundary violations.

## 7.25.59 Seduction Interface

Ruleset 7.14 owns Seduction as a skill.

Seduction can:
- communicate attraction;
- improve flirtation;
- develop reciprocal tension where plausible;
- read and respond to adult signals.

Seduction cannot manufacture attraction outside the target's plausible range.

## 7.25.60 Romance Interface

Ruleset 7.26 and later sections will govern:
- flirting;
- courtship;
- relationship formation;
- established romance;
- intimacy;
- compatibility conflicts.

Attraction supplies one input.

It is not itself a relationship.

## 7.25.61 Design Standard

An attraction mechanic should be rejected or revised if it:
- uses one universal attraction score;
- treats aesthetic attraction as romance;
- treats adult sexual attraction as consent;
- assumes orientation guarantees attraction;
- lets Seduction override hard attraction constraints;
- treats compatibility as one universal score;
- makes repeated proximity automatically create romance;
- assumes rejection immediately erases attraction;
- treats attraction as entitlement;
- makes player status automatically attractive;
- applies adult sexual mechanics to characters under 18.

## Governing Rule

**Attraction should emerge from orientation, preferences, perception, chemistry, history, and context, with each form of attraction remaining distinct from affection, compatibility, relationship commitment, and consent.**
