# 7.3 — NPC Identity & Baseline Profile

Status: Provisional design section pending explicit approval.

Ruleset 7.3 defines the relatively stable information that answers:

**Who is this person before the current situation starts affecting them?**

The baseline profile should give the simulation enough structure to produce consistent behavior without turning every NPC into an enormous character sheet.

It should distinguish:
- established facts;
- relatively stable personality and identity;
- slowly changing life circumstances;
- context-dependent presentation;
- temporary states owned elsewhere.

## 7.3.1 Baseline Identity Principle

An NPC's baseline profile is the persistent foundation used by later systems.

It should influence:
- what goals they tend to form;
- what they notice or care about;
- how they interpret social behavior;
- what kinds of relationships they prefer;
- what they consider acceptable or unacceptable;
- how they communicate;
- how they respond to stress, authority, affection, conflict, humiliation, risk, or obligation.

It should not directly determine every action.

The same personality can produce different behavior when:
- knowledge differs;
- stakes differ;
- relationships differ;
- emotions differ;
- duties differ;
- available options differ.

## 7.3.2 Identity Categories

A full Major NPC baseline may contain the following categories.

### A. Core Biographical Identity
- name;
- aliases or titles;
- age;
- birth date where relevant;
- gender identity;
- pronouns;
- birthplace;
- current residence;
- nationality / country;
- village affiliation;
- clan;
- family branch where relevant;
- social class or upbringing;
- occupation;
- shinobi status;
- shinobi rank;
- formal office or station;
- education;
- major formative institutions.

These are descriptive facts rather than personality.

### B. Physical & Presentation Identity
Where relevant:
- general appearance;
- height/build;
- notable scars or visible features;
- clothing style;
- grooming;
- posture;
- voice;
- speech pattern;
- habitual expressions;
- mannerisms;
- typical public presentation.

Physical capability itself belongs to the appropriate character/combat systems.

### C. Cultural Identity
- village culture;
- clan culture;
- family traditions;
- regional customs;
- religious or spiritual beliefs where applicable;
- social etiquette;
- attitudes toward hierarchy;
- traditions surrounding family, death, honor, courtship, duty, and community;
- languages or dialects;
- cultural taboos.

Culture should influence rather than completely determine behavior.

Two members of the same clan may interpret clan tradition differently.

### D. Social Role Identity
NPCs often think of themselves through roles such as:
- shinobi;
- medic;
- teacher;
- parent;
- sibling;
- clan heir;
- shopkeeper;
- commander;
- researcher;
- protector;
- student;
- criminal;
- artist;
- caretaker.

Track which roles matter most to their self-concept.

Conflicts between roles can later become important motives.

Example:
A character may see themselves both as:
- a loyal shinobi;
- and a protective older sibling.

A mission order threatening their sibling creates meaningful internal conflict.

## 7.3.3 Personality Structure

Personality should be represented through a limited set of meaningful traits rather than a universal personality score.

Traits may describe tendencies such as:
- patient / impatient;
- cautious / bold;
- warm / reserved;
- trusting / skeptical;
- disciplined / impulsive;
- idealistic / pragmatic;
- forgiving / grudging;
- competitive / cooperative;
- private / open;
- serious / playful;
- ambitious / content;
- conventional / rebellious;
- empathetic / detached;
- orderly / spontaneous.

NPCs should not require a fixed standardized trait list.

Use only traits that materially affect behavior.

Major NPCs may have several nuanced traits.
Minor NPCs may need only two or three.

## 7.3.4 Trait Strength & Conditionality

Traits should have:
- direction;
- approximate strength;
- conditions or exceptions where relevant.

Example:

**Reserved**
- strong with strangers;
- moderate with coworkers;
- weak with close family.

This is preferable to treating "reserved" as universal behavior.

## 7.3.5 Temperament

Temperament describes how quickly and strongly an NPC tends to react.

Relevant tendencies may include:
- emotional intensity;
- emotional recovery speed;
- irritability;
- patience;
- stress tolerance;
- sensitivity to rejection;
- sensitivity to disrespect;
- novelty seeking;
- risk tolerance;
- conflict tolerance.

Temperament is relatively stable but not absolute.

Current emotion is owned by the Emotional State system.

## 7.3.6 Values

Values describe what the NPC believes is important.

Examples:
- family;
- loyalty;
- village;
- clan;
- freedom;
- honor;
- truth;
- compassion;
- strength;
- achievement;
- knowledge;
- tradition;
- justice;
- stability;
- wealth;
- reputation;
- duty;
- independence;
- creativity.

Values should have priorities rather than all being equally important.

A useful Major NPC profile may track:
- 3–6 major values;
- their relative importance;
- known conflicts between them.

Values help explain difficult choices.

## 7.3.7 Moral Boundaries

Track actions an NPC finds:
- acceptable;
- uncomfortable;
- unacceptable;
- absolutely intolerable.

Potential boundaries may involve:
- killing;
- torture;
- betrayal;
- theft;
- harming civilians;
- harming children;
- abandoning comrades;
- lying;
- breaking village law;
- violating clan rules;
- forbidden techniques;
- sacrificing one person to save many;
- manipulating loved ones.

Boundaries should be character-specific.

Crossing a moral boundary may be possible under extraordinary circumstances, but it should require appropriate causal pressure and may have serious psychological or social consequences.

## 7.3.8 Biases, Assumptions & Prejudices

NPCs may possess:
- cultural biases;
- class biases;
- clan rivalries;
- rank assumptions;
- professional stereotypes;
- personal prejudices;
- historical grievances;
- learned fears.

These should arise from plausible background and experience rather than being randomly assigned for drama.

Biases affect interpretation, not objective truth.

They may:
- strengthen;
- weaken;
- be challenged;
- be reinforced;
- disappear over time.

## 7.3.9 Communication Style

Track persistent communication tendencies where relevant:
- direct / indirect;
- formal / casual;
- verbose / concise;
- blunt / tactful;
- emotionally expressive / restrained;
- sarcastic;
- humorous;
- deferential;
- authoritative;
- evasive;
- confrontational;
- diplomatic.

Communication style affects how identical intentions are expressed.

It should not be mistaken for honesty.

A polite character may lie.
A blunt character may be deeply trustworthy.

## 7.3.10 Social Style

Social style may include:
- ease with strangers;
- preference for small groups or crowds;
- desire for attention;
- comfort with authority;
- tendency to initiate contact;
- hospitality;
- competitiveness;
- protectiveness;
- conflict style;
- apology style;
- help-seeking tendency;
- comfort with vulnerability;
- privacy preferences.

These become inputs into relationship and social-interaction systems.

## 7.3.11 Romantic Baseline

Because romance receives dedicated simulation, relevant NPCs may possess baseline romantic traits.

Potential fields include:
- romantic orientation;
- genders they may be romantically attracted to;
- general attraction preferences;
- relationship style preferences;
- interest in casual or committed relationships;
- comfort with public affection;
- courtship style;
- comfort initiating romance;
- jealousy tendency;
- exclusivity expectations;
- importance of family/clan approval;
- long-term partnership goals;
- marriage interest;
- desire for children where relevant;
- privacy boundaries;
- romantic dealbreakers.

These are tendencies and preferences, not guarantees.

An NPC does not need a complete romance profile unless romance becomes relevant.

## 7.3.12 Adult Sexual Baseline

For adult NPCs only, where relevant to the simulation, baseline state may include broad sexual-orientation and intimacy preferences necessary to determine compatibility and boundaries.

This should remain:
- non-graphic;
- character-focused;
- private unless revealed in-world;
- separate from current arousal;
- separate from consent;
- separate from romantic affection.

Adult sexual preferences should not be generated or exposed unless they are actually relevant.

Current adult arousal belongs to relationship/current-state mechanics rather than baseline identity.

## 7.3.13 Attachment & Relationship Tendencies

Without assigning clinical diagnoses, the simulation may track stable relationship tendencies such as:
- slow to trust;
- bonds quickly;
- fiercely loyal once close;
- conflict avoidant;
- highly independent;
- reassurance seeking;
- protective;
- emotionally guarded;
- easily jealous;
- uncomfortable with dependence;
- values frequent contact;
- needs substantial personal space.

These tendencies help differentiate relationships between characters.

They should not mechanically force behavior.

## 7.3.14 Habits & Preferences

Useful persistent preferences may include:
- food;
- hobbies;
- favorite locations;
- daily rituals;
- training habits;
- sleep habits;
- spending tendencies;
- social activities;
- entertainment;
- preferred gifts;
- dislikes;
- pet peeves.

Only store preferences that may matter.

These details can make NPCs feel alive without needing mechanical weight.

## 7.3.15 Competence & Expertise References

Ruleset 7 may record references to socially relevant competencies such as:
- excellent negotiator;
- poor liar;
- experienced teacher;
- formal etiquette training;
- skilled interrogator.

The actual numeric attributes, skills, mastery, and progression remain owned by Ruleset 1.

The baseline profile should reference capability rather than duplicate it.

## 7.3.16 Public Persona vs Private Self

Some NPCs intentionally present themselves differently depending on context.

The profile may distinguish:
- public persona;
- professional persona;
- family persona;
- private self.

Example:
A commander may be:
- stern and unemotional publicly;
- playful with siblings;
- anxious in private.

These are not contradictions.

The simulation should know which contexts activate which presentation.

## 7.3.17 Self-Concept

Major NPCs may track beliefs about themselves.

Examples:
- "I am the responsible one."
- "I am a failure."
- "I am destined to lead."
- "I am not worthy of my clan."
- "People depend on me."
- "I survive because I never trust anyone."

Self-concept can strongly affect:
- pride;
- shame;
- ambition;
- defensiveness;
- susceptibility to praise;
- reactions to failure.

Self-concept is a belief about oneself, not necessarily objective truth.

## 7.3.18 Identity Contradictions

Good NPCs may contain internal contradictions.

Examples:
- values honesty but lies to protect family;
- believes in duty but resents authority;
- acts confident but privately fears inadequacy;
- loves clan tradition but wants personal freedom;
- appears cold but forms intense attachments.

Contradictions should be coherent rather than random.

They often generate strong motives and character development.

## 7.3.19 Stable vs Changeable Identity

Baseline traits should have different change speeds.

### Very Stable
Usually change rarely:
- birthplace;
- family origin;
- major formative history;
- clan lineage;
- established identity facts.

### Slowly Changeable
Can change through meaningful experience:
- values;
- moral boundaries;
- worldview;
- communication style;
- romantic expectations;
- self-concept;
- prejudices;
- relationship tendencies.

### Moderately Changeable
May change through lifestyle or circumstance:
- occupation;
- rank;
- residence;
- public persona;
- habits;
- affiliations.

### Temporary — Not Baseline
Owned elsewhere:
- current anger;
- current fear;
- current suspicion;
- current romantic interest;
- current adult arousal;
- current goals;
- current relationship scores;
- immediate willingness;
- temporary injury or fatigue.

## 7.3.20 Character Development

Baseline personality is persistent but not frozen.

Major experiences may change an NPC over months or years.

Possible causes:
- war;
- loss;
- mentorship;
- parenthood;
- betrayal;
- romance;
- ideological conflict;
- promotion;
- exile;
- long-term friendship;
- repeated success or failure;
- major injury;
- exposure to new cultures.

Changes should be:
- causal;
- gradual unless the event is extreme;
- recorded as character development;
- consistent with prior history.

## 7.3.21 Profile Depth by Simulation Tier

### Major NPC
May receive:
- rich biographical identity;
- several personality traits;
- temperament;
- prioritized values;
- moral boundaries;
- communication/social style;
- self-concept;
- meaningful habits;
- relevant romantic baseline;
- public/private persona;
- internal contradictions.

### Supporting NPC
Usually needs:
- concise biography;
- 3–5 defining traits;
- major values;
- a few boundaries;
- communication style;
- relevant relationship tendencies;
- optional romance data if relevant.

### Persistent Minor NPC
Usually needs:
- name;
- role;
- origin/affiliation;
- 2–3 traits;
- one or two preferences;
- one major value or tendency where useful.

### Background NPC
Generate only what the immediate situation requires.

## 7.3.22 Procedural Generation Order

When generating an NPC, use causal order:

1. Determine location and culture.
2. Determine age/life stage.
3. Determine family/clan/social background.
4. Determine occupation or role.
5. Determine education/training.
6. Determine likely formative experiences.
7. Generate compatible personality traits.
8. Generate values and boundaries.
9. Generate communication/social style.
10. Add only relevant preferences, habits, contradictions, and romance information.

This should produce more coherent people than randomly selecting disconnected traits.

## 7.3.23 Avoiding Random-Trait Soup

A profile should not resemble:

- shy;
- reckless;
- loves tea;
- hates authority;
- romantic;
- perfectionist;
- snake phobia;
- loves gambling;
- secret poet;

unless those traits fit together through actual history or context.

Prefer:
- fewer traits;
- stronger causal connections;
- behaviorally meaningful details.

## 7.3.24 Canon NPC Baselines

Canon characters should begin from established information appropriate to the campaign timeline.

The simulation may expand unknown areas, but should not casually contradict:
- established history;
- personality;
- affiliations;
- relationships;
- known values;
- known capabilities.

Unknown detail may be filled in conservatively.

Canon characters can still change through new experiences once the simulation diverges.

## 7.3.25 Hidden vs Player-Known Profile

The baseline profile is primarily simulation state.

The player only knows:
- what their character has observed;
- what the NPC has revealed;
- what reliable sources have established;
- what the character could reasonably infer.

Exact internal traits, values, boundaries, orientation, private habits, or self-concept should not automatically be exposed.

## 7.3.26 Design Standard

A baseline-profile mechanic should be rejected or revised if it:
- determines behavior with a single personality label;
- duplicates Ruleset 1 attributes or skills;
- treats culture as destiny;
- gives every NPC the same standardized trait grid regardless of relevance;
- generates contradictory personality traits without causal explanation;
- exposes private profile information automatically;
- treats romantic or adult sexual preferences as public knowledge;
- confuses current emotion with stable identity;
- confuses current attraction or adult arousal with baseline orientation;
- makes personality impossible to change through major life experience;
- requires excessive detail for incidental NPCs;
- reduces canon characters to stereotypes rather than their established characterization.

## Governing Rule

**An NPC baseline should contain enough persistent information to make their behavior recognizable and coherent across time, but no more detail than the simulation currently needs.**
