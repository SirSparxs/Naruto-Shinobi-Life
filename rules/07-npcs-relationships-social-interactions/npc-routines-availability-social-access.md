# 7.36 — NPC Routines, Availability & Social Access

Status: Provisional design section pending explicit approval.

Ruleset 7.36 defines where NPCs are, what they are doing, when they are available, who can access them, and how social or institutional barriers affect interaction.

The governing principle is:

**NPCs should exist in time and place rather than waiting for the player, with access determined by routine, circumstance, relationship, privacy, rank, and actual opportunity.**

## 7.36.1 Scope

This section governs:
- daily and weekly routines;
- location and activity;
- work, training, sleep, and family time;
- social availability;
- scheduled vs spontaneous interaction;
- locating NPCs;
- interruptions;
- privacy;
- appointments;
- visitors;
- gatekeepers;
- restricted access;
- relationship-based access;
- rank and status effects on access.

It does not replace:
- travel and movement systems;
- formal organizational scheduling;
- mission assignment systems;
- stealth or infiltration rules;
- legal trespass rules.

Those systems may provide state that Ruleset 7 consumes.

## 7.36.2 NPCs Exist in Time and Place

An NPC should normally have:
- a current location;
- a current activity;
- a rough schedule;
- a reason for being there.

They should not automatically appear wherever the player wants to interact with them.

## 7.36.3 Routine Structure

A routine may include:
- sleep;
- meals;
- work;
- training;
- missions;
- travel;
- family time;
- social time;
- errands;
- personal hobbies;
- rest.

Not every routine needs minute-by-minute tracking.

## 7.36.4 Routine Precision

Use only as much detail as needed.

### Major NPC
May have detailed recurring schedule blocks.

### Supporting NPC
Use broad daily or weekly routine.

### Minor / Background NPC
Generate likely location and activity from role and time.

The simulation should avoid maintaining exact schedules for irrelevant NPCs.

## 7.36.5 Routine Is Probabilistic, Not Mechanical

A routine represents what an NPC usually does.

It can change because of:
- mission;
- injury;
- weather;
- emergency;
- invitation;
- personal choice;
- celebration;
- conflict;
- illness.

Routine should create expectation, not determinism.

## 7.36.6 Scheduled Obligations

Some activities have stronger priority:
- work shift;
- mission briefing;
- academy class;
- patrol;
- medical duty;
- formal meeting.

Leaving them may require:
- permission;
- urgency;
- personal motive.

## 7.36.7 Flexible Time

Other activities may be easier to interrupt:
- shopping;
- leisure;
- casual training;
- social visit.

Availability depends on character and context.

## 7.36.8 Sleep

NPCs should normally sleep according to:
- age;
- profession;
- shift;
- current fatigue;
- mission demands.

Waking someone at an unreasonable hour may produce:
- irritation;
- concern;
- suspicion;
- urgency recognition.

## 7.36.9 Work

During work, an NPC may be:
- fully unavailable;
- briefly available;
- available only for work-related topics;
- able to schedule later contact.

Professional context should affect conversation.

## 7.36.10 Training

Training may occupy:
- solo practice;
- team exercise;
- instructor session;
- sparring.

Interruptions may be acceptable or disruptive depending on urgency.

## 7.36.11 Family Time

NPCs may deliberately prioritize:
- children;
- partner;
- relatives;
- household duties.

Strong work or player relevance should not automatically override family commitments.

## 7.36.12 Social Time

NPCs may spend free time with:
- friends;
- partners;
- teammates;
- clubs;
- community groups.

This can create off-screen relationship opportunities.

## 7.36.13 Personal Time

NPCs may intentionally want time alone.

Being alone does not automatically mean they are available.

## 7.36.14 Availability Defined

Availability asks:

**Can this NPC reasonably engage in the requested interaction right now?**

Possible states:
- fully available;
- briefly available;
- distracted;
- busy but interruptible;
- unavailable;
- unreachable.

## 7.36.15 Availability Depends on Interaction Type

An NPC may have time for:
- quick question;

but not:
- long emotional conversation.

Availability should match the request's expected duration and seriousness.

## 7.36.16 Urgency

Urgent situations may override normal availability.

Examples:
- emergency warning;
- medical crisis;
- immediate mission threat.

NPCs still decide whether they believe the urgency.

## 7.36.17 Interruptions

Interrupting an NPC may alter context.

Possible reactions:
- welcome;
- annoyance;
- concern;
- professional redirection;
- refusal.

Repeated unnecessary interruptions can become socially meaningful.

## 7.36.18 Timing Matters

The same request may succeed differently depending on timing.

Examples:
- asking commander during crisis;
- asking after mission;
- asking in private.

Timing should influence context rather than become a rigid bonus table.

## 7.36.19 Appointment

Some NPCs may prefer or require scheduled interaction.

Examples:
- leader;
- healer;
- administrator;
- wealthy patron;
- clan elder.

An appointment increases access but does not guarantee cooperation.

## 7.36.20 Spontaneous Access

Close friends, family, or teammates may permit spontaneous contact more easily.

This is a relationship benefit.

It should not imply unlimited access.

## 7.36.21 Relationship-Based Access

High:
- Trust;
- Familiarity;
- Affection;
- professional connection;

may increase access to:
- private time;
- home;
- informal meetings.

Access is contextual and revocable.

## 7.36.22 Rank and Status

High rank or status may affect:
- ease of getting meetings;
- priority;
- gatekeeper behavior.

Status should not automatically erase privacy or institutional restrictions.

## 7.36.23 Gatekeepers

Access may be controlled by:
- assistant;
- receptionist;
- guard;
- clan member;
- subordinate;
- secretary;
- security officer.

Gatekeepers may:
- schedule;
- filter;
- deny;
- verify identity;
- assess urgency.

## 7.36.24 Bypassing Gatekeepers

Trying to bypass access controls may cause:
- suspicion;
- reputation damage;
- institutional consequences.

Stealth or infiltration belongs to other systems.

## 7.36.25 Restricted Locations

Some locations may require:
- membership;
- clearance;
- invitation;
- escort;
- rank.

Ruleset 7 handles the social access consequences.
Formal security mechanics belong elsewhere.

## 7.36.26 Private Spaces

Private spaces may include:
- home;
- bedroom;
- office;
- clan area;
- hospital room;
- training room.

Entering or requesting access may have different social meaning.

## 7.36.27 Home Access

Being invited to someone's home may indicate:
- Trust;
- Familiarity;
- closeness;
- cultural hospitality.

It should not be granted automatically by friendship level.

## 7.36.28 Workplace Access

A workplace may allow:
- public visitors;
- only staff;
- appointment-only access;
- secure areas.

Relationship with one employee should not automatically grant access to all areas.

## 7.36.29 Hospital Access

Medical settings may restrict access due to:
- patient privacy;
- treatment;
- infection control;
- security.

Close relationships may matter, but medical context still applies.

## 7.36.30 Clan Access

Clan compounds or private areas may use:
- family membership;
- invitation;
- status;
- trust.

One clan friend should not automatically grant unrestricted access.

## 7.36.31 Leadership Access

Important leaders may have limited availability because of:
- meetings;
- security;
- administration;
- emergencies.

High relationship may improve access without making them constantly available.

## 7.36.32 Mission Access

An NPC currently:
- deployed;
- undercover;
- missing;
- traveling;

may be unreachable.

The player should not simply summon them into a scene.

## 7.36.33 Communication Access

If in-person access fails, characters may use:
- messenger;
- letter;
- intermediary;
- official communication channel.

The message still requires:
- delivery;
- opportunity;
- recipient access.

## 7.36.34 Delayed Response

NPCs do not need to respond immediately.

They may:
- answer later;
- forget;
- deprioritize;
- intentionally delay.

Delay should have a reason.

## 7.36.35 Ignored Messages

An NPC may ignore contact because of:
- resentment;
- avoidance;
- distrust;
- overload;
- disinterest.

Repeated ignored contact should alter expectations.

## 7.36.36 Locating an NPC

Finding someone may involve:
- knowing their routine;
- asking contacts;
- checking workplace;
- sending message;
- Gather Information.

The difficulty depends on:
- secrecy;
- mobility;
- network access;
- current activity.

## 7.36.37 Known Routine

Close contacts may know:
- usual workplace;
- favorite location;
- training time;
- home.

This can make locating them easier.

## 7.36.38 Hidden Routine

Some NPCs may deliberately conceal:
- meetings;
- safehouse;
- romantic visits;
- criminal activity.

Knowledge and Deception systems govern discovery.

## 7.36.39 Random Variation

Minor routine variation can keep the world natural.

Examples:
- stops at market;
- visits friend;
- changes training location.

Variation should remain plausible.

## 7.36.40 Routine and Personality

Personality may affect schedule:
- disciplined NPC keeps stable routine;
- spontaneous NPC varies more;
- workaholic has little free time;
- social NPC seeks gatherings.

## 7.36.41 Routine and Goals

Goals can reshape availability.

A character preparing for:
- exam;
- promotion;
- mission;
- wedding;

may temporarily devote more time to relevant tasks.

## 7.36.42 Routine and Relationship Changes

A new:
- partner;
- child;
- teammate;
- job;

may alter routine and create new access patterns.

## 7.36.43 Routine and Injury

Ruleset 4 injury may cause:
- hospital stay;
- home recovery;
- reduced work;
- caregiver contact.

Routine updates accordingly.

## 7.36.44 Routine and Emotional State

Grief, fear, depression-like withdrawal, excitement, or anger may temporarily alter:
- social activity;
- work attendance;
- preferred company.

Ruleset 7.8 owns emotion.

## 7.36.45 Routine and Rank

Higher rank may change:
- meeting load;
- responsibilities;
- security;
- free time.

Promotion should therefore change social access organically.

## 7.36.46 Routine and Age

Children, students, adults, elders may have different:
- school;
- work;
- sleep;
- supervision;
- family;

patterns.

## 7.36.47 Routine and Culture

Villages and clans may differ in:
- work hours;
- meal customs;
- social gatherings;
- privacy expectations.

Routine should support worldbuilding.

## 7.36.48 Scheduled Social Events

Events such as:
- festival;
- clan dinner;
- team gathering;
- memorial;
- celebration;

can temporarily alter many routines and create high social opportunity.

## 7.36.49 Chance Encounters

Characters may encounter NPCs unexpectedly when:
- routes overlap;
- shared locations;
- social hubs.

Chance encounters should use actual routines rather than arbitrary convenience.

## 7.36.50 Encounter Probability

A chance meeting is more plausible when:
- both frequent same place;
- same time window;
- same social network.

It should be rare across unrelated routines.

## 7.36.51 Waiting

A character may wait for someone at:
- workplace;
- home;
- meeting place.

Whether this is normal or unsettling depends on:
- relationship;
- context;
- duration;
- privacy.

## 7.36.52 Repeated Uninvited Presence

Repeatedly showing up without invitation may cause:
- discomfort;
- suspicion;
- avoidance;
- boundary setting.

Social access cannot be brute-forced through persistence.

## 7.36.53 Access Escalation

Access may gradually increase through:
- Trust;
- relationship depth;
- institutional role;
- reputation;
- invitation.

Example:
- public meeting -> private office -> home.

This should be causal, not a fixed level ladder.

## 7.36.54 Access Reduction

Access may decrease through:
- betrayal;
- conflict;
- suspicion;
- rank change;
- security concern.

Previous access is not permanent.

## 7.36.55 Emergency Access

In emergencies, normally restricted access may temporarily open.

Example:
- close family admitted to injured shinobi.

Institutional policy still matters.

## 7.36.56 Social Cost of Access

Obtaining access may require:
- favor;
- introduction;
- appointment;
- waiting;
- reputation.

This creates meaningful value for social networks.

## 7.36.57 Competing Demands

NPCs may have multiple people seeking them.

They prioritize based on:
- urgency;
- relationship;
- duty;
- status;
- personal goals.

The player should not always be prioritized.

## 7.36.58 Player Relevance Is Not Priority

NPCs should not cancel important:
- work;
- family;
- missions;

simply because the player wants a conversation.

## 7.36.59 NPC-Initiated Scheduling

NPCs may say:
- "Not now. Meet me after training."
- "Come by tomorrow."
- "Send me a message."

This creates believable future access.

## 7.36.60 Missed Meetings

A character may:
- arrive late;
- forget;
- be delayed;
- intentionally skip.

This can affect:
- Trust;
- reliability;
- interpretation.

## 7.36.61 Punctuality Expectations

Different characters may value punctuality differently.

Repeated lateness may become a relationship pattern.

## 7.36.62 Waiting Tolerance

How long someone is willing to wait depends on:
- relationship;
- urgency;
- personality;
- cultural norm.

## 7.36.63 Social Access and Secrets

Access to a person does not imply access to:
- their private files;
- conversations;
- secrets;
- restricted areas.

Ruleset 7.6 governs information access.

## 7.36.64 Availability and Consent

An NPC being physically present or alone does not mean they are willing to engage in:
- emotional conversation;
- romantic interaction;
- adult intimacy.

Availability and willingness are separate.

## 7.36.65 Off-Screen Routine Simulation

Routines should continue off-screen enough to support:
- social opportunity;
- rumor spread;
- relationships;
- work.

No need for minute-by-minute simulation unless important.

## 7.36.66 Routine Compression

For broad time periods, use:
- typical schedule;
- exceptions;
- major events.

Example:
- "Karn spends most weekdays at the training grounds and evenings with family."

## 7.36.67 Routine Record

For important NPCs, the engine may store:

**Home**
**Primary work / duty location**
**Typical sleep period**
**Recurring training**
**Regular social contacts**
**Flexible free-time blocks**
**Restricted / secret activities**
**Current exceptions**

## 7.36.68 Access Record

Where relevant, store:
- public access;
- appointment required;
- gatekeeper;
- private access;
- emergency access;
- relationship-specific privileges;
- current restrictions.

## 7.36.69 Player-Facing Information

The player learns routines through:
- observation;
- prior meetings;
- asking;
- schedules;
- social networks.

Exact hidden schedules should not be exposed without knowledge.

## 7.36.70 Routine Uncertainty

The player may know:
- "Rena usually works mornings."

That does not guarantee she is there today.

Routine knowledge should preserve uncertainty.

## 7.36.71 Design Standard

A routine/access mechanic should be rejected or revised if it:
- makes NPCs always available;
- requires minute-level scheduling for everyone;
- makes routine perfectly deterministic;
- lets relationships grant unlimited access;
- ignores privacy;
- lets the player summon NPCs from missions;
- treats physical presence as social willingness;
- makes gatekeepers irrelevant;
- makes one prior invitation permanent access;
- prioritizes the player over all NPC obligations.

## Governing Rule

**NPC routines and access should anchor social interaction in believable time, place, obligation, privacy, and relationship, ensuring that meeting someone is itself part of the simulation rather than a guaranteed menu action.**
