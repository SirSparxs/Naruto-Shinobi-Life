# 7.31 — Breakups, Separation & Romantic Reconciliation

Status: Provisional design section pending explicit approval.

Ruleset 7.31 defines how romantic relationships end, how separation affects both people and their networks, how ex-partner relationships persist, and how reconciliation may or may not occur.

The governing principle is:

**Ending a relationship changes the relationship structure, not the shared history, emotions, memories, obligations, or social consequences that already exist.**

## 7.31.1 Scope

This section governs:
- breakup decisions;
- mutual and one-sided separation;
- temporary separation;
- post-breakup emotional aftermath;
- ex-partner relationships;
- shared-home and shared-network complications;
- contact after breakup;
- on-again/off-again patterns;
- romantic reconciliation.

It does not replace:
- general betrayal mechanics;
- institutional divorce law;
- property law;
- custody systems;
- general consent or boundary rules.

## 7.31.2 Breakup Is a Decision, Not a Meter Threshold

There should be no universal rule such as:
- Satisfaction below 30 -> breakup.

A breakup becomes plausible when one or both partners decide that continuing the relationship is no longer acceptable or desirable.

Relevant inputs may include:
- low Satisfaction;
- incompatibility;
- repeated conflict;
- betrayal;
- changing goals;
- loss of Romantic Interest;
- distance;
- resentment;
- safety;
- external pressure.

The final decision remains character-specific.

## 7.31.3 Love Does Not Prevent Breakup

A character may still possess:
- Affection;
- Attachment;
- Romantic Interest;
- sexual attraction;
- Loyalty;

and nevertheless choose to leave.

Examples:
- incompatibility;
- broken Trust;
- incompatible future goals;
- repeated harm;
- exhaustion.

This is essential for believable relationships.

## 7.31.4 Loss of Love Is Not Required

A relationship may end because:
- it no longer works;
- circumstances changed;
- goals diverged;
- one partner no longer wants commitment.

Not every breakup should be framed as:
- "I stopped loving you."

## 7.31.5 Mutual Breakup

A mutual breakup occurs when both partners agree that the relationship should end.

They may still differ in:
- timing;
- emotional readiness;
- interpretation;
- desired post-breakup contact.

Mutual does not mean painless.

## 7.31.6 One-Sided Breakup

One partner may end the relationship while the other wants to continue.

The relationship ends because continued romantic participation requires both people.

The rejecting partner does not need the other's permission to leave.

## 7.31.7 Breakup Initiation

A character may initiate because of:
- dissatisfaction;
- values conflict;
- betrayal;
- incompatibility;
- changing attraction;
- future goals;
- emotional exhaustion;
- external obligations.

The engine should preserve the actual reasons rather than using a generic breakup state.

## 7.31.8 Breakup Communication

A breakup may be communicated:
- directly;
- gently;
- abruptly;
- angrily;
- by letter or message;
- through disappearance;
- through formal social channels where culturally relevant.

Communication style affects emotional aftermath.

## 7.31.9 Ghosting / Disappearance

Abruptly cutting contact without explanation may create:
- confusion;
- abandonment feelings;
- anger;
- uncertainty;
- prolonged hope.

In dangerous worlds, disappearance may also be misinterpreted as:
- death;
- capture;
- mission secrecy.

## 7.31.10 Temporary Separation

Partners may choose a temporary separation to:
- reduce conflict;
- think;
- recover;
- handle distance;
- reassess expectations.

A separation should specify, when known:
- contact expectations;
- exclusivity;
- duration or review point;
- living arrangements.

Ambiguity can create later conflict.

## 7.31.11 Breakup vs Separation

A breakup means the romantic partnership has ended.

A separation may mean:
- relationship still exists;
- commitment is paused or uncertain;
- partners are living apart.

The engine should preserve the distinction.

## 7.31.12 Emotional Aftermath

Possible post-breakup emotions include:
- grief;
- relief;
- anger;
- longing;
- shame;
- guilt;
- resentment;
- hope;
- numbness;
- jealousy.

Multiple states may coexist.

## 7.31.13 Grief

A breakup may produce genuine grief because the character loses:
- partner;
- routines;
- future plans;
- identity;
- shared home;
- social network.

Grief intensity depends on Attachment and meaning, not only relationship duration.

## 7.31.14 Relief

Leaving a stressful relationship may create strong relief even while Affection remains.

Relief should not be treated as proof the relationship was meaningless.

## 7.31.15 Post-Breakup Attachment

Attachment may remain high after separation.

This can cause:
- longing;
- checking on ex;
- difficulty adjusting;
- nostalgia.

Attachment should generally decay or transform through time and new experience rather than disappear instantly.

## 7.31.16 Romantic Interest After Breakup

Romantic Interest may:
- persist;
- fade;
- briefly intensify;
- return later.

Persistent interest does not recreate the relationship automatically.

## 7.31.17 Sexual Attraction After Breakup

For adults, sexual attraction or chemistry may persist after breakup.

This does not imply:
- reconciliation;
- renewed commitment;
- automatic intimacy.

## 7.31.18 Post-Breakup Contact

Former partners may choose:
- no contact;
- limited contact;
- friendship;
- professional-only interaction;
- continued close contact.

This depends on:
- history;
- emotional state;
- shared obligations;
- social context.

## 7.31.19 No-Contact Periods

Temporary or long-term no-contact choices may help:
- emotional recovery;
- reduce conflict;
- establish boundaries.

No-contact should be treated as an actual boundary state when communicated.

## 7.31.20 Friendship After Romance

Former partners may become friends.

This may require:
- reduced Romantic Interest;
- repaired Trust;
- acceptance of changed expectations;
- enough time.

Friendship should not be assumed automatically.

## 7.31.21 Former Partners Who Remain Hostile

A breakup may leave:
- Resentment;
- Hostility;
- rivalry;
- avoidance.

Shared history may intensify later conflict.

## 7.31.22 Shared Social Networks

Breakups may affect:
- mutual friends;
- squads;
- clans;
- workplaces;
- family ties.

Network members may:
- remain neutral;
- take sides;
- lose contact;
- maintain both relationships.

Ruleset 7.22 governs the network layer.

## 7.31.23 Friends Should Not Automatically Choose Sides

Shared friends may maintain relationships with both ex-partners.

Taking sides should depend on:
- loyalty;
- perceived wrongdoing;
- closeness;
- group dynamics.

## 7.31.24 Group Fallout

A breakup within:
- squad;
- workplace;
- clan;
- organization;

may create:
- awkwardness;
- gossip;
- cohesion problems;
- scheduling issues.

It should not automatically make professional cooperation impossible.

## 7.31.25 Shared Home

Cohabiting partners may need to resolve:
- who leaves;
- timing;
- possessions;
- privacy;
- temporary arrangements.

Formal ownership or property law belongs elsewhere.

Ruleset 7 owns the social consequences.

## 7.31.26 Shared Possessions

Items may carry:
- practical value;
- sentimental value;
- symbolic meaning.

Disputes over possessions may become emotionally significant even when monetary value is low.

## 7.31.27 Shared Responsibilities

Former partners may continue sharing:
- caregiving;
- mission obligations;
- financial commitments;
- dependents;
- pets or equivalent responsibilities.

The breakup does not erase unrelated obligations.

## 7.31.28 Children

Where applicable, parents may remain connected through:
- caregiving;
- co-parenting;
- family networks.

Detailed custody and legal systems belong elsewhere.

The romantic relationship may end while the parental relationship continues.

## 7.31.29 Family After Breakup

A former partner may have meaningful relationships with the ex's:
- parents;
- siblings;
- clan members.

Those relationships do not automatically disappear.

## 7.31.30 Public Breakup

A publicly known relationship may produce:
- rumors;
- speculation;
- reputation effects;
- social pressure.

The public may misunderstand why the relationship ended.

## 7.31.31 Private Breakup

A private or secret breakup may remain known only to:
- participants;
- close confidants.

Outsiders may continue assuming the couple is together.

## 7.31.32 Breakup Narrative

Each former partner may interpret the breakup differently.

Example:
- A: "We wanted different futures."
- B: "They chose their career over me."

Both interpretations may coexist.

## 7.31.33 Blame

Characters may assign blame to:
- self;
- ex-partner;
- third party;
- circumstances;
- institution.

Blame affects resentment, guilt, and reconciliation.

## 7.31.34 Breakup Memory

Important breakup details may become high-salience memories:
- words used;
- location;
- perceived betrayal;
- abandonment;
- kindness;
- humiliation.

These memories may influence later relationships.

## 7.31.35 Post-Breakup Rumors

Other people may spread:
- true explanations;
- partial stories;
- false blame;
- scandal.

Ruleset 7.21 governs propagation.

## 7.31.36 Rebound Relationships

A character may enter a new relationship soon after breakup.

Possible motives:
- genuine interest;
- distraction;
- validation;
- loneliness;
- revenge.

"Rebound" should describe context, not automatically invalidate the new relationship.

## 7.31.37 Jealousy After Breakup

An ex may feel jealous when the former partner dates someone else.

The emotion is plausible.

The breakup means they no longer possess relationship claims unless specific ongoing agreements exist.

## 7.31.38 Comparing New Partners

Characters may compare a new partner to an ex.

This may affect:
- expectations;
- insecurity;
- attraction;
- gratitude;
- resentment.

It should not happen universally.

## 7.31.39 On-Again / Off-Again Relationships

Some couples may repeatedly:
- separate;
- reconcile;
- repeat unresolved conflict.

The system should track the pattern rather than treating each reconciliation as a clean reset.

## 7.31.40 Reconciliation Defined

Reconciliation is the deliberate restoration of a romantic relationship after separation or breakup.

It requires renewed mutual willingness.

Lingering love alone is insufficient.

## 7.31.41 Reconciliation Preconditions

Plausible reconciliation may require:
- continued or renewed Romantic Interest;
- willingness from both people;
- enough Trust;
- changed circumstances;
- addressed breakup causes.

The exact requirements depend on why the relationship ended.

## 7.31.42 Cause Must Matter

If a relationship ended because:
- one wanted children and the other did not;

reconciliation without changed goals leaves the core incompatibility intact.

If it ended because of:
- temporary deployment;

reconciliation may be much easier.

The original cause should remain central.

## 7.31.43 Apology Is Not Reconciliation

An apology may repair:
- Respect;
- Trust;
- Resentment.

It does not require the former partner to resume romance.

Forgiveness and reconciliation remain separate.

## 7.31.44 Trust Repair

If breakup involved betrayal, reconciliation may require:
- acknowledgment;
- truth;
- restitution;
- changed behavior;
- time;
- transparency.

Trust should not reset when the couple gets back together.

## 7.31.45 Changed Behavior

Promises matter less than demonstrated change.

Repeated past patterns should make reassurance less effective until behavior supports it.

## 7.31.46 Rekindling

Rekindling may occur after:
- time apart;
- personal growth;
- changed circumstances;
- renewed contact;
- rediscovered attraction.

It should be possible without being guaranteed.

## 7.31.47 Nostalgia

Nostalgia may increase desire to reconnect.

But nostalgia may idealize the past and underweight:
- conflict;
- incompatibility;
- reasons for breakup.

## 7.31.48 Familiarity Advantage

Former partners possess:
- deep Familiarity;
- shared history;
- known compatibility;
- established chemistry.

This may make reconnection easier.

It may also reactivate old conflict quickly.

## 7.31.49 Reconciliation Conversation

A reconciliation discussion may clarify:
- what changed;
- why try again;
- what expectations differ now;
- whether exclusivity resumes;
- whether old boundaries change.

The conversation does not itself guarantee success.

## 7.31.50 Reconciliation Trial Period

Some former partners may cautiously resume:
- dating;
- limited contact;
- rebuilding Trust;

before fully restoring the relationship.

This can be modeled as renewed courtship.

## 7.31.51 Reconciliation Is a New Relationship State

When former partners reconcile, preserve:
- old history;
- prior breakup;
- repaired and unrepaired issues.

The relationship is not reset to its earlier state.

## 7.31.52 Multiple Breakups

Repeated breakups may affect:
- Trust;
- security;
- commitment credibility;
- social-network reactions.

Friends may become less willing to invest in each reconciliation.

## 7.31.53 External Pressure to Reconcile

Family, clan, friends, or institutions may pressure ex-partners to reunite.

Pressure can affect context.

It cannot create mutual willingness.

## 7.31.54 Political or Arranged Reconciliation

Some relationships may have:
- clan;
- political;
- family;

incentives to reconcile.

These incentives affect decisions without replacing personal agency.

## 7.31.55 Refusing Reconciliation

A former partner may refuse despite:
- lingering love;
- apology;
- changed behavior.

Possible reasons:
- Trust never recovered;
- life moved on;
- new relationship;
- incompatibility remains;
- emotional cost too high.

This is a valid final outcome.

## 7.31.56 Reconciliation After New Relationship

If an ex is now partnered with someone else, renewed attraction does not automatically reopen the old relationship.

Current commitments and agreements matter.

## 7.31.57 Post-Breakup Growth

Breakups may influence:
- self-knowledge;
- future preferences;
- boundaries;
- expectations;
- attachment patterns.

The simulation may preserve meaningful lessons without forcing every breakup to become "character growth."

## 7.31.58 Former Relationship Reputation

Others may remember the couple as:
- ideal;
- volatile;
- tragic;
- politically significant.

Public narrative may persist after the relationship ends.

## 7.31.59 Death Instead of Breakup

If a relationship ends through death, grief and bereavement differ from voluntary separation.

Ruleset 7.8 and relevant mortality systems govern the emotional state.

The romantic relationship becomes historical rather than "ex-partner" status.

## 7.31.60 NPC-Initiated Breakups

NPCs may independently decide to:
- separate;
- end a relationship;
- request reconciliation.

They should not require player permission to exercise their own agency.

## 7.31.61 NPC-to-NPC Breakups

NPC relationships may end off-screen.

Important consequences may reach the player through:
- changed social networks;
- rumors;
- household changes;
- group tension.

## 7.31.62 Player Agency

NPCs may end relationships with the player character.

The player controls their own response, not whether the NPC remains romantically involved.

Likewise, the player may end a relationship without needing a successful social roll.

## 7.31.63 Breakup Outcome Record

For important relationships, store:
- breakup/separation date;
- initiator;
- stated reasons;
- underlying major causes;
- public/private status;
- post-breakup contact state;
- unresolved obligations;
- reconciliation status.

## 7.31.64 Post-Breakup Audit

For an important breakup, the engine should be able to answer:

1. Why did the relationship end?
2. Did both people agree with that interpretation?
3. What feelings remain?
4. What obligations remain?
5. What shared networks remain?
6. What contact is currently expected?
7. What unresolved issues persist?
8. What would have to change for reconciliation to become plausible?

## 7.31.65 Design Standard

A breakup mechanic should be rejected or revised if it:
- automatically ends love or attraction;
- requires mutual agreement to leave a relationship;
- resets relationship history;
- assumes all exes become enemies;
- assumes all exes become friends;
- makes reconciliation automatic after apology;
- ignores the original breakup cause;
- treats getting back together as a clean reset;
- forces friends to choose sides;
- makes breakup impossible because Attachment is high.

## Governing Rule

**Breakups should alter the structure of a relationship while preserving its history, emotional residue, obligations, and social consequences, with reconciliation possible only through renewed mutual choice and meaningful change in the conditions that caused the separation.**
