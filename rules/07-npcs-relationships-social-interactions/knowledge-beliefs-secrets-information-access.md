# 7.6 — Knowledge, Beliefs, Secrets & Information Access

Status: Provisional design section pending explicit approval.

Ruleset 7.6 defines what an NPC knows, what they believe, how certain they are, where information came from, what remains hidden from them, and how their beliefs change.

The governing principle is:

**NPCs act on accessible information and personal belief, not on the simulation's objective truth.**

## 7.6.1 Objective Truth vs NPC Belief

The simulation should distinguish:

### Objective State
What is actually true in the world.

### NPC Belief
What a specific NPC currently believes is true.

These may differ.

An NPC may:
- know the truth;
- know only part of the truth;
- believe something false;
- suspect something;
- be uncertain;
- have outdated information;
- have intentionally manipulated information.

NPC behavior must use NPC belief rather than omniscient world-state knowledge.

## 7.6.2 Knowledge State Categories

Relevant information may be represented as:

- **Known:** NPC possesses strong evidence or direct knowledge.
- **Believed:** NPC accepts the information as true but lacks certainty.
- **Suspected:** NPC considers it plausible.
- **Uncertain:** NPC recognizes multiple possibilities.
- **Disbelieved:** NPC has heard it but considers it false.
- **Unknown:** NPC has no meaningful awareness of it.
- **Misbelieved:** NPC confidently believes an incorrect alternative.

These labels are conceptual and need not always be exposed numerically.

## 7.6.3 Confidence

Beliefs may have different confidence levels.

Possible qualitative states:
- tentative;
- plausible;
- confident;
- highly confident;
- effectively certain.

Confidence affects:
- willingness to act;
- willingness to accuse;
- willingness to share information;
- openness to contradictory evidence;
- urgency.

Confidence is separate from truth.

A false belief may be held with extreme confidence.

## 7.6.4 Information Sources

Important beliefs should be traceable to plausible sources.

Sources include:
- direct observation;
- personal experience;
- trusted testimony;
- rumor;
- official reports;
- intercepted communication;
- interrogation;
- family;
- clan networks;
- village intelligence;
- media/public notice;
- forged evidence;
- inference;
- sensory techniques;
- genjutsu;
- written records.

Source quality influences confidence.

## 7.6.5 Direct Observation

Direct observation may still be incomplete or misinterpreted.

An NPC may:
- see someone leaving a building;
- correctly know they were there;
- incorrectly infer why.

Observation grants access to evidence, not automatic perfect interpretation.

## 7.6.6 Testimony

NPCs should evaluate testimony using:
- trust in speaker;
- speaker reputation;
- relationship;
- consistency;
- motive to lie;
- corroboration;
- expertise;
- context.

A trusted friend may be believed with little evidence.
A known liar may require proof.

## 7.6.7 Expertise

Knowledge interpretation may depend on expertise.

Examples:
- a medic notices symptoms a civilian misses;
- an ANBU operative recognizes surveillance behavior;
- a seal specialist recognizes fuinjutsu architecture;
- a merchant detects unusual pricing manipulation.

Expertise does not create information that was never available.

It improves interpretation of available evidence.

## 7.6.8 Inference

NPCs may infer unknown facts from evidence.

Inference can be:
- correct;
- partly correct;
- wrong;
- overconfident.

Ruleset 2 may resolve uncertain deductions where needed.

## 7.6.9 Assumptions

NPCs may fill gaps using assumptions based on:
- experience;
- prejudice;
- culture;
- habit;
- reputation;
- pattern recognition.

Assumptions can be useful or misleading.

## 7.6.10 Information Age

Knowledge can become outdated.

Track recency where relevant.

Examples:
- old mission orders;
- former relationship status;
- previous address;
- outdated enemy strength;
- old political allegiance.

NPCs should not automatically update unless information reaches them.

## 7.6.11 Information Provenance

For important facts, the simulation should know:

- what the NPC believes;
- why they believe it;
- who or what supplied it;
- how confident they are;
- when they learned it.

This makes later contradictions and rumor tracing possible.

## 7.6.12 Secret Classification

Secrets may be categorized by scope.

Examples:
- personal secret;
- family secret;
- clan secret;
- team secret;
- village secret;
- classified intelligence;
- operational secret;
- criminal secret;
- romantic secret;
- medical secret.

The category influences:
- who is expected to know;
- consequences of disclosure;
- how aggressively it is protected.

## 7.6.13 Secret Knowledge Lists

Important secrets should track:
- who definitely knows;
- who suspects;
- who has partial information;
- who has false information;
- who is authorized to know.

This prevents accidental omniscience.

## 7.6.14 Compartmentalization

Sensitive information should often be compartmentalized.

An NPC may know:
- mission objective;
but not:
- full strategic purpose.

Or know:
- a prisoner exists;
but not:
- where they are held.

Need-to-know rules should matter in shinobi institutions.

## 7.6.15 Access Rights

Information access may depend on:
- rank;
- role;
- clan membership;
- security clearance;
- mission assignment;
- family relationship;
- friendship;
- romance;
- professional privilege;
- criminal network access.

Access creates opportunity to know.
It does not guarantee the NPC actually obtained the information.

## 7.6.16 Information Barriers

Barriers may include:
- secrecy;
- distance;
- language;
- encryption;
- seals;
- bureaucracy;
- social exclusion;
- mistrust;
- physical security;
- misinformation;
- compartmentalization.

## 7.6.17 Deliberate Concealment

NPCs may intentionally hide information through:
- omission;
- lying;
- misdirection;
- coded language;
- secrecy agreements;
- destroyed records;
- false records;
- compartmentalization.

Concealment belongs to social behavior, while the underlying knowledge state remains tracked here.

## 7.6.18 Partial Knowledge

Information should not always be all-or-nothing.

Example:
An NPC may know:
- a meeting happened;
- approximately when;
- one attendee;

but not:
- the purpose;
- all participants;
- the outcome.

Partial knowledge is especially important for investigation gameplay.

## 7.6.19 Conflicting Information

NPCs may receive contradictory reports.

They may:
- choose one;
- remain uncertain;
- seek corroboration;
- split confidence;
- reject both.

Decision depends on:
- source trust;
- evidence quality;
- existing bias;
- prior beliefs;
- urgency.

## 7.6.20 Confirmation Bias

NPCs may favor evidence supporting what they already believe.

This should be stronger for:
- emotionally invested beliefs;
- ideological beliefs;
- high-pride characters;
- existing prejudices.

Contradictory evidence can still change them over time.

## 7.6.21 Belief Updating

Beliefs may change when new evidence appears.

Useful conceptual process:

1. New information arrives.
2. NPC evaluates source.
3. Compare against existing belief.
4. Assess contradiction.
5. Update confidence.
6. Accept, reject, or remain uncertain.

Major belief changes should usually require proportionate evidence.

## 7.6.22 Sudden Revelation

A highly credible direct revelation may change belief immediately.

Examples:
- personally witnessing betrayal;
- seeing verified documents;
- confession by a trusted source;
- unmistakable sensory evidence.

But the NPC may still emotionally resist accepting it.

## 7.6.23 Denial

An NPC may temporarily reject strong evidence because accepting it threatens:
- identity;
- relationship;
- worldview;
- reputation;
- emotional stability.

Denial should not become permanent immunity to evidence.

## 7.6.24 Forgetting

Knowledge may degrade over time.

Minor details may:
- fade;
- become uncertain;
- merge with other memories;
- lose source information.

Major emotionally significant or repeatedly reinforced facts persist longer.

Detailed memory mechanics are owned by Ruleset 7.7, but 7.6 consumes memory quality when evaluating knowledge.

## 7.6.25 Rumor

Rumor is information transmitted without full verification.

Rumors may change through:
- simplification;
- exaggeration;
- omission;
- reinterpretation;
- intentional alteration.

Detailed propagation belongs later to the Rumor system.

This section tracks what a particular NPC currently believes after receiving one.

## 7.6.26 Lies and Misinformation

A successful lie does not automatically create permanent belief.

It may create:
- temporary acceptance;
- uncertainty;
- lowered suspicion;
- a false belief;
- a misleading interpretation.

Later evidence can challenge it.

## 7.6.27 Disinformation Campaigns

Organized misinformation may:
- create competing narratives;
- overwhelm reliable evidence;
- exploit existing biases;
- damage source credibility.

These effects should spread through actual information channels, not globally.

## 7.6.28 Secret Exposure

When a secret is disclosed, determine:
- who directly learns it;
- who can plausibly hear about it;
- whether they believe it;
- whether they repeat it;
- whether the disclosure can be traced.

Exposure is not equivalent to universal public knowledge.

## 7.6.29 Information Leakage

Sensitive information may leak accidentally through:
- careless speech;
- documents;
- overheard conversation;
- visible behavior;
- repeated patterns;
- romantic intimacy;
- family discussion;
- intoxication;
- intercepted messages.

Leakage should have a plausible path.

## 7.6.30 Relationship-Based Disclosure

NPCs may share more information with:
- trusted friends;
- romantic partners;
- family;
- mentors;
- subordinates;
- allies.

But trust does not automatically override:
- duty;
- secrecy;
- professional ethics;
- clan rules;
- operational security.

## 7.6.31 Romantic and Sexual Privacy

For adults, romantic or sexual information may itself be secret.

Examples:
- attraction;
- relationship status;
- affair;
- sexual preferences;
- prior partners;
- current adult arousal.

The simulation should distinguish:
- what is objectively true;
- who knows;
- who suspects;
- what has been disclosed.

Private adult sexual state is never automatically public knowledge.

## 7.6.32 Interrogation and Knowledge

Interrogation cannot extract information the target does not possess.

A target may provide:
- accurate knowledge;
- mistaken belief;
- partial knowledge;
- speculation;
- deliberate lies;
- refusal.

The interrogator may incorrectly assume false information is true.

## 7.6.33 False Confessions

Under pressure, an NPC may provide false information simply to end an interrogation or satisfy the interrogator.

Therefore:
**information obtained under pressure is not automatically reliable.**

## 7.6.34 Deception Detection

Insight or Perception should not function as automatic truth detection.

A character may detect:
- hesitation;
- stress;
- inconsistency;
- unusual behavior.

They must still interpret what that means.

Someone can appear nervous while telling the truth.

## 7.6.35 Information Verification

NPCs may verify information through:
- witnesses;
- records;
- surveillance;
- experts;
- physical evidence;
- sensory techniques;
- cross-examination;
- independent sources.

Verification consumes time and resources.

## 7.6.36 Information Value

Information may have value based on:
- rarity;
- sensitivity;
- strategic usefulness;
- urgency;
- credibility;
- exclusivity.

NPCs may trade, protect, sell, or exploit valuable information.

Economic specifics remain owned by later economic systems.

## 7.6.37 Knowledge and Reputation

Reputation should affect what people initially believe.

Example:
If someone is widely known as reliable, their testimony may receive higher initial confidence.

But reputation is not proof.

## 7.6.38 Knowledge Networks

NPCs have practical information networks:
- family;
- clan;
- team;
- workplace;
- intelligence contacts;
- merchants;
- criminal contacts;
- political allies.

These networks constrain plausible knowledge spread.

## 7.6.39 Geographic Limits

Distance matters.

News may travel slowly or unevenly between:
- neighborhoods;
- villages;
- countries;
- remote regions.

Fast communication methods may create exceptions.

## 7.6.40 Institutional Knowledge

An organization may possess information no single member fully knows.

Example:
- one operative has the target name;
- another has the location;
- command knows the objective;
- archives contain historical context.

Institutional knowledge should not automatically become individual knowledge.

## 7.6.41 Knowledge After Organizational Change

Transfers, promotions, demotions, resignations, imprisonment, or exile may alter access.

Past knowledge remains unless forgotten or altered.
Future access changes.

## 7.6.42 Canon Knowledge

Canon NPCs should only know what they plausibly know at the current simulation date.

Later-canon revelations cannot leak backward merely because the model knows them.

Alternate timeline divergence must update knowledge accordingly.

## 7.6.43 Player Knowledge vs Character Knowledge

The user may know facts that the player character does not.

The simulation should preserve this boundary.

Player choices may be constrained by what the character can reasonably know.

Meta-knowledge should not silently become character knowledge.

## 7.6.44 Hidden Rolls

Where knowledge acquisition is uncertain, Ruleset 2 may resolve hidden checks.

Examples:
- noticing a contradiction;
- overhearing conversation;
- recognizing a seal;
- remembering a detail;
- spotting surveillance.

The player should not always know that a check occurred or failed.

## 7.6.45 Knowledge Depth by Simulation Tier

### Major NPC
Track:
- important facts;
- confidence;
- source;
- secrets;
- suspicions;
- false beliefs;
- information networks.

### Supporting NPC
Track:
- relevant knowledge;
- important misconceptions;
- active secrets;
- one or two relevant sources.

### Persistent Minor NPC
Track:
- key facts they possess;
- rumors they believe;
- important secret if relevant.

### Background NPC
Generate only the information required by immediate context.

## 7.6.46 Information Ledger

For major secrets or mysteries, the simulation may maintain a compact ledger:

**Fact:** Dagar met a missing-nin outside the village.
- Rex: unknown
- Karn: suspects
- Rena: believes false alibi
- Village Intelligence: partial evidence
- Dagar: knows truth
- Witness: knows meeting occurred, not identity

This structure can prevent continuity errors.

## 7.6.47 Design Standard

A knowledge mechanic should be rejected or revised if it:
- gives NPCs omniscient access to world state;
- treats observation as perfect understanding;
- treats suspicion as knowledge;
- treats confidence as truth;
- makes secrets globally known after one disclosure;
- lets interrogation create information the target lacks;
- makes Insight automatic lie detection;
- ignores source credibility;
- ignores outdated information;
- ignores compartmentalization;
- leaks future-canon knowledge backward;
- allows player meta-knowledge to become character knowledge automatically;
- requires detailed tracking of irrelevant facts for every background NPC.

## Governing Rule

**Every important NPC belief should have a plausible path from information source to interpretation, and NPCs should only act on what they can reasonably know or believe.**
