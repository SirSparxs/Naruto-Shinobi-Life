# Ruleset 7 — NPCs, Relationships & Social Interaction: Simulation Quick Reference

## Core Principle

NPCs are autonomous people, not player-facing reward systems. Social outcomes emerge from identity, goals, beliefs, memory, relationships, emotions, context, skills, reputation, obligations, and current circumstances.

NPCs and players generally use the same social rules.

## 1. NPC State

For important NPCs, preserve:
- identity and role;
- goals and priorities;
- knowledge, beliefs, suspicions, and secrets;
- major memories;
- current emotional state;
- important relationships;
- obligations and promises;
- routine, location, and availability;
- reputation and group standing.

NPCs act on what they believe, not objective omniscience.

Simulation detail scales by NPC tier:
1. Major
2. Supporting
3. Persistent Minor
4. Background

Promote/demote detail without rewriting established history.

## 2. Relationships

Relationships are directional and multidimensional. A->B may differ from B->A.

Core dimensions:
- Trust
- Respect
- Affection / Attachment
- Loyalty
- Fear
- Familiarity
- Obligation / Indebtedness
- Resentment
- Hostility
- Romantic Interest
- Adult Sexual Chemistry (18+ only)

Internal values may use 0–100, but normal gameplay should not expose meters.

Relationship labels such as friend, sibling, rival, mentor, teammate, partner, or ex describe structure, not total relationship quality.

No cross-dimension purchase:
- Affection does not buy Trust.
- Trust does not buy attraction.
- Loyalty does not erase Fear.
- Romantic Interest does not create consent.
- Sexual Chemistry does not create commitment or exclusivity.

Relationship change must be:
- causal;
- proportional;
- dimension-specific;
- history-sensitive;
- resistant to grinding.

Repeated minor interactions have diminishing impact. Major betrayal, sacrifice, rescue, or shared hardship can cause larger change.

## 3. Memory, Belief & Emotion

NPC memory is imperfect and may:
- fade;
- distort;
- compress into patterns;
- retain incorrect beliefs.

Track important memories separately from objective event history.

Emotions are temporary states, not personality. Mixed emotions are valid.

Relevant emotions include:
- anger, fear, grief, joy, guilt, shame, jealousy, affection, anxiety, suspicion, loneliness, confidence, excitement, disgust, and adult arousal.

Emotion influences decisions but does not dictate them.

Adult arousal is 18+ only and never equals:
- attraction;
- romance;
- consent;
- willingness.

## 4. Social Resolution

Default sequence:

Context
→ Actor Intent
→ Desired Outcome
→ Possibility Check
→ Target Willingness / Resistance
→ Need for Roll?
→ Relevant Skill + Attribute + Context
→ Ruleset 2 Resolution
→ NPC Decision / Response
→ Immediate Consequence
→ Relationship / Memory / Belief / Reputation Update

Do not roll when:
- the outcome is obvious;
- the target already wants it;
- the outcome is impossible.

Possible willingness states:
- eager;
- willing;
- leaning willing;
- undecided;
- reluctant;
- strongly resistant;
- firmly opposed.

Social success changes plausible outcomes; it does not override agency.

Exceptional success is the best plausible result, not reality-breaking influence.

## 5. Social Skills

Ruleset 1 owns skill progression.

Core social skills:
- Persuasion
- Negotiation
- Deception
- Insight
- Etiquette
- Leadership
- Intimidation
- Teaching
- Interrogation
- Performance
- Diplomacy
- Seduction (18+ only)

There is no Charisma attribute.

Use contextually relevant Attributes, commonly:
- Intelligence;
- Perception;
- Willpower;
- occasionally Agility, Strength, or others when justified.

Skill determines method quality. It cannot bypass reality, knowledge, prerequisites, or autonomy.

Insight is not mind reading or a lie detector.

## 6. Persuasion, Deception & Coercion

Persuasion may change how someone evaluates a plausible option.

It cannot:
- instantly rewrite core values;
- force impossible outcomes;
- manufacture attraction;
- create consent.

Deception changes beliefs, not reality. Track target belief, evidence, contradictions, and source confidence.

Interrogation cannot extract truthful information the target does not know. False confessions, omissions, lies, and mistaken memories are possible.

Intimidation creates fear of consequences.

Fear-based compliance is not:
- Loyalty;
- Trust;
- genuine agreement.

Commands depend on recognized authority. Formal rank does not guarantee genuine followership.

Coercion must remain tagged as coercion; compliance under severe threat is not freely chosen willingness.

## 7. Romance & Adult Sexual State

Attraction is multidimensional:
- aesthetic;
- emotional/interpersonal;
- Romantic Interest;
- adult Sexual Attraction;
- interpersonal chemistry;
- adult Sexual Chemistry.

Attraction may be asymmetric.

Mutual attraction does not automatically create:
- dating;
- relationship;
- compatibility;
- consent.

Flirting communicates or tests interest.

Seduction is an adult-only social skill for developing reciprocal romantic/sexual tension where plausible. It cannot manufacture attraction or override refusal.

Courtship requires mutual participation. A relationship forms through mutual recognition/agreement, not a threshold.

Relationship structure may include:
- casual dating;
- exclusive courtship;
- monogamy;
- consensual non-monogamy;
- long-term partnership.

Exclusivity is not assumed unless established.

Established relationships continue evolving through:
- expectations;
- compatibility;
- communication;
- maintenance;
- conflict;
- shared history;
- continued choice.

NPC romances continue off-screen and are not reserved for the player.

### Important
Section 7.29 — Boundaries, Consent & Compatibility — remains intentionally undeveloped.

Existing governing principles still apply:
- arousal never equals consent;
- prior consent is not permanent;
- rejection ends the current pursuit;
- repeated unwanted advances worsen context;
- severe coercion cannot be converted into genuine willingness.

## 8. Jealousy, Breakups & Repair

Jealousy is an emotional response to perceived relational threat. It is not proof of love, betrayal, or ownership.

Breakups may be:
- mutual;
- one-sided;
- temporary separation;
- permanent.

Ending a relationship does not erase:
- Attachment;
- attraction;
- history;
- obligations;
- shared networks.

Reconciliation requires renewed mutual willingness and meaningful changed conditions.

Apology does not automatically produce:
- forgiveness;
- Trust restoration;
- reconciliation.

Forgiveness does not require renewed relationship.

Serious betrayal may leave permanent scars even after repair.

## 9. Promises, Favors & Obligations

Promises and obligations arise from actual commitments, accepted help, shared norms, and history.

Do not use universal favor points.

Distinguish:
- promise;
- favor;
- gratitude;
- debt of gratitude;
- role obligation;
- social obligation.

Favors must remain proportional.

A favor can increase willingness but cannot compel:
- immoral acts;
- extreme sacrifice;
- permanent service;
- romance or intimacy.

Life debts may exist as personal/cultural beliefs, not universal ownership.

Promises may be:
- fulfilled;
- partially fulfilled;
- broken;
- renegotiated;
- forgiven;
- disputed.

Intentional deception at the time of promising is worse than sincere later failure.

## 10. Reputation, Rumors & Networks

Reputation is distributed belief, not objective truth.

Track by:
- audience;
- domain;
- confidence;
- reach.

Fame = recognition reach.
Notoriety = recognition associated with danger/scandal/fear.
Public image = dominant visible narrative.

Rumors spread only through actual channels and may:
- mutate;
- distort;
- branch;
- be false;
- be corrected imperfectly.

Social networks consist of actual ties, bridges, hubs, gatekeepers, teams, families, factions, and institutions.

No global reputation teleportation.

## 11. Groups & Leadership

Group relationship is separate from relationships with individual members.

Possible group dimensions:
- belonging;
- identification;
- group Trust;
- group Respect / legitimacy;
- Loyalty;
- obligation;
- attachment;
- alienation;
- Resentment;
- Fear.

Leadership may come from:
- formal authority;
- competence;
- legitimacy;
- Trust;
- Respect;
- Loyalty;
- fear;
- shared purpose.

Formal authority and actual social authority are separate.

Dissent does not automatically equal disloyalty.

Fear-based obedience is fragile and not Loyalty.

Informal leaders may emerge without rank.

## 12. NPC-to-NPC Simulation

NPC social life continues off-screen.

NPCs may independently:
- become friends;
- form rivalries;
- date;
- marry;
- break up;
- reconcile;
- exchange favors;
- spread rumors;
- change alliances.

Use relevance-weighted and event-driven simulation.

Do not simulate every possible NPC pair.

Prioritize:
- active relationships;
- plausible new connections;
- event-relevant pairs.

Stable relationships may remain stable. Do not generate drama merely for excitement.

## 13. Routines & Social Access

NPCs exist in time and place.

Track enough routine to know:
- where they usually are;
- what they are doing;
- whether they are available.

Availability may be:
- fully available;
- briefly available;
- distracted;
- busy but interruptible;
- unavailable;
- unreachable.

Relationship can improve access but does not erase:
- work;
- sleep;
- missions;
- privacy;
- security;
- family responsibilities.

Gatekeepers, rank, invitations, appointments, and institutional restrictions matter.

Physical availability is not the same as social willingness.

## 14. Social Consequences & Access

Relationships can causally change access to:
- information;
- introductions;
- invitations;
- shelter;
- emotional support;
- mentorship;
- recommendations;
- assistance;
- opportunities.

Access is domain-specific.

High Trust does not unlock all secrets.
Friendship does not bypass formal clearance.
Gifts do not unlock relationship tiers.

Access can be:
- temporary;
- conditional;
- revoked;
- restored.

## 15. Hidden Social State

The engine may know exact internal state. The player usually should not.

Player-facing information comes through:
- behavior;
- dialogue;
- tone;
- routines;
- observable changes;
- explicit disclosures;
- investigation;
- Insight.

Do not announce hidden checks.

Successful deception should actually deceive when the character lacks contrary evidence.

Track separately:
- objective truth;
- NPC belief;
- player-character knowledge;
- suspicion;
- rumor.

Debug/GM knowledge does not automatically become character knowledge.

## 16. Social Learning

Ruleset 7 defines meaningful practice; Ruleset 1 owns actual progression.

Social growth comes from:
- challenging interactions;
- feedback;
- mentorship;
- observation;
- reflection;
- cultural exposure;
- real responsibility.

No generic Social XP.

No substantial progression from repetitive trivial actions.

Failure can teach only when the character can learn something from it.

High mastery improves judgment, subtlety, adaptability, and efficiency—not agency override.

## 17. Procedural NPC Generation

Generate NPCs from plausible lives rather than disconnected random stats.

Preferred order:
life context → role → age/experience → history → personality/values → competencies → relationships → goals → current pressures → surface details.

Skills should match training and profession.

Important NPCs need strengths and weaknesses.

Do not overproduce:
- prodigies;
- rare bloodlines;
- tragic backstories;
- forbidden techniques;
- secret destinies.

NPCs should already have plausible social networks.

Adults are not automatically single.

Never generate a guaranteed romantic partner, mentor, or solution specifically for the player without independent world justification.

## 18. Persistence

Persist anything needed for future causality:
- identity;
- relationships;
- important memories;
- beliefs;
- secrets;
- obligations;
- goals;
- routine;
- location/status;
- social access;
- reputation;
- major social-event history.

Keep:
- current state;
- major causal history.

Compress routine detail.

Do not reroll already-resolved hidden outcomes after save/load.

NPC decisions and player decisions persist equally.

After canon diverges causally, established simulation history becomes authoritative for that timeline.

## 19. Cross-Ruleset Ownership

Ruleset 7 owns:
- social meaning;
- relationships;
- beliefs;
- influence;
- social consequences.

Ruleset 1 owns:
- Attributes, Skills, progression.

Ruleset 2 owns:
- probability, difficulty, opposed/hidden resolution.

Ruleset 3 owns:
- chakra, jutsu, supernatural compulsion/memory/emotion alteration.

Ruleset 4 owns:
- injury, pain, fatigue, medical state.

Future systems own their formal domains:
- missions;
- institutions;
- economy;
- law;
- travel;
- world events.

Ruleset 7 consumes their results socially without duplicating them.

## 20. Anti-Exploit Rules

Never allow:
- social reroll spam;
- compliment farming;
- gift farming;
- favor farming;
- relationship-meter grinding;
- Persuasion mind control;
- Seduction-to-consent;
- Insight mind reading;
- interrogation omniscience;
- apology-reset loops;
- schedule summoning;
- reputation teleportation;
- friendship bypassing institutions;
- social-skill bypassing other rulesets;
- save/load rerolling;
- protagonist privilege.

A new attempt requires a real causal change.

Repeated unwanted behavior may worsen the situation.

NPCs receive the same anti-exploit protections as the player.

## 21. Simulation Audit

For an important social event, the engine should be able to answer:

1. What does each participant want?
2. What does each participant know or believe?
3. What relationship exists already?
4. What context matters?
5. Is the desired outcome possible?
6. Is a roll actually necessary?
7. Which skill and Attribute apply?
8. What does Ruleset 2 resolve?
9. What does the NPC voluntarily decide?
10. What relationship, memory, belief, obligation, reputation, or access changes afterward?
11. What information is player-visible versus hidden?
12. What state must persist?

## Governing Rule

**NPCs remain autonomous, relationships remain multidimensional, social skill influences rather than controls, information remains perspective-bound, consequences persist, and the world continues socially whether or not the player is present.**
