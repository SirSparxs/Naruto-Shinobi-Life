# 7.8 — Emotional & Mental State

Status: Provisional design section pending explicit approval.

Ruleset 7.8 defines temporary emotional and psychological states that influence perception, judgment, social behavior, and decision-making.

The governing principle is:

**Emotion changes how an NPC experiences and evaluates a situation, but does not replace identity, values, knowledge, relationships, or agency.**

## 7.8.1 Emotion vs Personality

Personality describes relatively stable tendencies.

Emotion describes current or recent state.

Examples:
- a patient person may currently be furious;
- a confident person may currently be afraid;
- a reserved person may currently be euphoric;
- a forgiving person may still experience resentment.

Temporary emotion should not silently rewrite baseline identity.

## 7.8.2 Core Emotional Dimensions

The simulation may track relevant emotions such as:
- anger;
- fear;
- sadness;
- grief;
- joy;
- relief;
- pride;
- shame;
- guilt;
- embarrassment;
- jealousy;
- envy;
- affection;
- hope;
- despair;
- anxiety;
- frustration;
- excitement;
- disgust;
- loneliness;
- confidence;
- suspicion;
- curiosity;
- adult arousal where applicable.

Not every NPC needs every emotion tracked at once.

Only emotionally relevant state should remain active.

## 7.8.3 Mixed Emotions

NPCs may experience multiple emotions simultaneously.

Examples:
- proud but worried;
- angry and ashamed;
- relieved but grieving;
- affectionate and resentful;
- attracted but distrustful;
- jealous but supportive;
- frightened but determined.

Mixed emotions should be normal rather than treated as contradictions.

## 7.8.4 Emotional Intensity

Each active emotion may have qualitative intensity such as:
- faint;
- mild;
- moderate;
- strong;
- overwhelming.

Intensity affects how much the emotion influences:
- attention;
- interpretation;
- patience;
- risk perception;
- impulse strength;
- communication;
- memory salience.

## 7.8.5 Emotional Duration

Different emotions persist for different periods.

Possible durations:
- momentary;
- short-lived;
- lingering;
- sustained;
- chronic / recurring.

Duration depends on:
- event severity;
- personality;
- relationship importance;
- repeated triggers;
- unresolved causes;
- coping;
- new information.

## 7.8.6 Emotional Triggers

Emotions should normally have identifiable causes.

Triggers may include:
- direct events;
- memories;
- people;
- locations;
- words;
- symbols;
- rumors;
- anniversaries;
- perceived disrespect;
- rejection;
- success;
- danger;
- physical intimacy between adults;
- betrayal.

Triggers may be:
- known to the NPC;
- partly understood;
- unconscious.

## 7.8.7 Emotional Recovery

Emotions should decay or transform over time.

Recovery may be affected by:
- time;
- sleep;
- safety;
- reassurance;
- social support;
- successful resolution;
- apology;
- revenge;
- evidence;
- distance from trigger;
- personality;
- temperament;
- ongoing stress.

Some emotions persist if the cause remains unresolved.

## 7.8.8 Emotional Carryover

Emotion from one event may affect later unrelated interactions.

Examples:
- anger after a mission makes an NPC less patient at home;
- grief reduces interest in socializing;
- pride after promotion increases confidence;
- anxiety from investigation increases suspicion.

Carryover should weaken with time unless reinforced.

## 7.8.9 Mood vs Emotion

A mood is broader and less tied to a single trigger.

Examples:
- irritable;
- upbeat;
- melancholy;
- tense;
- relaxed;
- restless.

Mood may influence multiple interactions over hours or days.

Specific emotions can exist within a mood.

## 7.8.10 Stress

Stress is a broader pressure state rather than a single emotion.

Sources include:
- workload;
- danger;
- family problems;
- financial pressure;
- mission failure;
- secrecy;
- relationship conflict;
- sleep deprivation;
- injury.

Stress may:
- reduce patience;
- narrow attention;
- increase irritability;
- reduce social nuance;
- increase mistakes;
- amplify other emotions.

## 7.8.11 Confidence

Confidence may vary by context.

An NPC may feel:
- confident in combat;
- insecure romantically;
- uncertain politically.

Confidence affects:
- initiative;
- willingness to speak;
- risk-taking;
- persistence after failure.

It should not be one universal number.

## 7.8.12 Suspicion

Suspicion is a temporary or sustained state of doubt toward:
- a person;
- claim;
- situation;
- institution.

Suspicion may be triggered by:
- inconsistency;
- rumor;
- prior betrayal;
- unexplained behavior;
- evidence.

Suspicion should influence verification behavior without automatically proving wrongdoing.

## 7.8.13 Fear

Fear should influence behavior through:
- threat perception;
- urgency;
- avoidance;
- appeasement;
- defensive aggression;
- seeking protection;
- concealment.

Fear does not equal obedience.

Different NPCs respond differently.

## 7.8.14 Anger

Anger may increase:
- confrontation;
- impatience;
- punitive interpretation;
- risk tolerance;
- harsh language.

But anger can also produce:
- cold withdrawal;
- silence;
- delayed retaliation.

Expression depends on personality and context.

## 7.8.15 Grief

Grief may affect:
- motivation;
- attention;
- social availability;
- sleep;
- irritability;
- attachment;
- memory salience.

Grief should not follow one universal sequence.

NPCs may:
- withdraw;
- seek support;
- overwork;
- become angry;
- become numb;
- pursue memorial goals.

## 7.8.16 Shame & Guilt

### Shame
Focuses on the self:
- "I am bad / weak / disgraced."

### Guilt
Focuses on behavior:
- "I did something wrong."

They may create different responses.

Shame may produce:
- hiding;
- defensiveness;
- withdrawal.

Guilt may produce:
- confession;
- apology;
- restitution.

Either can also create denial or anger.

## 7.8.17 Pride

Pride may arise from:
- achievement;
- recognition;
- identity;
- family;
- clan;
- skill.

Healthy pride may improve confidence.

Threatened pride may increase defensiveness or resistance.

## 7.8.18 Jealousy & Envy

### Jealousy
Fear of losing a valued relationship or position to someone else.

### Envy
Desire for something another person possesses.

These should be distinct.

Jealousy may exist in:
- romance;
- friendship;
- mentorship;
- family;
- professional relationships.

It does not automatically produce hostility.

## 7.8.19 Affection

Affection is an emotional state tied to warmth, care, or fondness.

It may occur within:
- friendship;
- family;
- mentorship;
- romance.

Affection is related to but distinct from the persistent Affection relationship axis.

Current affection may temporarily intensify or fade.

## 7.8.20 Hope & Despair

Hope reflects belief that a desired outcome remains possible.

Despair reflects severe loss of expected possibility.

These states may influence:
- persistence;
- risk;
- help-seeking;
- surrender;
- motivation.

Despair should not automatically imply self-harm or passivity.

## 7.8.21 Adult Arousal

For adult characters only, arousal may exist as a temporary physiological/emotional state.

It may be influenced by:
- attraction;
- physical intimacy;
- fantasy;
- context;
- memory;
- presentation;
- explicit sexual stimulation;
- relationship dynamics.

Arousal may:
- increase attention to sexual cues;
- increase sexual motivation;
- alter embarrassment or confidence;
- interact with attraction and desire.

Arousal does not:
- create attraction automatically;
- create romantic affection;
- override boundaries;
- imply initiation;
- imply consent;
- persist indefinitely.

## 7.8.22 Arousal vs Relationship Dimension

Ruleset 7 may track both:

### Current Arousal
Immediate temporary state.

### Arousal / Sexual Chemistry Toward Specific Person
Persistent or recurring relationship tendency.

These should not be merged.

Someone may have high baseline sexual chemistry with another adult but no current arousal.

Or temporary arousal may occur without deeper relationship interest.

## 7.8.23 Emotional Suppression

NPCs may deliberately hide or suppress emotional expression.

Suppression changes:
- outward behavior;
not necessarily:
- internal emotion.

A shinobi may appear calm while afraid.

A politician may smile while furious.

## 7.8.24 Emotional Expression

Expression depends on:
- personality;
- culture;
- context;
- rank;
- privacy;
- audience;
- self-control;
- trust.

The same emotion may be expressed differently across NPCs.

## 7.8.25 Emotional Masking

NPCs may intentionally display a different emotion.

Examples:
- pretend confidence;
- hide jealousy;
- fake warmth;
- conceal fear.

This may involve social skill or deception when relevant.

## 7.8.26 Emotional Awareness

NPCs differ in how accurately they recognize their own emotions.

Possible levels:
- highly self-aware;
- moderately aware;
- confused;
- mislabeling emotion;
- actively denying emotion.

Example:
An NPC may interpret jealousy as anger.

## 7.8.27 Emotional Regulation

NPCs differ in ability to regulate emotion.

Regulation may involve:
- breathing;
- withdrawal;
- reframing;
- discipline;
- meditation;
- social support;
- distraction.

Ruleset 1 may own underlying learned skills if formalized.

## 7.8.28 Emotional Spillover Into Social Resolution

Emotions may modify:
- willingness;
- perceived credibility;
- threshold for offense;
- openness;
- patience;
- trust interpretation;
- risk.

Ruleset 2 owns numerical resolution.

Ruleset 7 supplies emotional context.

## 7.8.29 Emotional Threshold Events

Some emotions may reach intensity where behavior changes meaningfully.

Examples:
- mild anger -> irritation;
- strong anger -> confrontation more likely;
- overwhelming fear -> panic becomes possible.

Thresholds should influence options, not force one exact action.

## 7.8.30 Emotional Contradiction With Values

An NPC may feel an emotion that conflicts with their values.

Examples:
- jealous of a friend's success while valuing loyalty;
- attracted to someone they believe they should avoid;
- angry at a child while valuing patience.

This should create internal conflict rather than automatically resolving one side.

## 7.8.31 Emotional Memory Feedback

Strong emotions increase memory salience.

Memories can later reactivate emotions.

This creates a feedback loop:

**Event -> Emotion -> Strong Memory -> Trigger -> Emotion**

The system should avoid runaway escalation by allowing recovery, reinterpretation, and changing context.

## 7.8.32 Physical State Interface

Ruleset 4 may supply:
- pain;
- exhaustion;
- injury;
- intoxication;
- illness.

Ruleset 7 interprets how those conditions influence emotional state and social behavior.

It does not duplicate physical mechanics.

## 7.8.33 Chakra / Jutsu Interface

Ruleset 3 owns:
- genjutsu;
- supernatural emotion manipulation;
- mind-affecting techniques.

Ruleset 7 tracks:
- resulting emotion;
- social behavior;
- aftermath;
- relationship consequences.

## 7.8.34 Social Contagion

Emotions may spread socially.

Examples:
- panic in a crowd;
- enthusiasm in a team;
- grief in a family;
- tension in a room.

Spread depends on:
- visibility;
- relationship;
- group norms;
- trust;
- intensity.

It should not be automatic.

## 7.8.35 Emotional Baseline

Some NPCs return more naturally toward certain baseline moods.

Examples:
- generally upbeat;
- generally tense;
- generally calm;
- generally irritable.

This should derive from temperament rather than becoming a permanent emotion.

## 7.8.36 Long-Term Emotional States

Some emotional states may persist because circumstances remain unresolved.

Examples:
- ongoing grief;
- chronic resentment;
- prolonged anxiety;
- long-term hope.

When persistent enough to become identity-changing, the system may update:
- values;
- habits;
- goals;
- relationship tendencies;
- self-concept.

## 7.8.37 Emotional State by Simulation Tier

### Major NPC
Track several active emotions, causes, intensity, and important triggers.

### Supporting NPC
Track dominant emotional state when relevant.

### Persistent Minor NPC
Track only strong state affecting current interaction.

### Background NPC
Use immediate situational emotion only.

## 7.8.38 Player-Facing Emotional Information

Exact emotional values should remain hidden.

Players perceive:
- facial expression;
- tone;
- body language;
- behavior;
- context;
- known history.

Perception and social skills may improve inference.

The simulation should allow misreading.

## 7.8.39 Emotional Misinterpretation

A character may mistake:
- fear for guilt;
- embarrassment for attraction;
- anger for hatred;
- silence for indifference;
- arousal for consent.

The simulation must preserve the true internal state separately from the observer's interpretation.

## 7.8.40 Emotional Update Procedure

When a meaningful event occurs:

1. Identify relevant interpretation.
2. Compare with goals, values, memories, and relationships.
3. Determine emotional response.
4. Set intensity.
5. Identify likely duration.
6. Apply immediate decision/social effects.
7. Decay, reinforce, or transform over time.
8. Create memory if sufficiently salient.

## 7.8.41 Design Standard

An emotional mechanic should be rejected or revised if it:
- treats emotions as deterministic commands;
- assumes everyone reacts identically;
- collapses personality into mood;
- makes emotion permanent without cause;
- ignores mixed emotions;
- exposes exact internal state automatically;
- treats jealousy as automatic abuse or hostility;
- treats fear as automatic obedience;
- treats arousal as attraction or consent;
- duplicates medical or supernatural systems;
- tracks dozens of irrelevant emotions continuously.

## Governing Rule

**Emotions should alter attention, interpretation, motivation, and willingness in ways consistent with the character, while leaving final behavior to the broader decision system.**
