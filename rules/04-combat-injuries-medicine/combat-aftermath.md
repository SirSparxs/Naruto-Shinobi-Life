# 4.27 — Combat Aftermath

Status: Provisional design section pending explicit approval.

This section defines what happens immediately after active combat ends and how encounter consequences are converted into persistent world state.

Combat aftermath may include:
- casualty accounting;
- medical triage;
- prisoner security;
- body recovery;
- equipment recovery;
- battlefield search;
- evidence;
- structural damage;
- contamination and hazards;
- mission-objective resolution;
- reports;
- attribution;
- long-term consequences.

The governing rule is:
**Combat ends when active hostilities stop. Its consequences continue until they are resolved by the world.**

## 4.27.1 Encounter End Does Not Reset State

When active combat ends, the following remain:
- injuries;
- fatigue;
- chakra depletion;
- conditions;
- prisoners;
- dropped weapons;
- destroyed equipment;
- corpses;
- fire;
- smoke;
- flooding;
- collapsed structures;
- poison contamination;
- traps;
- damaged terrain.

Nothing automatically returns to default.

## 4.27.2 Aftermath Begins When Immediate Hostile Timing No Longer Dominates

An encounter may transition into aftermath when:
- enemies are defeated;
- enemies retreat;
- surrender is accepted;
- sides disengage;
- objective is completed and contact breaks.

Combat can restart if:
- hidden enemies emerge;
- prisoners resist;
- reinforcements arrive;
- retreating enemies return.

Aftermath is a state, not a guarantee of safety.

## 4.27.3 Immediate Security Comes First

Before detailed recovery activity, survivors may need to determine:
- whether threats remain;
- whether traps are active;
- whether structures are unstable;
- whether fire, gas, poison, or flooding continues;
- whether enemies are feigning incapacity.

A battlefield may be quiet without being safe.

## 4.27.4 Casualty Accounting

Teams should establish:
- who is present;
- who is injured;
- who is missing;
- who is incapacitated;
- who is dead;
- who is captured.

This is especially important after:
- chaotic battles;
- split-team operations;
- large-scale engagements.

## 4.27.5 Medical Triage Continues After Combat

The end of hostile action often increases medical options because:
- treatment can become more deliberate;
- medics gain time;
- casualties can be moved;
- supplies can be redistributed.

However, deterioration continues.

The most urgent patients must still be prioritized.

## 4.27.6 Stabilization Before Debrief

Characters should not immediately begin:
- interrogations;
- looting;
- reports;
- investigation

while a teammate is actively dying unless circumstances force that choice.

Aftermath priorities remain contextual, but immediate medical threats usually matter.

## 4.27.7 Dead, Dying, and Uncertain Status

Some casualties may require confirmation of:
- consciousness;
- vital function;
- reversibility;
- death.

Rulesets 4.12 and 4.18 govern confirmation.

A motionless body is not automatically confirmed dead.

## 4.27.8 Body Recovery

Bodies may matter because of:
- burial;
- identity;
- intelligence;
- clan secrets;
- bloodline theft risk;
- forensic evidence;
- cultural or personal obligations.

Recovery may therefore become an immediate tactical or logistical objective.

## 4.27.9 Enemy Bodies

Enemy dead may contain:
- identifying documents;
- equipment;
- poison;
- mission orders;
- hidden seals;
- intelligence.

They may also be dangerous through:
- traps;
- contamination;
- body-altering techniques;
- delayed effects.

Search should still respect information and hazard rules.

## 4.27.10 Prisoner Security

Captured opponents may need:
- medical assessment;
- disarmament;
- search;
- restraint;
- chakra suppression;
- guard assignment.

A defeated enemy is not automatically a secure prisoner.

## 4.27.11 Prisoner Medical State

A live-capture objective remains at risk if the prisoner is:
- bleeding;
- poisoned;
- unconscious;
- unstable;
- injured by restraints.

Maintaining the prisoner's life may require treatment during aftermath.

## 4.27.12 Prisoner Search

Searches may attempt to find:
- weapons;
- poison;
- explosive tags;
- communication tools;
- hidden compartments;
- sealing items;
- intelligence documents.

Ruleset 2 governs whether concealed items are found.

## 4.27.13 Equipment Recovery

After combat, characters may recover:
- dropped weapons;
- thrown kunai;
- bags;
- armor;
- medical supplies;
- mission-critical tools.

Items remain where they physically ended unless moved or destroyed.

## 4.27.14 Equipment Damage Assessment

Recovered equipment may be:
- intact;
- damaged;
- breached;
- contaminated;
- empty;
- broken.

Equipment state should persist into future scenes.

## 4.27.15 Ammunition and Supply Reconciliation

After significant encounters, the engine may update:
- remaining ammunition;
- explosive tags;
- antidotes;
- medical supplies;
- restraints;
- food/water where relevant.

Routine supply reconciliation can be compressed.

## 4.27.16 Battlefield Search

A deliberate search may identify:
- hidden enemies;
- traps;
- secret exits;
- lost equipment;
- evidence;
- bodies;
- tracks;
- concealed compartments.

Search quality depends on:
- time;
- Perception;
- expertise;
- environmental conditions.

## 4.27.17 Evidence Persists

Combat may leave:
- blood;
- footprints;
- chakra residue;
- weapon marks;
- damaged structures;
- bodies;
- spent tools;
- scorch marks;
- poison residue.

These can later be examined by investigators.

## 4.27.18 Evidence Can Be Altered

Characters may intentionally:
- clean;
- remove;
- conceal;
- destroy;
- plant;
- preserve

evidence.

Ruleset 2 governs whether manipulation is noticed or convincing.

## 4.27.19 Attribution Is Not Automatic

The world does not automatically know who caused every effect.

Observers may infer responsibility from:
- witnesses;
- technique signatures;
- residue;
- weapon injuries;
- known abilities;
- reports.

A battle can therefore have uncertain attribution.

## 4.27.20 Witnesses Matter

Surviving witnesses may remember:
- participants;
- techniques;
- statements;
- sequence of events;
- injuries.

Their knowledge may be:
- incomplete;
- biased;
- confused;
- deliberately false.

## 4.27.21 Technique Exposure

Using rare or secret abilities may reveal information to:
- enemies;
- allies;
- civilians;
- investigators.

The aftermath should preserve who plausibly observed what.

This can create long-term intelligence consequences.

## 4.27.22 Structural Damage Assessment

After combat, the area may contain:
- breached walls;
- unstable buildings;
- fires;
- craters;
- blocked roads;
- damaged bridges;
- flooded spaces.

Ruleset 4.17 records the physical state.

Aftermath determines whether characters:
- evacuate;
- repair;
- report;
- exploit;
- abandon it.

## 4.27.23 Persistent Hazards

Danger may remain after fighting stops.

Examples:
- active fire;
- unstable debris;
- toxin cloud;
- contaminated water;
- unexploded tags;
- exposed electrical hazard.

Aftermath actions may need to neutralize these before ordinary activity resumes.

## 4.27.24 Civilian Casualties

If civilians were present, aftermath may include:
- rescue;
- triage;
- identification;
- evacuation;
- shelter;
- casualty reporting.

Civilian harm should remain part of mission consequence.

## 4.27.25 Missing Persons

A battle may end without locating everyone.

Characters may be:
- buried;
- separated;
- captured;
- fleeing;
- unconscious elsewhere.

Search and rescue may become the next objective.

## 4.27.26 Search and Rescue

Rescue may require:
- debris removal;
- sensory techniques;
- medical teams;
- tracking;
- structural assessment.

The simulation should not assume missing characters are dead unless confirmed.

## 4.27.27 Mission Objective Resolution

After combat, the game should determine whether the actual mission objective was achieved.

Examples:
- target captured;
- target killed;
- target escaped;
- client survived;
- intelligence recovered;
- bridge destroyed;
- civilians evacuated.

Winning the fight and completing the mission are separate.

## 4.27.28 Tactical Victory and Mission Success Are Separate

A team may:
- defeat every enemy but fail the mission;
- retreat successfully while preserving the objective;
- capture the target but lose critical intelligence;
- survive while allowing the enemy to complete their goal.

Aftermath is where this becomes explicit.

## 4.27.29 Partial Mission Success

Mission outcomes may be:
- complete success;
- partial success;
- mixed outcome;
- failure.

These should derive from objective completion rather than arbitrary score thresholds.

## 4.27.30 Collateral Consequences

Aftermath should record:
- damaged property;
- infrastructure loss;
- civilian injury;
- fire;
- contamination;
- environmental destruction.

These can influence:
- reputation;
- compensation;
- political reaction;
- future missions.

Those broader consequences belong to later systems.

## 4.27.31 Friendly Casualties

The aftermath should preserve:
- who was injured;
- severity;
- whether evacuation occurred;
- whether permanent consequences are likely;
- whether anyone died.

These outcomes can affect team composition and future availability.

## 4.27.32 Enemy Casualties

The world should also track:
- enemy deaths;
- injuries;
- survivors;
- prisoners;
- escapees.

An injured enemy who escapes may reappear later with persistent consequences.

## 4.27.33 Retreating Enemies Persist

An enemy who escapes is not removed from the simulation.

They may:
- seek treatment;
- warn allies;
- spread intelligence;
- return later;
- change plans.

Combat aftermath therefore directly feeds world simulation.

## 4.27.34 Looting Is Not Automatic

Characters may choose to take:
- weapons;
- money;
- documents;
- supplies;
- rare materials.

Whether this is legal, ethical, permitted, or practical depends on world context.

Ruleset 4 only tracks physical possession and condition.

## 4.27.35 Mission-Critical Evidence and Items

Some objects may need to be:
- secured;
- sealed;
- transported;
- protected from contamination.

Losing them after winning the battle may still fail the mission.

## 4.27.36 Intelligence Recovery

Aftermath may reveal:
- maps;
- orders;
- enemy identities;
- coded messages;
- technique information;
- village affiliations.

Interpretation belongs to information/intelligence systems.

## 4.27.37 Interrogation Is Not Part of Combat Resolution

A secured prisoner may later be questioned.

Detailed interrogation belongs to another ruleset.

Ruleset 4 only establishes:
- prisoner condition;
- consciousness;
- restraints;
- medical stability.

## 4.27.38 Battlefield Cleanup

Cleanup may include:
- extinguishing fires;
- removing traps;
- collecting weapons;
- recovering bodies;
- sealing hazardous material;
- clearing routes.

The level of detail should depend on mission importance.

## 4.27.39 Emergency Repair

Teams may perform temporary repairs to:
- bridges;
- shelter;
- barriers;
- transport equipment.

Long-term reconstruction belongs to broader world systems.

## 4.27.40 Medical Evacuation

Casualties may require:
- stretcher transport;
- summons;
- vehicles;
- escorted movement;
- rapid return to village.

The patient's current medical state determines transport risk.

## 4.27.41 Evacuation Order

When transport capacity is limited, the team may need to prioritize:
- near-death;
- unstable;
- stable but immobile;
- walking wounded.

This interfaces with triage.

## 4.27.42 Dead and Living May Compete for Transport

A team may have limited capacity to move:
- injured survivors;
- prisoners;
- bodies;
- mission cargo.

These logistical choices may have serious consequences.

## 4.27.43 Chakra and Fatigue After Combat

The end of combat does not instantly restore:
- chakra;
- stamina;
- focus.

Characters may still be:
- exhausted;
- vulnerable;
- unable to use advanced techniques.

This affects what aftermath actions are possible.

## 4.27.44 Rest Immediately After Combat

Short rest may improve:
- breathing;
- acute fatigue;
- concentration.

It does not automatically:
- restore chakra;
- heal injuries;
- remove poison;
- fix equipment.

## 4.27.45 Hidden Threats Can Exploit Aftermath

Enemies may deliberately wait until:
- medics are occupied;
- formations collapse;
- survivors relax;
- chakra is depleted.

The simulation should allow ambush during aftermath if circumstances support it.

## 4.27.46 Reports

Formal organizations may require reports containing:
- objective outcome;
- casualties;
- prisoners;
- enemy identities;
- techniques observed;
- equipment lost;
- collateral damage;
- unusual events.

Report systems belong to broader organization simulation.

Ruleset 4 supplies the factual combat record.

## 4.27.47 Personal Accounts May Differ

Different participants may remember events differently.

A formal report may therefore contain:
- uncertainty;
- conflicting statements;
- mistaken conclusions.

The engine truth remains separate from reported knowledge.

## 4.27.48 Combat Record

A consequential encounter should preserve:
- participants;
- location;
- start/end time;
- objective;
- major actions;
- injuries;
- deaths;
- prisoners;
- escapees;
- equipment loss;
- environmental damage;
- techniques revealed;
- mission outcome;
- unresolved hazards;
- recovered intelligence.

This can be compressed for routine encounters.

## 4.27.49 Long-Term Consequence Hooks

Aftermath may generate future events such as:
- medical leave;
- funeral;
- promotion review;
- investigation;
- revenge;
- prisoner exchange;
- repair project;
- diplomatic complaint;
- enemy adaptation;
- new intelligence lead.

Ruleset 4 records the triggers.
Other systems resolve the later consequences.

## 4.27.50 Encounter Closure

An encounter should only be considered fully closed when:
- immediate hostile interaction is over;
- persistent state has been recorded;
- unresolved casualties and prisoners are assigned somewhere;
- battlefield hazards are either resolved or intentionally left;
- mission outcome is recorded.

The narrative may move on before every consequence is solved, but the simulation should retain them.

## 4.27.51 Suggested Aftermath Pipeline

1. Confirm immediate security.
2. Account for allies, enemies, civilians, prisoners, and missing persons.
3. Perform urgent medical triage.
4. Secure prisoners and dangerous equipment.
5. Address active environmental hazards.
6. Recover bodies and mission-critical items where possible.
7. Search battlefield if time permits.
8. Reconcile equipment and supplies.
9. Determine mission-objective outcome.
10. Record collateral and structural damage.
11. Record observed techniques and intelligence.
12. Plan evacuation, transport, or continued movement.
13. Generate any unresolved follow-up consequences.
14. Persist the encounter record into world state.

## 4.27.52 Compression

Routine aftermath can be summarized when:
- no one is seriously injured;
- no prisoner matters;
- no evidence or mission item matters;
- the battlefield is safe.

The simulation should zoom in when:
- casualties are unstable;
- someone is missing;
- prisoners are dangerous;
- evidence matters;
- the area remains hazardous;
- mission success is uncertain.

## Core Design Rule

Combat aftermath should convert the encounter from a temporary scene into **persistent world history**.

The fight is not truly over just because nobody is currently attacking.
Its injuries, deaths, prisoners, evidence, equipment loss, destruction, and mission consequences continue forward.
