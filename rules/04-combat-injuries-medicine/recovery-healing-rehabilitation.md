# 4.22 — Recovery, Healing & Rehabilitation

Status: Provisional design section pending explicit approval.

This section defines how injuries improve after stabilization and definitive treatment.

Recovery should model:
- biological healing;
- medical support;
- rehabilitation;
- restoration of function;
- return-to-duty decisions;
- reinjury risk;
- chronic or permanent consequences.

Recovery should not be represented as automatically restoring full capability after a fixed number of days.

## 4.22.1 Healing and Functional Recovery Are Separate

An injury can be structurally healed while function remains impaired.

Examples:
- bone united but strength remains reduced;
- tendon repaired but range of motion remains poor;
- nerve healed enough for continuity but fine control is not restored;
- organ repaired but endurance remains reduced.

Therefore recovery should track both:
- structural healing;
- functional restoration.

## 4.22.2 Recovery Begins From the Post-Treatment State

Recovery should inherit:
- original injury;
- treatment quality;
- complications;
- surgery;
- residual damage;
- stabilization;
- medical ninjutsu used.

A perfectly repaired injury should recover differently from a poorly aligned or partially treated one.

## 4.22.3 Healing Time Depends on Injury Type

Recovery rate should depend on:
- tissue type;
- severity;
- tissue loss;
- blood supply;
- alignment;
- contamination;
- age and physiology;
- medical treatment;
- nutrition;
- rest;
- reinjury.

Different tissues should not share one universal healing timer.

## 4.22.4 Recovery Uses Broad Phases

A useful general progression may be:

### Acute
Fresh injury; instability and inflammation dominate.

### Stabilized
Immediate danger is controlled.

### Early Healing
Tissue repair has begun, but the injury remains vulnerable.

### Functional Recovery
Structure is improving and controlled activity becomes possible.

### Rehabilitation
Strength, coordination, mobility, and technique are rebuilt.

### Healed
The injury no longer requires ordinary medical management.

### Healed With Residual Consequence
Healing is complete enough for long-term stability, but some permanent or chronic impairment remains.

Not every injury needs every phase explicitly tracked.

## 4.22.5 Time Should Advance Healing Continuously

Recovery should progress through elapsed time.

The engine may update at:
- daily;
- weekly;
- milestone

intervals depending on injury severity.

Routine recovery should not require minute-by-minute simulation.

## 4.22.6 Rest Helps but Is Not Always Sufficient

Rest can:
- reduce aggravation;
- support healing;
- lower fatigue;
- preserve stabilization.

It cannot:
- realign a displaced fracture;
- reconnect a severed tendon;
- remove dead tissue;
- repair catastrophic structural damage.

Some injuries require definitive intervention before meaningful recovery can occur.

## 4.22.7 Medical Treatment Changes the Recovery Curve

Treatment may:
- reduce healing time;
- improve alignment;
- reduce scar formation;
- preserve nerve function;
- lower complication risk;
- restore tissue directly.

The recovery system should reflect what treatment actually accomplished.

## 4.22.8 Medical Ninjutsu Can Accelerate Healing

Medical ninjutsu may:
- directly repair tissue;
- accelerate natural healing;
- improve blood supply;
- reduce inflammation;
- stabilize complex structures.

The technique should define whether it:
- completes repair;
- shortens recovery;
- improves final outcome;
- merely stabilizes.

## 4.22.9 Instant Repair Does Not Always Mean Instant Readiness

Even if a technique rapidly repairs tissue, the patient may still have:
- pain;
- weakness;
- fatigue;
- poor coordination;
- swelling;
- unfamiliar movement patterns.

Combat readiness may lag behind anatomical repair.

## 4.22.10 Recovery Can Be Local

A character may have:
- fully healed torso injury;
- partially recovered leg;
- ongoing hand weakness.

Recovery should be tracked per meaningful injury or body region rather than through one global recovery percentage.

## 4.22.11 Pain Can Outlast Structural Damage

Pain may persist because of:
- inflammation;
- nerve irritation;
- scar tissue;
- chronic damage;
- movement sensitivity.

Persistent pain can affect:
- sleep;
- training;
- concentration;
- willingness to use a body part.

Pain persistence should not automatically imply new structural damage.

## 4.22.12 Swelling and Stiffness

After injury or surgery, temporary swelling and stiffness may reduce:
- range of motion;
- speed;
- fine control;
- comfort.

These may improve gradually even after the underlying injury is stable.

## 4.22.13 Muscle Loss and Deconditioning

Prolonged inactivity may reduce:
- strength;
- endurance;
- balance;
- coordination.

A shinobi immobilized for weeks should not return at full physical performance immediately.

This decline should be moderate and targeted, not punitive micromanagement.

## 4.22.14 Rehabilitation Restores Function

Rehabilitation may include:
- range-of-motion work;
- strength rebuilding;
- balance training;
- motor retraining;
- endurance rebuilding;
- chakra-control retraining;
- technique adaptation.

Recovery of combat function may therefore require active training.

## 4.22.15 Rehabilitation Uses Existing Skill and Growth Systems

Rehabilitation should not create an entirely separate progression engine.

Ruleset 1 can handle:
- practice;
- relearning;
- temporary effective reductions;
- restoration toward baseline.

Ruleset 4.22 defines the medical constraints on that training.

## 4.22.16 Return to Activity Should Be Graduated

A character may progress through:

### Protected Rest
No meaningful stress on the injured structure.

### Light Activity
Basic movement and daily function.

### Controlled Training
Low-risk practice and rehabilitation.

### Restricted Duty
Some mission or combat activity with clear limitations.

### Full Training
Normal high-intensity practice resumes.

### Full Duty
No meaningful medical restriction remains.

The exact stages depend on injury.

## 4.22.17 Return-to-Duty Is Not Binary

A shinobi may be:
- able to walk;
- unable to sprint;
- able to perform Ninjutsu;
- unable to perform full taijutsu;
- fit for desk/intelligence work;
- unfit for frontline missions.

Duty readiness should be role-specific.

## 4.22.18 Mission Demands Matter

A character may be medically capable of one mission but not another.

Examples:
- escort mission may be acceptable;
- high-speed pursuit may not be;
- reconnaissance may be possible;
- heavy combat mission may be unsafe.

Return-to-duty decisions should compare current function against expected mission demands.

## 4.22.19 Training Too Early Can Slow Recovery

Excessive activity may:
- reopen wounds;
- worsen inflammation;
- destabilize repairs;
- prolong healing;
- create compensatory injuries.

This gives characters meaningful reason to respect recovery limits.

## 4.22.20 Controlled Stress Can Help Recovery

Too little activity can also delay functional restoration.

At the correct stage, controlled loading may improve:
- strength;
- mobility;
- tissue adaptation;
- coordination.

Rehabilitation should therefore balance protection and progressive use.

## 4.22.21 Reinjury Risk

Partially healed tissue may be more vulnerable than healthy tissue.

Reinjury risk depends on:
- healing stage;
- activity intensity;
- stabilization;
- rehabilitation quality;
- previous structural damage.

A repaired injury should not be treated as fully durable before recovery is complete.

## 4.22.22 Reinjury Can Reset or Worsen Recovery

Reinjury may:
- reopen wounds;
- refracture bone;
- tear healing tissue;
- destroy surgical repair;
- create new nerve or vessel damage.

Recovery time should update from the new actual state rather than simply adding a fixed penalty.

## 4.22.23 Compensatory Movement Can Create New Problems

If a character avoids using an injured limb, they may overload:
- opposite leg;
- hip;
- shoulder;
- back.

This should be modeled only when prolonged compensation becomes relevant.

## 4.22.24 Recovery Quality

A useful broad summary may include:

### Optimal
Healing and rehabilitation are progressing as well as expected.

### Adequate
Recovery is progressing normally with minor limitations.

### Delayed
Recovery is slower because of complications, poor treatment, overuse, or physiology.

### Complicated
Additional medical problems are interfering with healing.

### Plateaued
Further natural improvement is unlikely without additional intervention.

These describe recovery trajectory rather than injury severity.

## 4.22.25 Healing Can Plateau

Some injuries may stop improving naturally.

Examples:
- permanent nerve loss;
- chronic joint instability;
- severe scar restriction;
- missing tissue;
- lasting chakra-network damage.

At this point, additional improvement may require:
- surgery;
- medical ninjutsu;
- prosthetic adaptation;
- specialized rehabilitation.

## 4.22.26 Chronic Conditions

An injury may leave persistent states such as:
- chronic pain;
- weakness;
- limited range of motion;
- reduced sensation;
- instability;
- reduced endurance;
- recurring swelling;
- chakra-control impairment.

These become long-term character state.

## 4.22.27 Permanent Impairment

Permanent impairment may result when:
- tissue cannot be restored;
- treatment arrives too late;
- nerve damage persists;
- anatomy is lost;
- complications become irreversible.

Permanent does not necessarily mean completely disabling.

A character may adapt.

## 4.22.28 Adaptation

Characters can learn to compensate for lasting limitations.

Examples:
- altered fighting stance;
- one-handed seals;
- different weapon style;
- sensory substitution;
- prosthetic use;
- avoiding certain movements.

Adaptation should use normal skill-development rules.

## 4.22.29 Scars and Cosmetic Recovery

Scars may:
- fade;
- remain visible;
- restrict movement;
- alter sensation.

Cosmetic healing may continue after functional recovery.

Most scars should not create mechanical effects unless structurally relevant.

## 4.22.30 Psychological Recovery Is Separate

Fear, grief, trauma, confidence, or avoidance after severe injury may matter to character behavior.

These should not be automatically generated as universal penalties from every severe wound.

Detailed psychological systems belong elsewhere.

## 4.22.31 Nutrition and Hydration

Adequate:
- food;
- hydration;
- sleep

support recovery.

Poor conditions may slow healing and worsen fatigue.

These factors should matter mainly during:
- prolonged missions;
- poverty/resource scarcity;
- major recovery.

Routine healthy recovery can assume adequate basic care.

## 4.22.32 Sleep and Recovery

Sleep supports:
- fatigue recovery;
- cognitive function;
- tissue recovery.

Sleep deprivation may slow recovery and reduce training quality.

The system should abstract this unless sleep becomes a meaningful constraint.

## 4.22.33 Illness and Infection Can Delay Healing

Recovery may be slowed by:
- infection;
- disease;
- immune compromise;
- toxin exposure.

These should update the recovery trajectory rather than create arbitrary extra days.

## 4.22.34 Age and Physiology

Recovery rate may vary with:
- age;
- general health;
- bloodline physiology;
- regeneration;
- body modification.

Age should influence recovery plausibly without making older characters automatically ineffective.

## 4.22.35 Exceptional Regeneration

Regenerative physiology may:
- shorten healing time;
- reduce permanent damage;
- restore tissue that ordinary bodies cannot;
- reduce rehabilitation needs.

Its limits must be explicitly defined.

## 4.22.36 Regeneration May Have Costs

Possible costs include:
- chakra use;
- caloric demand;
- exhaustion;
- shortened resource reserves;
- instability;
- long-term biological consequences.

The relevant ability defines the cost.

## 4.22.37 Medical Leave

Characters may be placed on:
- bed rest;
- restricted training;
- restricted duty;
- medical leave.

These states should derive from actual functional limitations rather than arbitrary timers.

## 4.22.38 Ignoring Medical Restrictions

A character may choose to:
- train;
- deploy;
- fight

before clearance.

The system should allow this while applying:
- reduced performance;
- reinjury risk;
- delayed recovery;
- possible complication.

Medical advice restricts safety, not player agency.

## 4.22.39 Recovery and Rank Progression

Time spent recovering may reduce:
- training opportunities;
- mission participation;
- exam eligibility;
- promotion opportunities.

It should not directly reduce rank or learned mastery unless the injury actually affects performance.

## 4.22.40 Long Inactivity and Skill Rust

Very long inactivity may reduce immediate sharpness in:
- timing;
- conditioning;
- coordination.

This should usually be recoverable quickly through resumed practice.

Core learned mastery should not vanish after ordinary medical leave.

## 4.22.41 Technique Retraining After Injury

A changed body may require techniques to be relearned or modified.

Examples:
- altered hand function;
- prosthetic limb;
- reduced shoulder range;
- changed balance;
- lost eye.

The character may retain conceptual mastery while needing new execution patterns.

## 4.22.42 Recovery Milestones

For significant injuries, useful milestones may include:
- medically stable;
- pain controlled;
- independent movement;
- basic function restored;
- controlled training cleared;
- restricted duty cleared;
- full combat training cleared;
- full duty cleared.

Milestones are more useful than a single “days remaining” timer.

## 4.22.43 Prognosis

A medic may estimate:
- likely healing time;
- expected function;
- risk of permanent damage;
- rehabilitation needs.

Prognosis is uncertain and may change with:
- complications;
- treatment response;
- reinjury.

## 4.22.44 Prognosis Knowledge Is Observer-Specific

The engine may know the actual recovery trajectory more precisely than the patient.

A medic provides an estimate based on:
- diagnosis;
- experience;
- current progress.

Unexpected healing or complications can revise that estimate.

## 4.22.45 Time Skips

When the game advances days or weeks, the engine should update:
- structural healing;
- pain;
- swelling;
- functional capability;
- rehabilitation progress;
- complications;
- medical restrictions.

The simulation should never simply erase injuries because a time skip occurred.

## 4.22.46 Recovery Events

Meaningful recovery may generate events such as:
- wound reopened;
- medical clearance;
- rehabilitation milestone;
- complication detected;
- prosthetic fitted;
- chronic deficit confirmed.

Routine uneventful healing can remain compressed.

## 4.22.47 Return-to-Combat Capacity

Returning to combat should consider:
- current injury;
- functional domains;
- fatigue;
- pain;
- mission intensity;
- medical stability.

A character may be legally or medically cleared while still not being at peak performance.

## 4.22.48 Full Healing

An injury is fully healed when:
- the structural damage no longer requires ordinary treatment;
- expected recovery has stabilized;
- no active medical complication remains.

Full healing does not necessarily mean:
- no scar;
- no chronic consequence;
- complete restoration of prior performance.

## 4.22.49 Recovery Record

A meaningful recovery record should be able to store:
- injury;
- treatment received;
- current healing phase;
- structural progress;
- functional progress;
- pain;
- rehabilitation activity;
- restrictions;
- reinjury risk;
- complications;
- prognosis;
- expected next milestone;
- chronic/permanent outcome if known.

## 4.22.50 Suggested Recovery Pipeline

1. Start from the actual post-treatment injury state.
2. Determine tissue-specific healing requirements.
3. Apply treatment quality and medical-ninjutsu effects.
4. Apply physiology, age, rest, nutrition, and complications.
5. Advance elapsed time.
6. Update structural healing.
7. Update pain, swelling, and vulnerability.
8. Update functional capability.
9. Apply rehabilitation progress.
10. Reassess activity restrictions and reinjury risk.
11. Generate meaningful milestones or complications.
12. Continue until healed, plateaued, or converted into chronic/permanent state.

## 4.22.51 Compression

Routine recovery may be summarized over days or weeks.

The system should zoom in when:
- the player wants to return early;
- rehabilitation choices matter;
- reinjury occurs;
- complications develop;
- permanent consequences are being determined.

## Core Design Rule

Recovery should answer:

**What has physically healed, what function has returned, what remains vulnerable, and what must happen before this character can safely perform at their previous level?**

Healing is not a timer that restores a character to default.
It is a persistent process that can succeed fully, partially, slowly, or with lasting consequences.
