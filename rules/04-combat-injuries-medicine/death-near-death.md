# 4.12 — Death & Near-Death States

Status: Provisional design section pending explicit approval.

This section defines the boundary between severe injury, near-death, reversible vital failure, and death.

It must preserve the principles established in Sections 4.8–4.11:
- death must have a physiological cause;
- near-death must emerge from actual deterioration;
- rescue windows depend on the injury and treatment available;
- important characters do not receive hidden plot immunity;
- not every unconscious or critically injured character is dying.

## 4.12.1 Death Is a Physiological Outcome

Death should occur when injuries or ongoing deterioration produce irreversible failure of vital function.

Examples include:
- catastrophic destruction of the brain;
- complete destruction of critical vital organs;
- unsurvivable vascular injury;
- irreversible loss of circulation;
- irreversible loss of breathing with prolonged oxygen deprivation;
- overwhelming systemic trauma;
- technique-specific lethal effects.

The system should be able to explain what killed the character.

## 4.12.2 Do Not Use Universal Death Saves

There should be no generic rule such as:
- three failed death saves;
- survive for X rounds at zero health;
- random chance to remain alive after any lethal injury.

Uncertainty may still exist in borderline cases, but survival should be constrained by:
- injury type;
- vital function;
- elapsed time;
- treatment;
- physiology;
- exceptional abilities.

## 4.12.3 Near-Death Is Not Death

A near-death state exists when one or more vital systems are failing but meaningful rescue remains possible.

Examples:
- catastrophic hemorrhage with residual circulation;
- respiratory failure before irreversible oxygen injury;
- profound shock;
- severe neurological decline;
- cardiac or circulatory arrest within a potentially reversible window;
- toxin-induced collapse with available antidote or treatment.

Near-death is a medical emergency, not a guarantee of death.

## 4.12.4 Near-Death Requires a Cause

The engine should record:
- which vital system is failing;
- what injury or process caused it;
- how advanced the failure is;
- what interventions could plausibly reverse it;
- how time-sensitive rescue is.

There should be no abstract "near-death meter" detached from physiology.

## 4.12.5 Potentially Reversible Vital Failure

Some failures may be reversible if treated quickly enough.

Examples may include:
- severe airway obstruction;
- treatable respiratory arrest;
- profound blood loss before irreversible collapse;
- certain electrical effects;
- toxin-induced paralysis;
- temporary technique-induced suppression.

Whether reversal is possible depends on:
- elapsed time;
- tissue damage;
- treatment capability;
- environment;
- physiology.

## 4.12.6 Irreversible Injury

Some injuries may be immediately beyond recovery under the setting's currently available medicine.

Examples:
- catastrophic destruction of the brain;
- complete destruction of essential anatomy with no viable regenerative mechanism;
- extreme total-body destruction;
- prolonged irreversible oxygen deprivation;
- certain explicit lethal techniques.

The exact boundary may vary with exceptional medical technology or abilities.

## 4.12.7 Setting Capability Matters

Naruto-world medicine exceeds ordinary medicine in some areas.

Therefore, whether an injury is survivable depends on what resources actually exist:
- medical ninjutsu;
- specialist surgeons;
- regeneration;
- transplants;
- prosthetics;
- sealing techniques;
- blood replacement;
- clan-specific physiology;
- jinchūriki regeneration;
- exceptional research facilities.

The system should not assume modern real-world limits when the setting explicitly provides stronger options.

## 4.12.8 Exceptional Medicine Does Not Make Death Meaningless

Advanced healing should still have limits.

Possible limits include:
- destroyed tissue that cannot be reconstructed;
- insufficient time;
- excessive chakra cost;
- lack of specialist skill;
- irreversible neurological damage;
- incompatible body modifications;
- blood loss beyond recoverability;
- unavailable donor tissue;
- technique-specific restrictions.

Medical ninjutsu should increase survivability without making lethal combat consequence-free.

## 4.12.9 Instant Death Should Be Rare but Real

Some circumstances may be sufficiently catastrophic that no meaningful rescue window exists.

Examples:
- massive destruction of the brain;
- complete decapitation without a specific survival ability;
- total destruction of critical vital structures;
- overwhelmingly destructive techniques.

Instant death should follow actual anatomy and ability rules rather than a generic "instant-kill tag."

## 4.12.10 Lethal Technique Does Not Mean Guaranteed Death

A technique designed to kill may still fail to do so because of:
- partial contact;
- defensive mitigation;
- armor;
- poor placement;
- interruption;
- exceptional physiology;
- rapid treatment.

"Lethal" describes intended consequence and capability, not automatic resolution.

## 4.12.11 Critical Injury Can Become Fatal Through Delay

A survivable injury can become lethal if:
- bleeding continues;
- breathing remains impaired;
- treatment is delayed;
- transport worsens the injury;
- toxin progresses;
- shock deepens;
- complications develop.

This makes time-to-treatment meaningful.

## 4.12.12 Death From Accumulated Trauma

A character may die without any single individually fatal wound.

Multiple injuries can combine to produce:
- overwhelming blood loss;
- respiratory compromise;
- shock;
- systemic failure;
- exhaustion plus trauma;
- inability to compensate.

The system should evaluate combined physiology rather than requiring one "killing blow."

## 4.12.13 The Killing Blow Is Historical, Not Always Physiological

The final attack is not necessarily the sole cause of death.

Example:
- several wounds cause severe blood loss;
- a final minor exertion triggers collapse.

The death record should preserve the causal chain when meaningful.

## 4.12.14 Consciousness Does Not Define Life or Death

A character may be:
- conscious and dying;
- unconscious but stable;
- unconscious and near-death;
- awake during severe deterioration.

Loss of consciousness alone should never determine death.

## 4.12.15 Cardiac or Circulatory Arrest

Circulatory arrest should be treated as a critical state with a narrow potential rescue window where setting-appropriate treatment exists.

Outcome depends on:
- cause;
- elapsed time;
- oxygenation;
- trauma severity;
- available resuscitation;
- medical ninjutsu;
- neurological injury.

The system should not assume every arrest is instantly irreversible.

## 4.12.16 Respiratory Arrest

Respiratory arrest may remain reversible for a limited time if:
- circulation continues;
- the airway can be restored;
- oxygen deprivation has not caused irreversible injury.

The exact window should not be exposed as a universal countdown.

## 4.12.17 Brain Death / Irreversible Neurological Destruction

Irreversible destruction of the brain or equivalent central neurological function should normally count as death unless an explicit setting ability overrides it.

This is distinct from:
- unconsciousness;
- coma;
- temporary neurological suppression.

## 4.12.18 Death Confirmation

Characters may not always know immediately whether someone is dead.

Confirmation may require:
- checking breathing;
- circulation;
- neurological response;
- medical sensing;
- diagnostic ninjutsu.

A non-medical observer may misjudge profound unconsciousness or near-death as death.

## 4.12.19 Feigned Death

Characters may intentionally simulate death through:
- stillness;
- suppressed breathing;
- chakra suppression;
- blood or injury deception;
- techniques.

Ruleset 2 handles deception and detection.

Ruleset 4.12 establishes that death is an observable medical state, not automatic omniscient information.

## 4.12.20 Execution / Coup de Grâce

An incapacitated target may be deliberately killed if the attacker has:
- access;
- time;
- a valid lethal method;
- no successful intervention.

This should not require a normal combat exchange if the victim has no meaningful ability to resist.

However, armor, physiology, rescue attempts, hidden abilities, or uncertain anatomy may still matter.

## 4.12.21 Protecting a Dying Character

Allies may intervene by:
- controlling bleeding;
- restoring airway or breathing;
- carrying the casualty;
- shielding the medic;
- using medical ninjutsu;
- summoning specialist help;
- delaying enemies.

Near-death therefore creates tactical objectives during combat.

## 4.12.22 Treatment Under Fire

A character may be medically salvageable but tactically inaccessible.

Survival therefore depends not only on medical capability but on:
- battlefield control;
- extraction;
- protection;
- distance to care;
- available chakra;
- time.

This creates an important bridge between combat and medicine.

## 4.12.23 Rescue Window Is Dynamic

A rescue window can shorten or lengthen.

It may shorten because of:
- continued bleeding;
- exertion;
- further injury;
- loss of airway;
- delayed treatment.

It may lengthen because of:
- stabilization;
- cooling where relevant;
- blood replacement;
- medical ninjutsu;
- suppression of toxin;
- reduced exertion.

There is no universal fixed timer.

## 4.12.24 Recovery After Near-Death Is Not Immediate

Successful rescue from near-death should usually leave:
- severe fatigue;
- ongoing injury;
- neurological or systemic consequences;
- recovery time;
- possible permanent damage.

"Saved from death" should not mean "returned to full combat capability."

## 4.12.25 Failed Resuscitation

Treatment attempts can fail.

Failure may result from:
- excessive delay;
- insufficient skill;
- lack of resources;
- irreversible tissue damage;
- overwhelming injury;
- incorrect diagnosis;
- interrupted treatment.

Ruleset 2 resolves uncertain treatment execution when appropriate.

## 4.12.26 Resurrection Is Outside Ordinary Medicine

True resurrection, soul restoration, reanimation, or post-death revival should not be treated as ordinary medical care.

If such abilities exist, they belong to specific jutsu, sealing, forbidden-technique, or world systems with explicit costs and limitations.

Death should otherwise remain persistent.

## 4.12.27 Death and Clones / Constructs

The destruction of:
- clones;
- puppets;
- summoned constructs;
- artificial bodies

does not necessarily follow ordinary biological death rules.

Their relevant ability records should define:
- what counts as destruction;
- whether consciousness persists elsewhere;
- what information returns to the user;
- whether the entity can be reconstructed.

Ruleset 4.12 governs biological death unless a specific exception applies.

## 4.12.28 Death and Jinchūriki / Regeneration

Exceptional regenerative or host physiology may:
- extend rescue windows;
- restore circulation;
- repair severe trauma;
- prevent some forms of death.

These abilities must explicitly state what they can and cannot reverse.

They should modify the shared death framework rather than grant vague immortality.

## 4.12.29 Death and Soul-Based Effects

If a technique attacks:
- soul;
- life force;
- spiritual essence;
- another metaphysical survival component,

its Ruleset 3 record must define how that interacts with ordinary biological survival.

Ruleset 4 should not assume all lethal effects are purely anatomical.

## 4.12.30 Death Record

When a character dies, the persistent record should be able to store:
- date/time;
- location;
- immediate cause;
- underlying injuries;
- contributing conditions;
- whether treatment was attempted;
- whether death was witnessed or confirmed;
- relevant killer/attacker attribution where known;
- unresolved uncertainty if cause is not known.

This supports later mission, legal, relationship, and world-simulation systems.

## 4.12.31 Cause of Death Can Be Uncertain

Characters may not know exactly why someone died.

Example:
- corpse shows several wounds;
- internal cause unclear;
- poison possible.

Medical examination may later establish cause more precisely.

The engine may preserve hidden truth while observers hold incomplete conclusions.

## 4.12.32 Death Should Affect the World

Death may alter:
- missions;
- team composition;
- relationships;
- reputation;
- inheritance;
- political consequences;
- investigations;
- village manpower;
- future story events.

Those consequences belong to later systems, but Ruleset 4 must persist the event accurately.

## 4.12.33 NPC Death Is Not Automatically Abstracted Away

Important NPCs and ordinary NPCs follow the same lethal rules.

Simulation compression may summarize background deaths, but it should not secretly protect named characters or kill disposable NPCs without applying the same underlying principles.

## 4.12.34 Player Character Death

The player character should be subject to the same physiological death rules as others.

A lethal outcome should not be silently reversed because the character is player-controlled.

If the broader game permits continuation after player death, that belongs to game-structure or legacy systems, not to combat physiology.

## 4.12.35 Borderline Survival

Some cases may remain genuinely uncertain.

Examples:
- severe but not clearly irreversible neurological injury;
- borderline blood-loss state;
- uncertain internal destruction;
- treatment near the edge of its capability.

Ruleset 2 may resolve such uncertainty using:
- injury facts;
- treatment quality;
- elapsed time;
- physiology;
- medical skill;
- exceptional abilities.

The outcome should remain constrained by the established state.

## 4.12.36 No Plot-Based Survival Modifier

Narrative importance, fame, rank, relationship to the player, or future story usefulness should never improve survival probability by themselves.

Only in-world factors may do so.

## 4.12.37 No Arbitrary Random Death From Nonlethal States

Likewise, characters should not randomly die from injuries that are physiologically stable and nonlethal unless a plausible complication or new event occurs.

The system should preserve danger without inventing drama.

## 4.12.38 Suggested Survival-State Model

A useful medical-state ladder may be:

### Stable
No immediate vital failure.

### Unstable
Condition is worsening or risks significant deterioration.

### Critical
Major threat to survival; immediate treatment is important.

### Near-Death
Vital systems are actively failing; rescue remains possible.

### Vital Failure
Breathing, circulation, or equivalent function has ceased or become profoundly inadequate; rescue window may be extremely narrow.

### Dead
Vital failure has become irreversible under currently available capabilities.

These states summarize medical danger.
They do not replace actual injury and deterioration records.

## 4.12.39 State Transitions Must Have Causes

A character should not move from Critical to Near-Death because a generic timer expired.

The transition should be caused by something such as:
- blood loss crossing a functional threshold;
- airway becoming fully obstructed;
- neurological swelling worsening;
- circulation collapsing;
- toxin reaching a critical systemic effect.

Likewise, treatment should reverse the underlying process before the state improves.

## 4.12.40 Minimum Near-Death Record

For a character in near-death or vital failure, the engine should be able to track:
- failing vital system(s);
- cause;
- current reversibility;
- deterioration rate;
- treatment already performed;
- available rescue options;
- major contraindications;
- approximate rescue urgency;
- consciousness;
- circulation;
- breathing;
- neurological status where relevant.

## 4.12.41 Compression

For low-detail simulation, the engine may summarize:
- killed instantly by catastrophic trauma;
- critically wounded and died before treatment;
- rescued from near-death;
- died during evacuation.

If the death becomes important, the simulation should retain or reconstruct the causal chain.

## Core Design Rule

Death should occur when a character's vital systems become irreversibly unable to sustain life under the capabilities currently available to them.

Near-death should mean:

**The character is not yet irreversibly dead, but rescue now depends on time, treatment, and the specific failing system.**

No death saves. No boss immunity. No random fatality detached from physiology.
