# 4.20 — Medical Ninjutsu

Status: Provisional design section pending explicit approval.

This section defines chakra-based medical techniques used to diagnose, stabilize, repair, regenerate, and support living tissue.

Medical ninjutsu should be powerful because it:
- expands what injuries are treatable;
- reduces treatment time;
- improves precision;
- can directly manipulate tissue and chakra pathways;
- can stabilize otherwise fatal injuries.

It should not function as universal instant healing.

## 4.20.1 Medical Ninjutsu Is Skilled Biological Chakra Manipulation

Medical ninjutsu applies chakra with exceptional precision to living systems.

Possible functions include:
- diagnosis;
- bleeding control;
- tissue repair;
- wound closure;
- circulation support;
- chakra-network repair;
- toxin treatment;
- surgery assistance;
- regeneration support.

Its effectiveness depends on what the technique actually does.

## 4.20.2 Medical Ninjutsu Requires Exceptional Chakra Control

Medical techniques should generally demand high Chakra Control because small errors can:
- damage tissue;
- close wounds incorrectly;
- worsen bleeding;
- disrupt nerves;
- waste chakra;
- interfere with natural healing.

Ruleset 3 defines Chakra Control progression and technique requirements.

Ruleset 4.20 defines the medical consequences of that precision.

## 4.20.3 Medical Skill and Chakra Control Are Different

A practitioner may possess:
- excellent medical knowledge but mediocre chakra control;
- excellent chakra control but poor medical knowledge.

Both matter.

Medical knowledge answers:
**What should be done?**

Chakra control answers:
**Can the user manipulate the body precisely enough to do it?**

Neither should replace the other.

## 4.20.4 Technique Mastery Matters

Mastery can improve:
- chakra efficiency;
- treatment speed;
- precision;
- ability to treat complex structures;
- reliability under pressure;
- ability to multitask diagnosis and treatment.

An advanced medic should not merely use the same technique with a larger healing number.

## 4.20.5 Medical Ninjutsu Must Have Defined Functions

Each technique should specify what it can actually accomplish.

Examples:
- accelerate clotting;
- close superficial wounds;
- reconnect soft tissue;
- repair muscle;
- restore nerve continuity;
- stabilize organ damage;
- support circulation;
- clear chakra blockage.

Avoid generic:
“Restores 50 health.”

## 4.20.6 Diagnosis Often Precedes Treatment

Medical ninjutsu may require the user to identify:
- injury location;
- tissue involved;
- bleeding source;
- damaged chakra pathways;
- foreign material.

Treating without sufficient information may:
- waste chakra;
- repair the wrong structure;
- fail to stop deterioration.

Diagnostic techniques and medical expertise therefore remain important.

## 4.20.7 Medical Ninjutsu Can Also Diagnose

Some techniques may sense:
- tissue damage;
- internal bleeding;
- chakra disruption;
- organ function;
- foreign objects;
- poison effects.

The exact information revealed must be defined by the technique.

No generic medical scan automatically reveals everything.

## 4.20.8 Healing Is Not One Process

Medical ninjutsu may achieve different levels of intervention.

### Stabilization
Stops dangerous deterioration.

### Repair
Restores damaged tissue enough to recover normal structure or function.

### Accelerated Healing
Speeds the body's natural recovery.

### Regeneration
Replaces or reconstructs tissue that the body could not ordinarily restore quickly.

These should remain distinct.

## 4.20.9 Stabilization Is Easier Than Full Repair

Stopping bleeding or temporarily supporting tissue should usually be easier than:
- restoring an organ;
- reconnecting nerves;
- reconstructing destroyed muscle;
- rebuilding complex anatomy.

This gives lower-level medical techniques meaningful value.

## 4.20.10 Wound Closure

Closing skin is not necessarily equivalent to treating the wound.

A medic must consider:
- contamination;
- internal bleeding;
- damaged vessels;
- foreign objects;
- deeper tissue.

Closing the surface too early may conceal an untreated problem.

## 4.20.11 Bleeding Control

Medical ninjutsu may control bleeding by:
- constricting vessels;
- sealing damaged vessels;
- accelerating clotting;
- directly repairing the vessel.

These methods differ in:
- speed;
- durability;
- chakra cost;
- required precision.

## 4.20.12 Tissue Repair

Repair techniques may target:
- skin;
- muscle;
- tendon;
- ligament;
- vessel;
- nerve;
- organ tissue.

Complexity should increase with:
- anatomical precision;
- depth;
- amount destroyed;
- tissue specialization.

## 4.20.13 Bone Treatment

Medical ninjutsu may:
- support alignment;
- accelerate healing;
- repair fractures;
- reinforce healing bone.

A badly displaced or shattered fracture may still require:
- repositioning;
- surgical intervention;
- prolonged stabilization.

Healing chakra should not automatically align badly positioned anatomy.

## 4.20.14 Tendon and Ligament Repair

These structures require:
- accurate reconnection;
- proper tension;
- alignment.

Poor repair may leave:
- weakness;
- instability;
- reduced range.

This makes precision more important than raw chakra output.

## 4.20.15 Nerve Repair

Nerve treatment should be among the more difficult medical tasks.

Possible challenges:
- tiny structures;
- precise alignment;
- long recovery;
- incomplete regeneration.

High-level medical ninjutsu may improve outcomes that ordinary medicine cannot.

## 4.20.16 Organ Repair

Organ injuries may require:
- hemorrhage control;
- tissue reconstruction;
- restoring circulation;
- removing damaged tissue;
- monitoring systemic function.

Different organs may have different treatment difficulty.

## 4.20.17 Brain and Central Nervous System Injury

Neurological repair should have especially strict limits.

Medical ninjutsu may:
- reduce swelling;
- repair limited tissue damage;
- support function.

But catastrophic destruction or prolonged irreversible neurological failure should remain beyond ordinary healing unless a specific exceptional ability says otherwise.

## 4.20.18 Eye Repair

Eye injuries may require specialized techniques because of:
- delicate anatomy;
- optic nerve involvement;
- dōjutsu structures.

Repairing tissue does not automatically restore a destroyed or removed dōjutsu if its unique biological structures are gone.

Specific bloodline rules apply.

## 4.20.19 Chakra-Network Repair

Medical ninjutsu may treat:
- disrupted tenketsu;
- blocked pathways;
- damaged chakra channels;
- local control failure.

This may require different expertise from ordinary tissue healing.

A medic may be highly skilled in physical trauma but less skilled in chakra-network pathology.

## 4.20.20 Chakra-System Treatment Does Not Refill Reserves

Repairing chakra pathways is not the same as restoring spent chakra.

Likewise, restoring chakra reserves does not repair damaged pathways.

Ruleset 3 owns chakra reserves.
Ruleset 4.20 owns medical repair of the system that carries and controls chakra.

## 4.20.21 Poison Treatment

Medical ninjutsu may:
- identify toxin effects;
- slow distribution;
- support organs;
- extract or neutralize toxin;
- assist antidote action.

The exact effect depends on the poison and technique.

Medical ninjutsu should not automatically neutralize every toxin.

## 4.20.22 Infection and Disease

Some medical techniques may:
- support immune response;
- remove damaged tissue;
- treat infection;
- accelerate recovery.

Disease-specific effects belong to relevant medical records.

Medical ninjutsu should not be assumed to cure every disease.

## 4.20.23 Foreign Objects

Healing around:
- shrapnel;
- weapons;
- debris;
- poison needles

may be dangerous.

The medic may need to remove or account for the foreign object before completing repair.

## 4.20.24 Tissue Loss Matters

A clean cut with both tissue surfaces preserved is different from:
- crushed tissue;
- burned-away tissue;
- missing tissue.

Repair becomes harder as usable biological structure is lost.

## 4.20.25 Regeneration Is More Demanding Than Repair

Replacing missing tissue should generally require:
- greater chakra;
- greater skill;
- more time;
- specialized techniques;
- biological source material or exceptional physiology where applicable.

Ordinary medical ninjutsu should not casually regrow limbs.

## 4.20.26 Healing Speed Has Limits

Accelerating tissue repair does not necessarily mean:
“instant perfect recovery.”

A technique may:
- stabilize instantly;
- partially repair within minutes;
- reduce recovery from months to days;
- reduce days to hours.

The technique record should define its scale.

## 4.20.27 Tissue Can Be Repaired Before Function Fully Returns

Even after structural repair, the character may still have:
- weakness;
- pain;
- swelling;
- fatigue;
- neurological recovery needs;
- rehabilitation.

Medical repair and immediate combat readiness are separate.

## 4.20.28 Emergency Healing Versus Definitive Healing

Under combat pressure, a medic may choose:
- fast stabilization;
- rough temporary repair;
- slower complete treatment.

Emergency treatment may preserve life while leaving:
- scar tissue;
- reduced function;
- need for later surgery.

## 4.20.29 Treatment Precision and Speed Trade Off

Working faster may:
- increase chakra consumption;
- increase difficulty;
- reduce precision.

A medic with time may perform safer, more efficient treatment.

This creates meaningful emergency-treatment decisions.

## 4.20.30 Chakra Cost Should Scale With Medical Work

Cost may increase with:
- tissue volume;
- depth;
- complexity;
- severity;
- regeneration;
- treatment speed;
- precision required;
- patient instability.

A small cut should not cost what organ reconstruction costs.

## 4.20.31 Chakra Efficiency Matters

Experienced medical-nin may achieve the same result with less chakra because they:
- waste less;
- target tissue more precisely;
- avoid unnecessary regeneration;
- sequence treatment correctly.

This gives mastery a strong efficiency role.

## 4.20.32 Patient Condition Affects Difficulty

Treatment may become harder when:
- circulation is failing;
- tissue is badly crushed;
- the patient is moving;
- multiple injuries interact;
- poison continues progressing;
- the battlefield is unsafe.

A critically unstable patient is not simply a larger healing-number requirement.

## 4.20.33 Treatment Under Fire

Medical ninjutsu may require:
- touch;
- concentration;
- steady hand control;
- sustained chakra.

Enemy pressure can:
- interrupt treatment;
- force the medic to defend;
- degrade precision.

Allies may need to create a protected medical window.

## 4.20.34 Interruption

An interrupted medical technique may:
- preserve completed repair;
- leave partial stabilization;
- waste chakra;
- create risk if delicate work is interrupted.

The technique should specify whether interruption is especially dangerous.

## 4.20.35 Multiple Patients

A medic's limiting resources may include:
- chakra;
- time;
- concentration;
- supplies.

Treating many casualties therefore creates triage pressure even for powerful healers.

## 4.20.36 Self-Healing

A medical-nin may treat themselves if:
- conscious;
- able to reach or target the injury;
- capable of maintaining control;
- not prevented by the injury.

Self-treatment may be harder because of:
- pain;
- blood loss;
- one-handed limitations;
- impaired concentration.

## 4.20.37 Healing Another Person May Be Easier Than Self-Repair

Some advanced procedures require:
- two functional hands;
- visual access;
- stable posture.

A medic with a badly injured dominant arm may know exactly how to repair themselves but lack the physical ability to execute it.

## 4.20.38 Medical Teams

Several medics may cooperate.

Possible roles:
- diagnosis;
- hemorrhage control;
- chakra support;
- tissue repair;
- anesthesia/sedation;
- monitoring.

Coordination can allow treatment beyond one practitioner's safe capacity.

## 4.20.39 Chakra Transfer and Support

One practitioner may sometimes provide:
- chakra;
- stabilization;
- support technique

while another performs precise treatment.

Ruleset 3 defines chakra transfer mechanics.

Ruleset 4.20 defines how that support changes the medical procedure.

## 4.20.40 Medical Ninjutsu Can Cause Harm

Poorly executed healing may cause:
- tissue damage;
- abnormal closure;
- nerve injury;
- internal bleeding;
- chakra-network disruption;
- excessive scar formation.

Medical techniques therefore deserve genuine failure consequences.

## 4.20.41 Failure Should Be Procedure-Specific

A failed superficial healing technique may simply:
- waste chakra;
- produce incomplete closure.

A failed nerve repair or emergency organ procedure may have much more severe consequences.

Failure severity should scale with procedure risk.

## 4.20.42 Exceptional Success Does Not Create Impossible Healing

An extraordinary result may produce:
- faster repair;
- less chakra use;
- cleaner healing;
- reduced scarring;
- better functional preservation.

It should not regenerate a missing organ if the technique fundamentally cannot regenerate organs.

Ruleset 2 cannot override technique limits.

## 4.20.43 Medical Ninjutsu and Natural Healing

Treatment may:
- directly repair tissue;
- accelerate natural recovery;
- create better conditions for healing.

The injury's later recovery record should reflect what was actually changed.

## 4.20.44 Scarring

High-quality treatment may reduce scarring.

Severe:
- burns;
- tissue loss;
- contaminated wounds;
- emergency closure

may still leave scars.

Cosmetic outcomes can matter narratively even when function is restored.

## 4.20.45 Permanent Damage

Advanced medical ninjutsu may reduce the chance of:
- permanent weakness;
- nerve deficit;
- chronic pain;
- lost mobility.

But some damage may remain permanent if:
- treatment is too late;
- tissue is destroyed;
- technique capability is insufficient.

## 4.20.46 Regenerative Physiology

Characters with innate regeneration may:
- heal faster;
- require less external repair;
- recover from greater tissue loss.

Their physiology should define:
- speed;
- chakra or resource cost;
- tissue limits;
- whether regeneration restores complex organs;
- whether it restores brain tissue;
- whether repeated regeneration has consequences.

## 4.20.47 Medical Ninjutsu and Regeneration Are Not Identical

Regeneration may be:
- autonomous;
- innate;
- technique-driven.

Medical ninjutsu is deliberate treatment.

A regenerating patient may still need medical care for:
- toxins;
- foreign objects;
- chakra-network damage;
- injuries beyond regenerative limits.

## 4.20.48 Bloodline and Transplant Medicine

Advanced care may include:
- tissue grafting;
- organ transplantation;
- eye transplantation;
- bloodline-related compatibility issues.

These procedures belong primarily to surgery/advanced care in 4.21.

Medical ninjutsu may enable them without eliminating compatibility requirements.

## 4.20.49 Resurrection Boundary

Ordinary medical ninjutsu should treat living patients and potentially very recent reversible vital failure.

True resurrection after irreversible death belongs to specific forbidden or metaphysical techniques.

Medical skill alone should not cross that boundary.

## 4.20.50 Medical Ninjutsu Technique Record

A medical technique should define:
- medical function;
- valid target tissues/systems;
- range/contact requirement;
- Chakra Control requirement;
- medical skill requirement;
- chakra cost;
- treatment speed;
- maximum severity/complexity;
- whether it stabilizes, repairs, accelerates healing, or regenerates;
- limitations;
- interruption behavior;
- failure consequences;
- mastery improvements.

## 4.20.51 Suggested Treatment Pipeline

1. Identify or diagnose the medical problem.
2. Determine the required medical objective.
3. Select a technique capable of that objective.
4. Verify access, time, chakra, and physical ability.
5. Determine tissue complexity and patient instability.
6. Apply medical skill and Chakra Control.
7. Ruleset 2 resolves genuine uncertainty.
8. Apply stabilization or repair actually supported by the technique.
9. Spend chakra and supplies.
10. Update injury, deterioration, combat capacity, and recovery state.
11. Reassess for remaining hidden or untreated problems.

## 4.20.52 Compression

Routine low-risk medical ninjutsu can be summarized when:
- the diagnosis is obvious;
- practitioner skill greatly exceeds difficulty;
- no time pressure exists.

The system should zoom in when:
- the patient is critical;
- anatomy is complex;
- chakra is limited;
- treatment is interrupted;
- permanent damage is possible.

## Core Design Rule

Medical ninjutsu should be extraordinary because it allows skilled shinobi to perform medical work that ordinary treatment cannot.

Its power should come from:
**precision + medical knowledge + chakra control + appropriate technique + sufficient time/resources**

—not from a generic healing value.
