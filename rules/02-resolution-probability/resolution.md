# Resolution pipeline

Status: Established conceptual safeguards carried through S2 sections 1–30 and summarized in its consolidation. Formula details remain [Provisional](probability.md).

## Sequence

1. **Objective:** establish actor, intended result, method, priorities and accepted costs. Infer obvious intent from context; do not decide important choices for the player.
2. **Possibility:** check the method's real prerequisites, delivery conditions, available resources, compatible source, access and relevant throughput limits; determine automatic success, automatic failure, meaningful uncertainty or direct opposition. No universal minimum success chance can make an impossible action possible. A defined overexertion or exception mechanism may change which attempts are possible; a probability bonus alone cannot.
3. **Capability:** use the skill and foundations relevant to this objective, then applicable mastery.
4. **Opposition:** use the task's objective difficulty or the active opponent's relevant capability.
5. **Context:** apply causal numerical effects, structural changes and persistent conditions. Count each underlying cause once.
6. **Probability:** use the selected, versioned provisional model only when uncertainty remains.
7. **Outcome:** resolve success and degree from the same draw, bounded by what is possible.
8. **Consequence:** apply effects, hazards and costs appropriate to the action and exposure. Costs accrue at their defined execution stages, including before failure or interruption; this conceptual sequence does not defer every expense until success.
9. **Knowledge:** update who observed, learned or misunderstood what.
10. **Progression:** emit meaningful learning evidence; Ruleset 1 computes advancement.
11. **Persistence:** preserve resulting facts, resources and the consequential resolution record.

## Objective difficulty and meaningful stakes

Difficulty is the performance required by the task, not an actor-scaled challenge rating. Stakes describe consequences. An easy action can be dangerous; a hard puzzle can be harmless.

A catastrophic-range numerical result does not invent danger. Establish a plausible outcome ceiling and floor from the actual situation. Preparation may change the method, opposition, hazards or available outcomes rather than merely adding a bonus.

Opposed actions normally use one shared resolution for the same directly contested objective. This is not a rule that only one action in an exchange can succeed. Under Ruleset 3.28, non-intercepting attacks may both land, and clashes can produce partial cancellation or residual effects. Resolve distinct uncertainties only when they materially matter, without rolling twice for the same contest or rerolling for a preferred result. See [technique-resolution interfaces](technique-resolution.md). Rank is not a general success modifier; actual abilities determine capability.

## Stable facts and exceptions

State persists until an event changes it. Injuries, restraints, equipment loss and knowledge do not reroll their existence every turn. Generate relevant undefined facts from world logic before resolving their effects; never invent a hidden counter after seeing the outcome.

An ability must specify its capability, structural, information or consequence exception. A specific rule overrides a general rule only within that scope. A special label does not grant an automatic victory.

## Check suppression and retries

Suppress checks for established facts, impossible/certain outcomes, negligible uncertainty or actions already covered by a broader resolution. Before a retry, determine what meaningfully changed: method, time, tools, knowledge, help, condition or target. An unchanged attempt does not earn another lottery ticket.

Failure means the intended objective was not achieved. New opportunities may follow causally, but failure does not automatically become success with a complication. Neither protagonist favoritism nor forced dramatic failure is valid.
