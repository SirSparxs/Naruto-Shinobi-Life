# 6.11 — Jōnin-Sensei Evaluation & Genin Probation

**Ruleset:** 6 — Academy, Training, Missions & Careers  
**Status:** Provisional

## 6.11.1 — Purpose

Post-graduation evaluation determines whether newly graduated shinobi are ready to function safely and effectively under real professional supervision.

It may evaluate:

- Teamwork
- Judgment
- Discipline
- Adaptability
- Practical competence
- Communication
- Mission mindset
- Response to pressure
- Reliability
- Ability to follow and interpret orders

This stage exists because Academy graduation and professional field readiness are related but not identical.

---

## 6.11.2 — Graduation Is Not Invalidated by Probation

A graduate who struggles during post-graduation evaluation has not necessarily "failed the Academy."

The Academy certified minimum educational readiness.

The instructor is now evaluating whether that graduate is ready for:

- A specific team
- A specific instructor
- Current field expectations
- Real mission responsibility

Failure at this stage may indicate poor fit, insufficient maturity, or need for more development rather than academic incompetence.

---

## 6.11.3 — Probation Is Optional by Village

Not every village must use formal Genin probation.

Possible systems include:

- Immediate full Genin status
- Short instructor evaluation
- Multi-week probation
- Mission-based probation
- Formal provisional Genin status
- Department-specific evaluation

Village doctrine and era determine the system.

---

## 6.11.4 — Evaluation Format

Post-graduation evaluation may take the form of:

- Practical test
- Team exercise
- Mock mission
- Survival exercise
- Controlled spar
- Objective-capture scenario
- Observation over several days
- Several supervised missions
- Interview and practical review

The classic bell test is one possible format, not a universal template.

---

## 6.11.5 — The Stated Objective May Not Be the Only Objective

An instructor may design an exercise where the visible task differs from the real evaluation focus.

For example, a test may appear to reward individual competition while actually examining teamwork.

However, such hidden objectives should be:

- Plausible
- Professionally relevant
- Consistent with instructor personality
- Fair enough to produce meaningful evidence

Secret objectives should not become arbitrary traps designed to make the player fail.

---

## 6.11.6 — Core Evaluation Domains

A Jōnin-sensei or equivalent instructor may assess:

- Individual competence
- Team cohesion
- Communication
- Initiative
- Discipline
- Judgment
- Safety
- Adaptability
- Stress response
- Objective focus
- Ability to accept correction

No single domain should automatically dominate every instructor's evaluation.

---

## 6.11.7 — Teamwork Is Often Central

Because Genin commonly operate in groups, teamwork may be one of the most important probationary competencies.

The instructor may observe:

- Whether information is shared
- Whether teammates support one another
- Whether stronger members abandon weaker ones
- Whether conflict disrupts objectives
- Whether plans adapt collaboratively
- Whether students understand their roles

A team of individually talented students may still fail probation if they cannot function together.

---

## 6.11.8 — Judgment Under Pressure

Probation should test more than rehearsed Academy skills.

The instructor may create uncertainty requiring students to decide:

- Whether to pursue or retreat
- Whether to protect a teammate
- Whether to question an order
- How to prioritize objectives
- When to conserve resources
- When to ask for help

Ruleset 2 resolves uncertain outcomes.

Ruleset 6 evaluates the professional quality of the decisions.

---

## 6.11.9 — Initiative vs Obedience

Good shinobi need both discipline and independent judgment.

The instructor should distinguish:

- Reckless disobedience
- Blind obedience
- Appropriate initiative
- Responsible questioning
- Tactical adaptation

Probation should not reward either absolute obedience or constant rule-breaking as universal virtues.

---

## 6.11.10 — Instructor Philosophy Matters

Different instructors may emphasize different qualities.

One instructor may prioritize:

- Team loyalty

Another:

- Tactical independence

Another:

- Discipline

Another:

- Survival

Another:

- Adaptability

These differences should reflect instructor personality, experience, doctrine, and assignment.

---

## 6.11.11 — Instructor Bias Still Applies

Instructor evaluation is not infallible.

A sensei may:

- Misread a student
- Favor one personality type
- Judge too harshly
- Judge too leniently
- Project past experiences
- Be influenced by clan reputation

Ruleset 6.7 and Ruleset 7 govern those distortions.

---

## 6.11.12 — Individual vs Team Evaluation

A team may pass while one member requires extra support.

Likewise, one exceptional student should not automatically carry an otherwise dysfunctional team.

Possible results include:

- Entire team passes
- Entire team fails
- Team passes with conditions
- Individual remediation
- Individual reassignment
- Team restructuring

This allows more nuanced outcomes than all-or-nothing team testing.

---

## 6.11.13 — Provisional Genin Period

Where used, probation may last beyond a single test.

During this period, Provisional Genin may:

- Train with their instructor
- Perform supervised low-risk duties
- Participate in mock missions
- Receive limited field assignments
- Undergo additional evaluation

The probation period should consume real simulation time.

---

## 6.11.14 — Supervised Mission Probation

Some villages may evaluate Genin through actual low-risk missions rather than artificial tests.

The instructor may assess:

- Punctuality
- Preparation
- Client interaction
- Mission discipline
- Team cooperation
- Reporting
- Adaptation
- Safety

This can provide more realistic evidence of professional readiness.

---

## 6.11.15 — Mission Risk During Probation

Probationary missions should normally be selected to minimize avoidable danger.

However, missions can still become unexpectedly complicated.

If risk escalates beyond reasonable expectations, the instructor should respond as a professional leader rather than continue the test at all costs.

A probationary evaluation should never justify deliberately allowing preventable catastrophic harm.

---

## 6.11.16 — Instructor Intervention

The instructor may step in when:

- A student is in serious danger
- Civilians are endangered
- The team loses control
- The exercise no longer produces useful evidence
- External circumstances invalidate the evaluation

Intervention may itself become evidence about the team's readiness.

---

## 6.11.17 — Failure Is Diagnostic

A failed probation should identify why the student or team is not ready.

Possible causes include:

- Poor teamwork
- Unsafe behavior
- Weak practical foundations
- Lack of discipline
- Poor judgment
- Immaturity
- Instructor mismatch
- Team incompatibility
- Insufficient confidence

Failure should create a development path rather than merely a rejection screen.

---

## 6.11.18 — Remediation After Failure

Possible responses include:

- Additional team exercises
- Targeted skill training
- Individual mentoring
- Temporary return to Academy instruction
- Supplemental coursework
- Counseling or discipline
- New instructor
- Team restructuring

The remedy should address the actual cause.

---

## 6.11.19 — Return to Academy

A graduate may temporarily return to Academy instruction if foundational deficiencies are severe.

This does not necessarily erase graduation.

Possible statuses include:

- Graduate in Remediation
- Provisional Genin in Remediation
- Re-enrolled for specific modules

Full loss of graduate status should be rare unless local policy explicitly allows it.

---

## 6.11.20 — Reassignment

Poor team fit may lead to reassignment rather than failure.

A student could be perfectly capable but incompatible with:

- Current teammates
- Instructor style
- Team specialization
- Operational needs

Reassignment should not automatically carry stigma.

---

## 6.11.21 — Team Restructuring

If the team itself is dysfunctional, the village may:

- Swap one member
- Change instructors
- Split the team
- Merge students with another group
- Move members into specialist tracks

This reinforces that team composition is institutional state, not a permanent party.

---

## 6.11.22 — Conditional Pass

A team may be accepted with conditions.

Examples include:

- Required teamwork drills
- Extra supervision
- Limited mission rank
- Mandatory weekly review
- Additional technique training
- Temporary leadership restrictions

Conditional acceptance allows the team to gain real experience while addressing weaknesses.

---

## 6.11.23 — Full Confirmation

A Provisional Genin becomes fully confirmed when the responsible authority determines they can safely and reliably enter normal Genin service.

Confirmation may require:

- Instructor approval
- Completion of probation
- Successful supervised mission
- Meeting remedial conditions
- Administrative registration

The exact process depends on the village.

---

## 6.11.24 — Confirmation Does Not End Training

Passing probation does not mean the instructor's teaching role ends.

A newly confirmed Genin still requires substantial development.

The transition means:

> Ready for ordinary supervised professional service.

It does not mean:

> Fully trained shinobi.

---

## 6.11.25 — Repeated Probation Failure

Repeated failure may trigger formal review.

Possible outcomes include:

- Extended remediation
- Different instructor
- Different team
- Specialist pathway
- Delayed field service
- Recommendation against active shinobi service

Permanent removal should require substantial evidence, not one bad test.

---

## 6.11.26 — Instructor Accountability

The instructor is also being evaluated indirectly.

Patterns such as:

- Repeated team failures
- Excessive student injury
- Poor development
- Biased evaluation
- Unsafe testing

may trigger administrative scrutiny.

This prevents Jōnin-sensei from having unlimited authority without institutional consequence.

---

## 6.11.27 — Evaluation Records

The engine should preserve:

- Evaluation date
- Instructor
- Team
- Test format
- Key observations
- Result
- Remediation
- Restrictions
- Confirmation date
- Significant disputes

These records may affect future assignments and recommendations.

---

## 6.11.28 — Hidden Evaluation Notes

Students may not know everything their instructor reports.

Hidden notes may include:

- Leadership potential
- Security concerns
- Personality risks
- Specialist potential
- Team compatibility concerns
- Promotion-related observations

Ruleset 13 governs what is surfaced to the player.

---

## 6.11.29 — NPC Probation

NPC graduates use the same logic.

Background probation may be resolved abstractly.

Important NPC outcomes should depend on:

- Actual ability
- Team compatibility
- Instructor quality
- Judgment
- circumstances

NPC teams should be capable of failing, restructuring, or delaying service.

---

## 6.11.30 — Governing Principle

Post-graduation probation should answer:

> **Can this graduate function safely, reliably, and professionally within the specific team and supervision structure they have been assigned?**

Academy graduation establishes educational readiness.

Probation establishes practical professional readiness.

A failure should therefore diagnose what still needs to change rather than simply declaring the graduate unworthy of becoming a shinobi.
