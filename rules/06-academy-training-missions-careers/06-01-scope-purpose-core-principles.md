# 6.1 — Scope, Purpose & Core Principles

**Ruleset:** 6 — Academy, Training, Missions & Careers  
**Status:** Provisional  
**Purpose:** Establish the governing principles for all Academy, training, mission, promotion, specialization, and career systems.

---

## 6.1.1 — Core Function

Ruleset 6 governs the structures through which characters are educated, trained, employed, evaluated, promoted, specialized, and assigned work.

It answers questions such as:

- Who may enter the Academy?
- What does an Academy student actually do?
- How does instruction affect development?
- What can a character train, when, and under whom?
- How are missions created, classified, assigned, and evaluated?
- What does shinobi rank mean institutionally?
- How are promotions decided?
- How do specialties and professional careers emerge?
- How do injury, reputation, relationships, politics, village need, and historical circumstances affect a career?
- How can a character leave, change, or return to shinobi service?

Ruleset 6 is primarily a **structure, access, opportunity, scheduling, and professional-consequence ruleset**.

---

## 6.1.2 — Ruleset Boundaries

Ruleset 6 does not independently redefine mechanics owned elsewhere.

It delegates:

- Attributes, Skills, Potential, mastery, and long-term character growth to Ruleset 1.
- Checks, probability, difficulty, uncertainty, and outcome resolution to Ruleset 2.
- Chakra, jutsu, chakra control, technique requirements, and special abilities to Ruleset 3.
- Combat, injuries, treatment, recovery, and medical consequences to Ruleset 4.
- NPC psychology, relationships, trust, mentorship bonds, rivalry, reputation between individuals, and social interaction to Ruleset 7.

Ruleset 6 may provide the **context and modifiers** that those systems consume.

Example: Ruleset 6 can determine that a character receives three hours of one-on-one instruction from an Expert teacher while well-rested and using a specialized facility. Ruleset 1 determines how that experience translates into growth; Ruleset 2 resolves uncertain training outcomes where necessary; Ruleset 3 governs the technique itself.

---

## 6.1.3 — Rank Is Not Power

Shinobi rank is an institutional designation, not a direct measurement of combat strength.

Rank may reflect:

- Professional trust
- Leadership ability
- Judgment
- Experience
- Reliability
- Mission history
- Tactical competence
- Independence
- Specialized qualifications
- Village need
- Political circumstances
- Formal examination or evaluation

A Genin may possess an ability or attribute exceeding that of some Chūnin. A Chūnin may be promoted primarily because of leadership and judgment. A Tokubetsu Jōnin may be exceptional within a narrow professional specialty while lacking the broad competence expected of a full Jōnin.

Therefore:

**No universal stat total, combat rating, or level automatically grants shinobi rank.**

---

## 6.1.4 — Professional Status Is Multidimensional

The engine must not collapse a shinobi's career into one progression number.

At minimum, the following concepts remain distinct:

- Institutional rank
- Combat capability
- Technical skill
- Mission experience
- Leadership competence
- Professional qualifications
- Specializations
- Reputation
- Trust/clearance
- Current assignment
- Service history
- Career standing

These dimensions may influence one another without becoming interchangeable.

---

## 6.1.5 — Training Is Activity, Not XP Conversion

Training represents specific actions performed for a specific amount of simulation time under specific conditions.

A character does not simply select "train" and receive a fixed quantity of progression.

Training effectiveness may depend on factors such as:

- Training method
- Targeted ability
- Current competence
- Relevant Attributes and Skills
- Difficulty of the exercise
- Teacher quality
- Teacher compatibility and availability
- Facilities and equipment
- Physical condition
- Fatigue
- Injury
- Motivation
- Time invested
- Previous practice
- Quality of feedback
- Opportunity to apply the skill
- Environmental conditions

The same hour of training can produce very different results for different characters or circumstances.

---

## 6.1.6 — Time Is a Real Resource

Academy attendance, missions, travel, training, recovery, sleep, employment, relationships, and personal activity all consume simulation time.

A character cannot meaningfully perform unlimited training simply because the player wishes to grind.

Choosing one activity can create opportunity cost elsewhere.

This principle is essential for making:

- Downtime meaningful
- Training choices meaningful
- Career assignments consequential
- Relationships compete naturally for time
- Injury and recovery matter
- Long journeys matter
- Age and historical progression matter

---

## 6.1.7 — Access Must Exist In-World

Being mechanically capable of learning or performing something does not automatically grant access to it.

Training, careers, missions, techniques, facilities, teachers, documents, and qualifications may require believable access through:

- Institutional permission
- Rank
- Clearance
- Employment
- Clan membership
- Personal relationships
- Teacher willingness
- Geography
- Money or resources
- Reputation
- Recommendation
- Prior qualifications
- Village need
- Historical circumstance

Conversely, unusual opportunities can arise when the world plausibly creates them.

The engine must not invent arbitrary restrictions merely to slow the player, nor remove established restrictions merely to accommodate the player.

---

## 6.1.8 — Requirements and Outcomes Are Different

Meeting minimum eligibility requirements means a character may be considered; it does not guarantee success.

Examples:

- Academy eligibility does not guarantee admission.
- Graduation requirements do not guarantee exceptional evaluations.
- Meeting promotion criteria does not guarantee promotion.
- Being rank-eligible for a mission does not guarantee assignment.
- Possessing prerequisites for a technique does not guarantee successful learning.
- Applying for a specialized department does not guarantee selection.
- Being talented does not guarantee access to a suitable teacher.

Whenever selection involves uncertainty, institutional judgment, competition, or limited availability, the appropriate resolution and world-state systems determine the result.

---

## 6.1.9 — Careers Are Not Linear

A shinobi career is not required to follow:

Academy Student → Genin → Chūnin → Jōnin → Kage.

Characters may:

- Remain at a rank for many years
- Specialize without receiving conventional rank advancement
- Enter administrative or support positions
- Become instructors
- Transfer departments
- Change specialties
- Become temporarily inactive
- Lose command authority
- Receive restricted duty
- Retire early
- Return from retirement
- Become medically unable to perform field work
- Leave shinobi service
- Pursue civilian work
- Advance unusually quickly under extraordinary conditions

Horizontal career development is as legitimate as vertical promotion.

---

## 6.1.10 — Mission Rank Describes Expected Conditions

Mission rank represents the village's classification based on known or reasonably believed information at the time of assignment.

Mission rank is **not an engine guarantee of actual danger**.

A mission may become easier or harder than expected because of:

- Incorrect intelligence
- Concealed information
- Changed circumstances
- Client dishonesty
- Enemy action
- Political interference
- Weather
- Coincidence
- New objectives
- Unforeseen third parties

Escalation must emerge from the simulated situation rather than arbitrary difficulty scaling.

---

## 6.1.11 — The World Does Not Level-Scale to the Player

The simulation must not automatically adjust teachers, opponents, missions, promotions, examinations, opportunities, or threats to match the player's current power.

A dangerous person remains dangerous because of who they are, not because the player has reached a particular level.

Likewise, trivial work may genuinely be trivial to an experienced character.

Difficulty should emerge from:

- World state
- Assignment
- Geography
- opposition
- Information
- Timing
- Character capabilities
- Preparation
- Random uncertainty where appropriate

This permits genuine overqualification, underqualification, caution, preparation, avoidance, and growth.

---

## 6.1.12 — Player Agency Does Not Mean Guaranteed Opportunity

The player may pursue goals, request training, seek mentors, apply for positions, volunteer for missions, request promotion consideration, change specialties, or attempt unconventional career paths.

The world must respond meaningfully to these attempts.

However, the simulation does not guarantee that the desired opportunity exists or that NPCs and institutions cooperate.

The player's agency is expressed through **meaningful attempts and decisions**, not automatic world compliance.

---

## 6.1.13 — No Player Privilege

The player character is not granted special institutional treatment merely because they are the player.

Unless circumstances justify otherwise:

- Entry requirements apply.
- Mission risks apply.
- Failure remains possible.
- Promotions can be denied.
- Teachers can refuse.
- Rivals can outperform the player.
- Positions can be filled by others.
- Opportunities can be missed.
- Injuries can interrupt development.
- NPCs can receive rare opportunities first.
- Careers can stall or change direction.

Exceptional treatment must have an in-world cause.

---

## 6.1.14 — NPCs Use the Same Career Logic

NPCs progress through the same underlying principles as the player.

Important NPCs may receive detailed simulation. Less relevant NPCs may use abstraction, but abstraction must approximate the same rules rather than secretly using a different progression system.

NPC advancement should reflect factors such as:

- Time
- Training
- Aptitude
- Opportunities
- Teachers
- Missions
- Experience
- Injury
- Relationships
- Village need
- Historical events
- Personal goals

An NPC should not remain frozen solely because the player is not interacting with them.

---

## 6.1.15 — Institutions Are Imperfect

Shinobi institutions are composed of people and may be imperfect, inconsistent, political, biased, resource-constrained, or mistaken.

Possible institutional effects include:

- Uneven teaching quality
- Poor personnel allocation
- Delayed promotions
- Favoritism
- Clan influence
- Political appointments
- Bureaucratic mistakes
- Resource shortages
- Incorrect mission classification
- Conflicting priorities
- Corruption
- Exceptional leadership
- Effective reform

These possibilities must arise from actual world conditions and NPC behavior rather than arbitrary engine interference.

---

## 6.1.16 — Historical and Village Context Matters

Academy systems, promotion standards, mission practices, career structures, and professional culture may differ by:

- Village
- Nation
- Era
- Wartime or peacetime conditions
- Leadership
- Population
- Military doctrine
- Resources
- Local traditions
- Political structure

Ruleset 6 provides the general simulation framework. Individual villages and eras instantiate that framework differently.

---

## 6.1.17 — Failure Creates State, Not Just Penalty

Failure should change the simulation rather than merely subtracting points.

Examples include:

- Failing an Academy assessment can trigger remediation.
- Failing graduation can delay entry into service.
- Failing a mission can damage trust or create new threats.
- Poor leadership can affect later assignments.
- Injury can redirect a career.
- Failed promotion can influence future development.
- Being rejected by a teacher can motivate another training route.
- Mission mistakes can create political or interpersonal consequences.

Failure is a source of persistent world state and emergent narrative.

---

## 6.1.18 — Success Also Creates State

Success does more than grant rewards.

Repeated performance can create:

- Trust
- Reputation
- New responsibilities
- Higher-risk assignments
- Leadership opportunities
- Mentorship interest
- Department recruitment
- Political attention
- Rivalry
- Expectations
- Increased workload
- Access to restricted training

Success can therefore create new pressures as well as advantages.

---

## 6.1.19 — Information May Be Hidden

Characters do not automatically know the engine's complete evaluation of their career.

Potentially hidden information includes:

- Internal instructor evaluations
- Promotion deliberations
- Classified mission details
- Competing candidates
- Department recruitment interest
- Secret recommendations
- Political objections
- Reliability assessments
- Security-clearance concerns
- Hidden mission intelligence

The player receives only information their character could reasonably know or infer, subject to Ruleset 13's player-facing meta-information standards.

---

## 6.1.20 — Simulation Detail Scales With Relevance

The engine should not fully simulate every class period, practice repetition, mission, or career decision for every person in the world.

Detail may operate at different levels:

- **Direct simulation:** important player-facing scenes and decisions.
- **Condensed simulation:** meaningful periods summarized with relevant choices and outcomes.
- **Background simulation:** offscreen NPC careers and routine institutional activity.
- **Historical abstraction:** large-scale past events or long periods where only important results need preservation.

Abstraction must preserve causal consistency with the detailed rules.

---

## 6.1.21 — Canon Establishes Possibility, Not Universal Frequency

Canonical Naruto examples establish that unusual events, careers, promotions, training methods, and exceptional individuals can exist.

They do not imply that exceptional cases are routine.

Examples of prodigious graduation, unusually young promotion, forbidden instruction, rare mentorship, or extraordinary mission assignment should remain exceptional when the setting and rarity rules support that interpretation.

The simulation must distinguish:

- Possible
- Plausible
- Common
- Rare
- Exceptional
- Historically extraordinary

---

## 6.1.22 — Emergent Careers Over Character Classes

A character should not normally select a permanent RPG-style profession class such as "Medic," "Tracker," or "Assassin" and instantly receive a package of abilities.

Careers and specialties emerge from:

- Skills learned
- Training pursued
- Qualifications earned
- Missions performed
- Mentors encountered
- Department experience
- Personal interests
- Demonstrated competency
- Institutional opportunities
- Reputation and service history

Labels describe what the character has become; they do not create the character's competence.

---

## 6.1.23 — Persistent Professional History

Important education and career events become persistent character/world state.

The engine should preserve relevant records such as:

- Academy enrollment and graduation
- Significant evaluations
- Team assignments
- Instructors and mentors
- Mission history
- Promotions
- Qualifications
- Specializations
- Department assignments
- Leadership history
- Commendations
- Disciplinary actions
- Major failures
- Classified service where internally appropriate
- Career interruptions
- Retirement or departure from service

Past professional history may influence future opportunities indefinitely where relevant.

---

## 6.1.24 — Core Design Test

Any Ruleset 6 mechanic should pass the following test:

> **Does this mechanic make Academy life, training, missions, or careers behave like parts of a persistent living shinobi world rather than a progression menu built around the player?**

If a mechanic exists primarily to produce predictable game progression while contradicting simulation logic, it should be redesigned.

---

## 6.1.25 — Summary Principle

Ruleset 6 treats shinobi development as a **life and career simulation**.

Characters learn because they spend time learning.  
They gain opportunities because circumstances produce opportunities.  
They receive missions because institutions need work performed.  
They advance because their professional history supports advancement.  
They specialize because of accumulated choices and experience.  
They succeed or fail inside a world that continues to exist independently of them.

The player can strongly influence that life, but the world is never required to organize itself around the player.
