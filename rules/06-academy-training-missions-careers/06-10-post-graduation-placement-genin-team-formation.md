# 6.10 — Post-Graduation Placement & Genin Team Formation

**Ruleset:** 6 — Academy, Training, Missions & Careers  
**Status:** Provisional

## 6.10.1 — Purpose

Post-graduation placement determines how Academy graduates enter supervised professional service.

Placement may involve:

- Team assignment
- Instructor assignment
- Specialist placement
- Delayed deployment
- Provisional status
- Remedial placement
- Administrative holding
- Alternative service tracks

Team formation should reflect institutional needs, available personnel, student records, and local doctrine rather than an arbitrary party-building algorithm.

---

## 6.10.2 — Graduation Does Not Automatically Create a Team

A graduate may be fully qualified and still wait for placement.

Possible causes include:

- Too few instructors
- Too many graduates
- Team restructuring
- Medical delay
- Political review
- Uneven cohort size
- Specialist routing
- War or emergency reassignment

Graduation certifies readiness.

Placement determines where that readiness is used.

---

## 6.10.3 — Team Size Is Not Universally Fixed

Three Genin plus one Jōnin-sensei may be a common Konoha-style model, but Ruleset 6 should not make it universal.

Possible team structures include:

- Three Genin + one instructor
- Two Genin + one instructor
- Four or more junior shinobi + one leader
- Temporary mission squads
- Specialist cells
- Apprenticeship-based placements
- Rotating instructor groups
- Department-based junior teams

Village doctrine, population, instructor availability, and mission needs determine the structure.

---

## 6.10.4 — Placement Inputs

The village may consider:

- Student strengths
- Student weaknesses
- Teamwork history
- Leadership tendencies
- Personality
- Academic record
- Practical performance
- Special interests
- Clan background
- Existing relationships
- Instructor recommendations
- Medical restrictions
- Available teachers
- Village manpower needs
- Political considerations
- Security concerns

No single factor should control placement in every case.

---

## 6.10.5 — Competency Balance

Villages may try to avoid teams with catastrophic shared weaknesses.

For example, planners may prefer not to create a team where:

- No one can navigate
- Everyone is poor at teamwork
- All members have identical limitations
- No one is suited to field observation

However, the engine should not perfectly optimize every team.

Real institutions operate with incomplete information, personnel shortages, conflicting priorities, and human error.

---

## 6.10.6 — Complementary Strengths

A village may prefer teams whose members offer different strengths.

Possible combinations include:

- Strong combat + strong observation
- Tactical thinker + physically gifted teammate
- Sensor potential + strong frontline ability
- Support-oriented student + aggressive fighter

Complementarity should emerge from actual capabilities rather than rigid RPG roles.

A character should not be labeled "the tank," "the healer," or "the rogue" simply to fill a party slot.

---

## 6.10.7 — Similarity Can Also Be Intentional

Not every team should be balanced.

A village may intentionally create:

- Tracking teams
- Heavy-combat teams
- Reconnaissance teams
- Clan-oriented teams
- Specialist development teams

This depends on doctrine and mission expectations.

Team formation should therefore support both mixed and specialized compositions.

---

## 6.10.8 — Teamwork History

Previous Academy teamwork may influence placement.

Planners may consider:

- Proven cooperation
- Persistent conflict
- Leadership compatibility
- Communication
- Trust
- Dependency
- Rivalry

Good friends are not automatically ideal teammates.

Rivals are not automatically bad teammates.

Ruleset 7 governs the relationships; Ruleset 6 evaluates whether the combination appears professionally workable.

---

## 6.10.9 — Existing Relationships

The institution may intentionally:

- Keep close partners together
- Separate unhealthy dependencies
- Split disruptive rivalries
- Preserve productive rivalries
- Separate siblings
- Group clan members
- Mix backgrounds

The choice depends on educational philosophy and current needs.

There should be no universal "friends stay together" rule.

---

## 6.10.10 — Leadership Distribution

Team formation may consider which students show leadership tendencies.

A team may benefit from:

- One clear leader
- Shared leadership
- A leader plus strong second
- Multiple competing leaders for developmental reasons

Too many dominant personalities can create conflict.

Too little initiative can also be a problem.

These are considerations, not deterministic formulas.

---

## 6.10.11 — Clan Influence

Clan politics may affect placement.

Possible effects include:

- Requests to keep heirs near trusted instructors
- Pressure to separate clan techniques
- Strategic pairing of bloodlines
- Avoiding sensitive combinations
- Favoring prestigious families
- Deliberately mixing clans

Such influence should arise from actual politics rather than arbitrary favoritism.

---

## 6.10.12 — Security Considerations

Some students may require special placement because of:

- Sensitive lineage
- Restricted techniques
- Jinchūriki status
- Foreign origin
- Political value
- Intelligence concerns
- Family connections

Security-sensitive placement may prioritize supervision over ideal training compatibility.

---

## 6.10.13 — Medical Considerations

Medical restrictions may affect:

- Mission eligibility
- Instructor assignment
- Team role
- Training intensity
- Deployment timing

A student can still be placed while carrying restrictions if appropriate supervision exists.

---

## 6.10.14 — Instructor Availability

A team cannot receive an instructor who is unavailable.

Instructor availability may be affected by:

- Missions
- Injury
- Death
- Retirement
- Department duty
- Existing students
- Administrative obligations
- War

This may force imperfect placement or delayed deployment.

---

## 6.10.15 — Instructor Capacity

One instructor should not automatically receive unlimited students.

Capacity may depend on:

- Teaching skill
- Mission schedule
- Rank
- Other duties
- Experience
- Village policy

Overloaded instructors may produce lower supervision quality.

---

## 6.10.16 — Instructor Compatibility

The village may consider whether a teacher is suited to a team.

Relevant factors include:

- Subject expertise
- Teaching ability
- Personality
- Leadership style
- Experience
- Team needs
- Clan knowledge
- Specialization

A powerful Jōnin may be a poor match for a particular group.

---

## 6.10.17 — Instructor Specialization

Some teams may be assigned instructors because of specific needs.

Examples:

- Sensor-oriented team → experienced sensor instructor
- Medical trainee → medical-capable mentor
- Tracking team → tracking specialist
- High-risk personalities → disciplined veteran instructor

This should improve access and guidance without guaranteeing ideal outcomes.

---

## 6.10.18 — Placement Committees and Decision Makers

Placement may be decided by:

- Academy leadership
- Jōnin commander
- Hokage/Kage
- Department heads
- Senior instructors
- Administrative staff
- Joint committee

The decision process itself can vary by village.

This also creates room for political influence and disagreement.

---

## 6.10.19 — Information Is Imperfect

Placement officials do not know exact hidden stats or future Potential.

They rely on:

- Academy records
- Recommendations
- Observations
- Test results
- Reputation
- Interviews
- Institutional knowledge

Therefore, placements can be mistaken.

A student may be assigned to a poor fit despite everyone acting reasonably.

---

## 6.10.20 — Placement Goals

A village may optimize for different goals, including:

- Immediate mission effectiveness
- Long-term development
- Leadership training
- Clan integration
- Specialist cultivation
- Political stability
- Instructor workload
- Security
- Manpower efficiency

These goals may conflict.

The engine should preserve which priorities drove a decision when relevant.

---

## 6.10.21 — Developmental Teams

Some teams may be built primarily for student growth rather than immediate mission optimization.

Examples include:

- Balancing strengths and weaknesses
- Pairing students with complementary personalities
- Assigning a patient instructor to immature graduates
- Grouping students who need teamwork development

These teams may initially be less efficient but stronger developmentally.

---

## 6.10.22 — Operational Teams

Other teams may be built for immediate mission needs.

This is more likely during:

- War
- Crisis
- Manpower shortage
- High-demand periods

Operational placement may sacrifice long-term educational quality for short-term usefulness.

---

## 6.10.23 — Specialist Routing

Some graduates may enter specialized development tracks instead of ordinary Genin teams.

Examples include:

- Medical apprenticeship
- Intelligence support
- Communications
- Research
- Administrative training
- Clan-specific tutelage

This should be uncommon unless the village supports such pathways.

Specialist routing does not automatically imply higher status.

---

## 6.10.24 — Unassigned Graduates

A graduate may temporarily remain unassigned.

Possible activities include:

- Supplemental training
- Administrative duty
- Remedial work
- Temporary academy assistance
- Waiting for instructor availability
- Medical recovery

Unassigned status should not freeze development or time.

---

## 6.10.25 — Reserve Graduates

Some villages may maintain reserve graduates for:

- Emergency team replacement
- Future cohort balancing
- Specialist assignment
- Wartime mobilization

Reserve placement can be legitimate, but prolonged inactivity may create frustration or slower development.

---

## 6.10.26 — Provisional Team Placement

A team assignment may begin provisionally.

The instructor or village may evaluate:

- Compatibility
- Discipline
- Safety
- Practical readiness
- Group cohesion

A provisional team can later be:

- Confirmed
- Restructured
- Split
- Reassigned
- Returned for remediation

---

## 6.10.27 — Team Restructuring

Teams are not permanent.

Restructuring may occur because of:

- Death
- Injury
- Promotion
- Transfer
- Retirement
- Poor compatibility
- Instructor loss
- Department reassignment
- War
- Specialized recruitment

The engine should treat team composition as persistent but mutable state.

---

## 6.10.28 — Replacement Members

A team losing a member may receive:

- Another Genin
- A temporary Chūnin
- A specialist attachment
- No replacement
- Full restructuring

Replacement decisions depend on mission demand and available personnel.

---

## 6.10.29 — Cross-Cohort Teams

Teams need not contain graduates from the exact same class.

A village may combine:

- New graduates
- Repeat-year students
- Veteran Genin
- Transfer students
- Reserve graduates

This is especially likely in smaller villages or after casualties.

---

## 6.10.30 — Veteran Genin Placement

Experienced Genin may be paired with new graduates.

This can provide:

- Practical guidance
- Team stability
- Informal mentorship

But it may also create:

- Status tension
- Unequal responsibility
- Resentment
- Dependency

These effects should emerge through actual personalities and relationships.

---

## 6.10.31 — Political Placement

Some assignments may be influenced by:

- Clan deals
- Diplomacy
- Leadership preference
- Favoritism
- Security concerns
- Patronage
- Public symbolism

Political placement should remain a real possibility in imperfect institutions.

It should not be the default explanation for every unusual team.

---

## 6.10.32 — Student Preference

Graduates may be allowed to express preferences regarding:

- Teammates
- Instructor
- Specialty
- Department
- Training focus

The village may consider those preferences.

It is not obligated to honor them.

This preserves agency without world compliance.

---

## 6.10.33 — Instructor Preference

Instructors may also request or reject certain assignments where policy allows.

Reasons may include:

- Prior relationships
- Specialist fit
- Personal interest
- Conflict
- Capacity
- Security concerns

Instructor preference can matter without giving teachers total control.

---

## 6.10.34 — Placement Notification

The character should normally receive:

- Team assignment
- Instructor identity
- Reporting time
- Initial meeting location
- Any special conditions
- Provisional status if applicable

Internal reasoning may remain hidden.

---

## 6.10.35 — Placement Records

The engine should preserve:

- Placement date
- Team identifier
- Members
- Instructor
- Placement type
- Known rationale
- Provisional status
- Restrictions
- Subsequent changes

This becomes part of permanent service history.

---

## 6.10.36 — NPC Placement

NPC graduates follow the same logic.

Background team formation may be abstracted.

Important NPC teams should reflect:

- Available personnel
- Records
- relationships
- Instructor capacity
- Village priorities
- Political conditions

NPCs should not receive perfectly tailored teams solely because the future story needs them.

---

## 6.10.37 — Hidden Placement Factors

The player may not know:

- Why a teammate was selected
- Why another student was excluded
- Which instructor requested the team
- Political pressure
- Security concerns
- Long-term development plans
- Specialist recruitment interest

Ruleset 13 governs what meta-information may be revealed.

---

## 6.10.38 — Governing Principle

Post-graduation placement should answer:

> **Given the graduates, instructors, institutional goals, and current world conditions, how would this village plausibly assign these new shinobi?**

Team formation should feel intentional but imperfect.

The village may try to build effective teams, but it operates with limited personnel, incomplete information, competing priorities, and human judgment.

A team should therefore feel like an institutional decision inside a living world, not a perfectly optimized RPG party.
