# 6.4 — Academy Structure, Calendar & Attendance

**Ruleset:** 6 — Academy, Training, Missions & Careers  
**Status:** Provisional

## 6.4.1 — Purpose

Academy education occupies real simulation time.

The Academy is not a background label that characters carry until graduation. It is a structured institution with:

- Academic years or equivalent terms
- Class schedules
- Training periods
- Attendance requirements
- Breaks and holidays
- Instructor assignments
- Evaluations
- Homework or independent practice
- Extracurricular opportunities
- Disciplinary and remedial consequences

The exact structure varies by village and era.

---

## 6.4.2 — Academy Duration

Each Academy should define a standard expected duration of study.

That duration may vary by:

- Village
- Historical era
- Wartime conditions
- Entry age
- Curriculum intensity
- Student placement
- Accelerated progression
- Remediation
- Transfer credits

Standard duration is an institutional expectation, not a guaranteed personal timeline.

Students may graduate early, on time, late, or not at all.

---

## 6.4.3 — Academic Years and Terms

Academies may divide education into:

- Years
- Terms
- Semesters
- Seasonal training blocks
- Continuous cohort progression

The simulator should preserve whatever structure the local Academy uses.

Each term should define:

- Start and end dates
- Instructional days
- Breaks
- Major assessments
- Promotion or placement reviews

This creates predictable institutional rhythm without forcing every Academy into a modern school calendar.

---

## 6.4.4 — School Week

Academies should define how many days per week students normally attend.

A typical week may include:

- Classroom instruction
- Physical training
- Chakra practice
- Weapons practice
- Team exercises
- Independent study

War, crisis, special events, or local culture may shorten or intensify the week.

Attendance frequency must consume actual calendar time.

---

## 6.4.5 — Daily Schedule

Each Academy day should be divided into blocks rather than treated as one generic action.

Possible blocks include:

- Academic instruction
- Physical conditioning
- Chakra theory
- Practical ninjutsu
- Weapons practice
- Strategy
- Fieldcraft
- Team exercises
- Lunch or rest
- Supervised study
- Instructor consultation

Not every day requires every subject.

Daily scheduling should create meaningful variation and opportunity cost.

---

## 6.4.6 — Class Length and Training Blocks

Different subjects may require different time blocks.

Examples:

- Short academic lectures
- Long physical drills
- Half-day survival exercises
- Full-day field assessments
- Multi-day supervised exercises

The engine should treat long practical activities as actual time commitments rather than compressing everything into identical class periods.

---

## 6.4.7 — Cohorts and Classes

Students should normally belong to a cohort or class group.

A cohort may share:

- Instructor
- Schedule
- Curriculum stage
- Classroom
- Practical exercises
- Assessments

Cohort membership matters socially and mechanically because students repeatedly encounter the same peers.

Ruleset 7 governs the resulting relationships.

---

## 6.4.8 — Class Size

Class size affects educational quality.

Smaller classes may provide:

- More instructor attention
- Faster correction
- More individualized practice
- Better monitoring

Larger classes may create:

- Less individual feedback
- More competition for facilities
- More peer interaction
- Greater risk that struggling students are overlooked

Class size should matter without becoming a simple universal bonus or penalty.

---

## 6.4.9 — Instructor Assignment

Academies should track which instructors teach which subjects or cohorts.

Possible structures include:

- One primary classroom instructor
- Multiple subject specialists
- Rotating instructors
- Guest shinobi
- Assistant instructors

Instructor changes can affect continuity, teaching quality, relationships, and curriculum emphasis.

---

## 6.4.10 — Required Attendance

Most Academy students are expected to attend scheduled instruction.

Attendance records may influence:

- Eligibility for exams
- Academic standing
- Instructor evaluations
- Remediation
- Discipline
- Graduation eligibility

The exact threshold depends on local policy.

Attendance is not the same as performance, but chronic absence should have consequences.

---

## 6.4.11 — Excused Absence

An absence may be excused for reasons such as:

- Illness
- Injury
- Family emergency
- Bereavement
- Official summons
- Religious or cultural observance
- Approved travel
- Village emergency

Excused absence may still create missed learning even when no disciplinary penalty applies.

---

## 6.4.12 — Unexcused Absence

Skipping Academy without authorization may affect:

- Discipline
- Instructor trust
- Academic progress
- Eligibility for activities
- Family notification
- Graduation readiness

Repeated truancy can escalate from minor correction to formal intervention.

Consequences should depend on age, frequency, cause, and local policy.

---

## 6.4.13 — Tardiness and Partial Attendance

Late arrival or leaving early should be tracked when meaningful.

Occasional lateness may have little impact.

Persistent lateness may signal:

- Poor discipline
- Family problems
- Transportation problems
- Employment obligations
- Avoidance
- Health issues

The engine should preserve cause when known rather than treating every late arrival as identical misconduct.

---

## 6.4.14 — Illness and Injury During School

Students may attend while mildly impaired, stay home, or receive temporary restrictions.

Possible responses include:

- Excused absence
- Reduced physical participation
- Medical observation
- Alternative coursework
- Delayed practical assessment

Ruleset 4 governs the underlying medical condition.

Ruleset 6 governs educational consequences.

---

## 6.4.15 — Holidays and Breaks

Academies may observe:

- National holidays
- Village festivals
- Seasonal breaks
- Memorial days
- Religious observances
- Administrative closures

Breaks create real downtime.

Students may use them for:

- Rest
- Family
- Independent training
- Travel
- Social activity
- Clan instruction

Breaks should therefore create meaningful player choices.

---

## 6.4.16 — Emergency Closures

Academies may close because of:

- War
- Attack
- Epidemic
- Severe weather
- Infrastructure damage
- Political crisis
- Disaster

Closure can interrupt curriculum and alter graduation timelines.

Long disruptions may trigger:

- Compressed replacement schedules
- Remote or decentralized instruction
- Temporary training sites
- Accelerated emergency programs

---

## 6.4.17 — Homework and Independent Study

Some Academy systems may assign work outside scheduled hours.

Possible activities include:

- Reading
- Written exercises
- Technique practice
- Physical conditioning
- Memorization
- Equipment maintenance
- Team assignments

Homework consumes personal time.

Completing it may support performance; ignoring it may hinder progress.

---

## 6.4.18 — Independent Training Outside Class

Students may train beyond required Academy hours.

This is voluntary unless assigned.

Independent training can improve development but competes with:

- Sleep
- Family
- Social life
- Recovery
- Other studies
- Employment
- Leisure

Ruleset 6.12 onward governs training effectiveness.

---

## 6.4.19 — Clan Training Alongside Academy

Clan students may receive additional training before or after school.

This can create large developmental differences between classmates.

Clan instruction may:

- Reinforce Academy material
- Teach separate techniques
- Create fatigue
- Consume social time
- Conflict with Academy instruction

The engine should account for both benefits and opportunity costs.

---

## 6.4.20 — Extracurricular Activities

Academies may offer optional programs such as:

- Sparring clubs
- Weapons clubs
- Strategy groups
- Survival training
- Academic tutoring
- Medical fundamentals
- Tracking exercises
- Student leadership roles

Availability depends on staffing, culture, resources, and era.

Extracurricular participation should create real schedule commitments.

---

## 6.4.21 — Remedial Instruction

Students falling behind may receive:

- Tutoring
- Additional practice
- Smaller-group instruction
- Repeated coursework
- Modified exercises
- Individual instructor attention

Remediation should target actual weaknesses rather than imposing a generic penalty.

---

## 6.4.22 — Advanced Instruction

Students substantially ahead of their cohort may receive:

- Advanced coursework
- Higher-level practice
- Independent assignments
- Older-cohort instruction
- Specialist tutoring

Advanced instruction should require institutional recognition and available resources.

A talented student is not automatically given perfect individualized teaching.

---

## 6.4.23 — Mixed-Age and Mixed-Skill Instruction

Some Academies may group students by ability rather than age.

This is especially plausible in:

- Small villages
- Wartime
- Specialist programs
- Accelerated tracks
- Remedial programs

Mixed groups may affect both learning and social dynamics.

---

## 6.4.24 — Field Days and Practical Exercises

Academy instruction may periodically leave the classroom.

Field activities can include:

- Orienteering
- Survival
- Tracking
- Team exercises
- Stealth drills
- Mock objectives
- Controlled sparring

These may occupy a full day or multiple days.

They should create fatigue, risk, and developmental opportunity proportionate to their intensity.

---

## 6.4.25 — Overnight Exercises

Older or advanced students may participate in supervised overnight training.

Possible goals include:

- Campcraft
- Night navigation
- Watch rotations
- Team coordination
- Fatigue management
- Survival practice

Such exercises should remain age-, era-, and institution-appropriate.

---

## 6.4.26 — Attendance During Special Training

Students temporarily assigned to approved external training may be counted as present or excused.

Examples include:

- Clan instruction authorized by the Academy
- Medical apprenticeship exposure
- Specialist tutoring
- Village ceremonies
- Official competitions

The institution must recognize the activity for it to substitute for normal attendance.

---

## 6.4.27 — Discipline and Attendance Intervention

Attendance problems should escalate gradually where appropriate.

Possible responses include:

1. Informal warning
2. Instructor conference
3. Guardian notification
4. Remedial attendance plan
5. Loss of privileges
6. Formal probation
7. Delayed testing
8. Suspension
9. Dismissal in extreme cases

Severity depends on context and local rules.

---

## 6.4.28 — Academy Suspension

Temporary suspension removes a student from ordinary instruction.

Possible causes include:

- Serious misconduct
- Violence
- Security violations
- Repeated truancy
- Dangerous misuse of techniques

Suspension creates educational consequences and consumes time.

It should not be used casually.

---

## 6.4.29 — Academy Withdrawal

A student may leave before graduation because of:

- Family decision
- Personal choice
- Illness
- Injury
- Financial hardship
- Relocation
- Political change
- Disciplinary removal
- Alternative career

Withdrawal should remain part of persistent educational history.

Reentry may be possible later.

---

## 6.4.30 — Academic Calendar and Aging

Calendar progression must remain synchronized with character aging.

If one Academy term lasts four months, the character actually becomes four months older.

Long training periods, holidays, illness, failed years, and delayed graduation therefore affect age naturally.

The engine must never let characters complete years of education without corresponding passage of time.

---

## 6.4.31 — Time Compression

Routine Academy periods may be condensed.

The simulator does not need to roleplay every school day.

Compression may summarize:

- Attendance
- Training exposure
- Academic progress
- Relationship changes
- Significant incidents
- Fatigue
- Special opportunities

The player should regain direct control when meaningful decisions or events arise.

---

## 6.4.32 — Events That Break Compression

Academy time compression should stop when something important occurs, such as:

- Major examination
- Conflict
- Injury
- New friendship or rivalry
- Instructor intervention
- Special training opportunity
- Disciplinary issue
- Family event
- Technique breakthrough
- Academy crisis

The engine should move from summary back into direct simulation when player agency becomes important.

---

## 6.4.33 — NPC Academy Scheduling

NPC students follow the same calendar.

Background students may be processed in aggregate.

Important NPCs should preserve:

- Attendance patterns
- Major absences
- Training load
- Academic performance
- Special opportunities

This allows classmates to develop naturally alongside the player.

---

## 6.4.34 — Governing Principle

Academy structure should answer:

> **How does this institution organize a student's time, and what does that structure realistically permit them to accomplish?**

Academy life should create rhythm, constraint, opportunity, and tradeoffs.

School is not merely a waiting room before becoming a Genin.

It is a major developmental period whose schedule shapes training, relationships, health, and future career outcomes.
