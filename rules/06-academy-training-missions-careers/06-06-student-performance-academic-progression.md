# 6.6 — Student Performance & Academic Progression

**Ruleset:** 6 — Academy, Training, Missions & Careers  
**Status:** Provisional

## 6.6.1 — Purpose

Student performance determines how effectively a character is progressing through Academy education.

Performance should reflect:

- Actual competency
- Classroom participation
- Practical execution
- Academic understanding
- Attendance
- Reliability
- Effort
- Improvement
- Instructor observations

It should not become a second XP system layered on top of Ruleset 1.

---

## 6.6.2 — Performance Is Subject-Specific

Students should not have one universal "Academy Grade."

Performance should be evaluated separately across meaningful domains such as:

- Academics
- Physical conditioning
- Taijutsu
- Weapons
- Chakra theory
- Chakra control
- Ninjutsu fundamentals
- Stealth
- Observation
- Survival
- Tactics
- Teamwork
- Professional conduct

A student may be exceptional in one area and weak in another.

---

## 6.6.3 — Performance Is Not the Same as Skill

Academic evaluation reflects how the institution perceives current performance.

Ruleset 1 still owns actual Attributes and Skills.

This distinction matters because:

- An instructor may underestimate a student.
- A student may perform poorly under test conditions.
- A student may possess skill but lack discipline.
- A student may understand theory but execute poorly.
- A student may improve faster than grades reflect.

The Academy's evaluation is evidence, not omniscient truth.

---

## 6.6.4 — Evaluation Components

A subject evaluation may draw from:

- Written work
- Practical demonstrations
- Sparring
- Drills
- Field exercises
- Instructor observation
- Homework
- Team exercises
- Oral questioning
- Formal exams

Different subjects should use different evidence.

A survival course should not be judged primarily through written tests if practical competence is the main objective.

---

## 6.6.5 — Evaluation Scales

Each Academy may use its own grading language.

Possible systems include:

- Numerical scores
- Letter grades
- Ranked categories
- Pass / Needs Improvement / Fail
- Instructor narrative evaluations
- Competency bands

The simulation should not require every village to use the same grading scale.

Internally, the engine may map these to comparable performance states where needed.

---

## 6.6.6 — Recommended Internal Performance Bands

For simulation purposes, a useful internal scale is:

- **Severely Deficient**
- **Below Standard**
- **Developing**
- **Meets Standard**
- **Above Standard**
- **Exceptional**

These are not necessarily shown directly to the player.

They represent the institution's current assessment of a student's performance relative to the curriculum.

---

## 6.6.7 — Current Performance vs Potential

The Academy should never equate present performance with long-term ceiling.

A student may currently be weak because of:

- Late exposure
- Poor teaching
- Illness
- Lack of practice
- Anxiety
- Family problems
- Fatigue
- Missing prerequisites
- Slow initial development

Likewise, an early prodigy may later plateau.

Potential remains a separate hidden concept governed by Ruleset 1.

---

## 6.6.8 — Improvement Trend

Evaluations should track not only current performance but direction.

Possible trends include:

- Rapidly Improving
- Improving
- Stable
- Inconsistent
- Declining

A student who is slightly below standard but improving quickly may be treated very differently from one who is equally below standard and deteriorating.

---

## 6.6.9 — Effort and Engagement

Instructors may separately assess:

- Effort
- Participation
- Preparation
- Persistence
- Willingness to accept correction

Effort should not override competence.

A hardworking student may still fail a practical requirement.

But strong effort may influence:

- Remediation opportunities
- Instructor support
- Recommendations
- Teacher relationships

---

## 6.6.10 — Conduct and Reliability

The Academy may evaluate professional behavior separately from academic ability.

Relevant factors include:

- Attendance
- Punctuality
- Following safety rules
- Equipment care
- Honesty
- Team reliability
- Respect for instructions
- Responsible use of techniques

A highly skilled but dangerously reckless student should not receive the same institutional evaluation as an equally skilled and reliable student.

---

## 6.6.11 — Teamwork Evaluation

Teamwork must be evaluated independently from individual combat performance.

Possible factors include:

- Communication
- Cooperation
- Role awareness
- Supporting teammates
- Sharing information
- Adapting plans
- Handling disagreement
- Protecting weaker teammates

This becomes especially important for later team placement and promotion.

Ruleset 7 governs relationship dynamics; Ruleset 6 evaluates professional teamwork behavior.

---

## 6.6.12 — Instructor Bias and Error

Instructor evaluations are imperfect.

Possible distortions include:

- Favoritism
- Dislike
- Clan prejudice
- Reputation effects
- Overvaluing visible performance
- Undervaluing quiet students
- Misreading anxiety as incompetence
- Mistaking confidence for skill

The engine may preserve both:

- Actual performance
- Institutional evaluation

These values do not always match.

---

## 6.6.13 — Multiple Evaluators

Important decisions should preferably draw from more than one source when the institution has the resources.

Examples include:

- Classroom instructor
- Practical instructor
- Specialist teacher
- Exam proctor
- Field exercise evaluator

Multiple evaluators reduce but do not eliminate bias.

---

## 6.6.14 — Routine Progress Reviews

Academies should periodically review student progress.

Reviews may occur:

- Monthly
- Per term
- Per semester
- Before major exams
- After major exercises

A review may update:

- Subject performance
- Improvement trend
- Remediation needs
- Advanced placement eligibility
- Behavioral concerns
- Graduation readiness

---

## 6.6.15 — Progression Between Curriculum Stages

Advancement should depend on sufficient readiness for the next stage.

A student does not need excellence in every subject.

Progression may require:

- Minimum performance in critical subjects
- Acceptable attendance
- Safe practical behavior
- Completion of required coursework
- Instructor approval

Minor weaknesses may carry forward.

Critical weaknesses may block advancement.

---

## 6.6.16 — Critical vs Non-Critical Competencies

Not every Academy subject should be equally important for progression.

Critical competencies might include:

- Basic safety
- Minimum chakra handling
- Basic combat defense
- Mission discipline
- Team reliability
- Foundational academics

Non-critical weaknesses might be tolerated if they do not prevent safe or competent service.

For example, weak shuriken accuracy may not automatically block graduation if all essential requirements are met.

---

## 6.6.17 — Conditional Advancement

A student may advance while carrying unresolved weaknesses.

Possible conditions include:

- Mandatory tutoring
- Extra practice
- Retesting
- Instructor monitoring
- Restricted participation in advanced exercises

This allows realistic progression without forcing repetition of an entire year for one weak subject.

---

## 6.6.18 — Remediation

Remediation should target actual deficiencies.

Possible responses include:

- One-on-one tutoring
- Extra drills
- Smaller-group instruction
- Foundational review
- Modified training methods
- Additional homework
- Peer-assisted learning
- Specialist evaluation

The chosen intervention should fit the problem.

---

## 6.6.19 — Reassessment After Remediation

Remediation should lead to a later reassessment.

The engine should check whether:

- The underlying weakness improved
- The student merely memorized the test
- A different teaching method worked
- Additional support is still needed

This prevents remediation from being an automatic "pay time, remove penalty" mechanic.

---

## 6.6.20 — Repeating Coursework

Students may repeat:

- A subject
- A practical module
- A term
- A full year

Repeating an entire year should usually be reserved for substantial deficiencies or missed instruction.

The engine should prefer targeted remediation when plausible.

---

## 6.6.21 — Failure to Progress

If a student repeatedly fails critical requirements, possible outcomes include:

- Extended remediation
- Delayed advancement
- Repeated term
- Alternative curriculum
- Withdrawal
- Dismissal
- Transfer to civilian education

The outcome depends on the student's circumstances and Academy policy.

---

## 6.6.22 — Accelerated Progression

Students who consistently exceed the curriculum may advance faster.

Possible options include:

- Skipping material already mastered
- Joining advanced classes
- Independent study
- Early practical assessment
- Early graduation candidacy

Acceleration should require both competence and institutional approval.

---

## 6.6.23 — Acceleration Risks

Moving ahead too quickly can create weaknesses.

Possible risks include:

- Missing social development
- Uneven foundational knowledge
- Physical immaturity
- Emotional immaturity
- Poor teamwork experience
- Excessive pressure
- Isolation from peers

Exceptional academic or technical ability does not automatically mean readiness for every aspect of advancement.

---

## 6.6.24 — Exceptional Students

The Academy may formally recognize students who demonstrate unusual performance.

Recognition may produce:

- Advanced training
- Recommendations
- Leadership opportunities
- Specialist observation
- Mentorship interest
- Early graduation review

It may also produce:

- Increased expectations
- Pressure
- Rivalry
- Scrutiny
- Reduced tolerance for mistakes

Success creates state, not merely bonuses.

---

## 6.6.25 — Struggling Students

Students performing poorly should remain full characters rather than disposable failures.

Their struggles may arise from:

- Weak foundations
- Poor fit with teaching style
- Family stress
- Health
- Disability
- Low motivation
- Bullying
- Lack of resources
- Language barriers
- Late enrollment

Intervention should depend on cause.

---

## 6.6.26 — Academic Standing

The Academy may maintain broad standing categories such as:

- Good Standing
- Advanced Standing
- Remedial Standing
- Academic Probation
- Disciplinary Probation

Standing is an institutional status modifier.

It does not replace detailed subject evaluations.

---

## 6.6.27 — Performance Records

The Academy should preserve important records such as:

- Term evaluations
- Major exam results
- Remediation history
- Instructor comments
- Attendance
- Disciplinary actions
- Advanced placements
- Significant practical assessments

Not every daily score needs permanent storage.

The engine should preserve information likely to matter later.

---

## 6.6.28 — Student and Guardian Feedback

Depending on age and local culture, students or guardians may receive:

- Formal reports
- Instructor meetings
- Warnings
- Recommendations
- Progress summaries
- Graduation concerns

The player should not automatically see hidden institutional notes unless Ruleset 13 allows them.

---

## 6.6.29 — Performance and Team Placement

Late-Academy performance may influence post-graduation team formation.

Relevant information can include:

- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Teamwork
- Leadership
- Reliability
- Special interests
- Personality concerns
- Instructor recommendations

Performance should influence placement without making teams perfectly optimized.

---

## 6.6.30 — Performance and Graduation

Graduation should require minimum readiness across critical areas.

The exact requirements belong to Ruleset 6.9.

Ruleset 6.6 supplies the ongoing evidence used to determine whether a student is ready.

A strong final exam should not automatically erase years of serious deficiencies unless local policy explicitly allows that.

---

## 6.6.31 — No Hidden "Academy Level"

The engine should not create an invisible global Academy Level that duplicates Skills and Attributes.

Student progression should emerge from:

- Actual curriculum exposure
- Actual Skills and Attributes
- Evaluated competency
- Completed requirements
- Institutional decisions

This avoids scope creep and double-counting progression.

---

## 6.6.32 — NPC Progression

NPC students use the same logic.

Background NPC progression may be summarized using:

- Current competency
- Training exposure
- Attendance
- Aptitude
- Instruction quality
- Effort
- Personal circumstances

Important NPCs should receive detailed subject-specific evaluation when relevant.

---

## 6.6.33 — Governing Principle

Student performance should answer:

> **How well is this student currently meeting the Academy's expectations, where are they strong or weak, and what does the institution do about it?**

Performance should create educational consequences, opportunities, and interventions.

It should never become a second character-level system.
