# 6.9 — Graduation Requirements & Graduation Examination

**Ruleset:** 6 — Academy, Training, Missions & Careers  
**Status:** Provisional

## 6.9.1 — Purpose

Graduation determines whether an Academy student has demonstrated the minimum readiness required to leave Academy education and enter the next stage of shinobi service.

Graduation should evaluate:

- Foundational knowledge
- Practical competency
- Safety
- Reliability
- Mission readiness
- Team behavior
- Professional conduct
- Required techniques
- Physical and chakra readiness
- Institutional judgment

Graduation is a certification of minimum readiness, not proof of mastery.

---

## 6.9.2 — Graduation Is a Threshold, Not a Power Level

Graduation means:

> The student has met the Academy's minimum standard for progression.

It does not mean:

- The student is equally skilled to every other graduate
- The student is ready for every Genin mission
- The student has mastered Academy material
- The student is stronger than all non-graduates
- The student is immediately suited for independent fieldwork

Graduates may leave the Academy with significantly different strengths and weaknesses.

---

## 6.9.3 — Graduation Requirements Are Local

Each village may define its own graduation standard.

Requirements may vary by:

- Village
- Era
- Current leadership
- War or peace
- Academy resources
- Military doctrine
- Mission needs
- Political priorities
- Available instructors

A Konoha graduation standard should not automatically apply to Kiri, Suna, Kumo, Iwa, Kusa, or smaller villages.

---

## 6.9.4 — Required Graduation Domains

A typical Academy may require minimum competency in areas such as:

- Foundational academics
- Shinobi law and conduct
- Physical readiness
- Basic taijutsu
- Weapon safety
- Chakra control
- Required Academy techniques
- Fieldcraft
- Survival
- Observation
- Tactics
- Teamwork
- Mission procedure
- First aid
- Professional reliability

Exact domains depend on the local curriculum.

---

## 6.9.5 — Critical Requirements

Some graduation requirements should be non-compensable.

A student should not be able to offset a dangerous deficiency with excellence elsewhere.

Potential critical requirements include:

- Basic safety
- Minimum chakra control
- Basic defensive capability
- Mission discipline
- Team reliability
- Required foundational technique execution
- Ability to follow lawful orders
- Minimum physical readiness

A student who is exceptional academically but dangerously unreliable in practical training may not be ready to graduate.

---

## 6.9.6 — Compensable Weaknesses

Not every weakness must block graduation.

A student may still graduate while being weak in areas such as:

- Shuriken accuracy
- Academic history
- Sprint speed
- Tracking
- Specific elective subjects
- One nonessential technique

The institution may accept these weaknesses if the student meets all essential safety and service requirements.

---

## 6.9.7 — Minimum Standard vs Strong Performance

Graduation standards should distinguish:

- Barely Meets Standard
- Meets Standard
- Strong Graduate
- Exceptional Graduate

These labels describe graduation performance, not permanent character tiers.

A barely passing graduate and an exceptional graduate both become graduates if minimum requirements are satisfied.

Their later opportunities may differ.

---

## 6.9.8 — Ongoing Record Matters

Graduation should not depend exclusively on one final exam.

The Academy should consider cumulative evidence such as:

- Term evaluations
- Attendance
- Practical assessments
- Conduct
- Remediation history
- Team performance
- Required coursework
- Instructor recommendations
- Final examination results

One unusually good or bad day should not automatically erase years of evidence unless local policy explicitly gives the final exam overriding authority.

---

## 6.9.9 — Graduation Review

Before final graduation, the Academy may conduct a formal review.

The review may include:

- Academic record
- Practical competency
- Attendance
- Conduct
- Safety record
- Required technique completion
- Instructor evaluations
- Medical readiness
- Outstanding remediation

The review determines whether the student is:

- Cleared to graduate
- Cleared conditionally
- Required to retest
- Required to remediate
- Delayed
- Denied graduation

---

## 6.9.10 — Graduation Examination

The final examination may be:

- A single practical test
- A composite exam
- Several tests over multiple days
- A field exercise
- An instructor panel review
- A combination of these

The exam structure depends on village practice.

There should be no universal world-wide graduation exam.

---

## 6.9.11 — Required Techniques

Some villages may require demonstration of specific Academy techniques.

The required list should reflect:

- Local doctrine
- Curriculum
- Era
- Available training
- Professional expectations

Konoha may require techniques such as Transformation or Clone-related fundamentals, while another village may choose different baseline techniques.

Technique requirements should be institutional, not universal laws of shinobi capability.

---

## 6.9.12 — Reliable Technique Execution

For graduation purposes, executing a required technique once may not be enough.

The Academy may require evidence that the student can:

- Perform it repeatedly
- Use acceptable chakra control
- Perform safely
- Execute under mild pressure
- Recover normally afterward

Reliability matters more than a lucky success.

---

## 6.9.13 — Practical Readiness

Graduation should reflect whether the student can function in basic professional training and supervised field conditions.

Practical readiness may include:

- Following instructions
- Handling tools safely
- Basic self-defense
- Movement
- Situational awareness
- Team communication
- Mission discipline

The Academy is not certifying the student as an independent operative.

It is certifying readiness for the next supervised stage.

---

## 6.9.14 — Teamwork Requirement

A student who performs well alone but consistently sabotages team exercises may be delayed.

Graduation may require a minimum level of:

- Cooperation
- Communication
- Reliability
- Mutual support
- Role discipline

This does not require the student to be socially popular.

It requires them to function professionally with others.

---

## 6.9.15 — Conduct Requirement

Serious conduct problems may block graduation even if technical standards are met.

Examples include:

- Dangerous recklessness
- Deliberate harm
- Repeated severe insubordination
- Security violations
- Chronic dishonesty in mission-critical contexts
- Repeated refusal to follow safety procedures

Technical competence does not automatically override professional unsuitability.

---

## 6.9.16 — Medical Readiness

The student may require medical clearance for ordinary shinobi training.

A temporary injury may delay graduation without constituting academic failure.

Possible outcomes include:

- Graduation delayed until recovery
- Graduation approved with restrictions
- Alternative testing
- Medical review
- Transition to another role

Ruleset 4 governs the underlying condition.

---

## 6.9.17 — Graduation With Restrictions

Some students may graduate while carrying defined limitations.

Examples include:

- Temporary physical restrictions
- Required remedial training
- Provisional mission eligibility
- Mandatory follow-up evaluation
- Additional supervision

Restricted graduation should only occur where the student still meets essential safety requirements.

---

## 6.9.18 — Conditional Graduation

A student may be permitted to graduate conditionally if a minor requirement remains unresolved.

Conditions might include:

- Passing one remaining assessment
- Completing remedial instruction
- Demonstrating one required technique
- Maintaining conduct during probation
- Receiving medical clearance

Failure to satisfy the condition may suspend or reverse the progression depending on local policy.

---

## 6.9.19 — Early Graduation

Exceptional students may become eligible to graduate ahead of schedule.

Early graduation should require more than being ahead academically.

The Academy should consider:

- Technical competency
- Physical readiness
- Emotional maturity
- Teamwork
- Judgment
- Professional conduct
- Instructor confidence
- Available next-stage placement

A prodigy may be capable of advanced techniques while still being judged unready for professional service.

---

## 6.9.20 — Early Graduation Should Remain Exceptional

Canon establishes that very young graduates can exist.

It does not make them ordinary.

Early graduation should require an unusual combination of:

- Ability
- Opportunity
- Institutional approval
- Historical conditions
- Developmental readiness

War may increase frequency, but that should be treated as a major historical condition.

---

## 6.9.21 — Wartime Graduation

War may lower, alter, or accelerate graduation standards.

Possible changes include:

- Shortened curriculum
- Younger graduates
- Reduced academic requirements
- Greater emphasis on combat basics
- Emergency field preparation
- Faster placement

These changes should create downstream consequences.

A village graduating students early may gain manpower while producing shinobi with weaker foundations and greater risk of casualties.

---

## 6.9.22 — Emergency Graduation

Severe crises may cause students to be advanced outside normal policy.

Possible causes include:

- Invasion
- Catastrophic casualties
- Village destruction
- Sudden manpower shortage

Emergency graduation should be recorded as such.

It should not retroactively imply that normal requirements were unnecessary.

---

## 6.9.23 — Failure of Graduation Examination

Failing the final exam should not automatically end a shinobi career.

Possible outcomes include:

- Immediate retest
- Delayed retest
- Targeted remediation
- Repeat practical module
- Additional term
- Full-year repetition
- Withdrawal
- Transfer

The response should depend on the reason for failure.

---

## 6.9.24 — Partial Failure

If a student passes most graduation components but fails one area, the Academy should normally consider whether only that area needs remediation.

Example:

A student passes academics, fieldwork, teamwork, and taijutsu but fails a required technique demonstration.

The likely response should be technique remediation and retesting rather than repeating the entire Academy program.

---

## 6.9.25 — Repeated Failure

Repeated failure of critical requirements may lead to:

- Extended Academy enrollment
- Formal review
- Alternative educational pathway
- Recommendation against shinobi service
- Withdrawal
- Dismissal

The institution should distinguish lack of current readiness from permanent inability.

---

## 6.9.26 — Retesting

Retesting should occur after enough time or intervention for the result to meaningfully change.

A student should not be able to retry a failed technique every minute until random chance produces success.

Retest timing may depend on:

- Nature of failure
- Remediation
- Instructor recommendation
- Recovery
- Administrative schedule

---

## 6.9.27 — Appeals and Review

Some institutions may permit students, guardians, instructors, or clans to challenge a graduation decision.

Possible reasons include:

- Examiner misconduct
- Administrative error
- Bias
- Invalid test conditions
- Incorrect records

An appeal reviews the process.

It does not guarantee graduation.

---

## 6.9.28 — Graduation Ceremony

Villages may formally recognize graduation through:

- Ceremony
- Headband or insignia issuance
- Registration
- Oath
- Official records
- Family attendance
- Public announcement

Ceremony is culturally important but should remain separate from the underlying competency decision.

---

## 6.9.29 — Shinobi Registration

Graduation may trigger creation or activation of official records such as:

- Shinobi identification
- Registration number
- Rank record
- Personnel file
- Initial clearance
- Mission eligibility
- Pay status

Exact bureaucracy varies by village.

---

## 6.9.30 — Academy Graduation vs Genin Rank

Academy graduation and Genin status should be separate concepts.

Possible models include:

### Direct Model
Graduation immediately grants Genin rank.

### Provisional Model
Graduation creates Academy Graduate or Provisional Genin status until further evaluation.

### Placement Model
Genin status becomes active only after assignment to a team or instructor.

Different villages and eras may use different systems.

---

## 6.9.31 — Graduation Does Not Guarantee Team Placement

Even a qualified graduate may experience:

- Delayed assignment
- Instructor shortages
- Team restructuring
- Medical hold
- Political delay
- Additional evaluation

Graduation certifies readiness.

It does not force the institution to have an immediate placement available.

---

## 6.9.32 — Graduation Record

The engine should preserve:

- Graduation date
- Age
- Academy
- Exam format
- Major results
- Overall standing
- Special recognition
- Restrictions
- Early or emergency graduation status
- Failed prior attempts where relevant
- Instructor recommendations

This becomes part of the character's permanent service history.

---

## 6.9.33 — Graduation Honors and Recognition

Some Academies may recognize unusual graduates.

Possible forms include:

- Highest academic performance
- Exceptional practical performance
- Leadership recognition
- Instructor commendation
- Advanced placement recommendation

Honors may influence future opportunities but should not create automatic career success.

---

## 6.9.34 — NPC Graduation

NPC students follow the same graduation logic.

Background cohorts may be resolved in aggregate.

Important NPC graduation should reflect:

- Actual competency
- Attendance
- instruction
- exam performance
- conduct
- institutional circumstances

An NPC should not automatically graduate merely because they need to exist later as a shinobi.

---

## 6.9.35 — Hidden Graduation Deliberations

Students may not know all factors considered.

Hidden information may include:

- Instructor disagreement
- Security concerns
- Political pressure
- Placement shortages
- Internal recommendations
- Special recruitment interest

Ruleset 13 governs what is surfaced to the player.

---

## 6.9.36 — Governing Principle

Graduation should answer:

> **Has this student demonstrated enough reliable, safe, and professionally acceptable competency to leave Academy education and enter the next supervised stage of shinobi service?**

Graduation certifies readiness for progression.

It does not certify mastery.

It does not guarantee immediate team placement.

It does not prove combat superiority.

It marks the point where the institution believes the student is ready to begin becoming a professional shinobi rather than merely training to become one.
