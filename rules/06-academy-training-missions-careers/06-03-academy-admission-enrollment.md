# 6.3 — Academy Admission & Enrollment

**Ruleset:** 6 — Academy, Training, Missions & Careers  
**Status:** Provisional

## 6.3.1 — Purpose

Academy admission determines whether and how a character formally enters shinobi education.

Admission is an institutional decision, not a universal stat threshold.

A village may consider:

- Age
- Residency or citizenship
- Clan affiliation
- Family consent
- Aptitude
- Health
- Prior education
- Behavior
- Political status
- Sponsorship
- Village manpower needs
- Wartime conditions
- Available Academy capacity

Different villages and eras may weigh these factors differently.

---

## 6.3.2 — No Universal Admission Standard

There is no single world-wide Academy admission rule.

Each village may define its own:

- Minimum age
- Maximum normal entry age
- Entrance examination
- Required documentation
- Residency rules
- Tuition or public funding
- Clan privileges
- Civilian access
- Medical requirements
- Class capacity
- Wartime exceptions

The engine should use the local institution's rules rather than silently applying Konoha standards everywhere.

---

## 6.3.3 — Normal Entry Age

Each Academy should define a normal entry age range rather than one absolute age.

A typical student enters when developmentally capable of:

- Following structured instruction
- Reading or learning foundational literacy
- Participating safely in physical training
- Understanding basic rules and discipline
- Beginning controlled chakra education where appropriate

The exact age may vary by era, village, family, and historical circumstance.

Age alone does not guarantee readiness.

---

## 6.3.4 — Early Admission

Exceptionally young applicants may be admitted early.

Early admission should require an actual institutional reason, such as:

- Unusually advanced cognitive development
- Exceptional physical coordination
- Early chakra awareness
- Clan preparation
- Strong instructor recommendation
- Wartime necessity
- Demonstrated maturity

Early admission must remain rare where the culture treats it as rare.

Being talented is not by itself enough if the child lacks emotional, behavioral, or developmental readiness.

---

## 6.3.5 — Late Admission

Older applicants may enter after the normal starting age.

Possible causes include:

- Civilian childhood
- Immigration
- Family opposition
- Prior illness
- Late chakra development
- Transfer from another education system
- Former apprenticeship
- Political displacement
- War or disaster
- Personal career change

Late admission may require placement testing or accelerated foundational coursework.

Older age can provide advantages in maturity and education while also creating disadvantages in social integration or missed foundational training.

---

## 6.3.6 — Civilian Applicants

Children from non-shinobi families may apply if the village permits civilian enrollment.

Civilian applicants should not be assumed inferior.

They may simply have less prior exposure to:

- Chakra theory
- Shinobi culture
- Weapons
- Clan training
- Tactical terminology
- Physical conditioning

Some civilian students may outperform clan students through aptitude, discipline, education, or later development.

Background affects starting opportunity, not destiny.

---

## 6.3.7 — Clan Applicants

Clan children may possess advantages such as:

- Early training
- Family tutors
- Familiarity with shinobi culture
- Political sponsorship
- Better knowledge of Academy expectations
- Existing chakra practice
- Established professional networks

These advantages should arise from actual clan resources and traditions.

Clan membership should not automatically grant superior Potential or guaranteed admission unless local law or politics makes it so.

---

## 6.3.8 — Family Consent and Guardianship

Depending on village law and era, minors may require parental or guardian approval.

Possible exceptions include:

- Orphans under village guardianship
- Wartime mobilization
- State-sponsored wards
- Clan authority overriding individual guardians
- Emergency decrees
- Older adolescents allowed to self-enroll

Family consent should be treated as a legal and social factor, not merely a narrative detail.

---

## 6.3.9 — Orphans and Wards of the Village

Orphans may have special enrollment procedures.

A village may:

- Sponsor tuition and supplies
- Assign guardians
- Encourage Academy enrollment
- Require state approval
- Provide residential support
- Place children in civilian education instead

Being orphaned should not automatically force a shinobi career.

Local policy and individual circumstances determine whether Academy enrollment occurs.

---

## 6.3.10 — Residency and Village Affiliation

Most hidden villages have security reasons to control who receives shinobi training.

Admission may require:

- Village residency
- National citizenship
- Family registration
- Background investigation
- Loyalty screening
- Sponsorship
- Security approval

Foreign applicants may face greater scrutiny.

The more sensitive the training environment, the more likely the institution is to restrict access.

---

## 6.3.11 — Foreign and Immigrant Applicants

Foreign-born applicants may be:

- Fully accepted
- Accepted after residency
- Accepted with sponsorship
- Restricted from certain curriculum
- Subject to loyalty review
- Denied entirely

The response should depend on:

- Relations between nations
- Applicant age
- Family history
- Security concerns
- Village culture
- Current leadership
- War or peace

The engine should not treat foreign origin as automatically disqualifying or automatically irrelevant.

---

## 6.3.12 — Transfer Students

Students may transfer between Academies when politically and administratively possible.

Transfer evaluation may examine:

- Previous coursework
- Training records
- Age
- Technique competency
- Academic progress
- Security background
- Reason for transfer
- Curriculum differences

A transfer student may enter the equivalent cohort, move forward, move backward, or receive individualized placement.

---

## 6.3.13 — Academic Readiness

Admission may test foundational academic ability.

Possible areas include:

- Literacy
- Numeracy
- Memory
- Instruction-following
- Basic reasoning
- Communication

Poor academic readiness does not necessarily require rejection.

Possible alternatives include:

- Preparatory education
- Remedial classes
- Delayed admission
- Conditional enrollment
- Additional tutoring

This prevents Intelligence from functioning as an arbitrary Academy entrance lock.

---

## 6.3.14 — Physical Readiness

A village may evaluate whether a child can safely participate in training.

This may include:

- Basic coordination
- Mobility
- Endurance
- Injury status
- Motor control

Physical weakness should not automatically bar admission if accommodation or development is possible.

The question is usually whether the student can safely participate and progress, not whether they are already combat-capable.

---

## 6.3.15 — Chakra Readiness

Academies may assess chakra-related aptitude differently.

Possible policies include:

- No chakra requirement at admission
- Basic chakra detection
- Minimum ability to access chakra
- Later chakra evaluation after enrollment
- Special support for delayed development

A child should not necessarily be rejected because they cannot yet perform controlled chakra techniques.

The Academy exists partly to teach these skills.

---

## 6.3.16 — Medical Evaluation

Some villages may require medical screening before admission.

This can identify:

- Conditions requiring accommodation
- Training restrictions
- Chronic illness
- Injury
- Sensory impairment
- Chakra abnormalities
- Developmental concerns

Medical screening should primarily determine safe participation and needed support.

It should not automatically exclude disabled or chronically ill characters unless the institution has a legitimate reason and local policy supports that exclusion.

---

## 6.3.17 — Behavioral and Psychological Readiness

Institutions may consider whether the applicant can function in a structured and potentially dangerous training environment.

Relevant factors may include:

- Ability to follow safety instructions
- Severe aggression
- Persistent rule-breaking
- Emotional regulation
- Cooperation
- Maturity

This is not a universal "mental fitness score."

Behavior should be evaluated contextually and with uncertainty.

A difficult child may still be admitted with supervision or support.

---

## 6.3.18 — Aptitude Screening

Some Academies may perform entrance aptitude testing.

Aptitude screening can sample:

- Coordination
- Memory
- Perception
- Problem-solving
- Physical aptitude
- Chakra sensitivity
- Tactical reasoning
- Learning speed

The purpose is normally placement and risk assessment, not perfect prediction of future ability.

Potential should never be fully visible to institutions or to the player.

A child who tests poorly may later develop dramatically.

---

## 6.3.19 — Entrance Examinations

Where formal entrance exams exist, they may include:

- Written questions
- Physical exercises
- Coordination tasks
- Interviews
- Basic chakra exercises
- Observation periods
- Instructor evaluation

The exam's actual difficulty depends on the institution.

Failure should produce an outcome such as rejection, delayed admission, remedial preparation, or retesting rather than an abstract game-over.

---

## 6.3.20 — Capacity and Class Size

Even qualified applicants may encounter limited Academy capacity.

Capacity can depend on:

- Number of instructors
- Facilities
- Funding
- Village population
- Political priorities
- War losses
- Expected military demand

This creates circumstances where admission may become more selective or where class sizes become larger and educational quality suffers.

---

## 6.3.21 — Sponsorship and Recommendation

A recommendation may improve access without guaranteeing admission.

Potential sponsors include:

- Clan leaders
- Jōnin
- Academy instructors
- Government officials
- Department heads
- Village leadership

A recommendation may influence:

- Whether an unusual applicant is considered
- Access to early testing
- Scholarship or support
- Placement
- Administrative exceptions

Recommendations should reflect real relationships and influence.

---

## 6.3.22 — Political Influence and Favoritism

Institutions may sometimes admit applicants because of:

- Clan power
- Family reputation
- Political pressure
- Leadership intervention
- Patronage
- Diplomatic considerations

Such decisions are possible but not mandatory.

Favoritism should emerge from world politics and NPC behavior rather than being randomly assigned for drama.

It may also create later resentment or scrutiny.

---

## 6.3.23 — Tuition, Equipment, and Financial Access

Academies may be:

- Fully village-funded
- Partially subsidized
- Fee-based
- Clan-sponsored
- Scholarship-supported

Enrollment costs may include:

- Tuition
- Uniforms
- Weapons
- Books
- Meals
- Training supplies
- Transportation

Where financial barriers exist, they should meaningfully affect access.

A village that considers shinobi education a strategic military investment may heavily subsidize training.

---

## 6.3.24 — Conditional Admission

Applicants who do not fully meet ordinary standards may receive conditional enrollment.

Conditions might include:

- Academic remediation
- Medical monitoring
- Behavioral review
- Probationary attendance
- Sponsorship
- Minimum performance
- Restricted participation

Conditional admission creates a middle state between full acceptance and rejection.

---

## 6.3.25 — Rejection

Rejection should have a stated or inferable reason where the institution would reasonably provide one.

Possible causes include:

- Too young
- Not developmentally ready
- Failed entrance requirements
- Security concerns
- No available capacity
- Residency restrictions
- Guardian refusal
- Severe medical limitation
- Disciplinary history
- Political exclusion

Rejection does not permanently end the character's possibility of becoming a shinobi unless the reason itself is permanent.

---

## 6.3.26 — Retesting and Reapplication

Rejected applicants may sometimes reapply.

The institution should define:

- Waiting period
- Maximum attempts if any
- Conditions for reconsideration
- Required preparation
- Whether previous results remain on record

Repeated attempts consume real time and can affect relationships, confidence, and alternative life paths.

---

## 6.3.27 — Wartime Admission

War can alter Academy policy significantly.

Possible changes include:

- Lower entry age
- Larger class sizes
- Shortened curriculum
- Reduced entrance standards
- State-directed enrollment
- Accelerated graduation
- Increased funding
- Greater physical emphasis
- Reduced individual instruction

These changes should create consequences.

Expanding enrollment quickly may increase manpower while reducing educational quality and increasing later casualties.

---

## 6.3.28 — Crisis and Emergency Exceptions

Non-war crises may also alter admission.

Examples include:

- Natural disasters
- Village destruction
- Epidemics
- Major population loss
- Political collapse
- Refugee influx
- Sudden military threats

Emergency policies should be temporary unless later institutionalized.

---

## 6.3.29 — Enrollment Date and Cohort

Upon admission, the engine should record:

- Enrollment date
- Age at enrollment
- Academy
- Cohort or class
- Entry pathway
- Any conditions
- Sponsor if relevant
- Prior education
- Starting accommodations
- Special placement

This creates persistent educational history.

---

## 6.3.30 — Cohort Placement

Students should normally be placed based on some combination of:

- Age
- Previous education
- Skill
- maturity
- Curriculum progress
- Academy policy

Age alone should not universally determine placement.

Accelerated, delayed, remedial, and transfer students may be assigned differently.

---

## 6.3.31 — Enrollment Does Not Equal Equal Starting Ability

Students entering the same class may begin with very different capabilities.

One student may already know:

- Basic weapon handling
- Chakra exercises
- Clan theory
- Survival skills

Another may be encountering those ideas for the first time.

The Academy provides a shared educational structure, not identical starting conditions.

---

## 6.3.32 — Hidden Institutional Evaluation

Some admission information may remain hidden from the player.

Examples include:

- Instructor concerns
- Political objections
- Security flags
- Internal aptitude estimates
- Sponsor influence
- Capacity prioritization

The player should see the official outcome and whatever explanation their character reasonably receives.

Ruleset 13 determines how much meta-information may be surfaced.

---

## 6.3.33 — NPC Admission

NPCs use the same admission logic.

Background NPCs may be resolved abstractly using:

- Local admission rate
- Family background
- aptitude
- Village demand
- Social access
- Historical conditions

Important NPC admissions should be simulated in greater detail when relevant.

---

## 6.3.34 — Core Admission Principle

Academy admission should answer:

> **Would this institution, in this village, during this era, plausibly accept this applicant under current conditions?**

The answer should come from the world state rather than from a universal game rule.

Talent matters.

Age matters.

Background matters.

Politics matter.

Capacity matters.

War matters.

But no single factor should automatically decide every case.
