# 6.8 — Academy Tests, Practical Assessments & Examinations

**Ruleset:** 6 — Academy, Training, Missions & Careers  
**Status:** Provisional

## 6.8.1 — Purpose

Academy assessments measure whether students can demonstrate required knowledge and competencies.

Assessment may evaluate:

- Academic knowledge
- Practical execution
- Physical readiness
- Chakra control
- Technique use
- Tactical judgment
- Teamwork
- Safety
- Professional conduct
- Field application

Assessments should provide evidence of readiness rather than function as arbitrary progression gates.

---

## 6.8.2 — Assessment Types

The Academy may use:

- Written tests
- Oral examinations
- Practical demonstrations
- Physical evaluations
- Sparring assessments
- Chakra-control tests
- Technique demonstrations
- Team exercises
- Field exercises
- Survival assessments
- Instructor observation
- Composite examinations

Different competencies should be tested through appropriate methods.

---

## 6.8.3 — Knowledge Tests

Written or oral exams may assess:

- History
- Law
- Geography
- Tactics
- Chakra theory
- Mission procedure
- First aid
- Professional conduct

Knowledge tests should not substitute for practical competency where actual performance matters.

---

## 6.8.4 — Practical Demonstrations

Practical assessments require the student to perform a skill under controlled conditions.

Examples include:

- Throwing weapons
- Executing a technique
- Demonstrating hand seals
- Performing a movement drill
- Applying first aid
- Navigating a course
- Identifying tracks
- Using stealth

Ruleset 2 resolves uncertain execution where needed.

---

## 6.8.5 — Physical Assessments

Physical evaluations may measure:

- Endurance
- Speed
- Coordination
- Mobility
- Strength
- Recovery
- Obstacle performance

These should be appropriate to age and curriculum stage.

A physical test should measure relevant readiness, not simply reward naturally gifted students.

---

## 6.8.6 — Chakra-Control Assessments

Chakra evaluations may test:

- Awareness
- Output control
- Precision
- Stability
- Sustained use
- Recovery after expenditure

The Academy should evaluate practical competency without exposing hidden exact Chakra Control values to the institution or player.

---

## 6.8.7 — Technique Demonstrations

When a technique is required, the assessment should consider more than binary success.

Relevant factors may include:

- Correct execution
- Reliability
- Chakra efficiency
- Control
- Speed
- Safety
- Ability to repeat the technique
- Performance under mild pressure

A student who succeeds once by chance may not yet be considered competent.

---

## 6.8.8 — Sparring Assessments

Sparring may evaluate:

- Distance management
- Defense
- Decision-making
- Technique selection
- Restraint
- Adaptability
- Awareness
- Safety

Winning is not necessarily the primary objective.

A student may lose a spar and still perform very well.

---

## 6.8.9 — Team Assessments

Team exercises should evaluate:

- Communication
- Role assignment
- Coordination
- Information sharing
- Adaptation
- Mutual support
- Objective focus

Individual brilliance should not automatically compensate for sabotaging the team.

---

## 6.8.10 — Field Assessments

Field evaluations place students in more realistic environments.

Examples include:

- Navigation
- Tracking
- Survival
- Reconnaissance
- Mock escort
- Capture objectives
- Stealth exercises
- Rescue scenarios

Field tests may contain uncertainty and incomplete information.

They should remain age- and stage-appropriate.

---

## 6.8.11 — Composite Exams

Major examinations may combine several domains.

For example:

1. Written knowledge
2. Technique demonstration
3. Physical course
4. Team field exercise
5. Instructor review

Composite exams are useful for major advancement decisions because they reduce dependence on one narrow skill.

---

## 6.8.12 — Difficulty Should Match Purpose

Assessment difficulty should be calibrated to what the exam is intended to establish.

A routine term test should measure current curriculum.

A graduation exam should test minimum readiness for Genin service.

An advanced-placement exam should intentionally exceed ordinary curriculum.

The engine should not scale exams to the player's strength merely to keep them challenging.

---

## 6.8.13 — Standardized vs Instructor-Created Tests

Academies may use:

- Standardized examinations
- Instructor-designed assessments
- Department-wide tests
- Village-mandated final exams

Standardization improves consistency.

Instructor-created tests may better reflect local teaching.

Both can contain flaws.

---

## 6.8.14 — Test Conditions Matter

Performance may be affected by:

- Fatigue
- Injury
- Stress
- Illness
- Poor sleep
- Hunger
- Environmental conditions
- Equipment
- Time pressure

These conditions should influence the actual attempt rather than silently altering final scores.

---

## 6.8.15 — Examination Pressure

Some students perform differently under formal evaluation than in ordinary training.

Possible effects include:

- Anxiety
- Overconfidence
- Hesitation
- Rushing
- Improved focus
- Strong competitive performance

One exam should therefore not always outweigh all prior evidence.

---

## 6.8.16 — Reliability Over One-Time Success

For important competencies, the Academy should prefer evidence of repeatability.

A student may need to:

- Perform multiple successful repetitions
- Demonstrate competency on different days
- Succeed under varied conditions
- Show prior classroom consistency

This is especially important for dangerous techniques and safety-critical skills.

---

## 6.8.17 — Hidden Observation

Not every assessment needs to be announced.

Instructors may observe:

- Leadership
- Honesty
- Safety
- Team behavior
- Response to failure
- Treatment of weaker classmates
- Adaptability

These observations should not become secret gotcha tests designed solely to trick the player.

They should reflect normal professional evaluation.

---

## 6.8.18 — Instructor Judgment

Many assessments require human evaluation.

Judgment may consider:

- Quality
- Safety
- Decision-making
- Improvement
- Context
- Professional behavior

Instructor bias and error remain possible under Ruleset 6.7.

---

## 6.8.19 — Objective Scoring

Some tests can be measured more directly.

Examples include:

- Time
- Accuracy
- Number of correct answers
- Distance
- Repetitions
- Completion of required steps

Objective metrics reduce ambiguity but do not always capture full competence.

---

## 6.8.20 — Pass Thresholds

Each assessment should define what constitutes:

- Passing
- Conditional pass
- Failure
- Exceptional performance

Thresholds depend on purpose and institutional policy.

Passing means the student met the required standard, not that they achieved mastery.

---

## 6.8.21 — Partial Success

Not every exam result must be binary.

A student may:

- Pass some components
- Fail one critical component
- Require remediation
- Receive conditional advancement
- Need only a partial retest

This should be preferred over forcing complete repetition when unnecessary.

---

## 6.8.22 — Critical Failures

Some mistakes may cause automatic failure regardless of total score.

Examples include:

- Dangerous loss of control
- Severe safety violation
- Deliberate harm
- Abandoning an essential objective
- Serious misconduct

Critical failures should be explicitly tied to essential standards.

---

## 6.8.23 — Retesting

Students may be allowed to retest after:

- Additional practice
- Remediation
- Recovery from illness
- Administrative review

Retesting consumes time.

The Academy may impose waiting periods or limits depending on the exam.

---

## 6.8.24 — Multiple Attempts

Repeated attempts should not guarantee eventual success.

A student may improve through practice, but:

- Fatigue
- discouragement
- persistent deficiencies
- poor instruction
- inadequate prerequisites

can continue to matter.

The engine should resolve each meaningful attempt under current conditions.

---

## 6.8.25 — Accommodations

Students may receive reasonable assessment accommodations for:

- Injury
- Disability
- Sensory limitation
- Temporary illness
- Language issues
- Documented learning needs

Accommodation should change how competency is measured without automatically lowering essential professional standards.

---

## 6.8.26 — Alternative Assessment

Where a standard test poorly measures a student's ability, an institution may allow another method.

Examples include:

- Oral instead of written examination
- Modified physical test
- Alternate technique demonstration
- Extended time
- Different equipment

This depends on Academy policy and the competency being tested.

---

## 6.8.27 — Cheating

Students may attempt to cheat.

Possible forms include:

- Copying answers
- Concealed notes
- Unauthorized assistance
- Equipment tampering
- Using prohibited techniques
- Impersonation
- Manipulating another student's performance

Whether cheating succeeds is resolved normally.

Discovery creates academic and disciplinary consequences.

---

## 6.8.28 — Permitted Deception

Some shinobi examinations may intentionally reward observation, information gathering, or clever rule interpretation.

The exam must distinguish between:

- Prohibited cheating
- Allowed deception
- Intended problem-solving

This distinction should be established by the test's actual rules and purpose.

---

## 6.8.29 — Examiner Interference

Evaluators should not arbitrarily manipulate outcomes.

However, legitimate intervention may occur for:

- Safety
- Rule violations
- Equipment failure
- Unexpected emergencies
- Administrative errors

Any intervention should become part of the assessment record.

---

## 6.8.30 — Invalidated Assessments

An exam may be invalidated because of:

- Equipment failure
- Instructor error
- Security breach
- External attack
- Severe weather
- Rule ambiguity
- Administrative mistake

Invalidation should normally lead to reassessment rather than treating the result as valid.

---

## 6.8.31 — Exam Records

Important assessments should preserve:

- Date
- Exam type
- Components
- Result
- Evaluators
- Exceptional performance
- Critical failures
- Accommodations
- Retest requirements
- Relevant comments

Routine minor quizzes need not remain permanent.

---

## 6.8.32 — Player-Facing Feedback

The student should normally receive enough information to understand:

- Whether they passed
- What they did well
- What needs improvement
- Whether remediation is required
- When they may retest

Hidden evaluator notes may remain internal.

Ruleset 13 controls additional meta-information.

---

## 6.8.33 — NPC Assessments

NPC students undergo the same types of assessment.

Background students may be resolved abstractly.

Important NPC exam outcomes should reflect their actual competency, condition, and circumstances rather than predetermined narrative roles.

---

## 6.8.34 — Governing Principle

Academy assessment should answer:

> **Can this student reliably demonstrate the knowledge, judgment, and practical competency this institution currently requires?**

Tests should measure readiness.

They should not exist merely to create dramatic obstacles, nor should one lucky or unlucky roll completely redefine years of accumulated evidence without a compelling reason.
