# 6.13 — Training Time & Scheduling

**Ruleset:** 6 — Academy, Training, Missions & Careers  
**Status:** Provisional

## 6.13.1 — Purpose

Training must occur inside the same calendar used for every other activity.

A character's available training time is constrained by:

- Sleep
- Academy
- Missions
- Employment
- Travel
- Meals
- Hygiene
- Medical care
- Recovery
- Relationships
- Family obligations
- Administrative duties
- Personal recreation

Training therefore competes with the rest of life.

---

## 6.13.2 — No Separate Training Clock

The simulator must never create a hidden pool of "training hours" outside normal time.

If a character trains from 18:00 to 20:00, those two hours are unavailable for other activities.

Every training action must occupy real calendar time.

---

## 6.13.3 — Daily Time Budget

A day contains finite usable time.

The engine should account for:

- Required obligations
- Biological needs
- Transit
- Recovery
- Optional activities
- Training

The player may choose an intense schedule, but the engine must preserve the consequences of doing so.

---

## 6.13.4 — Sleep Is a Real Constraint

Characters require age- and condition-appropriate sleep.

Chronic sleep reduction may affect:

- Concentration
- Learning quality
- Reaction time
- Mood
- Injury risk
- Chakra recovery
- Physical recovery
- Decision-making

The engine should not allow sleep to be endlessly sacrificed without cumulative consequences.

---

## 6.13.5 — Meals, Hygiene, and Basic Needs

Routine biological needs should consume some time even when not individually roleplayed.

The simulator does not need to narrate every meal.

However, compressed scheduling should preserve enough time for ordinary daily functioning.

A schedule that allocates every waking minute to school, missions, and training is not valid simply because meals were omitted from the narration.

---

## 6.13.6 — Fixed Obligations

Some activities occupy predetermined blocks.

Examples include:

- Academy attendance
- Scheduled missions
- Work shifts
- Department duty
- Instructor appointments
- Medical treatment
- Formal meetings

Training must fit around these unless the obligation itself is changed.

---

## 6.13.7 — Flexible Time

After fixed obligations and basic needs, characters may have flexible time.

This can be spent on:

- Training
- Relationships
- Rest
- Study
- Hobbies
- Errands
- Travel
- Personal projects

This is the main pool from which voluntary training is scheduled.

---

## 6.13.8 — Minimum Useful Session Length

Some training activities need enough uninterrupted time to be productive.

Examples:

- Brief hand-seal drills may work in short sessions.
- Sparring requires setup, partner availability, and cooldown.
- Field survival training may require hours.
- Sealing experiments may require substantial preparation.

There should be no universal minimum duration.

The useful session length depends on the activity.

---

## 6.13.9 — Setup and Transition Time

Training may require time beyond the core exercise.

Possible overhead includes:

- Traveling to a training ground
- Preparing equipment
- Warming up
- Setting seals or targets
- Reviewing instructions
- Cleanup
- Medical check
- Cooldown

The engine should account for meaningful overhead when it matters.

---

## 6.13.10 — Training Frequency

Some skills benefit from frequent short practice.

Others benefit more from fewer long sessions.

Examples:

- Hand seals may benefit from daily repetition.
- Strength training may require recovery days.
- Long field exercises cannot be repeated constantly.
- Complex theory may benefit from regular study plus practical application.

Training frequency should reflect the nature of the target.

---

## 6.13.11 — Consecutive Training Days

Training every day is not inherently invalid.

However, the effect depends on:

- Intensity
- Target
- Age
- Recovery
- Injury
- Nutrition
- Sleep
- Training method

Light technical practice may be sustainable daily.

Repeated maximal conditioning may not be.

---

## 6.13.12 — Recovery Time

Training can create recovery demand.

Recovery may include:

- Sleep
- Rest
- Reduced-intensity activity
- Nutrition
- Medical treatment
- Chakra recovery

The required recovery depends on the session.

Ruleset 4 governs injuries.

Ruleset 6 governs scheduling consequences.

---

## 6.13.13 — Same-Day Multiple Sessions

Characters may train more than once per day.

Possible structure:

- Morning conditioning
- Afternoon Academy
- Evening technique practice

However, later sessions may be affected by:

- Accumulated fatigue
- Reduced concentration
- Depleted chakra
- Physical soreness

The engine should not treat each session as if the character began fully fresh.

---

## 6.13.14 — Training Order Matters

The order of activities can affect performance.

Examples:

- Heavy conditioning before sparring may reduce sparring quality.
- Technique practice before exhaustion may improve precision.
- Deliberate fatigue training may intentionally reverse this order.

Schedule design can therefore matter strategically.

---

## 6.13.15 — Training Before Missions

Training immediately before deployment may reduce mission readiness if it causes:

- Fatigue
- Injury
- Chakra depletion
- Poor sleep

Characters and instructors should consider upcoming obligations when scheduling hard sessions.

---

## 6.13.16 — Training After Missions

Post-mission training may be limited by:

- Exhaustion
- Injury
- Debriefing
- Travel
- Emotional strain
- Equipment maintenance

A character returning from a difficult mission should not automatically have a full normal training day remaining.

---

## 6.13.17 — Academy Student Schedules

Academy students must schedule training around:

- School
- Homework
- Clan instruction
- Family obligations
- Sleep
- Social development

This naturally limits how much extra training a child can perform.

Prodigious development should generally require unusual efficiency, support, talent, opportunity, or sacrifice rather than impossible schedules.

---

## 6.13.18 — Active Shinobi Schedules

Active shinobi schedules are irregular.

Their training availability may vary according to:

- Mission tempo
- Travel
- Standby duty
- Department work
- Recovery
- Team exercises

One week may allow extensive training.

Another may allow almost none.

---

## 6.13.19 — Instructor Scheduling

Training with a teacher requires both parties to be available.

Instructor access may be constrained by:

- Missions
- Work
- Other students
- Family
- Injury
- Administrative duty

A willing mentor does not imply unlimited access.

---

## 6.13.20 — Partner Scheduling

Partner training also requires overlapping availability.

This matters for:

- Sparring
- Team drills
- Medical practice
- Tracking exercises
- Cooperative techniques

The engine should not assume NPCs are always free whenever the player wants to train.

---

## 6.13.21 — Facility Scheduling

Some facilities may need reservations or shared use.

Examples include:

- Sparring halls
- Medical labs
- Weapons ranges
- Sealing rooms
- Specialized training grounds

Limited access can influence when training occurs.

---

## 6.13.22 — Weather and Environmental Scheduling

Some training may depend on conditions.

Examples:

- Water training requires suitable access.
- Outdoor tracking may be altered by rain.
- Extreme heat may limit conditioning.
- Night exercises require nighttime.

The calendar and environment should matter when relevant.

---

## 6.13.23 — Interruptions

Training plans may be interrupted by:

- Missions
- Injury
- Family events
- Emergencies
- Instructor absence
- Weather
- Political events
- Village attacks

Interrupted time should not be automatically recovered later.

The character must reschedule if they still want the training.

---

## 6.13.24 — Missed Sessions

Missing one session should rarely matter much.

Repeated missed sessions may reduce:

- Consistency
- Momentum
- Instructor confidence
- Plan effectiveness

The effect should depend on the type of training.

---

## 6.13.25 — Flexible Training Plans

Training plans should support adjustment.

A character may:

- Move sessions
- Lower intensity
- Replace one method
- Add rest
- Pause after injury
- Increase focus before an exam

Good plans adapt to changing circumstances.

---

## 6.13.26 — Multi-Day and Multi-Week Plans

Long-term training may be organized across days or weeks.

A plan may include:

- Training days
- Rest days
- Instructor sessions
- Independent practice
- Evaluation days
- Recovery periods

This supports realistic development without resolving every hour individually.

---

## 6.13.27 — Overbooking

The engine should reject or correct schedules that physically overlap.

A character cannot:

- Attend Academy
- Spar with a teammate
- Work a shift
- And meet a mentor

at the same time.

Conflicts should require a choice.

---

## 6.13.28 — Unrealistically Dense Schedules

Even schedules without direct overlap can be unrealistic.

Example:

- Academy: 8 hours
- Travel: 2 hours
- Training: 6 hours
- Homework: 2 hours
- Sleep: 4 hours
- No meals or rest

The engine may allow this briefly if physically possible, but it should produce serious fatigue and sustainability problems.

---

## 6.13.29 — Sustainable vs Temporary Schedules

The engine should distinguish:

### Sustainable Schedule
Can reasonably continue long-term.

### Intensive Schedule
Can be maintained temporarily with elevated fatigue.

### Unsustainable Schedule
Likely to produce rapid performance decline, injury, or forced rest.

This classification depends on the character and activities.

---

## 6.13.30 — Intentional Training Camps

Characters may deliberately enter short periods of concentrated training.

Examples include:

- Pre-exam preparation
- Team training camp
- Specialist intensive
- Recovery-to-return program

Training camps can temporarily prioritize development while reducing normal social, work, or mission activity.

They do not eliminate recovery needs.

---

## 6.13.31 — Downtime

Periods with few obligations create valuable training opportunities.

Downtime may occur:

- Between missions
- During school breaks
- During leave
- During travel pauses
- During peacetime low demand

Characters decide how much of this time to devote to training versus other parts of life.

---

## 6.13.32 — Schedule Opportunity Cost

Every substantial training commitment excludes something else.

Possible costs include:

- Less sleep
- Less social time
- Fewer hobbies
- Lost work
- Reduced family time
- Reduced recovery
- Missed opportunities

This opportunity cost is a core balancing mechanism.

---

## 6.13.33 — Time Compression

Long schedules may be compressed into weekly or monthly blocks.

The engine should still preserve:

- Total training time
- Training frequency
- Intensity
- Recovery
- Missed sessions
- Major interruptions
- Significant choices

Compression changes narration, not the time economy.

---

## 6.13.34 — Player Scheduling Interface

The player should be able to express schedules naturally.

Examples:

- Train taijutsu for two hours after class.
- Spend mornings on conditioning this week.
- Meet the instructor every third day.
- Rest completely tomorrow.
- Use the next two weeks primarily for chakra control.

The engine should translate these intentions into plausible calendar blocks.

---

## 6.13.35 — NPC Scheduling

NPCs also have finite schedules.

Their training time should reflect:

- Work
- Missions
- Family
- Relationships
- Health
- Motivation
- Access

NPCs should not gain unrealistic development through invisible unlimited training time.

---

## 6.13.36 — Governing Principle

Training scheduling should answer:

> **When does this character actually have time to train, and what are they giving up or risking to make that time available?**

Time is one of the simulation's most important resources.

The engine should reward disciplined scheduling and meaningful sacrifice while preventing impossible daily routines and consequence-free grinding.
