# 6.12 — Training Action Framework

**Ruleset:** 6 — Academy, Training, Missions & Careers  
**Status:** Provisional

## 6.12.1 — Purpose

The Training Action Framework defines how any deliberate training activity is represented in the simulation.

A training action should identify:

- What is being trained
- Why it is being trained
- How it is being trained
- How long it lasts
- Where it occurs
- Who is involved
- What resources are available
- How difficult the training is
- What condition the trainee is in
- What risks or opportunity costs exist

Ruleset 6 defines the training situation.

Ruleset 1 determines growth.

Ruleset 2 resolves uncertain performance.

Ruleset 3 governs chakra and technique-specific mechanics.

---

## 6.12.2 — No Generic "Train" Action

The engine should not allow a meaningless action such as:

> Train for 4 hours.

Training must have a defined target and method.

Examples:

- Practice basic chakra control with leaf-balancing drills
- Improve taijutsu footwork through controlled sparring
- Study fire-nature theory with an instructor
- Drill a specific hand-seal sequence
- Practice shuriken accuracy at increasing distance
- Conduct endurance running through forest terrain

Specificity determines what can plausibly improve.

---

## 6.12.3 — Training Target

Every training action should identify one or more targets.

Targets may include:

- Attribute development
- Skill development
- Technique learning
- Technique refinement
- Chakra Control
- Tactical knowledge
- Professional qualification
- Physical conditioning
- Team coordination

One action may affect multiple areas, but it should still have a primary purpose.

---

## 6.12.4 — Primary and Secondary Effects

Training may have:

### Primary Target
The main intended development.

### Secondary Effects
Additional growth produced naturally by the activity.

Example:

Tree climbing primarily trains Chakra Control.

It may also secondarily train:

- Balance
- Endurance
- Concentration

Secondary effects should be smaller unless the activity genuinely trains them substantially.

---

## 6.12.5 — Training Method

The method describes what the character actually does.

Common methods include:

- Repetition
- Drills
- Sparring
- Study
- Observation
- Meditation
- Conditioning
- Guided instruction
- Experimentation
- Simulation
- Field practice
- Partner exercises
- Team exercises

Different methods suit different targets.

---

## 6.12.6 — Instruction vs Independent Practice

Training should distinguish:

### Independent Practice
The character trains without active instruction.

### Guided Training
An instructor observes and provides correction.

### Demonstration-Based Training
The learner studies someone performing the skill.

### Collaborative Training
Peers train together and exchange feedback.

### Apprenticeship
Long-term structured instruction under a mentor.

Instruction can improve efficiency, error correction, and access, but does not guarantee progress.

---

## 6.12.7 — Training Objective

The trainee may pursue different objectives even within the same skill.

Examples include:

- Learn the basics
- Improve consistency
- Increase speed
- Increase efficiency
- Increase precision
- Reduce chakra cost
- Perform under pressure
- Adapt the technique
- Correct a weakness

This allows mastery to develop dimensionally instead of through one generic "skill up."

---

## 6.12.8 — Prerequisite Check

Before meaningful training begins, the engine should verify relevant prerequisites.

These may include:

- Required Attributes
- Required Skills
- Required Chakra Control
- Technique knowledge
- Physical capability
- Equipment
- Instructor access
- Prior theory
- Medical clearance

If prerequisites are missing, the action may:

- Fail to produce useful progress
- Produce only foundational progress
- Reveal what prerequisite is missing
- Create elevated risk

The engine should not always block the attempt outright.

---

## 6.12.9 — Training Difficulty

Training should have a difficulty relative to the trainee.

A useful conceptual range is:

- Trivial
- Easy
- Productive
- Challenging
- Severe
- Beyond Current Capacity

The best growth often occurs when the activity is difficult enough to demand adaptation but not so difficult that meaningful practice collapses.

Ruleset 6.15 develops this further.

---

## 6.12.10 — Duration

Training consumes actual simulation time.

A training action must have a duration or natural endpoint.

Examples:

- 20-minute drill
- 2-hour practice session
- Full afternoon
- Multi-day training camp
- 3-week apprenticeship block

Longer sessions do not scale linearly forever.

Fatigue and diminishing returns apply.

---

## 6.12.11 — Training Intensity

Training intensity should be distinct from duration.

Two characters can train for two hours at very different intensity.

Useful categories may include:

- Light
- Moderate
- Hard
- Very Hard
- Maximum / Near-Limit

Higher intensity can increase stimulus but also increases:

- Fatigue
- Error
- Injury risk
- Recovery needs

---

## 6.12.12 — Environment

Training location can materially affect the session.

Relevant factors include:

- Terrain
- Weather
- Space
- Privacy
- Noise
- Safety
- Equipment
- Environmental hazards

A training yard, forest, hospital lab, river, and battlefield all provide different opportunities and risks.

---

## 6.12.13 — Facilities and Equipment

Some training requires or benefits from resources such as:

- Targets
- Weights
- Practice weapons
- Medical equipment
- Scrolls
- Sealing materials
- Protective gear
- Chakra-testing tools

Lack of ideal resources may reduce efficiency or force improvisation.

---

## 6.12.14 — Instructor Contribution

When an instructor is present, the engine should consider:

- Subject competence
- Teaching Skill
- Attention available
- Compatibility
- Ability to demonstrate
- Ability to diagnose mistakes
- Willingness to teach

Instructor quality changes the training environment rather than applying a flat XP multiplier.

---

## 6.12.15 — Training Partners

Partners can alter training quality.

A partner may provide:

- Resistance
- Feedback
- Competition
- Cooperation
- Unpredictability
- Team coordination practice

Partner ability matters.

A much weaker partner may provide little challenge.

A much stronger partner may overwhelm the trainee unless they deliberately scale the exercise.

---

## 6.12.16 — Sparring as Training

Sparring should specify:

- Rules
- Intensity
- Allowed techniques
- Win conditions
- Safety limits
- Instructor supervision
- Protective equipment

Sparring can train:

- Timing
- Adaptation
- Defense
- Decision-making
- Technique application
- Stress response

It should not be treated as identical to real combat.

---

## 6.12.17 — Study and Theory

Training can be nonphysical.

Study may improve:

- Tactical knowledge
- Chakra theory
- Medical knowledge
- Sealing knowledge
- History
- Intelligence methods
- Technique understanding

The effectiveness of study depends on:

- Material quality
- Prior knowledge
- Intelligence-related abilities
- Instructor support
- Concentration
- Time

---

## 6.12.18 — Observation

Characters may learn by watching others.

Observation may provide:

- Conceptual understanding
- Timing insight
- Tactical insight
- Technique familiarity

Observation alone should rarely grant full practical competency.

Knowing what something looks like is not the same as being able to do it.

---

## 6.12.19 — Experimentation

Characters may attempt to discover:

- New applications
- Technique variants
- Better methods
- Personal adaptations
- Non-canon techniques

Experimentation should require:

- Appropriate knowledge
- Time
- Trial and error
- Relevant Skills
- Risk where applicable

Ruleset 3 governs technique creation and modification.

---

## 6.12.20 — Training While Fatigued

Fatigue may reduce:

- Precision
- Learning quality
- Concentration
- Safe execution

Some intentional fatigue training may improve:

- Endurance
- Stress tolerance
- Performance under exhaustion

But training while exhausted should not universally produce bonus growth.

---

## 6.12.21 — Training While Injured

Injury can:

- Prevent training
- Restrict available methods
- Reduce intensity
- Increase reinjury risk
- Redirect training toward unaffected areas

Ruleset 4 determines the medical limits.

Ruleset 6 determines what training remains possible.

---

## 6.12.22 — Training Risks

Training can carry consequences.

Potential risks include:

- Fatigue
- Overuse
- Injury
- Chakra exhaustion
- Equipment damage
- Psychological strain
- Time loss
- Relationship neglect

Dangerous training should only be worthwhile when its actual benefits justify the risk.

---

## 6.12.23 — Session Outcome

A training action may result in:

- Strong progress
- Normal progress
- Limited progress
- No meaningful progress
- Insight without immediate improvement
- Discovery of a weakness
- Technique breakthrough
- Fatigue or injury

Ruleset 1 determines quantitative progression.

Ruleset 6 records the training context and consequences.

---

## 6.12.24 — Insight

Some sessions should produce knowledge rather than immediate Skill gain.

Examples:

- Realizing hand-seal timing is the problem
- Discovering insufficient chakra control
- Identifying a stance error
- Understanding why a technique fails

Insight may improve future training choices.

This allows failed practice to still have value.

---

## 6.12.25 — Training Logs

For significant training, the engine should preserve:

- Target
- Method
- Duration
- Instructor
- Difficulty
- Important results
- Breakthroughs
- Injuries
- Newly discovered weaknesses

Routine sessions can be compressed.

Long-term progression should remain explainable from accumulated activity.

---

## 6.12.26 — Repeated Training

Repeated identical training should eventually become less useful if the trainee has adapted to it.

The engine should encourage:

- Greater difficulty
- New drills
- Better opponents
- Improved methods
- More advanced instruction

Ruleset 6.16 governs plateaus and diminishing returns.

---

## 6.12.27 — Training Plans

Characters may create structured plans spanning multiple sessions.

A plan may specify:

- Goal
- Schedule
- Methods
- Instructor
- Milestones
- Rest days
- Evaluation points

Plans improve organization.

They do not guarantee success.

---

## 6.12.28 — Training During Time Compression

Routine training may be summarized over days or weeks.

The engine should still account for:

- Total time
- Training method
- Fatigue
- Missed sessions
- Instructor availability
- Significant setbacks
- Breakthroughs

Compression should not convert training into free background XP.

---

## 6.12.29 — NPC Training

NPCs use the same underlying framework.

Background NPC training may be abstracted from:

- Available time
- Goals
- Access
- Instructor quality
- Aptitude
- Current competence
- Motivation

Important NPC training should be simulated in greater detail.

---

## 6.12.30 — No Guaranteed Grind

The player should never be able to guarantee advancement merely by repeating the same input indefinitely.

Growth depends on:

- Quality of training
- Appropriate challenge
- Recovery
- Prerequisites
- Instruction
- Current competence
- Potential
- Time

Training should reward intelligent preparation and sustained effort rather than menu repetition.

---

## 6.12.31 — Governing Principle

A training action should answer:

> **What is this character deliberately doing to improve, under what conditions, and why would that activity plausibly produce the desired development?**

Training is an in-world activity.

It consumes time, uses resources, creates fatigue, requires appropriate methods, and produces results through the existing character-growth systems.

There is no abstract XP button.
